Chapter 289

Where Nothing Will Hit You

3,508 words · 15 min read

Kotori stood where nothing would hit her and where she could see everybody, and for once there was nobody to heal.

That was the rule of a duel. Nobody from outside the ring. You could watch, and that was all. No ting on the beat, no warmth into a shin a breath before the pike arrived. No standing on a post in a fen keeping five people alive. She’d brought two cups instead, one in each hand, for after. She’d stood at the edge of the seven silver lines, beside the ring, where the floor of the Seven Below was paler. Close enough to see their faces. Far enough that neither of them would have to think about her.

She’d never once watched Suzu fight a person and not been on the beat for him.

She’d only watched him do it once, in fact. In the fen, by a channel, in the first winter, from her post in the water. She’d been on the beat then, frightened out of her mind, keeping five people up while Nightjar came across the planks. She’d seen him go off the boardwalk into the black water on the right. She’d seen him come up beside the spear and the staff, and the scythe go round low and level, and two people go down at once. And then she’d seen his face in the green light, when the staff’s spell went off into the sky.

She’d never told anybody about the face. It hadn’t been hers to tell.

She looked at it now, across the ring.

The Seven Below was round and pale and very still. Seven silver lines meeting in a ring in the middle. Seven tunnels going out into the dark under the round town. In the north-east one, the old mark on the wall from a Falling Cut, a year and a half ago.

Xav in the ring with his blades out and down. Calm. The way he stood now, since the ditch. Not waiting. Just there.

And across from him, three paces off: Suzu.

Not calm. Not anything. Kotori had known him in here two and a half years, and she’d never been able to say what his face was doing. Ice said he could read everybody but Suzu. Kotori couldn’t read Suzu either. But she could see him. She’d always been able to see him. That was a different thing.

He had the scythe in both hands, low. Its long curved blade near the floor behind him, the edge Ina had put on it on Thursday catching the lamp. His long silver hair was over one shoulder. His eyes were about two thirds open.

That was more than usual.

He’d come past her on the way into the ring. He’d stopped beside her at the edge of the silver lines, the way he stopped beside her at the kettles in the Hart, and looked at the two cups.

“Don’t heal me,” said Suzu.

“I can’t,” said Kotori. “It’s a duel. Nobody from outside the ring.”

“I know.” He looked at her. “I mean don’t look like you want to.”

Kotori looked back at him. She’d stood behind him for two and a half years, on posts and stones and hills, keeping him up. She’d never once not wanted to.

“I’ll look however I like,” she said.

Suzu thought about that. Then, very slightly, he nodded. As though it were a fair answer, and he’d expected it, and was glad. And walked into the ring.

On the step, Malcolm had his notebook open to a clean page. At the top, in his small careful hand: SKETCH v. SUZUYUNN. And under it, already, two columns. X and S.

“Begin,” said Malcolm.

Xav didn’t move.

Kotori had known he wouldn’t. She’d heard about it from the step, for a year and a half. In the fourth fight Xav had stood still for four minutes at the start, and Ice had come all the way in and found nothing to meet. Oh. In the fifth, Ice had gone first so it wouldn’t happen again. Xav had learned to stand still. It was the thing he’d learned that Ice hadn’t given him. He’d got it off Malcolm, in a ditch full of moonbells.

So Xav stood still and waited, the way you’d wait by a moonbell for it to forget you were there.

And Suzu sat down.

On the floor of the Seven Below. In the ring, three paces from a man with two blades. He just folded up and sat, cross-legged, with the scythe across his knees, the way he sat on the north steps with Isa every morning. He looked up at Xav, about two thirds awake.

The step made a noise. A sort of collective gasp, like a lot of people forgetting to breathe all at once.

“He’s sitting,” said Leaka, not quietly enough.

“He’s sitting,” agreed RX. Faintly.

Kotori didn’t say anything. She stood at the edge of the silver lines with her two cups and her heart going.

Xav looked down at Suzu, sitting on the floor in front of him.

He didn’t move. That was the ditch. You held still until the thing forgot you were there. But the thing in front of him wasn’t a moonbell. It wasn’t doing anything that could forget. It was sitting on the floor with its eyes two thirds open, looking at him, doing exactly as much as he was. Which was nothing.

A minute.

Two.

Kotori watched Xav’s face. It had been calm at the start. It was still calm. But round the eyes, very slightly, something had started. Not worry. Something else. A man who’d come to a fight expecting a fight, and been given a floor.

Three minutes.

She could see what it was doing to him. She could see what it would do to anybody. The ditch only worked if somebody else had to move. If they both held still, nothing happened. Ever. Until one of them decided the nothing had gone on long enough.

It’ll be March, she thought. Malcolm’s line. She saw Malcolm’s pencil stop on the page.

At four minutes, Xav went.

He went the way he always went with Ice. The river.

Kotori had heard about it a hundred times. A breath ahead of where the other person was going to be. Already there before they moved, and then not, and then somewhere else. Ice had it off Lucan, on a bridge in Masmark. Xav had it off Ice, and had practised it on RX all down a road until RX dreamed about it. Xav came across the three paces in the river, beautiful, already where Suzu was going to get up to.

Suzu didn’t get up.

He didn’t go anywhere Xav was going to be. He stayed exactly where he was, sitting on the floor. The river went past him. Xav arrived, a breath early, at the place Suzu was going to stand up in. Suzu never stood up in it, and there was nobody there. Xav had to stop. Turn. Look down.

And Suzu wasn’t sitting there any more either.

Kotori saw it. She’d been looking at him, not at Xav, because she was a healer and she watched the person who’d be hurt. And it wasn’t Suzu who was going to be hurt. She saw him go. Not up. Sideways and back, on the floor, one long roll on the pale stone, out of the place Xav had just turned to look at. Into the place behind Xav’s left heel. Where Xav had just been, a breath ago, before the river took him somewhere else.

The place nobody was looking.

He came up on one knee there, with the scythe already going.

Shhhhk.

Low and level, the way it had gone over the black water in the fen. The way it went through moonbells and Unwatched and grass. Behind Xav’s heels, across the backs of both legs at once, in one stroke.

A soft green light came up off the cut and ran back along the scythe’s blade into Suzu’s hands. Kotori knew that one. One who cuts a wide row, and gathers what falls. Every cut he made, the scythe gave a little back. Not much. A breath of it. It didn’t matter on Unwatched. It mattered in a duel.

Xav turned round.

He’d gone very still. Not the ditch. Something else. He looked at Suzu, on one knee behind him with the scythe across his body. And Kotori saw his face do a thing she’d only ever seen it do in front of Ice. Once, in the fourth fight, at the start, when Ice came all the way in and found nothing.

Oh.

After that, Xav tried everything.

Kotori watched him do it. She’d never seen so much of Xav at once. In front of Ice, Xav had always had a plan. Something new he’d brought: the river, the rain, the one after, the ditch. He’d lay it out over the fight like a map and follow it. You could see the map, if you’d watched enough. Kotori had heard about it from the step for a year and a half.

He didn’t have a map for Suzu. Nobody did. So he opened everything he had, one after the other, as fast as he could, to see which one would work.

The river again. Suzu wasn’t where it was going.

The rain. Three cuts that weren’t meant, from three sides, and the fourth from wherever you weren’t looking. It was what Ice had brought out of a war, and Xav had learned to do it back, reading Val’s page. Kotori saw the three come in at Suzu, fast, light, from everywhere.

Suzu didn’t watch them. He didn’t read them. He didn’t read the one after either, the way Xav did, the way Val’s page said. He just wasn’t in any of the four places. Not because he’d read where they’d be. Because he’d gone somewhere nobody would put a cut, because it was a silly place to stand. Right up close, inside Xav’s elbow, too close for the fourth cut to land. Where there was no room for anything. Where it was least effort, because nothing could reach.

Shhhhk.

Short, this time. The butt of the snath, not the blade. Into Xav’s ribs, under his arm, from no distance at all.

“He’s inside,” said Gabe, on the step, very loudly. “He’s inside his arm. How is he inside his arm—“

“Gabe,” said Rae.

Kotori didn’t look at the step. She was looking at Suzu’s face.

It had happened. She’d been waiting for it since he sat down. Since before that, since the Wednesday at Hobb’s table, since the back room at Melaka Raya, out there, when Ice said you’d have beaten him. She’d been waiting for it for two and a half years, if she was honest. Since a green light in a fen.

At the corner of his mouth, very slightly. About a millimetre.

Then a bit more than that.

Xav got him. Twice.

Kotori was glad. She’d have been frightened if he hadn’t. She’d have thought something was wrong with the world. Xav was the best she’d ever seen in a ring, apart from one, and he’d drawn with that one in January. He got Suzu once on the rain, a cut from the left that Suzu had to take on the snath. It skidded off and opened his shoulder. Tsk. [ Suzuyunn · 77% ] And once at the end of a river. Suzu had rolled out of it a hair late, and Xav’s right blade went across his back. [ Suzuyunn · 58% ]

But every time Xav got him, Suzu was somewhere else after. Nowhere anybody would have put him. Behind Xav’s heels. Inside his elbow. Once, flat on his back on the floor of the Seven Below. Xav’s two blades went through the air over him where his chest would have been if he’d been standing up like a sensible person. He wasn’t. He was lying down, and the scythe came up off the floor across Xav’s knees from below.

“He lay down,” said RX, in a voice of real despair. “He lay down in the fight.”

“Least effort,” said Kotori.

She hadn’t meant to say it out loud. It came out of her the way thank you did now. It was true, so it came out.

RX looked at her. Everybody on the step looked at her.

“That’s what he says,” said Kotori. She went a bit red. She didn’t take her eyes off the ring. “He goes where it’s least effort. Everybody’s looking somewhere. That’s where all the effort is. So he goes where they aren’t.” She swallowed. “He’s not reading Xav. He’s not reading anything. He’s just lazy. Properly.”

On the step, Hyv let out a long slow breath. Like somebody who’d been trying to call a thing for two years and had just been told why she couldn’t.

It ended at eleven minutes.

Kotori saw it coming, the way she saw the hit coming to somebody she was healing. A breath before. Xav, at twenty-one per cent, came in with everything he had left. The river and the rain together, both blades, a breath early and from everywhere at once. It was the most beautiful thing she’d ever seen him do. If Suzu had been anywhere anybody would think to be, it would have been over.

Suzu sat down.

Again. In the middle of it. Just dropped onto the floor of the ring, cross-legged, under all of it. Both blades and the river and the rain went over his head through the place he’d been standing.

And Xav, with nowhere to put his weight, came down past him. Onto one knee, off balance, his blades out wide, in front of a man sitting on the floor with a scythe across his lap.

The scythe didn’t move far. It didn’t need to. Suzu just turned it a little, so that the blade’s long curve lay along the back of Xav’s neck. Not touching. Resting there, the way you’d rest a hand.

Nobody on the step breathed.

Xav, on one knee, with the scythe along his neck, looked at the floor of the Seven Below for a long time.

“Yield,” said Xav.

The step went off.

Kotori didn’t hear most of it. She was walking into the ring with the two cups, because the bout was over and nobody had said she couldn’t. Malcolm was saying one, Suzu, eleven minutes out loud as he wrote it. Hobb, at the top of the stair, said something about his slate in a very small voice. Leaka was standing on the step. Gabe had his hands on his head.

At the top of the stair, Hobb the turnip carter had his slate against the wall and his chalk out. He was crossing things off with a hand that wasn’t quite steady. Sketch 2–0 · evens, with a line through it. Most of the round town’s money had been on that line. Below it, the others, still standing. And at the very bottom, in very small writing, EVEN · 40 to 1, with one name beside it.

Hobb looked at the name for a long time. Then he looked down the stair at the man sitting cross-legged in the middle of the ring with a cup of tea, and went the colour of a turnip.

Xav was sitting on the floor of the ring where he’d gone down, laughing.

Not the laugh from the fifth fight, against the wall, the one that wasn’t quite a laugh. A real one. Out loud, on the floor of the Seven Below, with his blades beside him and his face in his hands.

Kotori gave him a cup. He took it without looking and kept laughing.

She gave the other one to Suzu.

Suzu was still sitting cross-legged in the middle of the ring with the scythe across his knees. He took the cup and drank half of it. He looked up at her.

“Thank you,” said Suzu.

“You lay down,” said Kotori. “In a fight. On the floor.”

“Yes.”

“You sat down. Twice.”

“It was least effort,” said Suzu.

She looked at his face for a long time.

“You’re having the best time of your life,” said Kotori.

Suzu thought about it. She watched him do it. It took a while.

“Yes,” he said.

Xav stopped laughing eventually. He put the cup down on the floor and looked across at Suzu.

“Why do you sit down,” he said.

“Because you’re looking at where I’m going to stand up,” said Suzu.

Xav stared at him.

“Everybody looks at where you’re going to be,” said Suzu. “You look a breath ahead. You got it off Ice. He got it off Lucan.” He drank the rest of the tea. “So I don’t go anywhere. And then I go where you were, a breath ago. Because you’re not looking there any more.”

Xav didn’t say anything for a long time.

“You’re reading me,” he said.

“No,” said Suzu. “I don’t read people. It’s effort.” He put the cup down. “I’m just not where you’re looking.”

Kotori looked up the stair. At the top, in the corner by the hatch behind Ama Corrie’s counter, where the lamp didn’t quite reach, Ice was standing in the dark. Nobody on the step had looked up there. Kotori could see him, because she always stood where she could see everybody.

He wasn’t smiling. He was doing something else. It looked like a man who’d been waiting a long time to see a thing, and was seeing it, and didn’t want to miss any of it.

On the floor of the ring, Xav picked up his blades. He got up. He looked down at Suzu, still sitting.

“Two more,” said Xav.

“Best of three,” agreed Suzu.

“I’m going to stop looking,” said Xav.

Suzu looked up at him. Kotori saw it. For a moment, at the corner of his mouth, it went past a millimetre and kept going.

“I know,” said Suzu. “I watched you get RX.”
