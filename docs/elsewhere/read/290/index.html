Chapter 290

On Time

3,363 words · 15 min read

Xav had spent a year and a half learning to be early, and he had five minutes to learn to be on time.

He sat on the floor of the ring between bouts with Kotori’s cup in his hands and thought about it. Not the fight. The fight was gone. Eleven minutes, and a scythe resting on the back of his neck like a hand, and yield. He’d been taken apart by a man who sat down. He’d laughed until his ribs hurt, which they did anyway, where the snath had gone in.

He thought about October.

The first one. A year and a half ago, in this room. He’d stood in the ring at Lv 41 with two blades and a sketchbook full of things he’d learned off Dec. He’d gone first, because he always went first then. Dec had stood there and let him come, and he’d landed three hits in the whole fight. Three. And afterwards he’d sat against the wall and thought, I’ll get there. And he had. Nine in January, fourteen in May, nineteen in September. Twenty-seven in January this year, against the wall, both at one per cent. Closer. Yes. Again. Yes.

He’d thought he knew what it felt like to be a long way off and get closer.

He’d been wrong. He’d been close to Dec for a year and a half. He’d never once been this far from anybody.

Oh, he’d said, on his knee, with the scythe on his neck. The same oh as the fourth fight, when Dec came all the way in and found nothing. He’d thought he’d never feel that again. He’d learned to stand still so nobody could do it to him twice.

Suzu had done it to him by sitting down.

He looked up the stair.

He didn’t know why. He hadn’t looked up the stair in any of his fights with Dec. You didn’t look up the stair. Everybody was on the step, below it. RX, Malcolm, Gabe. Hyvred on the bottom step with her knees up. The step was where people watched from.

He looked up it now.

At the top, behind Ama Corrie’s counter, in the corner by the hatch, where the lamp didn’t quite reach: Dec. Standing in the dark with his shield on his back. Not on the step with everybody. Up there, on his own, looking down.

Xav looked at him for a long time.

He’d never once seen anybody in that corner. Not in five fights. He’d never looked.

He looked at Suzu. Suzu was still sitting cross-legged in the middle of the ring with his empty cup, his scythe across his knees, eyes about two thirds open. Looking at nothing in particular. He didn’t look up the stair. He didn’t need to. Xav understood it then, the way you understand something you’ve been told a hundred times and never heard.

You think nobody sees the corner, somebody had said to somebody. He didn’t know who, or when. But he knew, all at once, who’d been standing up there in the dark for every one of his fights with Dec. Watching. Not on the step. Where nobody looked.

Five fights. Every one. The river and the rain and the one after and the ditch. All of it, from the corner by the hatch. Everything Xav had ever done in this room.

And Xav had never once seen Suzu do anything at all.

RX came down off the step to the edge of the silver lines. He didn’t come into the ring. He crouched at the edge of it with his elbows on his knees and looked at Xav.

“You’re going to do the RX thing,” said RX.

“The what?”

“The forty-first. Thursday morning. When you didn’t go left.” RX touched his ear, where the training stick had arrived. “You stood there and put the stick where I was going to be—“ He stopped. “No. Where I was. You put it where I was. I walked into it.”

“I don’t know if it’ll work on him.”

“It worked on me,” said RX. “After forty times in the ear.” He stood up. “You owe me forty ears.”

“I’ll buy you a chestnut.”

“Forty chestnuts,” said RX, and went back to the step.

“Ready?” said Malcolm, from the step.

Xav put the cup down on the floor. He got up and picked up his blades.

Don’t look for him where he’s going to be, Dec had said. Against the wall, a fortnight ago.

He’d thought it was advice. It wasn’t. It was the only way in.

“Ready,” said Xav.

Suzu sat down.

Xav had known he would. He’d have bet a silver on it, at Hobb’s, at any odds. Suzu folded up on the floor of the ring at begin, cross-legged, scythe across his knees, and looked up at him. Exactly as he had in the first one.

Xav sat down too.

He didn’t think about it. If he’d thought about it he’d have stood there being still in the ditch way. Holding still till the thing forgot him, waiting for it to move. It hadn’t worked. Suzu didn’t forget anything. He didn’t have to. He wasn’t waiting for Xav to move. He was waiting for Xav to look.

So Xav sat down on the floor of the Seven Below, three paces from Suzu, cross-legged, with his blades across his knees. And didn’t look anywhere.

Not at where Suzu was going to be. Not a breath ahead. Not at the place he’d roll to, or the place behind his heel, or inside his elbow. He looked at Suzu. At where Suzu was. Sitting on the floor in front of him, about two thirds awake. That was all.

On the step, there was a silence. Then a noise like a lot of people trying very hard not to laugh.

“They’re both sitting,” said Leaka. In a whisper that went right round the room.

“It’ll be March,” said Malcolm. Not to anybody. To his notebook. And then, after a moment, as though he’d surprised himself: “No. It won’t.”

Suzu looked at Xav for a long time.

Xav looked back.

It was the strangest thing he’d ever done in a ring. It wasn’t the ditch. The ditch was patient; you were waiting for something. This wasn’t waiting. He wasn’t expecting Suzu to do anything. He wasn’t expecting anything at all. He was just sitting on a floor with somebody, looking at them, the way you’d sit with a friend on a step.

And Suzu, who went where nobody was looking, found that Xav wasn’t looking anywhere. Every place in the room was a place Xav wasn’t looking. And none of them was, because he was only looking at Suzu.

Xav saw it land. He didn’t know how. Something very small happened in Suzu’s face, at the corner of his mouth.

Not a millimetre.

The other way.

Suzu got up.

He didn’t have to. Xav knew it. He could have sat there till March, the laziest man in the round town, on a cold floor, for nothing. He’d have been fine. He got up anyway. Xav understood why about a breath before he moved, and it nearly made him laugh again. He got up because he wanted to fight. Because sitting there wasn’t fun any more, and fighting was.

He came in low, the way he had over the black water in the fen. The wide row. The scythe going round at knee height in a long flat arc, with the whole of him behind it, from the left.

Xav didn’t look at where it was going. He looked at where it was.

And put his left blade there.

Not where the arc would end. Not where Suzu would be after. Where the scythe’s blade was, right now, in the air, coming. He put his blade in its way, not to block it. The way he’d put the training stick where RX’s ear was about to arrive at, on the forty-first time, on Thursday morning, without going left.

Tock.

The scythe hit his blade and stopped. Not turned; stopped. For one breath, Suzu was at the end of a stroke that hadn’t finished, with the whole of his weight in it, in front of a man sitting on the floor.

Xav’s right blade went across his forearm.

Suzu rolled away. Of course he did. He rolled out of it into the place behind Xav, where Xav had been a breath ago. Except Xav hadn’t been anywhere a breath ago. He’d been sitting there. He was still sitting there. The place behind him was just more floor, and Suzu came up on it on one knee and found Xav already turned round. Looking at him. At where he was.

Tock. Blade into blade.

On the step, somebody made a sound that wasn’t a word. Gabe, probably. Xav didn’t look. He’d stopped looking at anything but where Suzu was.

He heard Hyvred, though. Everybody did. On the bottom step, very quietly, the way she said things she’d only just seen.

“Oh,” said Hyvred. And then, louder, to nobody, to the whole room: “Oh. You don’t look where he’ll be. You look where he is. And then he’s got nowhere to go that you’re not looking.” A breath. “I could call that.”

Nobody answered her. She didn’t call anything. She sat on the bottom step with her hands over her mouth, watching. Like somebody who’d been trying to read a page for two years and had just been handed the first word.

It was nineteen minutes.

He’d remember all of it, afterwards. Every bit. More than any of the Dec fights, which were a blur of beats and waiting and BOOM. This was different. This was the first fight of his life where he’d never once been early.

He stood up, at about three minutes, because you couldn’t fight a whole bout sitting down. And after that he fought on time. He looked at where Suzu was and put a blade there, and Suzu went somewhere else, and he looked at where Suzu was again. It was like learning to walk. Every time he caught himself starting to look a breath ahead, at the place Suzu would be, Suzu wasn’t there. The scythe came out of the place he’d stopped looking at. Shhhhk. And he’d lose ten per cent, and remember.

Suzu’s face had changed. Xav didn’t know what it was doing. Kotori would have. But it was doing more than it had in the first one. It was doing something all the time now, not just at the corner of the mouth. Xav had seen it once before, on a different face. Against a wall in September, sitting beside Dec, looking at him, the small sudden one. The face of somebody who’d just been given something they hadn’t had in a long time.

Somebody good enough that you had to use all of it.

At eleven minutes, Suzu lay down again. The way he had in the first one. Flat on his back on the floor of the Seven Below, under both of Xav’s blades. The scythe was coming up off the floor across his knees from below.

Xav didn’t cut where Suzu’s chest would have been if he’d been standing up like a sensible person.

He looked down. At where Suzu was. Lying on the floor.

And put his foot on the snath.

Tock, again, but with a boot. The scythe pinned flat against the pale stone of the Seven Below, under Xav’s heel, before it had come up an inch. Suzu lay on his back under it, holding the other end, and looked up at him.

For a moment neither of them moved.

Then Suzu laughed.

Out loud. On his back on the floor of the ring, with a boot on his scythe. Xav had never once heard him do it. Not in the round town, not on the stream. He didn’t think anybody had, in here. It was quiet and short and completely surprised, and it went up off the floor of the Seven Below and round the room. On the step, everybody stopped breathing.

Xav had cut him while he was laughing. He hadn’t been able to help it.

It got hard after that. Harder than anything Xav had ever done.

Suzu stopped sitting down. He stopped lying down. He’d worked out, Xav thought, that Xav was looking at where he was. That meant there was no place in the room Xav wasn’t looking, because Xav was looking at him. So he stopped going anywhere silly. He just fought. Properly. The scythe going round in its long wide arcs, high and low. The snath coming in short from no distance. The Glean taking a breath back off every cut.

It was the best Xav had ever seen anybody fight who wasn’t Dec. It might have been better. He didn’t know. He didn’t have time to think about it.

He just kept looking at where Suzu was.

At nineteen minutes, Suzu came in with the wide row at knee height, the way he’d come in at the start. Xav put his left blade where the scythe was. Tock. And his right where Suzu was. Not where he’d go. Where he was: at the end of a stroke that hadn’t finished, with all his weight in it, one breath, open.

Suzu stood very still, at the end of the stroke, with Xav’s right blade resting along the side of his neck. Not touching. The way the scythe had rested on Xav’s, in the first one.

He looked at Xav for a long time. Then he looked down at his own scythe, stopped against Xav’s left blade.

“Yield,” said Suzu.

[ Bout two · Sketch ]

Xav sat down on the floor.

He hadn’t meant to. His legs just went. He sat down in the middle of the ring with both blades in his lap and looked at the floor of the Seven Below. He could hear the step going off round him, all at once. Gabe roaring. Leaka screaming. RX saying he got him, he GOT him, he got him on time over and over. Hobb at the top of the stair saying something desperate about his slate.

He didn’t look at any of it.

Suzu sat down across from him. Cross-legged, three paces off, with his scythe across his knees. Breathing hard, which Xav had never once seen him do. His long silver hair was all over his face. He pushed it back.

Kotori came into the ring with two cups. She gave one to each of them and didn’t say anything and went back to the edge of the silver lines.

“One each,” said Xav.

“Yes,” said Suzu.

“In October,” said Xav. He was looking at the cup. “A year and a half ago. The first one. If you’d been in the ring instead of Dec.” He made himself say it. “You’d have beaten me.”

Suzu drank some tea. He thought about it. He always did now.

“Yes,” he said.

“Easily.”

“Yes.”

“You were in the corner,” said Xav. “By the hatch. Every time. I never looked.”

“Nobody looks at the corner.”

“Dec does.”

“Dec sees everything I do,” said Suzu. “He just can’t tell where I’ll do it.” He looked at Xav over the cup. “Neither could you. Till today.”

Xav looked at him.

“I still can’t,” he said. “I just stopped trying.”

“Yes,” said Suzu. “That’s the only way.”

And there it was. At the corner of his mouth. Not a millimetre. Not anything small. It went right across his face, and stayed. Xav had never seen it before. He didn’t think anybody had, except maybe once, in the green light in a fen, from a post in the water.

“Good,” said Suzu.

Up the stair, in the corner by the hatch, Dec was still standing in the dark. Xav looked up at him. He didn’t decide to. He just did, the way he’d looked up after the first one.

Dec nodded. Once. The way he’d nodded at Kei’s list for the longest night, a long time ago. The way you’d nod at something that had gone exactly where you’d known it would.

On the step, Malcolm wrote 1 – 1 in his notebook, and underlined it twice, and then sat looking at it for a long time with his pencil in the air. He didn’t say it’ll be March. Xav thought it was the first time in his life he hadn’t.

Xav got out his sketchbook.

He didn’t decide to. His hands just did it. He sat in the middle of the ring between bouts with Kotori’s cup on the floor beside him and drew. Quickly, badly, a few lines. A man sitting cross-legged on a floor with a scythe across his knees and his hair pushed back. Smiling.

He got it right. First time. He’d never once got Dec right in a year and a half.

Suzu looked at the page upside down.

“That’s not what I look like,” he said.

“It is now,” said Xav.
