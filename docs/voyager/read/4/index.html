Regressor Diary of a Voyager · Volume 1: Thirty-Six Circles

Chapter 4

Heads

3,557 words · 15 min read

The light came down on Yan like warm water.

It fell from somewhere above the room, somewhere the ceiling should have been, a single straight column of gold, not white. It landed on him so gently that for a moment he thought he’d imagined it. Then he looked down at his hands and they were gilded, every line of his palms traced in faint gold, and the weight round his neck tightened.

Yun, said Suzu, in his head. Something is touching you.

“I know,” Yan murmured.

I don’t like it.

“I know.”

Tell it to stop.

“I don’t think it takes requests.”

Across the room, six other columns had come down at the same moment, and the whole ring had gone quiet to watch them. Yan counted them because he counted everything. One on the tall man, Deston, who’d met the light with the expression of someone being handed a clipboard he hadn’t asked for. One on Hana, whose crown was suddenly gold all over. One on the girl in the blue jersey, who’d looked up from her window with her mouth open in annoyance, as if the light had interrupted her mid-sentence. One on a man near the back, short and quiet and very still, with the face of someone used to waiting before he moved; Yan knew that face from Deston’s stories, and Gabe’s, and Ice’s. Xavier. One on Chris, who’d raised both hands as if under arrest. One on Hyvred, who’d finally stopped crying and was now staring up into the column falling on her with her mismatched golden eyes very wide, the left one with the little red reticle in it locking and unlocking.

[ Party Head ]

The window opened over his gilded hands. Then a second line, slower:

[ Choose your party. A party is five. Touch a Player’s shoulder to ask; they may accept or refuse. When every Player belongs to a party, the way will open. ]

“Okay,” said Chris, loudly, from the far side. “Okay. Okay. So it’s a draft.”

Nobody moved for about five seconds.

Yan had been in drafts before. He’d been the one people picked first, often, the one who called rotations and carried the last circle. He knew the particular silence before a draft, when everybody’s doing arithmetic and nobody wants to be seen doing it. He’d never been in one where the prize was surviving. He found it was exactly the same silence.

Suzu shifted on his collarbone, cool and dry. You’re doing the thing, she said. Where you think too long.

“I’m doing maths.”

Do it faster.

He did it faster.

Thirty-five people. Seven heads. Twenty-eight places. A lot of people in this room didn’t know each other. He’d known that the moment he woke up and looked around; Ice’s friends had always come in clusters, and the clusters had only met in the middle, in calls where Ice was the one holding everything together. The group from Singapore, the ones who called him Declan. The circle round Hana. The trio crowd. The newest friends, who’d come through Maigo. A group of five girls he’d never seen before in his life, one of whom was the girl in blue, now standing in a golden light looking as if she’d like to punch it.

And Yan’s own people. His squad. He found them without looking: Salty with his arm still round Wendy; Maigo sitting cross-legged in his circle with his hood up and his sunglasses on, absolutely silent; Niko standing a little apart with his hands in his pockets, thin and calm and put together, as if this were a slightly unusual Tuesday.

His four. The four he’d pick without thinking. That was a full party.

He didn’t move yet.

“Can I say something?”

Yan looked round. A short man with very big arms, Salty, had raised one hand, as if they were in a classroom.

“I’ve read this,” Salty said. “Not this. But I’ve read a lot of this. Tower, floors, party system. Tutorial floors, then it gets bad.”

“Same,” said Jared, from across the room, without much enthusiasm. “Hundred floors, they always start you off soft. First couple floors you’re killing rabbits. Then it’s not rabbits.”

“Every tower has a tutorial,” said the man in the green haori, Kei, with the little green snake on his wrist, very calmly. He’d known Kei for years. Yan had recognised the voice before he’d recognised the face; the face had been on Kei’s stream every night for three. “And the tutorial’s always a lie. It’s easy so you’ll be brave. Then it isn’t easy and you’re brave anyway.”

“So don’t be brave,” said Ina, smoothly. Of everyone in the room, Ina looked most like the thing people would be afraid of, a single purple horn rising out of his white hair and a grinning grey oni mask hanging from his hip. He sounded like a man reading the news in an old library. “That’s the lesson of every one of those stories, as far as I can tell. The brave ones are the first ones to go. Be cowardly. Be cowardly for months.”

“That’s terrible advice,” said Hako cheerfully, a tall, athletic man in his early twenties who looked about three hours away from optimism being his entire personality. “I love it.”

“We’re isekai’d,” said Calvin, from Deston’s side of the room, in a voice of total delight. “We are so isekai’d. Dest. Dest. This is literally a transmigration novel.”

“I know, Cal.”

“Like, we transmigrated. That’s what this is.”

“I know.”

“I’ve wanted this my whole life and I’m going to throw up.”

“Please don’t,” said Deston.

Yan saw Deston glance at Gabe as Kei said the tutorial’s always a lie. Gabe looked back. Something went between them that Yan couldn’t read, something private, with weight to it. Then Deston looked away and his hand went to his shirt pocket and stayed there.

In his circle, Maigo pushed back his hood.

He was very small, bark-brown, with two little branch antlers sprouting leaves above his glowing white eyes. He looked round at the room until people noticed him looking. Maigo could talk for forty minutes about a sandwich when he felt like it, and sit through a whole evening on a call without a word when he didn’t. Since he’d woken up he’d said one word. Yan had been watching that, quietly, the way he watched everything.

“Pick people you’d go back for,” Maigo said.

It wasn’t loud. It went through the room anyway.

Then he put his hood back up and went back to watching.

Deston moved first.

He walked across the ring to the three people from his old life who knew nobody else in the room: a woman with a permanent frown, a man who’d been talking with his hands since he woke up, and Calvin, still grinning. He touched each of them on the shoulder. Each of them said yes without thinking about it, and Yan watched the gold on Deston’s hands spread to each of them, a thin line of light from his palm to their shoulder that faded into a small golden mark.

[ Deston’s party · 4 / 5 ]

Deston turned to Gabe. Of course he did. Yan could see it from across the room: the obvious choice, the brother. Gabe was already stepping forward.

And Deston stopped.

He looked at Gabe for a second, and then past Gabe at Xav, who was standing in his own column of gold with his hands in his pockets, looking at the painted-over door again. Yan saw Deston’s face do something complicated.

“Gabe,” Deston said. “Go with Xav.”

Gabe stopped dead. “What?”

“Go with Xav.”

“Dest—“

“If Declan’s out there,” Deston said, quietly enough that Yan had to lean to hear it, “Xav’s going to go looking for him. You know he is. Look at him. He’s already looking.” He nodded at Xav’s back. “Somebody has to be with him when he does. And you’re going to look too. So you might as well look together.”

Gabe stared at him. Then at Xav. Xav had turned round and was watching them both, with no expression at all.

“And who’s going to be with you?” said Gabe.

“I’ll be fine. I’ll have Rae. She’ll keep me alive out of spite.”

“I will,” said Rachael, from behind him. “Out of nothing but spite.”

Xav crossed the room. He didn’t say anything; Xav often didn’t. He put his hand on Gabe’s shoulder, lightly, and waited.

Gabe looked at the hand for a long moment. Then at Deston.

“You owe me,” he said.

“Yeah,” said Deston.

“Yes,” said Gabe, to Xav, and the gold went from Xav’s palm into his shoulder.

The rest of Xav’s choices were obvious, and fast. The quick-eyed one, Rui Xiang. The tall confident one, John. The one who’d had his face in his hands since the light came on, Malcolm, who looked up when Xav touched his shoulder and said, “Sure. Yeah. We’re all going to die, but sure,” and then let out a breath and nodded properly. Five.

That left one of the Singapore crowd standing on his own: a big, broad man with an easy, sleepy face who’d been asleep through most of the first ten minutes. He looked round at Xav’s full party, and at Deston’s party of four, and spread his hands.

“And so,” he announced, to the room in general, “in a shocking twist, the hero of our story is left without a party. The crowd gasps. The music swells—“

“Azac,” said Deston. “Come here.”

“The music swells,” said Azac, crossing the room, “as he is selected at the very last moment by his oldest and tallest friend.”

“I’m going to regret this,” said Deston, and touched his shoulder.

[ Deston’s party · 5 / 5 ]

The girl in the blue jersey moved next, and it was harder to watch.

She went straight to the lavender-haired girl with gills, Xola, and to the girl with the short brown bob and the heart-shaped tuft who’d finally caught her penguin and was holding it in both arms like a baby. They said yes before she’d finished asking. Then a girl with very long, wavy dark-indigo hair and a big white ribbon, who looked at the girl in blue with calm, half-lidded eyes for a moment before nodding.

[ Althea’s party · 4 / 5 ]

Then the girl in blue stopped.

The other girl from that group, long silver hair with a lilac sheen, forget-me-nots pinned in it, stood a little way off with her arms crossed. She hadn’t moved toward the light. She hadn’t moved away from it either. She was standing very straight, the way people stand when they’re braced for something and want it over with.

The girl in blue looked at her.

Yan didn’t know them. He didn’t know anything about them, except what he could see: whatever this was, it had started a long time before this room. Xola had gone very still. The girl with the penguin had shut her eyes.

The girl in blue looked away. She crossed the room instead to the man with the 88EXP nameplate, Drew, and touched his shoulder.

“We’ve talked,” she said. Her voice was very bright, very pleasant, and not quite steady. “On calls. You’re nice. Please.”

Drew looked at her, then past her at the silver-haired girl, and something in his easy face went very gentle. He’d seen exactly what had just happened. Yan watched him decide not to say anything about it.

“Yeah,” he said. “Of course.”

[ Althea’s party · 5 / 5 ]

The silver-haired girl’s arms tightened over her chest. She looked at the ground. And Hyvred, standing in her own column of gold not three circles away, said loudly and immediately and with no hesitation at all:

“Gigi! With me! Come here, come here, come here.”

The silver-haired girl looked up. Hyvred was already halfway across the gap, horns and crimson streaks and tail swinging, and she grabbed both of Gigi’s hands without asking. The gold went into her shoulder so fast it was almost a flash.

“Yes,” said Gigi, a full second after it had already happened.

“Great. Perfect. You’re mine now. Bee, you too, obviously. Kei! Kei, are you with me? You’re with me.” Hyvred was talking very fast, the way people do when they’ve stopped crying and need something to do with all the leftover feeling. “And— oh. Oh, Hako. You know my sight thing. You were in that call. Please?”

Hako grinned as if someone had handed him a present. “Absolutely.”

[ Hyvred’s party · 5 / 5 ]

Hana took her people next, quietly and without fuss, the way she seemed to do everything. Milli first, obviously; she didn’t even let go of his hand to do it. Jared, who’d already drifted over. Aleaka, whose fox tail knocked over nothing, because there was nothing in the white room to knock over, and still somehow managed to look as if it had. And then, after a moment’s thought, a girl in a hoodie with an easy grin and a chin that said she would argue with God, standing next to Chris.

“Newish,” said Hana. “You know me. You’re in my chat all the time. You’re rude to my mods.”

“They deserve it,” said Newish.

“Will you come?”

Newish glanced at Chris. Chris made a little shooing gesture with both hands. “Go, go. You’ll be great. Be rude to everything.”

“I’m always rude to everything,” said Newish, and let Hana touch her shoulder.

[ Hanarisu’s party · 5 / 5 ]

Which left Yan, and Chris.

Chris came across the room to him, scratching the back of his head, looking slightly apologetic.

“So,” said Chris. “I’ve been doing maths.”

“Me too,” said Yan.

“I think we’ve done the same maths.” Chris counted on his fingers. “I’ve got Winter. I’ve got Ina.” The two of them were standing behind him, horns and silver and white and lavender, both looking at Yan with an expression Yan recognised: don’t make this weird. “Hako went with Hyvred, which, fair. Newish went with Hana, which, also fair. So I need two. And I don’t know anyone else in this room except you, and your lot.” He gestured across the ring at Salty, Wendy, Maigo and Niko. “Sorry. It’s not a great draft position.”

Yan looked at his squad.

He’d known it was coming since Hyvred said Hako. He’d watched the arithmetic close in like a circle in a final round. It was still harder than he’d expected.

Salty and Wendy were a pair; you didn’t split them. Salty looked back at him with his jaw set, already knowing, already not liking it. Wendy, next to him, gave Yan a small, tired smile: we know.

Maigo sat in his circle with his hood up, very small and very quiet.

Niko stood with his hands in his pockets.

Pick people you’d go back for, Maigo had said.

Yan would go back for every single one of them. That wasn’t the problem. The problem was that seven heads could only stand in seven places.

Yun, said Suzu softly in his head. You’re doing the thing again.

“I know,” Yan said.

He walked to Niko first, because Niko would make it easy. Niko always did. He was thin and short and looked as if a strong wind would take him, and he’d never once in his life been knocked off balance by anything Yan had seen.

“Niko,” Yan said quietly.

“I know,” said Niko pleasantly. “Go on.”

“Will you go with Chris?”

Niko looked at Chris, then back at Yan. The corner of his mouth went up very slightly.

“Sure,” he said. “He seems like he’ll need someone who doesn’t talk.”

“Hey,” said Chris. “Fair. But hey.”

Yan went to Maigo next. He crouched so they were level. Maigo looked at him from under the hood, white eyes glowing faintly behind the pixel sunglasses.

“Maigo.”

“Yeah.”

“Will you go with Chris? I’ll be one party over. I’ll see you every day.”

Maigo looked at him for a long moment.

“Yeah,” he said. “Okay.”

And then, because he was Maigo, and Maigo always said the thing that mattered when it mattered, he added, very quietly, just for Yan: “You’d come back for me.”

“I’d come back for you.”

“Okay.” He stood up. He came about to Yan’s elbow. He walked across to Chris and held up his arm for Chris to touch his shoulder, because Chris would have had to stoop. “Hi. I’m Maigo. I’m a tree.”

“Hi, Maigo,” said Chris, with enormous seriousness. “I’m Chris. I’m a guy.”

[ Chris’s party · 5 / 5 ]

So Yan stood in the middle of the room with two of his four and looked at who was left.

There were two. He’d known there would be. They were standing a little way apart from each other, the last two people without a gold mark on their shoulder: a man with messy chestnut hair, blue-grey eyes and an XX mark under one of them, in an open teal coat with a tie, standing there grinning as if being left until last were the funniest thing that had ever happened to him; and a man in a dark indigo jacket with round wire glasses and a small glass ink vial on a chain, standing very upright with his hands behind his back, looking as if he’d been here before and wasn’t impressed then either.

Tiger, and Cursiffe.

Yan knew them both: Tiger through Maigo, Cursiffe through Jared, both on calls, in games, never more than a voice and a name. He’d never in his life been in a party with either. He looked at them, and they looked back, and he knew that they knew they were the last two picks of the last party in the draft.

“Sorry,” said Yan. “That wasn’t an insult.”

“Oh, I know,” said Tiger. “It’s maths. Somebody had to be last.” He grinned wider. “Might as well be the fastest guy here. Saves time.”

“Are you the fastest guy here?”

“No idea. Probably. I’ve got legs like a tiger now. Look at them.” He looked down at himself. “I don’t know what that means, actually. I’ll let you know.”

Cursiffe inclined his head very slightly, like a man accepting a cup of tea he hadn’t asked for. “I’d have picked me last too,” he said. “I’m an acquired taste.”

Yan touched Tiger’s shoulder, and then Cursiffe’s. The gold went out of his hands for the last time and settled into them, and the column of light over him faded and went out, and he was just a man standing in a white room again, with a snake round his neck.

[ Suzuyun’s party · 5 / 5 ]

Salty clapped him on the back hard enough to rock him. “Good draft,” he said gruffly.

“It was a terrible draft.”

“Yeah. But you did it right.”

You gave away the quiet one, said Suzu.

“Maigo’s not the quiet one,” Yan said under his breath.

He is now.

Yan didn’t answer that. He looked across the room. Maigo was standing with his new party, next to Chris, looking up at Winter with what seemed to be polite interest, and saying nothing at all.

The last gold mark faded into the last shoulder, and every light in the room chimed together, one soft note.

“All Players belong to a party,” said thirty-five voices. “The way is open.”

On the far side of the ring, the faint, painted-over shape in the white wall began to brighten.

Yan watched it come back. The round top first, a line of light drawing itself in the air, then the sides, then the edges clouding from white to clear like breath leaving a cold window. Behind it there was green. Grass. A slope. A sky.

Gabe was already walking toward it. Xav was already beside him. Neither of them was running, and both of them looked as if it was costing them something not to.

He went that way, said Suzu, very quietly.

“I know,” said Yan.

He looked back. Before the light. He looked back at you.

Yan stood very still for a moment.

“What did he look like,” he said, “when he looked back?”

Suzu didn’t answer for a long time. When she did, she said only:

Like you, at your mother’s door. When you go out, and she’s asleep, and you don’t want to wake her.

Yan put his hand up to the white snake at his throat and left it there. He’d been his mother’s eyes for most of his life. He knew that look from the inside. He knew exactly what it cost.

Home was paused. Somewhere, his mother was asleep in her chair, exactly as he’d left her, and not one grain was falling. He held on to that.

Then he went to the door with the others, and out into the light.
