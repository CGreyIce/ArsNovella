Chris had a tab for everything, and he didn't have a tab for this.

He sat on the ledge round the eye of the dome with a greasy rag in his hands and watched something the size of a cart open its wings above him. He knew, quite clearly and calmly, the way he knew a number was wrong in a column, that he had never once in his life thought about what he'd do if a bird came through a roof.

The wings were black. They went out and out against the clouds, wider than the eye, wider than the ledge, so wide that the wind off them blew the lantern out. In the dark, the Old Rook leaned its great barrel of a head down over the open eye of the Hall of Roads. It looked past the four of them on the ledge as if they weren't there. It looked down, two hundred feet, at the floor of the Hall. At the lamps. At the counter. At the fat old book lying open on the counter under the lamps, with its brass corners, catching the light.

"It wants the book," said Hyvred.

She said it in her lake voice. Calm, low, a weather report. Chris could hear her hands shaking in it.

"It wants the shiny," said Gabe. "Birds like shiny." He was already standing up on the ledge with his hammer, which was a terrible idea, two hundred feet up. "Dec—"

The Rook folded its wings and dropped through the eye.

***

It didn't fly down. It fell. Straight down through the open eye into the round hall, wings tucked, like a stone into a well. The wind of it came up past the four of them on the ledge and nearly took Chris off. He grabbed the wheel in the wall, the one he'd spent four hours freeing, and held on. And far below, on the floor of the Hall, people started shouting.

Then the wings opened.

They filled the dome. From the ledge, Chris saw it all at once: the great black shape spreading out under the painted ceiling, the gold stars and the silver lines behind it. The lamps on their chains swinging wildly in the wind of it, the ones he'd mended. One of them broke loose from its hook and fell, and burst on the floor of the Hall, and oil went across the stone in a sheet of flame toward the counter.

Toward the book.

"*The Roll*," said Kei, beside him, in a voice Chris had never heard him use.

And then Kei was gone. Down the two hundred and twelve stairs in the wall, three at a time, in the dark. Chris went after him. Gabe came after both of them, with his hammer, swearing.

***

By the time they came out of the door behind the east door, onto the floor of the Hall, the fight had already started. It had started without anybody deciding to start it, the way the best ones did.

Ama Corrie was at the counter. She'd thrown a bucket of water on the burning oil and was standing between the fire and the Roll with the empty bucket in her hands, like a small sharp old woman facing down the end of the world. She wasn't moving.

And Ice was in front of her.

He'd come in through the north door, the one Chris had mended, and it hadn't stuck. Chris saw it in one glance. He'd crossed the floor of the Hall to the counter and set his feet on the stone in front of Ama Corrie and the book, with his shield up. And the Rook was coming down at the counter with its beak like a pickaxe, at the shiny brass corners of the book behind him, and Ice didn't move.

The beak hit the shield. The sound went round the whole round hall like a bell. Ice didn't make a sound. He never did. He just turned it, the way he turned everything, down and away, so that the beak went past into the stone of the counter and cracked it. And the Rook pulled back, screaming, and came again.

"*Gigi*," said Kei.

"I know," said Gigi.

She was already there. She'd been in the Hall since dusk, waiting, the way she'd waited on a step for five hours for someone to come up the south road. She cut the air in front of the counter with her hawthorn staff, a short hard stroke, and said the word.

"*Lie.*"

Thorn came up out of the floor of the Hall of Roads. Out of four-hundred-year-old stone, black and twisting, laying itself across the front of the counter beside Ice at knee height. Not a wall. A hedge. A thing you could only come through one way, slowly, bent over. The Rook came down at the counter again and its talons went into the thorn, and stuck.

"*Hold*," said Niko, from the west side of the Hall. He said it on the cut, and his sword went into the Rook's trailing wingtip, and the word went down the blade, and the wing went still, just there, for a heartbeat.

Winter threw.

From the north door, without a word. The harpoon went across the Hall and into the other wing, near the shoulder, with the rope paying out behind. Winter wrapped the end of the rope round the hinge of the north door. The hinge Chris had mended that afternoon, with a pin Gabe had straightened on an anvil in front of four hundred people. She pulled it tight. The Rook's wing was pinned to the door.

The door didn't move. It didn't stick, and it didn't move. It held.

Chris stood at the bottom of the stairs and looked at it.

*The north door of the Hall of Roads, mended.*

***

It was everyone.

That was the thing he'd remember. Not the Rook. Everyone. Every one of them who'd walked to this round hall down seven roads over a month was in it now. They were in the doors and on the floor and up on the ledge, and every one of them doing the thing they did.

Tiger went up the wall. Not up the stairs. Up the wall, the way he went up lock gates, kissing the stone, not landing, and onto the Rook's back between its pinned wings. Two short swords, in and out, behind the shoulder, and off again before it could turn its head. Drew stood in the middle of the floor with his knuckles up, watching its feet.

"*Left talon*," said Drew, calmly. "It's going to let go of the thorn. *Now*."

It let go of the thorn. Gabe was there. He stepped, one long step, the way he'd stepped into a pike in a fen. And the talon that had been going for Kotori, on the edge of the counter with her staff, went into Gabe's shield instead.

"*Mend*," said Kotori, without a single sorry. The light went into Gabe before the blow had finished arriving. Gabe laughed, and the hammer came round white.

Wendy was by the south door with Kite beside her. They were both drawing, both letting go, arrow after arrow into the Rook's head, so that it couldn't look at anything for long. Maigo had his staff out and was saying *hold* in the other way, the healer's way, joining the cuts on people as fast as the Rook made them. Kei was at the counter with his long plain knife, measuring three drops onto the blade.

And up on the ledge round the eye, two hundred feet up, Hyvred was calling everything. Every move, every turn of the head, every shift of the weight. All of it at once, because she could see all of it at once.

"*It's going to go up*," said Hyvred suddenly. "It's looking at the eye. It's going to pull loose and go up."

Chris saw it. The Rook had stopped looking at the book. It was looking up, at the open eye at the top of the dome, at the clouds and the dark and the way out. Its pinned wing was straining at Winter's rope. The north door groaned on its hinge.

"*Chris*," said Hyvred, from the ledge. "*The wheel.*"

He knew what she meant before she'd finished saying it.

"*Turn it*," he shouted up. "*Hyv. Turn the wheel. Left. Turn it left.*"

She turned it.

Two hundred feet up, in the wall of the dome, Hyvred put both hands on the wheel Chris had spent four hours freeing, and turned it. The great iron shutter of the eye of the Hall of Roads came rolling closed across the night on its clean track, with a long, smooth, iron sigh. It met in the middle with a soft clang just as the Rook tore its wing free of the rope and went up, and up, and found nothing above it but iron.

It hit the shutter, and fell.

***

It came down onto the floor of the Hall of Roads on its back, in the middle, in the light of the lamps. Not dead. Huge, and black, and screaming, and trying to get up.

"*Now*," said Kei. He'd stepped in under its flailing wing and put his knife in, once, where Ysolde had taught him the heart would be. Three drops. "It'll slow. Thirty seconds. Then it'll stop."

"*Ice*," said Drew.

"Holding," said Ice.

He'd held the counter for four minutes. Every blow the Rook had put into his shield had gone down into the dark under the floor of the Hall and stayed there. Chris knew what that meant. He'd made a sheet about it in December, after the Long Night. *Undertow — held vs. released.* He watched Ice set his feet.

"Chris," said Ice.

Chris looked at him.

"It's yours," said Ice. "The last one. You need point four."

Chris stood at the bottom of the stairs with his plain longsword in his hand and didn't move.

He knew how to do it. He'd done it every day for two months. Back foot. Shoulder. Breathe out. Bring it down. One, two, three, four. Falling Cut. Eighty-eight per cent, his best, on a drum, in a flooded mine. He started making the list. He couldn't help it. *Back foot. Shoulder.* He'd been making lists his whole life. They were how he knew things were real.

*Doum.*

He looked up.

Newish was sitting in the middle of the floor of the Hall of Roads, on the old stone, ten feet from a screaming bird the size of a cart. Her drum was between her knees. She was looking straight at him.

*Doum. Tek. Tek. Doum.*

"Stop listing," said Newish. Not shouting. Just saying it. "Chris. I walked to your ugly town. *Listen.*"

He listened.

He heard the drum, and the Hall, and the Rook screaming, and nineteen people breathing. He heard the beat under all of it, the beat that was in everyone in the room. Tiger on the wall and Drew watching feet and Gabe with his hammer white and Kotori's bell and Gigi's thorn and Kite's bow and Ice, holding. All of them moving on it, without anybody telling them to.

"*Now*," said Ice, on the *doum*, and let go.

Everything he'd held for four minutes came up out of the floor of the Hall into one cut. The Rook came across the stone toward him, dragged off its back by the wing like a boat on a chain, into the middle of the room. Right to where Chris was standing.

His back foot turned. On the beat.

His shoulder dropped. On the beat.

His breath went out. On the beat.

Not one, two, three, four. *Doum.*

The plain longsword came down.

It was very quiet in the Hall of Roads.

The Rook lay on the floor in the middle of the round room, under the shut eye, very still. The lamps swung on their chains, slowing. The thorn lay across the front of the counter, and behind it Ama Corrie stood with an empty bucket, and behind her the Roll lay open, unburnt. Nineteen people stood round the edges of the room and in the doors, breathing. Newish sat on the floor with her hands still on the drum.

Chris stood over the Rook with his sword in his hand and looked at the window.

*Level 15.*

He didn't write it down.

He thought about it. He thought about the forty-third sheet, *ELSEWHERE (ours)*, and the tab he'd made for levels, and the column with his name in it. *10. 10. 11. 13. 14. 14 (98). 14 (99.6).* He thought about putting *15* in the next cell, and he didn't. Not yet. He just stood there in the middle of the round hall with his sword, and looked at the number, and let it be true without writing it down.

Then he looked at the clock in the corner of his eye.

*11:47.*

"Thirteen minutes," he said.

His voice came out very strange. He didn't care.

Nobody moved. And then Gigi, from behind her hedge, said "*Well, don't just stand there*," in exactly the voice Granny Sloe would have used. Everybody laughed at once, too loud, the way you laugh when you've been holding your breath for four minutes. Newish was crying. She'd deny it forever. Tiger came down off the wall and fell over the Rook's wing and lay on the floor laughing.

And Kei came across the Hall to him from the counter, past the thorn and the dead bird, walking very carefully, the way he measured a dose. He stopped in front of Chris.

He didn't say anything. He didn't need to.

He just held out the pen.
