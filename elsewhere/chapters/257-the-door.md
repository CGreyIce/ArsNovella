The north door was two shields wide, and Gabe had one.

He stood in it at eight o'clock on Thursday night, the twenty-first of December, the longest night. With his hammer in one hand and his shield on the other arm and his feet set the way Salty had hit into him for three weeks. Wide. Down. Into the old stone of the step.

Behind him, the Hall of Roads. Ama Corrie at her counter with her ledger open and her broom across her knees. The banner room door, open, and through it the long table. Sixty-one chairs, mostly empty tonight, everybody on the board. And the chair by the north door, empty too. Isa was on the roof.

Above him, at the top of the door, in the little window: a candle.

Chernn had changed it at the first dawn, the way she'd changed it every morning for fifty days. Tall, white, plain, straight. *It stays lit. Till they're home.* It threw a small square of yellow light out over the north steps and up the north road. Toward ninety-four lamps on the backs of grey stones, standing straight. All the way up the moor. Toward the edge, and the nothing, and a hill.

On his left, Salty, with Right Size point-down on the step. On his right, Wendy, with her bow and her hands out of her pockets. And one pace behind him, where he could feel her without looking: Rae.

One pace.

"Okay?" said Salty.

It was the only thing Salty said, on the longest night. Gabe had heard about it. *Okay?* every twenty minutes, all night, last year, at this door. To Wendy, to Kite, to himself.

"Okay," said Gabe.

***

Nothing happened for four hours.

He'd known it wouldn't. Kei had told the long table. *It goes to the middle first. Isa says. To look at it. Then it comes south.* So the round town waited, the first four hours of the longest night, with every light lit. Four hundred and some. The baker's window, the blue door, the cookshop on Spoke Street. A candle in every window from Chernn and Mother Dipp and the whole of Tallow Green. And the ninety-four up the north road, standing straight. And the twenty-two past the old hall, a long way off. And past them, the edge.

And past the edge, nine people on a hill.

Gabe stood in the north door and thought about them. He couldn't not. Dec, at the front of a ring, with Hyv calling and Suzu at the flame. Kotori on the beat. Thea and Gigi, close now, Xola had written. Hako narrating. Somewhere out in the dark, a very long way north, keeping a light that had been lit three hundred years. So the dark would know where it was.

*Keep it lit*, Kei had written them. On the board. *The hill. You nine.*

Gabe hadn't written them anything. He'd tried. He'd sat in the back room at Melaka Raya at seven in the evening and tried to write Dec a letter. And got as far as *DEC* in capitals, and then cried. And put it away.

He'd see him after.

***

The calls came from the eye. Kei's, mostly, small and calm, in the corner of everybody's window, the way they'd come last year.

[ Kei · the eye · 20:47 ]
*North quiet. Road's lit, all ninety-four. Nothing moving.*

[ Kei · the eye · 22:15 ]
*Still quiet. Isa says it's at the hill.*

[ Kei · the eye · 23:52 ]
*Isa says it's stopped. At the middle. Looking.*

Gabe read that one twice.

*Looking.* At the middle. At a flame over a jar on a hill, with nine people round it.

Then, at four minutes past midnight:

[ Kei · the eye · 00:04 ]
*Isa says it's coming. Not looking. Just coming.*

And then, a minute later, smaller, as if Kei had typed it without meaning to:

[ Kei · the eye · 00:05 ]
*She says they held.*

Gabe stood in the north door and didn't say anything for a long time.

"They held," said Wendy, beside him. Very quietly.

"They held," said Salty.

Behind him, one pace, Rae didn't say anything at all. But he felt her hand, for a second, flat on his back between his shoulders. And then gone.

***

It came down the road at half past two.

He saw it from the north door. He'd seen it last year from the north steps, before Ice went under the floor. He'd thought he knew what it looked like.

It looked different from the front.

The far lamps went first. Up at the top of the moor, the ninety-fourth, the ninety-third, too far to see except as points. Going out. Not blown out; covered. One after another, north to south, the way they'd gone last year. As the dark came down over them. A black line, moving down the road, eating the lights.

Then the Unlit.

Hundreds. Coming down the road under the black line and out of the heather on both sides and up out of every pool on the moor. Tall and thin and swaying. Leaning toward the lights. Toward the round town, and the four hundred lights in it. Toward the north door, two shields wide, with a candle in the window over it.

"*Here we go*," said Wendy.

"Okay?" said Salty.

"Okay," said Gabe.

***

The first mile held for nineteen minutes.

He didn't see it. He couldn't. Tiger and Maigo and Niko were a mile up the road, past Kite's milestone, at the place where Tiger had stood on his own last year saying *not yet*. Gabe heard the calls. *First mile. Holding. Holding.* And then Tiger's voice, in the corner of his window, not a call, just Tiger. *Not yet. Not yet.* And then Kei: *Tiger, Maigo, Niko, COME IN.*

They came in. He saw them come, at the end, running, down the last of the road, with the dark behind them like a tide. Tiger at the back, of course. Walking backward, the last few paces, facing it.

They came up the north steps past Gabe and into the Hall. Tiger stopped, in the door, beside him. Breathing.

"It's not *looking*," said Tiger.

Gabe looked at him.

"Last year it was looking," said Tiger. "For something. Everything it did, it was looking. Reaching." He was white. "This year it's just coming. Like weather." He swallowed. "It's still all of it."

"Okay," said Gabe.

"Hold the door," said Tiger, and went in.

***

Then it was at the door.

He'd never been at the front of it. Not really. Not at the front of anything, when it mattered. He'd been the second tank, the one behind. Dec's. He'd stood behind Dec on a hundred roads, two years, and watched the dark come, and Dec stand there, and not break.

He stood there.

The Unlit came up the north steps. The first one, tall and thin, swaying, its face nothing. Leaning toward the candle in the window over the door. Toward the four hundred lights behind it.

It came into his shield.

He didn't move.

It was like Salty's turn. That was the thing he'd tell Dec, after, on the edge of a pod. Three weeks of Salty swinging Right Size into his shield at the third dawn, and him going back four paces, three, two, one. It was like that. Except it didn't stop. It came, and came, and came. One after another, up the steps, into the door, into his shield. And every one was Salty's turn, and he went back none.

Salty's black sword went past him on the left. Low, the full turn, slow, the whole way round. And the Unlit on the steps went back from it like grass from a scythe. Wendy's arrows over his right shoulder, four at a time. Bee's Walls, grey, coming up on either side of the door, six of them, held by a woman with wings and two pencils on the steps behind them.

And behind him, one pace, Rae. Saying nothing. Her hand coming up every few seconds. And his window going up, every time, a little, before whatever was coming had come. *She Mends where it's going to be*, Dec had said once. Gabe had never felt it from the front. He felt it now.

"Okay?" said Salty.

"Okay."

***

At four, it was the dark.

Not the Unlit. The thing behind them. The dark itself, edge to edge, the whole black of it. Coming down the north road after the last of them like the tide behind the waves. Eating every light. The first mile, and Kite's milestone, and the first waystone. Up to the bottom of the north steps.

And stopping.

At the light. At the round town's four hundred lights. At the candle in the window over the north door, throwing its small square of yellow out onto the steps.

It leaned.

Not looking. Tiger was right. Not reaching for anything. Just leaning, the way water leans against a wall. Because that was what it did. Because it was the longest night, and it always came south, and the round town was where it came.

It leaned on the north door.

On him.

***

He couldn't describe it, after. He tried. To Dec, on the edge of a pod, at eight in the morning, with two cups of kopi. He couldn't.

It wasn't heavy. That was the thing. Salty's turn was heavy. The Unlit were heavy, one after another, into his shield. The dark wasn't heavy. It didn't push. It just *leaned*. All of it, all at once, on the front of the north door. Patient. As if it had all night, because it did. As if it would lean there until something gave, because something always did.

Bee's Walls went grey, and then greyer. He heard Bee, behind him on the steps, make a sound.

Salty's turn went out into it, low, the whole way round, and the dark leaned back from the black blade, a pace. And then leaned again.

Wendy's arrows went into it and didn't come out.

And over his head, in the little window at the top of the north door, the candle flickered.

***

He felt it. He couldn't see it; it was over his head. But he felt the light on the steps in front of him go small. The little square of yellow from the window, the one Chernn had changed fifty mornings in a row. Going small.

*It stays lit*, Chernn had said. *Till they're home.*

He thought about the hill. Nine people, a very long way north. Holding a light for the dark to look at, so it would know where it was. They'd held. *She says they held.*

He thought about Dec. On the high fells, in September. *You stand where people are. And you don't ask if you're allowed. You're just there. So everybody behind you knows where you are.*

He thought about *I'm an Anvil. I don't break.* And Rae, on the White Hills, a long time ago. *That's a class description. Not a medical opinion.*

He set his feet.

Wider. Down. The way Dec had stood on the bridge, Salty said, for the slow one. All the way down into the old stone of the north step. So far down that he felt the round town under it. The Hall, and the stair, and the Seven Below, and the stream in the north tunnel. And all of it, four hundred lights and sixty people and an old woman on a roof, behind him.

He didn't push. You didn't push the dark. Dec had said that too.

He just didn't move.

***

"*Rae*," he said.

"I know," said Rae.

She was one pace behind him. Then she wasn't.

She came the pace. He felt her. Not her hand, flat between his shoulders, for a second. Her. Her back against his back, the whole of it, shoulder blades to shoulder blades, her staff up over her shoulder. Facing the other way. Into the Hall, at the four hundred lights and the sixty people. Holding him up from behind.

Zero paces.

"*Don't push it*," said Rae. Against his back.

He laughed. In the north door, on the longest night, with the dark leaning on his shield. Out loud. He couldn't help it.

"*I'm not pushing anything*," said Gabe. "*I'm the front. I'm not moving.*"

"*I know*," said Rae. "*That's why I'm here.*"

Over his head, in the little window, the candle stood up.

***

He didn't see it. He felt it. The square of yellow light on the steps in front of him growing back. Steady. Tall. Chernn's.

And the dark, leaning on the north door, leaning on him, two shields wide with one shield in it and a woman at his back, stopped.

Not went back. Stopped. Stayed there, at the bottom of the north steps, leaning. But not on him. On the light. The way water stops at the top of a wall it can't get over, and just stays there, flat, all night.

It couldn't get in. It didn't try.

It wasn't looking for anything.

***

It stayed there till six. Leaning. The longest night. Salty saying *okay?* every twenty minutes, and Gabe saying *okay*. Wendy's hands out of her pockets. Bee's Walls, grey, holding. Rae's back against his.

Kei's calls, from the eye, in the corner of his window, all night.

*Windows holding. Well holding. Market north holding.*

*North-east road: Meridian holding. Harlan says hi.*

*North-west road: holding.* And then, at about five, a different call, not Kei's. Kite's. Very short. *North-west. We're fine.*

Gabe read that one twice. *We're fine.* Vane's line.

*The banner room: holding. Cal says he went ahead in order to come back.*

At half past six the call came from the eye. Not John's, this year; John was in Masmark at the netloft. Higu's. Soft, and gold, the way her Lantern was.

[ Higu · the eye · 06:30 ]
*The lamps. They're coming back on. From the far end.*

Gabe looked up the north road.

Up at the top of the moor. A point of light. The ninety-fourth. Then the ninety-third. Then down, one after another, south toward the round town. The road of lights coming back on from the far end, behind the dark, as it sank.

Like last year, when an old woman walked home behind it with a lamp.

Except nobody was walking.

They were just coming back on. On their own. Because the dark had gone by, and it knew where its middle was. And it didn't need to take them with it.

***

It came at seven fifty-eight. The dark sinking into the moor like a tide going out, all the way up the road. The sky over the north road going grey, then pale. The first sun in thirty-five hours.

Gabe stood in the north door with his shield up and didn't put it down.

He didn't think he could. His arm had forgotten how.

"Gabe," said Rae. Behind him. Against his back.

"Mm."

"You can put it down."

"I know."

"You held," said Rae.

He didn't say anything. He couldn't.

Salty, on his left, pulled Right Size out of the step where he'd planted it at the end, and leaned on it, and looked at Gabe over the top of it.

"Okay?" said Salty.

"Okay," said Gabe.

And then, because it was the bit people forgot, Wendy sat down on the north step, with her bow across her knees. And moved over. And Salty sat down beside her. And Bee, white, with her pencils, came round the end of her last Wall and sat down on the step below. And Tiger came out of the Hall and sat. And Ama Corrie came out from behind her counter with her broom and stood in the north door behind them, and looked at the north road, all of it lit, and said *hm*.

Gabe put his shield down.

Rae came round in front of him. She looked up at him. At thirty-one per cent, in the north door, at the end of the longest night. With his face wet, because it always was now, and none of it was sad.

"One pace," she said.

"Zero," said Gabe. "You came the pace."

"I know," said Rae. "Don't push it."

She didn't go back the pace. She stood in front of him, in the north door, zero paces. And then, because he'd never once in his life asked permission, and Dec said that was the whole thing, he put his big heavy arms round her.

She let him.

Over their heads, in the little window, the candle burned. Tall, white, plain, straight. Chernn would change it at the first dawn, the way she'd changed it fifty-one mornings.

*It stays lit. Till they're home.*
