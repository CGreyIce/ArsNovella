[ Call · KeiKatamori, Maigo, Nikorasu, Drew ]

"So," said Maigo, the moment the fourth name came up. "We're making a guild."

"We're *talking* about making a guild," said Kei.

"We're making a guild," said Maigo, with total confidence. "I've already thought of eleven names. Most of them are bad. Two of them are really good. One of them has a pun in it that I'm not going to say yet because you'll all say no."

"No," said Niko.

"I haven't *said* it yet."

"No," said Niko again, peacefully, and Kei laughed for the first time in two days.

It was Friday night. Kei was in his wooden room in the mall with the rain on the window, and in Carrow Cross, on the steps of the Hall of Roads, with the lamps lit and his notebook on his knee. Maigo was in the Old Pippin, in Orchard End, sitting in the crook of the biggest branch, where the new leaves were. He'd said so twice. Niko was in his gorge town, on one of the high stone bridges over the fast water, with his sword and his book. And Drew was on the road. You could hear it in his voice: the steady crunch of boots on a long stony way in the dark, and every so often a pause while he looked at something.

Kei laid it out for them. The way he'd laid it out for himself on the steps the night before: plainly, in order, with the sums.

Twenty names. Fifteen or over. Thirty days, in one Hall, by hand. Five hundred gold. Twelve on the call, most of them twelve or under. Niko's four at ten, if that. Drew, Tiger, Hyvred and Bee, who weren't on the call but were on the map. Xav's eight, and Xav. Moonlight Harvest.

He didn't say anything about Ice yet. He wanted them to say it first.

"Okay," said Drew, when he'd finished. Crunch, crunch, on the road. "Can I ask the boring question?"

"That's why I asked you."

"Who's it not for?"

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"Because the writ says twenty," said Drew. "And it says no limit. So it's going to be twenty, and then it's going to be fifty, and then it's going to be people you've never met, if it works. Every guild in every game I've ever played went one of two ways. You let everybody in, and it's a crowd, and nobody knows anybody. Or you shut the door, and it's a club, and everybody inside it gets a bit worse every year." Crunch. "So who's it not for?"

Kei thought about it. He'd thought about it on the steps, a bit. Not enough.

"I don't know yet," he said honestly.

"That's fine," said Drew. "I didn't either, the first time." A pause, and Kei could hear him smiling. "There's a woman at an inn on this road. Nell. She keeps a gate. On the longest night she kept it open till they stopped coming, and she let in people she didn't know, and one of them had a bird on his collar." Crunch. "I asked her how she knew who to let in. She said, *if the gate's shut, you have to decide. Every time.* I said, *even if you don't know what they are?* She said, *especially then.*"

Kei wrote it down in the notebook, under the three names. *Every time.*

"So not a list," said Niko slowly. "Not a door. A gate. And somebody standing at it."

"Somebody standing at it," said Drew. "Every time."

"That's a lot of standing," said Maigo.

"Yeah," said Drew. "It's the job."

***

"Why, though," said Niko.

He said it after a long quiet, the way he said things. Chill, unhurried, as if he'd been turning it over on his bridge for a while.

"Why what?" said Maigo.

"Why a guild. We've got the call. Every night. Twelve of us, nobody hangs up." The sound of water, a long way down, under his bridge. "What's a guild got that the call hasn't?"

Kei had known somebody would ask that. He'd hoped it would be Niko, because Niko would ask it properly, and listen to the answer.

So he told them about the map.

He hadn't told anybody, really. Not all of it. He'd told the call it existed, once, in passing. But he'd never said what he'd thought, on the night he drew it, alone, with the lamp on, before the longest night. So he said it now. The dots. The lines that were already there, every one of them a real thing that had happened. A call at three in the morning, a raid, a game, a year. The little groups. Five here, six there, two on a bridge, eight on a riverbank. Each one keeping its own town. And almost every line going, in the end, through the same few dots.

"Everybody's in little groups," he said. "That's what the game does to you. Random towns. Parties of six. Companies of eight. That's fine. That's good. You need to be small to move." He touched the green tie in his hair. "But one day, I thought, you'd want something to hold them. All of them. Without making any of them stop being what they are. Moonlight Harvest stays Moonlight Harvest. Xav's eight stay Xav's eight. The six stay the six. But there's something round all of them, that they can all stand in." He stopped. "That's all. I didn't know what it'd be. I didn't think the game would just put it on a wall."

Nobody said anything for a while.

"Big enough to hold the little groups," said Niko eventually, "without making them stop being little."

"Yeah."

"Okay," said Niko. "I want to see what happens in the middle of that." A pause. "I'm in."

"I was in before you started," said Maigo. "I was in on the bus."

"You don't get the bus."

"I was in on the *metaphorical* bus, Niko."

"I'm in," said Drew. Crunch, crunch, on the road. "Tiger's in if I'm in. I haven't asked him. I don't need to."

Kei sat on the steps of the Hall of Roads with his notebook on his knee and the rain on a window in Australia. Something in his chest went very quiet and very warm, the way the spring did at Hallowell when the steam lifted off it at dawn.

"Then the first thing," he said, "before anything. We ask Ice."

"Obviously," said Maigo.

"And Xav," said Drew.

"And Xav."

"He'll say no," said Niko.

There was a short silence on the call.

"Ice will?" said Maigo. "Ice will say *no*? To *us*?"

"Not no," said Niko. "Not yet." The water under his bridge. "If he says yes, it's his. Thirty thousand people walk to Carrow Cross because the swamp guy's in it, and it's his, whatever we call it. He knows that. He'll have known it before Kei finished the first sentence."

Maigo was quiet for a moment, which was rare.

"Oh," he said. "Yeah. Oh."

"I know," said Kei. "I worked it out on the steps."

"Ask him anyway," said Drew. "You ask first. That's manners." Crunch. "Then he says not yet, and you go and make it big, and then you ask him again."

***

He asked Xav on Saturday.

Xav was where he always was now, at the inn by the east bridge in Brackenford, in a room on the first floor with a window on the river. Kei could hear the mill wheels on the call. Xav's eight were scattered over the map in eight towns, levelling, eight to ten each, still a long way from the bank. The charter in the top-left pigeonhole of the Adventurers' Hall still had a blank where the name should be.

Kei laid it out for him the same way. Plainly, in order, with the sums.

Xav listened all the way through without saying anything. That was Xav. When Kei had finished, there was a pause, and then a small sound that might have been a pencil being put down.

"Ask me when it's big," said Xav. "I'll be first through the door."

"That's a no."

"It's a *not yet*." Xav's plan voice, calm and level. "Kei. I've got eight idiots in eight towns, and I've told every one of them they're not allowed to answer anybody till they're standing on a riverbank. I haven't even got a name. I can't put another name over the top of a name I haven't got." A pause. "Get your twenty. Get your banner. Make it something. Then ask me again, and Riverbank—" He stopped. "Then ask me again."

"What was that?"

"Nothing."

"You said Riverbank."

"I didn't say anything," said Xav. "I'm a founder with a blank charter, and I never say anything." But Kei could hear him smiling.

"Xav," said Kei. "When I ask Ice."

"He'll say not yet too."

"I know."

"He'll say it the way he said it to me." The mill wheels, turning. "In the ring, before the Long Night, I told him to tell me Moonlight Harvest needed me, and I'd tell my eight no. And he said *no. Buy it.* And then he said he wanted to see what I'd build." A pause. "It was the nicest thing anybody's ever said to me. And it was no." Another pause. "He says no to things he likes. So they can grow. He'll say it to you backwards. Build it first. Then he'll come."

***

He asked Ice on Sunday morning.

Not in the game. On the phone. It was half past ten in Australia and half past seven in Malacca, which Kei knew was the half hour between the gym and the egg. Ice picked up on the second ring, out of breath.

"Kei?"

"Sorry. Are you—"

"Just back from the gym. It's fine." The sound of a door, and a fan, and somewhere in the background Gabe shouting something about eggs. Breakfast eggs, Kei thought. "Eight pull-ups," said Ice. "I'm telling everybody. Sorry. Go on."

Kei told him.

Plainly, in order, with the sums. He'd done it three times now. It came out cleaner every time. The writ. The twenty. Fifteen. The thirty days. The core four, the call, the gate. Who it wasn't for, and who it was. The map. He didn't say anything about Ice. He didn't need to.

Ice was quiet all the way through. Kei could hear him breathing, slowing down. When it was finished, there was a long pause.

"Not yet," said Ice.

"I know."

"Kei. Listen." Ice's voice was careful. Kei realised he was choosing every word, the way he chose where to stand. "Make it big. Then ask me again. And I'll say yes before you finish."

"I know," said Kei. "I worked it out on the steps."

Ice laughed. It was a real laugh, surprised, the one he did on stream when someone in chat caught him out.

"Of course you did," he said. "Course you did. You've got a map."

"If you put your name on it now, it's yours."

"Yeah."

"Thirty thousand people walk to Carrow Cross because the swamp guy's in it."

"Yeah."

"And I'm the guy who runs the swamp guy's guild."

"You'd hate it," said Ice.

"I'd hate it," agreed Kei.

They were both quiet for a moment. In Malacca, Gabe shouted something about toast. In Australia, it had stopped raining.

"I could pay the—" said Ice.

"No."

"I know," said Ice. "I had to say it."

"If you pay for it, it's yours too."

"I know." A pause. "I had to say it, Kei."

"I know you did," said Kei. And he did know. He'd known since Thursday night on the steps. He'd known Ice would offer, and he'd known he'd have to say no, and he'd known it would be fine. It was still strange, hearing it. A man who could buy the whole thing with the money in one pocket, offering it to you like a cup of tea, and minding when you said no.

"Okay. Then what I *can* do," said Ice, and his voice changed. The plan voice, the one he'd learned from Xav. "Anything you need from the fen, you get at cost. Same as anyone small. Draughts. Fen-heart. Silverleaf. Bell-metal, if we've got it. Same as Tavinho's lot and the others. That's not me being in it. That's just the list."

"That's fair."

"And when you're short," said Ice, "ask me who I know. I know some people who'd be good. Not famous. Good." He paused. "People who held some steps once."

"I'll ask."

"Who've you asked? Xav?"

"Yesterday."

"What did he say?"

"*Ask me when it's big. I'll be first through the door.*"

Ice laughed again, quieter. "Course he did."

"He also said Riverbank, by accident, and then pretended he hadn't."

"Did he," said Ice, very interested. "Did he really."

"Don't tell him I told you."

"I won't. I'll just say it in front of him one day, by accident." Another pause. Then, carefully again: "Kei. It's going to be big."

"You don't know that."

"I know who's on it," said Ice simply.

Kei didn't say anything. He sat at the desk in his room in Australia with the sheet of paper in front of him, the one with all the lines. He couldn't, for a moment.

"Can I say one thing," said Ice. "On stream. Today."

"What?"

"That I said no."

Kei frowned. "Why?"

"So that everybody who wants to join because of me knows it isn't mine," said Ice. "And everybody who wants to join because of you knows it's yours." A pause. "That's all. I won't say anything else. Not your name, if you don't want. Just that somebody's making something, and I'm not in it, and I'm not going to be. Not yet."

Kei looked at the sheet of paper for a long time.

"Okay," he said.

***

He watched it that afternoon, from his kitchen, on his phone.

He hadn't meant to. He'd meant to make lunch. But Ice went live at eight in the morning, Malacca time, the way he always did, and at the first dusk he was sitting on the plank at the narrows with the others while Gabe ate fen bread. And Kei found himself standing at the counter with a knife in his hand and no bread on the board, watching.

Ice didn't make a speech. He wasn't the kind. He just said it, between one thing and the next, the way he said everything on stream. Plainly. Looking out at the fen.

"Somebody asked me something today," he said. "About the paper. The writ."

Ice didn't say who it was.

He sat on the plank with his feet over the black water, and Gabe offered him the last bit of fen bread, and he took it. That was all. He didn't mention it again for the rest of the day.

Kei stood in his kitchen for a long time with his phone in his hand.

Then he made lunch. Then he went to the café early, in the rain, which had started again. In Carrow Cross he sat on the steps of the Hall of Roads, under the lamps, and opened his notebook to the page with three names on it.

Under them, in his careful hand, he wrote two more lines.

*Xav: "Ask me when it's big."*

*Ice: "Not yet."*

And under those, after a long time, one more.

*The twelve. Tomorrow.*
