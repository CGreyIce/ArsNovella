Tif had been in Ice's chat for every minute of every stream since the first night. So had Leaka. On Saturday, the tenth of April, at five o'clock in the afternoon, one of them wasn't.

Tif noticed at eight minutes past.

She was at her desk in Singapore with Ice's stream on her second screen, the way it always was. A plank in a fen, or a moor, or a cairn with a stone heron on it. Thirty thousand people. And the chat going past on the right. It went faster than anyone could read now. She hadn't tried to read all of it since December. She read it the way you'd listen to a crowd: for the shape of it. For the voices she knew.

And at eight minutes past five, she realised that one of the voices she knew hadn't said anything for eight minutes.

*CGI.*

Every few minutes, for five months. *CGI WHAT. CGI NO. CGI EAT SOMETHING GREEN.* Leaka's capitals, coming up through the chat like a bell in a crowd. Tif had never once noticed them. Not really. You didn't notice the sound of a fan until somebody turned it off.

Somebody had turned it off.

***

It was quiet.

Not really quiet. Thirty thousand people still typing. The ordinary noise of a stream, people saying *lol* and *what* and *the swamp guy did a thing*. But there was a hole in it. A hole the exact shape of a lot of capital letters. Tif could hear it all afternoon, the way you'd hear a step missing on a staircase you went up every day.

She wasn't the only one.

Thea and Xola were watching from wherever they were, the way they'd watched since the first weeks. Everybody who'd ever been in this chat for long had noticed the hole.

Ice noticed at about half past.

He was sitting on a cairn under a stone heron, somewhere north of the fen, with Deston and Suzu and Kotori and Leveti round him, eating fen bread. He glanced at the chat panel at the edge of his eye, the way he did. Tif watched him look at it for longer than usual. Then he laughed, quietly, on stream.

"It's quiet," he said.

"It will be hard," said Ice, to the fen. And Tif could tell from his face that he wasn't entirely joking.

***

She made a command.

It took her four minutes. She'd made Ice's commands since day one. *!dark*, on the first night, because he went dark and she wanted to count. *!stone*, because he kept looking at the Tally. *!sword*, *!scalped*, the healers, the ford, the dawn clock. Every time something happened often enough to count, she counted it. That was what a head mod did, Tif had decided, in the absence of anyone telling her what a head mod did. You counted the things that mattered, so that the person on the stream didn't have to.

*!leaka.*

Ice laughed out loud.

And then something happened that Tif hadn't planned. Somebody she'd never seen before, a lurker with no badge, typed *CGI*. In capitals. On its own. And then somebody else did. And then fifty people did. And then the chat was full of it, three letters in capitals going past faster than anyone could read. *CGI. CGI. CGI.* Thirty thousand people, most of whom had never spoken to Leaka in their lives, filling the hole she'd left with her own word.

"You're all saying CGI," he said.

Tif sat at her desk and watched thirty thousand people type three letters. She laughed out loud, alone in her room, until her eyes went wet.

***

Xyro rang at nine.

He'd been in the chat too. He'd typed *CGI* with everybody else, which he almost never did, and then he'd rung. He sounded the way he did when he wanted to talk about something he loved, which was often. Tonight it was a lot.

"Thea messaged," he said. "The group. All of us."

Tif knew before he said it. She'd known for a week. She'd seen it coming the way you'd see weather coming, on the call, in the way Thea had started saying *when* instead of *if*.

"Higu," said Xyro. "Higu messaged Thea. On Thursday. She said *come in. All of you. Bring everyone.*" He was talking very fast. "And Thea said yes. And Xola said yes. And PenguChi said *about time*. And Xola asked Slep, because obviously, and Slep said *if you're going, I'm going*, in that voice. And Thea asked us." He stopped. "Me and you. She asked if we're in."

Tif didn't say anything for a moment.

"Tif?"

"I'm here."

"Are we in?"

She looked at her second screen. At Ice's stream, still going, with Ice walking north from one stone heron to the next in the dark. And at the chat, still full of *CGI* half an hour later, because people had decided they liked it.

"Who'd mod Ice?" she said.

Xyro was quiet.

"If I'm in the egg," said Tif, "I can't type in his chat. Leaka can't. Nobody in there can. Satayboy's in there. Everybody's in there, Xy. Everybody but us. Everybody who ever helped him. Half the chat's diamonds can't type any more." She looked at the screen. "Who counts *!dark* if I'm in the egg?"

"Nightbot," said Xyro.

"Nightbot doesn't *mean* it."

Xyro laughed. Then he stopped, because he could hear she wasn't really joking.

"Tif," he said, gently. Xyro could be gentle when it mattered. "He'll be okay. He's got thirty thousand people. They just spent half an hour saying CGI for Leaka. You saw."

"I saw."

"They'll say it for you too."

Tif didn't say anything.

"And you've never played one," said Xyro. "Not once. Not ever. You've watched him play one for five months, every single minute. You know it better than anybody." A pause. "Don't you want to see it? From inside? With your own eyes?"

She thought about it.

She thought about the first night. Twenty-one viewers, a man in a swamp, a shield, and Leaka shouting. She'd typed *!dark* into a chat that didn't have a *!dark* yet, because she'd wanted to count something. She thought about a plank at three in the morning, twelve hours, a man who never made a sound. About a ford he lost at and then won, and a drowned church, and a bell girl. She'd been there for every minute of it. She'd been there for every minute of it on a screen.

"After his door," she said.

"What?"

"He's going north. Along the herons. There's a door at the end. You can see it in his face." She looked at the stream. "I want to be in his chat for that. For the door. The way I was for the church." She took a breath. "Then. After. I'll go in."

Xyro was quiet for a long time.

"After his door," he said. "Okay. I'll tell Thea."

***

She stayed up until two writing commands.

Not for now. For after. For the day she lay down in an egg for the first time and couldn't type in Ice's chat any more. She opened *!dark* up so that anybody could add to it, not just mods, once a minute and no faster. Thirty thousand people could count for him. She'd seen today that they would. *!stone* the same. *!leaka* would go on counting the days on its own. She wrote them all very carefully, line by line, the way you'd leave instructions for someone looking after your house.

Then, at the bottom, she wrote one more. She didn't turn it on. She just left it there, ready, for the day she'd need it.

*!tif*

*Tif's in the egg. Behave. She'll know if you don't.*

She looked at it for a long time, at two in the morning, at her desk in Singapore. Then she saved it, and turned off her screen, and sat in the dark for a while.
