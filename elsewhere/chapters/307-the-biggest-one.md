Gabe had kept one thing in his whole life, and it was a door, and on the Thursday morning Dec gave him another.

He was on the counter in the kitchen at Melaka Raya with his kopi, out there, at a quarter past eight. They'd come out of the eggs at the same time, the three of them, Dec and Suzu and Gabe, side by side in the back room. Suzu had gone straight back to bed. Gabe had come to the kitchen and sat on the counter the way he always did. And Dec had put the kettle on, the way he always did, and stood with his back to the room.

He didn't turn round. He stood at the counter with his hand on the kettle, waiting for it, and didn't say anything for a long time.

Gabe had known him eleven years. He knew what Dec's back looked like when he was about to say something.

"Dec," said Gabe.

"I'm going to ask her," said Dec.

The kettle clicked off.

Gabe sat on the counter with his kopi and didn't understand. For about a breath. He thought, *ask her what*. Then he looked at Dec's back, which hadn't moved. At his hand, still on the kettle, not pouring.

He understood.

He put the kopi down on the counter very carefully, the way you'd put down something that might go off.

"*Ask* her," said Gabe.

"Yes."

"Hyv."

"Yes."

"In *here*?"

"At the notch," said Dec. "Tomorrow. At the third dawn." He turned round at last. His face was doing nothing, very carefully. His ears weren't. "In here." He was quiet. "And I'm going to say out there too."

***

Gabe didn't say anything.

He'd said something about everything for eleven years. That was what he did. *That's my best friend*, to anybody, whether they'd asked or not. Because Dec was never going to be loud about himself, and somebody had to be. He'd been loud about Dec in every chat, at every gate, at Hyv's gate a year and a half ago. He'd been loud about Deston for twenty-two months about a sticker. He'd screamed into a pillow in this flat so often the auntie next door had started knocking before he'd finished.

He sat on the counter and couldn't make a single sound.

"Gabe," said Dec.

Gabe got down off the counter. He walked across the kitchen to Dec. He put his arms round him and held on. Hard. As hard as he'd held anything, a door on the longest night included.

Dec stood there in the kitchen with his hands at his sides for a moment, the way he did. Then he put his arms round Gabe too.

"Don't tell anybody," said Dec. Into Gabe's shoulder.

"I won't."

"Not Rae."

"Not *Rae*?"

"Not anybody," said Dec. "Not till after." He was quiet. "You're the first. I wanted you to be the first."

Gabe held on for a long time. He found he was crying, which he did, obviously, at everything. It wasn't the usual kind. It came from further down.

"Why me?" he said.

"Because you've been holding a door for me for eleven years," said Dec. "And I wanted somebody to know what it was for."

***

He went to his room and screamed into the pillow.

He didn't decide to. It came out. He went down the corridor and into his room and shut the door. He lay face down on the bed and screamed into the pillow for about a minute, as hard as he'd ever screamed at anything. Not words. Just the whole of it.

The auntie next door knocked on the wall. Once.

Then, after a moment, twice more. Quieter. A different sort of knock. As if she'd heard something in this scream that was different from all the other screams. And wanted him to know she'd heard.

Gabe lay on the bed with his face in the pillow and laughed until he cried again.

Then he got up, and washed his face, and went back to the kitchen. Dec was still there, with two cups of tea, waiting. He gave one to Gabe.

"I'm keeping it," said Gabe.

"I know," said Dec.

"It's the biggest one I've ever kept."

"I know," said Dec. "You told me. Last week. In the kitchen. *You keep the big ones.*"

"*You* told *me* that," said Gabe.

"I borrowed it back," said Dec.

***

Suzu came out of the guest room at noon, out there, which he never did, and found Gabe on the sofa staring at the wall.

He looked at Gabe for a long time with his eyes about a third open.

"You know," said Suzu.

Gabe sat up so fast he knocked a cushion onto the floor.

"How do you know I know?"

"Your face," said Suzu. He went to the kitchen and took a cup off the rack and looked at it, because there wasn't any tea made, and Dec was out. "You look like you've swallowed something warm."

"Do *you* know?"

"I can read," said Suzu. "Kei's board." He put the cup down. "And I saw him in the netloft door on Tuesday. Holding it."

"*Holding* it?"

"Small," said Suzu. "Black. With gold." He looked at Gabe. "You didn't know that part."

Gabe stared at him.

"I'm going to *scream*," said Gabe.

"The auntie's had a long week," said Suzu. Which was Dec's. And went back to bed.

***

It was the hardest day of his life.

In here, on the Thursday night, they climbed the crater road. Fifty-nine people and a sheep up the switchbacks of the inside of the crater wall, out of Masmark, west, in the long gold afternoon. The lake falling away below them, getting bigger as it got further off, the way it did. Gabe walked near the middle with Rae, at no paces. He kept it.

He kept it when Hyv walked past him on the third switchback, holding Dec's arm with both hands, talking to Thea about the drawing Xav had given her. It was in her coat, against her chest, the one where Dec didn't know. Gabe looked at her and looked away so fast he nearly walked off the edge of the road. Rae caught his sleeve.

He kept it at the water stop on the sixth switchback. Leaka sat beside him and said *what's wrong with your face* and he said *nothing* so loudly that the whole water stop turned round.

He kept it when Hyv caught him looking.

At the long rest on the ninth switchback, sitting on the wall of the road with her chin on Dec's shoulder, she looked up and found Gabe staring at her from across the road. He hadn't meant to stare. He'd been thinking about the kitchen and his face had just done it.

"Gabe," said Hyv. "Why are you looking at me like that?"

"I'm *NOT*," said Gabe.

Hyv narrowed her eyes at him. Edge to edge. The way she looked at things she was about to call. Gabe felt her see all of him in about a breath, the way she saw a hall. His face, his hands, the way he was sitting, the way he'd gone red. All of it.

"Oh," said Hyv. Very quietly.

Gabe stopped breathing.

Then Hyv looked away. On purpose. Gabe saw her do it. She turned her head and looked at the lake below, a long way down, and put her chin back on Dec's shoulder, and didn't look at Gabe again.

"Never mind," she said. To the lake.

Gabe sat on the wall of the road with his heart going. Beside him, Rae, who'd seen the whole thing, put her hand on his knee and said nothing at all.

He kept it when Dennis counted them in at the camp, below the rim, at dusk. A flat place under the last steep switchbacks, where the road went up to the notch. Dennis said *fifty-nine*, and then *and me*, and then *sixty, and the sheep*. Gabe heard himself say *here* in a voice that wasn't his.

He kept it when Deston fell into step beside him on the last switchback before the camp, enormous, with his book under his arm.

Deston didn't say anything for a long time. He was slow; he always had been. Gabe had been loud at him for twenty-two months about a sticker on a door.

"You're quiet," said Deston.

"I'm keeping something."

Deston looked at him sideways. Then, very slowly, his ears went red. Starting at the top.

"Oh," said Deston.

"*Don't*," said Gabe. "Don't say anything. *Please*."

"I wasn't going to," said Deston. He walked on for a while. "I was going to say: good." He was quiet. "You held me open for twenty-two months. Till I went through." He looked up the road, at the rim, at the gap in it. "Somebody's going through tomorrow."

Gabe couldn't answer. Deston didn't need him to. He walked beside Gabe up the last switchback, slowly, the way a candle burns all the way down.

Kei's map was open on a rock at the camp when they got there. Gabe saw it as he went past. The walk sheet, the one from the banner room, with the dotted line west and *MASMARK* and the bridge. And at the bottom, in pencil, small: *the notch · the second dawn out · early*.

Kei saw him see it.

"Pencil," said Kei.

"Pencil isn't knowing," said Gabe. He'd heard Kei say it. Everybody had.

"You know," said Kei.

"I'm *keeping* it."

Kei looked at him for a long time with his calm grey face.

"Good," said Kei. And rolled the map up, so nobody else would see it, and put it under his arm.

He kept it at the fire.

He kept it all evening. Sitting at the fire with Rae beside him, at no paces, not looking at the fire across the camp where Dec and Hyv were sitting with Suzu and Kotori. Hyv's chin on Dec's shoulder. Dec looking at nothing, which he did when he was carrying something. PenguChi had said that once, about the back.

"You're keeping something," said Rae.

Gabe jumped.

She was looking at the fire. Not at him. She'd said it the way she said everything, short, calm, as if it were obvious.

"Yes," said Gabe.

"A big one."

"The *biggest*."

Rae didn't say anything for a while. She didn't ask what. Gabe had known she wouldn't. She never asked; she just saw where you were going to be and got there first.

"You've been keeping it since the kitchen this morning," said Rae. "Out there. I could tell when you came in. Your face." She almost smiled. "You looked like you'd swallowed a lantern."

"I can't tell you."

"I know," said Rae. "Don't."

"I want to tell you *so much*—"

"I know," said Rae. "Don't." She put her hand on his knee, without looking. "Keep it. You're good at it now."

"I'm *terrible* at it."

"You've kept it fourteen hours," said Rae. "In here and out there. With Leaka asking." She looked at him at last. "That's not terrible. That's the best anybody's ever been at anything."

Gabe looked at her for a long time across the fire. At no paces.

He didn't tell her. He held on to her hand instead, very hard, and she let him.

***

He didn't sleep.

Nobody expected him to. He lay by the fire under his coat with Rae asleep beside him and the Anvil propped against a rock, and looked up at the sky over the crater rim. Black, with edges. Stars. The rim above them, a long black line against it. And in the middle of the line, where the road went through, the notch. Two black walls and a gap between them, open to the sky.

There was something in the notch. Small, grey and white, sitting. Facing west.

The goose.

It had been there two weeks. Since the evening they came in, from the other side. It had sat down in the notch facing the way they'd come, and wouldn't go down into Masmark. And it was still there. Gabe had seen it from the switchbacks that afternoon, a small grey shape in the gap at the top of the road. Sitting. Facing west. Waiting.

*It's waiting*, Kei had said, two weeks ago. *For what?* Gabe had said.

Gabe knew what for, now.

He lay by the fire and looked up at the goose in the notch and cried, quietly, so as not to wake Rae, for about an hour.

***

He went up to the notch once, before that, in the middle of the night. On his own. He couldn't help it.

Up the last switchbacks in the dark, quietly, the Anvil on his back, past PenguChi, who wasn't on her rock yet. To the top of the road, where it went through the gap between the two black walls. He stood at the edge of the notch and looked at the goose.

It was sitting in the middle of the gap, facing west, the way it had been for two weeks. It turned its head and looked at him. Grey and white and large, with an expression of permanent enormous offence. It had bitten him at the ferry, getting into the boat.

"Don't move," whispered Gabe. "Tomorrow. Don't move."

The goose looked at him.

"I know you know," whispered Gabe. "Everybody knows. Suzu knows. Hyv's *decided* not to know. You've been sitting here two weeks." His voice went. "Just don't move. Sit there. He's coming up at the third dawn and you have to be *here*."

The goose looked at him for a long time.

Then it hissed. Very quietly. Not at him, exactly. More as though it found the whole conversation beneath it, and wished he'd go away.

"*Thank* you," whispered Gabe, and went back down to the fire. He was crying so hard he could barely see the switchbacks.

***

At the end of the third dusk, in the dark before the dawn, somebody moved across the camp.

Gabe saw it because he was the only one awake. He'd thought he was the only one awake.

Dec. Getting up from the fire across the camp, very quietly, the way he did everything. Not waking Suzu, who was asleep sitting up against a rock. Not waking Kotori. He stood up and looked down at Hyv, asleep beside him with her knees up and her tail round her feet and Xav's drawing in her coat.

He crouched down beside her and said something. Gabe couldn't hear it. One word, probably. Her name.

Hyv opened her eyes.

She looked up at him. Gabe could see her face in the last of the firelight, from across the camp. She saw everything. She'd always seen everything. She looked at Dec crouched beside her in the dark before the dawn, and at the road going up behind him to the notch, and at the sky over the rim just starting to go grey.

She didn't say anything. She got up.

Dec held out his hand. She took it. Not his arm, with both hands, the way she'd held it for seven months. His hand. Just one. As if this were the kind of walk you did holding hands.

They went up the road toward the notch. The two of them, in the dark, on the last switchbacks, not looking back.

Gabe lay very still by the fire and watched them go.

Somebody else was awake.

At the bottom of the road, where it left the camp and started up the last steep switchbacks to the rim, somebody was sitting on a rock. With a long spear across her knees. A sheep lying beside her.

PenguChi.

She'd been sitting there, Gabe realised, since before Dec got up. Since before the dawn. On the rock at the bottom of the road, facing up it. At the back of everybody. Where nobody could come up the road behind the two people walking up it without going past her first.

She didn't look round at the camp. She sat there with her spear, facing up the road, very still. The way she'd stood at the back of Gabe at the north door on the longest night. And the way she'd lain down by the fire on the sixth night coming home from the hill, when he took the back for once, so she could sleep.

Gabe looked at her back for a long time.

He thought about getting up. Going to sit beside her. Being at the back of the back, for once.

He didn't. It was hers. He lay by the fire with Rae's hand in his and watched the grey come up over the rim of the crater, and the two small figures going up the last switchback, and the goose in the notch, waiting.

He kept it. All of it.

He'd never been so happy in his life.
