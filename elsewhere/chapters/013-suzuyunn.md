He'd watched Ice do all of this ten days ago. Every step, from inside Ice's eyes. The mirror, the slider, the rack. He'd watched it on his phone, on buses and in bed after, and he'd thought he knew what it would be like.

He moved his fingers, and they moved, with no gap, none. It was afternoon, and it was gold.

He came out onto a cobbled square at the top of a hill, and the whole world went down from it in long soft waves, green and gold and green, as far as he could see. Downs. Rolling chalk hills, with the white showing through the turf in places like bone through skin, and hedges running along the folds. Meadows of tall grass the colour of honey. A windmill on the next hill, sails turning slowly. Above it all, very high up, so small he could only find them by the sound, larks. Dozens of them, singing as if they'd been wound up and let go.

It was warm. The sun was on the back of his neck. It smelled of cut grass and dust and something sweet he couldn't name.

The woman at the end of the line was the oldest of them, and the only one who stopped when he reached the edge of the meadow.

She was small and very brown and very wrinkled, with white hair under her straw hat. A big shaggy dog lay in the shade of the hedge, watching everything with one eye. He mowed until the light went.

He stood in the line, the seventh mower, between Wenna and a big silent neighbour called Hob. He kept time with them. That was the strange part, the part he hadn't expected. After the first hour he stopped thinking about his own stroke and started *listening* to theirs. Six blades going *shhhhk* and his own going *shhhhk* a fraction late, then on time, then on time again. Eventually he couldn't hear his own at all, because it was part of the rest.

His grade went up to fifty, and fifty-five, and sat there. His arms burned and his back burned and his stamina emptied and filled and emptied. The long meadow went down in front of them a swathe at a time, gold, all the way to the hedge.

In the evening, Wenna fed him. Bread and a hard yellow cheese and an apple, on the step of the barn, with the dog's chin on his knee. It did nothing for his health bar and he ate every crumb.

"You keep time," said Wenna, watching him. "Most of your lot, when they came through this week, they'd swing once and look at the sky. You keep time."

The downs woke up at night.

Wenna had warned him. She'd said the barrows woke when the sun went down, the long low grassy mounds along the top of the ridge that were older than the town. He hadn't asked what that meant. He'd thought he'd see.

He saw.

He was walking back up toward the square in the blue dusk with his stick over his shoulder when the first one came up out of the turf beside the path. It was small, about the size of a cat, and grey, and not quite there: a shape made of mist and faint light, drifting a little above the grass like smoke that had decided to be an animal.

[ Barrow Wisp (Lv 2) ]

It came at him slowly. He hit it with the stick, because the stick was what he had, swinging it the way he'd swung the scythe all evening, low and round from the right with his back foot first and his breath going out. The wisp came apart.

And as it came apart, a light came out of it.

It wasn't the grey dust he'd watched rats turn into on Ice's stream. Something else came out of the grey, a single small point of blue, soft and bright, like a candle flame seen through water. It hung in the air where the wisp had been for a second. Then it began, slowly, to rise and fade.

He put out his hand without thinking, and it settled in his palm.

It was warm. Not hot. Warm the way a hand is warm, or a cup of tea after you've held it a while. And it hummed. Very faintly, more a feeling than a sound, up through the bones of his hand. A tiny, steady note.

He stood on the path in the blue dusk and looked at it, close, with his half-closed eyes. He wasn't afraid of it. He didn't think he could have been afraid of it if he'd tried. It was the most interesting thing he'd ever held.

It faded out in his palm, slowly, like a coal going grey.
