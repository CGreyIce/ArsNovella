Ice didn't know what Lucan would do, and it was the best feeling he'd ever had in a fight.

He'd read everybody. That was what he did. You looked at people until you knew what they'd do, and then you went first, and they answered. Kestrel from the moon. Xav for a year and a half, until Xav could make him sit down against a wall. The seven at the ring. The Drowned Crown and the Hour and the dark itself, on a hill, breathing through twelve gaps. He'd always known.

He couldn't read Suzu. He'd told Hyv that in a tunnel in May. But he knew where Suzu *wouldn't* be. That was something.

He couldn't read Lucan at all.

Three paces off, in the middle of the bridge over the third stream, at twenty-nine per cent. Flat-footed, feet pointing at Ice, sword low. Looking at where Ice was. Not where he'd be. Lucan had spent his whole life being early, and Ice had read him from a terrace in a month because early was easy to read. Early told you where it was going.

On time didn't tell you anything. On time just met you.

He stood at forty-nine per cent and looked at a man he'd beaten on this bridge a year ago. And he didn't know what the man would do next. And he was so happy he could hardly hold the sword.

***

So he made the time.

He hadn't planned it. He didn't plan things, in a fight. He just knew, the way you know where the step is in the dark. If Lucan was on time, then the only question left was: whose?

He turned his sword in his hand and tapped the flat of it on the rim of his shield.

*Tik.*

Small. Nobody on the terraces would hear it over the stream. Lucan would. Lucan was three paces off and looking at where Ice was, and where Ice was had just made a sound.

*Tik.*

He did it again, on the beat. The same gap. And again. *Tik. Tik.* Like Leaka's lion at New Year, with Curse in the back end who couldn't see, hearing the rim a breath ahead. Like the hill, and nine people round a fallen stone, waiting for the boom so they'd pull at once.

Tempo. It was for anything that needed to happen at the same time.

*Tik.*

And on the beat, he went.

The river. On the beat. Not a breath ahead now. Exactly on the *tik*. Lucan met him. *Tock*, Lucan's blade against his. On the beat, because Lucan was on time, and the time was the *tik*.

Ice felt it land in him like a door opening. Lucan was on time. For Ice's time.

*Tik.* The river, the other side. *Tock.*

*Tik.* Close. *Tock.*

*Tik.* Far. Lucan didn't come. He stood on the beat. *Tock*, when Ice came back to him.

They did it for six minutes. Ice making the time, a sound on a rim, steady. Lucan meeting him on it, every beat, better and better, because it was the easiest thing in the world to be on time for a beat you could hear. They hit each other, both ways, on the beat.

The terraces had gone quiet again. Not breath-held quiet. Something else. Half of Masmark listening. Ice thought they could hear it now, over the stream. *Tik. Tock. Tik. Tock.* Like a clock in a very big room.

***

He knew how it ended about a minute before it did.

Not because he'd read Lucan. Because he'd read himself. He was at thirty-four per cent and Lucan was at sixteen. And Lucan was on time for every beat, and getting better, and there was nothing in the river or the rain or the weight that Lucan couldn't meet now, on time. Lucan had learned all three on this bridge, from Ice, by losing to them. Then learned the fourth thing, from a man with a scythe on a floor in the west, by watching.

There was only one thing Ice had that Lucan didn't.

The time was his.

*Tik.*

*Tik.*

*Tik.*

He didn't do the next one.

***

It wasn't the fen. The fen was waiting for something to come at you. It wasn't a beat that never came, either. Not the way it had been last July, when Lucan was early and in the air and the beat he'd decided on wasn't there.

Lucan wasn't early. Lucan was on time.

On time for the *tik* that didn't come.

Ice watched it happen. Three paces off. Lucan, on the beat that wasn't there, meeting it. His sword going to the place it went to on every *tik*, to meet Ice where Ice was. Exactly on time. For a sound that had been coming every beat for six minutes and didn't come.

For one breath, Lucan was on time for nothing.

He'd practised that. On this bridge, in June, three minutes, for nothing. Ice had seen him do it at the start of this fight, four minutes and a half of standing still with Ice in the fen. Lucan could be on time for nothing better than anybody alive. He'd learned it.

But not when the nothing was Ice's.

The sword was already back. Low and long. Ice had brought it there on the last *tik*, before the one that didn't come, while Lucan was meeting the river. Planted, heavy, held. And on the beat that wasn't there, in the breath when Lucan's sword was on time for nothing, he started it.

Not swung. Started.

It came round slow. So slow you could see it the whole way. Lucan saw it. Of course he did; he was looking at where Ice was. He saw it the whole way.

And he was on time for the *tik*. Not for the sword.

*BOOM*.

***

Lucan went across the bridge.

All the way, along the planks, into the west rail. The same place he'd gone last July. Not as far. He'd learned to be heavy too. He hit the rail and slid down it and sat on the planks with his back against it and his sword beside him.

[ Lucan · 3% ]

The whole of the gorge was silent.

Ice stood in the middle of the bridge at thirty-four per cent with his sword at the end of the slow one, where it had ended. He was breathing hard. He hadn't breathed hard in a fight since the hill.

Lucan sat against the west rail and looked at him. Along the bridge, at where he was.

He didn't get up. He didn't reach for his sword. He sat there at three per cent with his knees up, the way he'd sat on the shingle at the foot of the island's tower a year ago, and on the wet stones under this bridge last night, with Drew.

"Whose time," said Lucan.

His voice was very quiet. It carried anyway. The whole gorge was listening.

"Whose time was I on," said Lucan.

"Mine," said Ice.

Lucan looked at him for a long time.

Then he laughed. On the planks, against the west rail, at three per cent. Out loud. Not the short surprised one. A long one, all of it, with his head back against the rail and his eyes shut. Ice had never once heard Lucan laugh like that. He didn't think anybody had.

"Yield," said Lucan, laughing.

***

The gorge went up.

Ice didn't hear most of it. It went up both walls at once, every terrace, half of Masmark, all the way to the sky over the lake. He heard one voice over all of it, from the north terrace, at the front. The loudest thing in Masmark, screaming. His other name. The one only Gabe and the poly lot used. He'd heard it at fifty-eight minutes from across the bridge and nearly turned his head, which he'd never once done in a fight in his life.

He didn't turn his head now either.

He walked down the bridge to the west rail. Slowly. He didn't hurry; there was plenty of time, and he'd decided to use all of it. He stopped in front of Lucan, sitting on the planks with his back to the rail. He looked down at him.

Lucan held out his hand. Palm up. The way he'd held it out on this bridge last July at six per cent, with his sword gone over the parapet into the stream. The way he'd held it out on the wet stones under the bridge last night. Ice had said *after*.

It was after.

Ice took it.

He pulled Lucan up off the planks, at three per cent, onto his feet. They stood at the west end of the bridge with their hands still holding. Neither of them let go for a moment.

"Again," said Lucan.

"Yes," said Ice.

"Next year."

"Yes."

"I'll learn your time," said Lucan. "The *tik*. I'll learn to hear it coming. Before you make it." He was still laughing, a bit, at the edges. "Or I'll learn to make my own."

"I know," said Ice.

"It'll take a year."

"I know."

"Good," said Lucan. Which was Suzu's, and Lucan had never once met Suzu. Ice thought it had probably come across the map in a clip.

***

Then Lucan did a thing Ice hadn't expected.

He let go of Ice's hand. He turned round, at the west end of the bridge, at three per cent. And he looked up.

At the north terrace. At the rail. Not at the front of it, where Hyv was screaming and Rafi was screaming and Gabe was crying. At the end of the rail. A little apart.

Kestrel.

She was standing exactly where she'd said she'd be. Ice could see her from the bridge. Small and still, in VANTA's black, at the end of the rail, with her bow on her back and a girl beside her looking at the gulls. She'd been crying; he could see that from here. She wasn't now. She was looking at Lucan.

Lucan looked up at her for a long time. The first time he'd looked up since March.

He didn't wave. He didn't say anything. He just looked. The way you'd look at somebody who'd told you they'd be somewhere, and was.

At the end of the north rail, Kestrel of VANTA raised her hand. Not high.

On the parapet at the east end, Ves wrote something on her slate. Ice saw her do it. Then she held it up, for the terraces, the way she had at fifty-eight minutes.

***

Hyv came down onto the bridge.

She'd never been on it. Not in a fight. She'd watched from the bottom step and from terraces and from the corner by the hatch. She came down the north terrace steps now at a run, through everybody, and out onto the planks. All the way along the bridge to the west end, where he was standing with Lucan. She stopped in front of him.

She was red past the horns. She'd been screaming for seventy-four minutes. Her voice was gone.

"You *didn't know*," said Hyv. A croak. "You didn't know what he'd do."

"No."

"For *seventy-four minutes*."

"Yes."

"You were *so happy*." Her eyes were doing the thing they did, sometimes, where they stopped looking at everything and looked at one thing. "I could see it from the *terrace*. I could see it from *across the gorge*."

"I know," said Ice. "You screamed my name."

"*Which* one?"

"The other one," said Ice.

Hyv stared at him. Then she put both hands flat on his chest and pushed him back against the west rail of the bridge over the third stream. In front of half of Masmark and Lucan of Sable Court at three per cent. And kissed him.

He'd gone first, all fight. She was going first now. It was fair.

The gorge went up again. Ice didn't hear it. He put his hand on the back of her head, between her horns, and kissed her back. And beside them, at the west rail, Lucan of Sable Court said, to nobody in particular, "*I heard her*. *All seventy-four minutes*."

***

Afterwards, a lot of things happened, and Ice remembered them in pieces.

Drew coming down onto the bridge, slowly, the way he climbed, and sitting down on the planks beside Lucan, a sword's length off, without saying anything. Lucan sitting down beside him. The two of them sitting there at the west end of the bridge while everybody else came and went, the way they'd sat on the shingle a year ago. The one who stays.

Kestrel at the east end of the bridge. Not coming further. Lucan seeing her there and getting up again, at three per cent, and walking the length of the bridge to her. Standing in front of her. Ice didn't hear what either of them said. He saw Kestrel's face. He'd never seen it do that.

Rafi at the front of the north terrace, a door, standing very straight. Shouting *BOSS* until somebody gave him a chestnut.

Gabe, crying, obviously, with Rae holding him up at no paces.

Aurelie on the west wall of the gorge, standing up out of her carried chair. Meridian round her in blue. Vane beside her saying something that made her laugh.

The Ninefold on their balcony, seven, very still. Teo at the front, looking not at the bridge but at the north terrace. At a man with long silver hair asleep sitting up against the rail, with an empty cup in his lap, who'd slept through the last ten minutes. Of course he had. It was a waste not to.

Xav, at the top of the north terrace steps, with his sketchbook open and his hand moving. He didn't look up when Ice came past. He said, still drawing: "You made the time."

"Yes."

"I never thought of that. In five fights." Xav's pencil went on. "You never did it to me."

"You were never on time," said Ice. "You were early. Then you were still." He looked at the page. Two people on a bridge, and between them, very small, a mark Xav had never drawn before: a short line, on the rim of a shield. "You're on time now. Since Suzu."

Xav stopped drawing.

"So next time," he said slowly, "you'll do it to me."

"If you beat Suzu," said Ice.

Xav looked up at him for a long time. Then he laughed, on the terrace steps, and went back to drawing, and didn't stop for an hour.

Kestrel, at the end of the bridge, much later, when Lucan had gone back to the planks to sit with Drew. She came along the north terrace rail to Ice, a little apart from everybody, the way she stood.

"I watched him," said Kestrel.

"I know," said Ice. "He looked up. At you."

"I know," said Kestrel. She was quiet. "I didn't decide anything." She looked down at the bridge, at the two of them sitting on the planks. "It was the best thing I've ever watched."

"Good for—"

"*Don't*," said Kestrel. And laughed, out loud, on the north terrace in front of VANTA, which Brann said afterwards was the second time in six years.

***

Out there, at a quarter past eight, he came out of the egg in the back room at Melaka Raya and sat on the edge of it for a long time with his feet on the floor.

Suzu was on the edge of the next one. Awake, for once. Looking at him.

"You made the time," said Suzu.

"You slept through it."

"I slept through the last ten minutes," said Suzu. "I saw the *tik*." He got up. "I knew what you'd do."

Ice looked at him.

"You can't read me," said Ice. "Nobody can, in a fight. Everybody says."

"I can't read you," agreed Suzu. "I just knew where you wouldn't be. On the beat." He went to the door. "So I went to sleep. It was least effort."

He went to the kitchen. A moment later Ice heard the kettle go on, and then Gabe's voice, very loud, saying *SEVENTY-FOUR MINUTES*. And then Suzu's, quietly, saying *morning*.

He sat on the edge of the egg in the grey light and put his hand on his chest, where in here there'd be a coat, and in the coat a small thing wrapped in red paper.

Out here there was nothing there. Just his hand.

He thought about the notch. About saying *not just here*. About saying the rest.

He sat there a long time.

***

He'd seen it at the third dawn, on the netloft's step, with Hyv asleep on his shoulder and her voice gone. He looked at it for a long time.

He didn't think about the level. He thought about seventy-four minutes. About a man who'd been early his whole life standing still on a bridge, on time, for nothing. About a *tik* that didn't come, and the laugh against the west rail. *Whose time. Mine.*

And about a small thing in his coat. Wrapped in a square of Curse's red paper. A band of black iron that didn't shine, out of the fourth tunnel, quenched in the dark. A small gold stone set in it that caught the light from wherever you looked at it. He hadn't shown it to anybody. He'd carried it fourteen days and seventy-four minutes on a bridge.

Not yet.

The second dawn out. Early.
