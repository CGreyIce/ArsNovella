Tif had the first day's chat log open on her second screen before she'd had her coffee, because she'd promised herself she would.

She'd saved it. Of course she'd saved it. She saved everything; that was the kind of mod she was. Every log of every stream for four years, in a folder on her desktop called *ice (do not delete)*. Inside it, another folder for the game, and inside that, the first one. *Mon 16 Nov 2026. ELSEWHERE day 1.*

She opened it at seven in the morning, Singapore time, in her room, with the curtains still shut and the egg in the corner humming to itself. Xyro was asleep in it. He'd gone in at six for the early dawn and wouldn't be out till six tonight. She sat on the edge of her bed in the blue light of the screen and scrolled up to the very top.

*tehpeng: here*

That was the first line. Teh Peng, who'd been watching for four years and never said where he lived. Then Frog. Then Mangoes, with her lectures muted in another tab in Ipoh. Then Tif herself.

*⚔ titanium_ii_fluoride: first*

She'd been second. She'd typed *first* anyway. She'd always typed *first*, even when she wasn't, because it annoyed him and he never said anything about it.

She scrolled down. Four watching. Seven. Ten. Salty, Niko and Aleaka, the way they were always there. Gabe from his work toilet. Hana, who said *the swamp guy did it*. Jared. Chris with his spreadsheets. Twenty-one, at the most, on the first day. She'd counted it at the time, and she counted it again now, slowly, every name, with her finger on the screen.

Twenty-one people, and a man in a fen with a sword and shield, looking at herbs.

She remembered making the counter. She'd done it at about four in the afternoon, when he'd cut the stream for the first time because he'd found the door. *Ice went dark.* She'd thought it was funny. She'd typed *!dark* and the bot had said *1*. She'd thought it would get to ten, maybe. If he was lucky.

She opened the bot's dashboard in another tab.

*!dark: 418*

"You idiot," said Tif, to the screen, very fondly, and went to make the coffee.

***

The studio put it up at noon.

She'd known it was coming. Everybody had. There'd been a countdown on the official site for a week, and a little gold *1* on the logo. The forums had been making lists of what it would be since October. A patch. A new continent. A level cap. A free pod for everybody who'd played since day one, which was never going to happen, and everybody knew it, and everybody hoped anyway.

It was none of those. It was a page.

*YEAR ONE*

*A year ago today, the doors opened, and you came through them. Thank you.*

*This is the Hall of First Clears. Every door, every hall, every place in Elsewhere that someone walked into first and came out of again. In the order it happened. With the names of the people who did it. They'll be on this page as long as there's a game.*

And then a list.

Tif read it on her phone, in the kitchen, with the coffee going cold in her hand.

It was long. Longer than she'd expected. The game was enormous; she knew that, but she didn't really know it. Every door anyone had ever opened first, anywhere in the world, all in a long gold column. Names she'd never heard of. A guild from Lagos, *First to the Bottom of the Wells*. A company from Lima. Three girls from Seoul with a door under a waterfall. Sable Court, out east, again and again, more than anyone. Meridian, out west, the big blue banner with the gold line, with their name on twelve.

She scrolled past all of it, because she was looking for one thing. And it was at the top.

Of course it was at the top. It was the first one. In the order it happened.

*Fri 11 Dec · Day 26*
*THE DROWNED CHOIR · Fernwick*
*The first five-player dungeon cleared in Elsewhere.*
*Moonlight Harvest — Ice · Kai · Sketch · Suzuyunn · Kotori*

Tif stood in the kitchen and looked at it for a long time.

She'd been there. Not there, in the game. She'd been in the chat. Eleven thousand people in the chat by then, and she'd been the only mod. She'd banned forty-one people that night and counted every single !dark. And then, at the end, the window had come up in the corner of his POV. And she'd sat in her room with her hands over the keyboard and not typed anything at all for a whole minute, because she couldn't.

She scrolled down.

*THE LONG WATCH*
*The first door of the second rank cleared in Elsewhere.*
*Moonlight Harvest — Ice · Kai · Suzuyunn · Kotori · Leveti*

And further down.

*THE FIRST HALL FOR TEN.*
*Crimson Ledger*

She made a face at that one, out of loyalty.

And further.

*Sat 21 Aug · Day 275*
*THE ELM BELOW · Elmcross*
*The first hall of the second rank kept in Elsewhere.*
*Constellation*

Under that one there were sixty names.

All sixty. Not just the ten who'd gone in on the fifth try. The studio had put the whole Roll. Every name on Ama Corrie's book, in the order they'd signed it. KeiKatamori at the top. Then higuVT, gigichiii_, Hyvred, Bee. Down and down, Drew and Tiger and Inaxho and Maigo and HakoP and Salty and itswendy. Rafi and Kite and Bram_V and Joy and Tobi. The nine from Moonlight Harvest, and the eight from Riverbank, and Hana's five. And at the bottom, the six who'd signed last, in August. PenguChi. Tif2. xyrolizea. sleptzzy. xolalotl. AltheaVT.

*Tif2.*

She looked at it.

She'd never cleared anything first in her life. She hadn't been in the Elm Below; she'd been on the stair with Kotori making tea. She'd been in the game four months. Her name was on the Hall of First Clears of the biggest game in the world, at the bottom of a list of sixty, because she'd signed a book in a round town in August. And because the studio had decided, when it came to that one, to put down everybody.

She didn't cry. She wasn't a crier. She put her phone face down on the kitchen counter and drank her cold coffee and looked at the wall for a while.

Then she picked the phone up and sent a message to the group chat, the one for Thea's six.

*[ tif ]: we're on the wall*

And then, because she couldn't help it:

*[ tif ]: thea you're at the bottom. last. very slow*

She got three replies in a minute. Thea's was a row of crying faces. Xola's said *WE'RE ON THE WALL*, in capitals. Chi's said *i was watching from the back*, which was how Chi said everything.

***

She decided at about two o'clock that she wasn't going in.

It wasn't a big decision. She'd been going in every evening since August. Into the egg, into the game, into Thea's six and the round town and the banner room. Tif2, healer, Lv 37, staff and a little Kindle she'd learned early. She liked it. She liked it a lot more than she'd thought she would. She'd never played an MMO before this; she'd only ever watched one man play them, for four years.

That was the thing.

For four years she'd watched. Every stream. First in, always, typing *first* even when she wasn't. Counting the !darks. Banning the idiots. Being the only mod, these days, because Jun Liang had gone quiet and the others had drifted off. And then in August she'd gone into the egg. And for four months she'd been in the game on the evenings he streamed. And you couldn't type in a Twitch chat from inside an egg.

So she hadn't been in his chat. Not properly. Not the way she used to be. Nightbot did the counters. A switched-off command she'd written back in April said *Tif's in the egg. Behave. She'll know if you don't.* She'd switched it on in August.

*Nightbot doesn't mean it,* she'd said to Ice, in April, in this room, on a call, when she was deciding. She'd been right. It didn't.

Today was a year.

She wasn't going in.

She messaged Thea. *not coming in tonight. modding.*

Thea wrote back in about four seconds. *GOOD.* And then, a minute later: *tell him we're on the wall.* And then: *actually don't, let him find it himself, he'll make the face.*

*he doesn't have a face*, wrote Tif.

*he has ONE face*, wrote Thea. *it's very small. you'll see it.*

***

He found it at about half past nine.

She'd thought he might never find it. He didn't read posts on stream; he didn't read the forums at all if he could help it. But at half past nine he was at the end of the north tunnel, sitting with his back to the cold wall where the old stone met the new, resting. And somebody in chat posted the link.

It wasn't her. It was Mangoes. *ice. the hall of first clears. you're at the top.*

He read it. The view went still.

Then a window came up in the corner of his POV, the browser in the egg, and he opened it.

She watched him scroll. She could see it all, everything he saw: the gold column, the long list, Lagos and Lima and Seoul and Sable Court and Meridian. He scrolled down, not up, the wrong way, past all of it. All the way to the bottom. Then he had to come back up, slowly, through four hundred lines. He didn't say anything the whole time.

He read all four hundred and twelve.

She timed it. Eleven minutes. Every first clear in the world, from the bottom up, in the cold at the end of a tunnel under the round town. Names he'd never heard of. Guilds he'd never met. He didn't skip any. She could see him not skipping them. The scroll went at reading speed, line by line, the whole way.

And then he got to the Elm Below, and the sixty names under it, and stopped.

He read the sixty. Slowly. All of them. When he got to the bottom of the sixty, to the six who'd signed last, she saw the view go very still on one line. *Tif2.*

Then he went on up.

The Long Watch. *Ice · Kai · Suzuyunn · Kotori · Leveti.* He didn't stop on that one. He went past it, quickly, the way you'd go past your own photo in somebody else's house.

And at the top.

*THE DROWNED CHOIR · Fernwick.*
*The first five-player dungeon cleared in Elsewhere.*
*Moonlight Harvest — Ice · Kai · Sketch · Suzuyunn · Kotori.*

He stayed on that one for a long time.

She knew what he was looking at. Not his own name. The one in the middle. *Sketch.* Xav had been in Moonlight Harvest for thirty-three days, a long time ago, and then he'd gone to wait for eight idiots on a riverbank. And there he was, still, on the wall. In the middle of the five. Where he'd been.

"Oh," said Ice.

That was all. *Oh.* Very quietly, at the end of a tunnel.

And there it was. Thea was right. She saw it in the POV, the way you could sometimes see his face without seeing it: the tiny tilt of the view, a breath let out. A very small face. The only one he had.

She did the thing at the end.

She'd planned it since the morning, since the log. She'd counted. It had taken her an hour after lunch, cross-checking names, and she'd checked it twice. At the end of the stream, at a quarter to midnight, when he was walking back up the tunnel toward the Seven Below with three Delver claws in his pack and Lv 45 just come up in the corner, she typed it.

⚔ titanium_ii_fluoride: ice
⚔ titanium_ii_fluoride: on day 1 there were 21 people in chat
⚔ titanium_ii_fluoride: 20 of them are here tonight. in chat or in the egg

He stopped walking.

He stood in the tunnel under the round town, in the dark, with his lamp. He didn't say anything for so long that chat started to wonder if he'd gone AFK. She didn't wonder. She knew what it looked like when he was reading something twice.

"Who's missing?" said Ice.

She hadn't expected that either. She should have. Of course he'd ask who was missing.

⚔ titanium_ii_fluoride: one. a guy called kopiCdude. hasn't been in since march

"Kopi," said Ice.

He thought about it. She watched him think, in the dark, standing still.

"He used to watch at lunch," said Ice. "On a phone. He said he was a bus driver. He watched with no sound in the canteen." He paused. "He said *ok* every time I died."

She hadn't known that. She'd banned forty-one people on the night of the drowned church and counted four hundred and eighteen !darks. She'd been there every stream for four years. She hadn't known that kopiCdude was a bus driver.

He'd known.

"Tell him hi," said Ice. "If he comes back. Tell him I said hi." And he started walking again, up the tunnel, with his lamp.

She sat at her desk in the dark with her hand over her mouth.

***

He ended the stream at midnight, at the third dawn, in the banner room, sitting in the first of the nine. The cake was still on the table. About half of it had gone. Somebody had eaten the *1*.

He didn't make a speech. He never made speeches.

"That's a year," he said, to the POV. To ninety-something thousand people, and the twenty. "Thank you for coming. All of you." He thought about it. "I didn't think anybody would. I thought it'd be ten."

And the stream went dark.

*!dark* didn't tick over. It didn't count ends of streams. She'd built it that way on the first day, on purpose, so it would only ever count the secrets. She sat and looked at it anyway. *418*.

Behind her, the egg's lid came up with a sigh. Xyro sat up in it, blinking, with the blanket she'd put over the lid falling off onto his lap.

"Why is there a blanket," said Xyro.

"No reason."

"Did he find it? The wall?"

"He read all four hundred and twelve," said Tif. "From the bottom."

Xyro looked at her for a long moment, in the dark, with the blanket in his lap. He'd known her two years. He knew her face.

"You're crying," he said.

"I'm not a crier," said Tif.

"You're crying a bit."

"I'm crying a *bit*," said Tif. "It's been a *year*." And she put her head down on the desk next to her cold bitter tea and laughed into her arms, and cried a bit, and laughed. Xyro got out of the egg and came and sat on the floor beside her chair and leaned his head on her knee. He didn't say anything, which was the right thing.

After a while she sat up and opened the dashboard and wrote a new command. She thought about it for a long time first. Then she typed it out and set it live.

*!yearone — 21 on day 1. 20 still here. one bus driver, missed. "tell him hi."*

She'd switch it off tomorrow, probably. Or next week. Or never.

She went to bed at one and slept until the egg woke her at ten, and went in, and found Thea's six in the banner room arguing about the cake. Somebody had saved her a piece. It was the bit with the edge of the *1* on it, in jam. She didn't ask who. She thought she knew.
