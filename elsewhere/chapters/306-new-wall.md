Xav had been drawing Dec for a year and a half, and he'd never once got his face right, and on the Saturday on the bridge he finally understood why.

He'd been on the north terrace steps, at the top, with his sketchbook. Not at the rail with everybody. Up the steps, where you could see the bridge whole. Drawing. He always drew in fights; it was how he watched. Seventy-four minutes. Two people on a bridge over a gorge, and half of Masmark on the walls.

At about fifty minutes, after the *BOOM*, he'd looked at Dec's face across the gorge and started drawing it. And it had stayed still.

That had never happened. Not once in a year and a half. Dec's face in a fight never stayed still long enough to draw. It did the small sudden thing, and then something else, and then it was already somewhere else, like the rest of him. A breath ahead of the pencil. Xav had a wall of them in Riverbank's rooms at the top of the netloft. A year and a half of Dec's face, pinned up one over another. Not one of them right.

On the bridge, at fifty minutes, it stayed still.

He'd drawn it in about forty seconds. Fast, a few lines. It was right. First time.

He'd sat on the terrace steps afterwards and looked at it for a long time, trying to work out what was different.

He'd worked it out at the end, when Lucan said *whose time* against the west rail, and Dec said *mine*.

Dec hadn't known what Lucan would do. For seventy-four minutes. That was what was different. Every other face Xav had ever drawn of him was a man who already knew. Who'd read the other person, and was a breath ahead, and was already somewhere else. You couldn't draw that. It wouldn't hold still.

You could draw a man who didn't know. He stayed exactly where he was, looking.

***

On the Monday, Val sat on the floor of Riverbank's rooms with her satchel and her pages spread out round her, and said *I need a new mark*.

"For what?"

"For Lucan," said Val. She had his page in front of her. She'd been keeping it for a year. Dashes and dots and three kinds of mark, the way he moved. Mostly one mark, over and over, a small arrow pointing forward. *Early.* "He's not early any more. I don't have a mark for it."

"On time," said Xav.

"I know what it's *called*. I don't know what it looks like."

Xav thought about it. He looked at the wall. At the one new drawing pinned over all the old ones: a man sitting cross-legged on a floor with a scythe across his knees, smiling.

"Suzu's are circles," he said. "You said. *Where he isn't.*"

"Mostly circles."

"Lucan's is where he *is*," said Xav. He picked up her pencil. On the corner of Lucan's page, he drew a short line. Just one, upright, small. "Like a tap. On a rim."

Val looked at it for a long time.

"That's the *tik*," she said.

"Dec's *tik*," said Xav. "Lucan was on time for it. Every beat. Until the one that didn't come." He put the pencil down. "That's what on time looks like. A line where the beat is."

Val took the pencil back. She looked at the line he'd drawn. Then, very carefully, under the year of small forward arrows on Lucan's page, she drew it. Again and again. A row of short upright lines, where the arrows had been.

"He's going to hate that it's Dec's," said Val.

"He's going to learn to make his own," said Xav. "He said. Against the rail." He looked at the wall. "Next year."

***

Lucan came to the netloft on the Tuesday, for Riverbank.

Not for Suzu; he'd been the night before, Rafi said, and asked, and been told *when you've learned his tik*. He came up the netloft's stairs to Riverbank's rooms at the top, which nobody from Sable Court had ever done, and stood in the door. He looked at the wall. At a year and a half of Dec's face, pinned up, never right. And the one new drawing in the middle.

"That's the one who sat down," said Lucan.

"Yes."

"I asked him," said Lucan. "To fight. He said *next year*." He was quiet. "I asked you nothing. I should have."

Xav looked at him.

"You learned on time from the clip," said Lucan. "From him. In a fortnight. Being hit in the ear by your friend." He looked at RX, on the bed, eating chestnuts. "I learned it from the clip too. From you. In a month. Being hit in the ear by mine."

"Ves?"

"With a slate," said Lucan. "She's very loud when she wants to be."

RX stopped eating.

"You *both* got hit in the *ear*," said RX. "For being *early*."

"Yes," said Lucan and Xav together.

RX lay back on the bed and laughed so much he dropped his chestnuts.

***

"Spar with me," said Lucan. "Over the winter. Here. On the bridge." He was looking at Xav. "You're staying in Masmark. Riverbank. I heard."

"Till September," said Xav. "Then I'm walking west. To fight Suzu again. At the ring. On a Sunday."

"Then till September," said Lucan. "And after you come back." He was quiet. "I need to learn his *tik*. I can't learn it off him; he's going home. You've been on the other side of him for a year and a half. You know his time better than anybody."

"I don't know his time," said Xav. "He never did it to me. In five fights. He only did it to you."

"Then we'll learn it together," said Lucan. "Off the clip. And off each other."

Xav thought about it. He'd been chasing Dec for a year and a half. On his own, mostly. With RX to practise on and Malcolm to say *it'll be March* and Val's pages. He'd never once had somebody else chasing the same thing. Next to him.

"Who'd stand next to you?" said Xav.

He didn't know why he said it. It was Kite's, and Rafi's at the door, and everybody's now.

Lucan looked at him.

"Ves," he said. "She counts." He thought about it. "And Drew, on the planks. He sits." He was quiet. "And you, on the bridge. If you'll spar."

"I'll spar," said Xav.

"That's three," said Lucan.

"You've got three now," said Xav.

***

Malcolm said *it'll be September* on the Tuesday night, and then looked surprised at himself.

"It *will* be September," said Xav. "That's when I'm going."

"I know," said Malcolm. "I meant it. I wasn't being—" He stopped. He looked at his notebook. "I said a month and it's the right month. I don't know what to do with that."

"Write it down."

"I'll walk with you," said Malcolm. "In September. West. To the ring." He wrote it down. "Somebody has to call it."

"You called the last one *even*."

"I know," said Malcolm. "It was the best thing I've ever said." He shut the notebook. "I'd like to say something else next time. I don't know what yet."

"That's new," said Xav.

"Everything's new," said Malcolm.

***

They sparred on the bridge that night. Late, at the third dawn, with nobody on the terraces.

Almost nobody. Ves on the parapet with her slate. And at the end of the north rail, a little apart, a small still figure in black with a bow, who'd come down from the east rim because she'd heard. Lucan didn't look up at her. Xav did, once. Then didn't again.

They stood in the middle of the bridge, three paces apart. Lucan with his sword. Xav with his two blades out and down. Both of them flat-footed, feet pointing at each other.

Neither of them went first.

That was the strange part. Xav had always gone first, against everybody but Dec. Lucan had always gone first against everybody, full stop. Both of them had learned not to, from a man who sat down. They stood on the bridge in the grey light looking at where each other was, and nobody moved, and the stream went under.

"It'll be March," said Xav, eventually.

Lucan almost laughed.

"Ves," he said, without looking round. "Say *begin*."

"I'm not saying it," said Ves, from the parapet. "Neither of you is going to go. I'm not saying *begin* to two people standing still."

So they both went at once. On no signal, at the same moment, because they'd both decided the standing had gone on long enough. And met in the middle.

*Tock.*

Blade into blade. On time. Both of them.

They looked at each other over the two blades, stopped, in the middle of the bridge.

"Oh," said Lucan.

"Yes," said Xav.

They went on for an hour. Nobody won. Nobody was trying to. Two people who'd learned the same thing off the same clip, finding out what it looked like from the other side. Every so often one of them would be early, the old way, without meaning to. The other would put a blade where he *was*, and say *early*. And they'd both laugh.

At the end of the north rail, Kestrel watched the whole hour. When it was over she went back up the terrace steps without saying anything. At the top she stopped and looked back. Not at Lucan. At both of them.

***

RX spent Suzu's forty silver on the Wednesday morning.

He'd been carrying it since May, in a pouch, not spending it, which for RX was a kind of miracle. He went to the chestnut man on the quay below the netloft and bought every chestnut the man had. Then he walked along the quay giving them out. To everybody. The whole guild, packing. The Ninefold in the lane. The fish man. Rafi in the door. The girl at the bread stall, who'd stopped laughing at Teo.

"*Forty ears*," RX said, to each of them. Very seriously, handing over a paper twist. "*For the ears.*"

Nobody understood. Everybody took one.

He gave the last one to Suzu, asleep on the quay under Kotori's blanket. He put it on Suzu's chest, carefully, without waking him.

"I taught him," RX said, to Xav, after. Standing on the quay with an empty pouch. "Forty times. In the ear. Nobody ever says that."

"Suzu said it."

"I know," said RX. "That's why I spent it." He looked at the empty pouch. "So it'd be somewhere. In everybody."

***

On the Wednesday, the last day, the guild packed.

Xav watched it from the top of the netloft. Fifty-nine people and a sheep on the quay, rolling up coats and blankets, filling bags. The fish man came along with a basket of dried fish for the road and gave it to Kotori without a word. Rafi stood in the door, not packing, because he was staying. He was the door. Ash and Kite and Drew sat on the step, a sword's length off each other, three. Leaka lost something, loudly, and Curse found it, and labelled it.

And the Ninefold, in the lane, asking.

They'd been asking all week. Ever since Suzu told Teo *take a tenth, somebody you can't decide*. Seven of them, in sea-blue, at every door in Masmark. Teo at the front with his hands at his sides, very badly, the way Marrok asked. *We're the Ninefold. We've never had a tenth. Would you.* Nobody had said yes. Most people didn't believe them. Teo kept asking anyway.

Xav watched him ask a girl selling bread on the quay, who laughed at him. He didn't go away. He stood there with his hands at his sides until she stopped laughing. Then he asked her something else, which Xav couldn't hear. She looked at him for a long time and then said something back.

Teo sat down on the quay beside her bread stall. Not joining. Just sitting, a sword's length off.

Xav drew it. Fast. He got it right first time.

***

He found Dec on the netloft's roof, at the second dusk, alone.

The roof was flat, tarred, with nets drying on poles. You could see the whole lake from it, gold in the dusk, with the island and the tower in the middle and the ring of lights coming on round the shore. And the crater wall going up behind the city, black. With dozens of roads cut into it, lit, going up to the notches on the rim.

Dec was standing at the edge, looking up at the crater wall. At one particular notch. The first one, going west.

Xav stood beside him for a while. He didn't say anything. Dec didn't either.

Then Xav took a page out of his sketchbook. Carefully, along the stitching. He held it out.

Dec looked at it.

It was the drawing from the bridge, at fifty minutes, after the *BOOM*. Dec's face across the gorge, looking at Lucan. A few lines. Fast. Right.

"You got it," said Dec. Very quietly.

"First time," said Xav. "In a year and a half." He was still holding it out. "You didn't know what he'd do. That's why. Every other time, you already knew. Your face was already somewhere else. I could never catch it." He looked at the drawing. "On the bridge you didn't know. So it stayed."

Dec took the page.

He looked at it for a long time. At his own face, not knowing.

"Give it to Hyv," said Dec.

"It's yours."

"I know," said Dec. "Give it to her anyway. She saw it from the terrace. She said." He handed it back. "She'd like to have it. The one where I didn't know."

Xav took it back. He looked at Dec. At his face, now, on the roof, looking up at a notch on the crater wall. It wasn't doing the small sudden thing. It wasn't a breath ahead. It was doing something Xav had seen once on the bridge, and never before that. Just there. Looking at a place.

Not knowing.

Xav got his pencil out. He didn't ask.

He drew it again. On the roof of the netloft, in the dusk, in about forty seconds. Dec looking up at the notch on the crater wall, with his hands at his sides, not knowing what would happen there.

It was right. Second time.

He didn't show it to Dec. He tore it out carefully along the stitching and put it in his coat. He didn't know why. He thought he might find out.

"September," said Xav.

"If you beat Suzu," said Dec. Still looking at the notch.

"I know," said Xav. "I tied him. That's not beating." He put the sketchbook away. "I'll beat him. Or I'll tie him again. Either way it'll be the best fight I've ever had." He was quiet. "You can have the one after."

Dec looked at him. Then he smiled. All of it.

"Okay," said Dec.

***

On the Thursday morning, at the third dawn, the guild went out of Masmark the way it had come. Up the lane from the quay, through the city, to the bottom of the crater road. Fifty-nine and a sheep.

Riverbank stood on the netloft's step and watched them go. Xav and RX and Malcolm and Val. Rafi in the door beside them, a door, waving his big black sword.

At the bottom of the lane, Hyvred turned round. She'd been holding Dec's arm. She let go of it and came back up the lane to the netloft step, to Xav. He gave her the drawing. She looked at it for a long time. At Dec's face across a gorge, at fifty minutes, not knowing.

She didn't say anything. She put it inside her coat, against her chest, the way Gigi had carried a sprig of hawthorn for fifteen days. And went back down the lane and took his arm again, with both hands.

Xav watched them go up the crater road. Fifty-nine people, small, getting smaller, going up the switchbacks toward the rim, toward the first notch going west.

He had the other drawing in his coat. Dec on the roof, looking up at that notch, not knowing.

He thought he knew now why he'd kept it.
