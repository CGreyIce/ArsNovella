Fenn had been twentieth of twenty his whole life, and he'd always looked up.

It wasn't a skill. It was just what you did when you were twentieth. You sat on the bench at the Elm Below while the windows came for other people, and you looked up at them. *Brann · Bulwark · Hold · 91%*. *Mags · Spear · Reach*. Kestrel's, which came so often nobody looked any more. You looked up because there was nothing else to look at, and because one day, maybe, one would come for you. And you wanted to have seen what they looked like.

Then Kestrel had picked him. Twentieth, for the Crown Below. *I want one person who looks up.*

He still didn't know why that was a thing you could be good at.

***

The sea gave Brann's shield back on Sunday morning.

Fenn saw it first. Of course he did. He was on the cliff edge at the first low water with his eyes on the bay, watching the sea go out. A mile of black sand coming up out of it, shining. Rocks, weed, channels, the rooftops of the drowned city. And on one of the rooftops, a long way out, near the black pool, something flat and iron-grey that hadn't been there yesterday.

"Brann," said Fenn.

Brann came and stood beside him with his arm still in Ottie's sling, which he didn't need. He had a new shield on his back. Pell Calder's. It had cost three times what it was worth. Kestrel had said *pay him*, and he had.

He looked where Fenn was looking for a long time.

"That's mine," said Brann.

The Tidebound brought it up. Marisol herself, at the head of a line of salt-white coats coming in off the flats with their nets. She had the tower shield over her shoulder with the net, as though it weighed nothing. She came up the hundred and forty steps without stopping. At the top she stood in front of Brann and held it out.

"On the old customs house," said Marisol. "Where the sea puts things it's finished with." She looked at him. "It doesn't usually give them back."

"Wren said," said Brann.

"Wren's right about the sea," said Marisol. "Mostly." She looked at the shield, at the dent in its face the size of a man's head. "What did you do to it?"

"Held a street," said Brann.

Marisol was quiet for a moment.

"*Hm*," she said. Which was what the old woman in the round town said, Fenn had heard. The one who kept the Roll. He didn't know what it meant there. Here it seemed to mean *good*.

Brann took the shield. He stood there holding two, his own and Pell Calder's. Then he turned round and gave Pell Calder's to Fenn.

"For your back," said Brann. "Bows get hit from behind."

Fenn held it. It was enormous. He couldn't have lifted it into a fight if his life depended on it.

"I can't use this," said Fenn.

"I know," said Brann. "Sell it back to Calder. For three times." And went down the stair to the harbour with his own shield on his good arm, dented, which was the closest Fenn had ever seen him come to making a joke.

***

They went straight to the square.

Down the black pool's stair, round and round, wet stone. Into the drowned city at the foot of the first street, the north one, at low water. Up it, past the Ninefold's scratches, nine, and seven, and seven. Through the Watch at the first corner, who came up out of their pools streaming. Brann met them with his own dented shield, and Mags went in low, and Ren's fire cracked the shell off their bronze. Past the cart-crab. Past the eels, which Ottie screamed at again, and healed herself on time again, which was becoming a habit.

Twenty. All the way down the first street to the gate at the end of it, iron, open. And through it.

The square.

Fenn stopped in the gate and looked up.

It was round. Of course it was. Round and wide, paved in green stone, with five gates in its walls where the five streets came in. And in the middle, a pool. Not black, like the one in the bay. Clear. Clear and deep and lit from somewhere at the bottom, green-white. The light came up through it and went across the ceiling of rock a long way overhead, in ripples. The whole square moved with it.

At the bottom of the pool, very far down, something shone. A stone. Green, like glass, the size of a cart. With the light in it.

***

It was the sea. That was the only way Fenn could think of it, afterwards. A shape of grey water standing up out of the clear pool. Tall as the old customs house. Wide as the square's gates. With no face, and no hands. With the green stone shining in the middle of it where a heart would be, if water had hearts.

It breathed in.

Fenn felt it. Everybody did. The whole square pulled. Toward the pool, toward the shape, like the floor had tilted. Water came in out of the five gates along the paving in thin fast sheets, running in toward it, ankle-deep and hard. Ottie went down. Mags caught her. Fenn's feet went out from under him and he slid three paces on the wet stone before Brann's hand came down on his collar like a gate.

"*Ebb*," said Kestrel. "*Anchor. Everybody*."

They held. Twenty people braced on green stone, leaning back against the pull. The shape in the middle getting bigger as it drank the water out of the city.

Then it breathed out.

It came off the pool in a wave. Not a wall, like in the street. A ring, going out from the middle in every direction at once, waist-high, white along the top. Across the whole square to the walls. Brann planted the dented shield and VANTA got behind it in a wedge, and the wave hit and split round them and went on past. And slammed into the square's walls and up them and fell back. Fenn came out of it on his knees behind Brann's back, soaked, coughing.

"*Again*," said Kestrel. And it breathed in.

***

The Ninefold came through the second gate on the fourth breath.

Seven of them. Teo at the front with his two short spears. They'd come down the second street, fast, the way they did everything. They hit the square at a run, all seven, straight at the shape in the pool. On the ebb, when it was drinking, while everybody else was braced.

Fenn watched them do it.

Teo went into the water of the shape with both spears. Fast. Beautiful. Fenn had never seen anybody fight like that. Not even Kestrel. And the spears went through it. Like spears going through the sea. Like nothing was there. Teo came out of the far side of it soaked to the skin with his spears in his hands. His face had stopped being a shut door and become something else.

Then it breathed out. And the Ninefold were in the middle of the square with no shield.

The wave took four of them across the paving to the walls. Fenn heard them hit.

[ Ninefold · 7 / 9 ]

They got up. They always got up. But Teo stood by the pool in the wet with his spears, looking at the shape. Then, slowly, across the square, at VANTA behind Brann's shield.

"You can't hit it," said Mags.

"Not while it's moving," said Kestrel. She had an arrow on the string. She hadn't drawn. "It's water. You don't hit water."

"Then *when*?"

Kestrel didn't answer.

She looked at Fenn.

***

He'd known since the gate. He hadn't known he'd known.

It was the stone. The green stone in the middle of the shape, where a heart would be. When the shape breathed in, the stone went dim, and the water round it ran, fast, in toward it. When the shape breathed out, the stone went dim, and the water round it ran out. And in between—

In between, for one breath, the water didn't run.

It stood still. Between drinking and pouring. The whole shape of the sea, standing in the pool, not moving. And the stone in its middle went bright and hard and clear, like a thing you could touch.

*Slack water*, the Tidebound called it. At the top of the tide and the bottom. The moment when it wasn't going out and wasn't coming in. Fenn had heard Marisol say it on the Wednesday and thought it was just a sailor's word.

But it didn't come every breath.

He'd watched four breaths now. Five. In and out, in and out. And the stone had only gone bright once. On the first breath, when they came through the gate. Not since.

Fenn closed his eyes.

He listened. Not to the shape. Not to the breath it was taking now. To the one before. Kestrel had said it to him on the terrace in Masmark, with the gulls. *Don't decide. Look.* And on the fourth bell, in the handbells, it had turned into something else. *I'm not deciding. I'm just listening to the one before.*

The shape breathed in, and the water came in through the five gates.

Not all at once.

One gate. Then another. Then another.

Fenn opened his eyes.

"*Brann*," he said. "It's the streets. It's drinking through the gates. In the order. The *method*."

Brann had already heard it. Fenn saw him hear it. He was standing behind the dented shield with the water running past his boots in sheets, his head a little on one side.

"Plain hunt," said Brann. "Again."

"When it comes round," said Fenn. "When it comes back to the start. One, two, three, four, five. In order. That's when it stops. That's slack." He swallowed. "That's the only time."

"Ten rows," said Brann.

"Ten rows," said Fenn. "Every ten rows. One breath."

Kestrel drew.

Not all the way. Just to her cheek, and held. She didn't look at the stone. She didn't look at the shape. She looked at Fenn.

"Tell me," said Kestrel. "Don't decide. Tell me when."

***

He listened.

The shape breathed in and out. In through the gates in the order and out across the square in a ring. And VANTA held behind Brann. The Ninefold held behind nothing, and went across the paving, and got up. And far off, through the five gates, a long way up the streets, Fenn could hear the sea in the bay. Waiting. The tide wasn't going to stay out forever.

*Three, five, one, four, two*.

*Three, one, five, two, four*.

He didn't count them out loud. He was afraid to. He just listened to each one. And each time, to the one before it.

*One, three, two, five, four*.

That was the ninth. He knew it. He'd heard Brann say it in the ash on the terrace. *Then it comes back round*. The next breath would be rounds. One, two, three, four, five. And at the end of it—

"*Now*," said Fenn.

He said it one breath early. On the one before. Kestrel had said *tell me*, and he did. Before the gates had finished. Before the water had stopped. Before the stone had gone bright.

Kestrel loosed.

She didn't decide. He saw her not decide. She'd been holding at her cheek for nine rows, and he said *now*, and her fingers opened. And the arrow went across the square through the shape of the sea while the water was still running, and Fenn's heart stopped. Because it was going to go through, the way Teo's spears had—

The water stopped.

All of it. At once. The shape stood in the pool, not moving, a statue of the sea. The stone in its middle went bright and hard and clear.

And the arrow went into it.

*Crack*.

The shape screamed. Not a sound. A movement, through the whole of it, like a wave going the wrong way. And then it breathed out, harder than before. And the ring went across the square shoulder-high, and Brann held, and they all held.

Across the square, by the second gate, Teo got up off the paving. He looked at the arrow in the stone. Then he looked at Fenn.

Fenn saw him understand.

***

The second time, ten rows later, they didn't just loose.

Teo came across the square on the ebb, under the pull, with his six. Not at the shape. At VANTA. He got behind Brann's shield with them, all seven, and crouched there. Twenty-seven people in a wedge behind one dented shield. Then he looked at Fenn.

"When," said Teo. Not a question. The way Brann said names.

"I'll say," said Fenn.

On the ninth row, Fenn said *now*. One breath early.

And twenty-seven people went. Kestrel's arrow and Ren's fire and Mags's spear and seven arrows from VANTA's bows. And the Ninefold, all seven, Teo at the front with both short spears. Across the square at a run, into the shape of the sea, as it stopped.

Into stone, this time. Into a thing you could touch.

[ The Ebb · 18% ]

They came out the far side as it breathed again and the ring took them. Fenn heard Teo laughing as he went across the paving. Not a shut door now. Not anything Fenn had seen on him.

And then, through the five gates, a long way up the streets, the sea.

"*Turn*," said Brann.

Fenn heard it. A low sound. A breath being drawn in very slowly by something much bigger than the shape in the pool. The tide in the bay, coming back. Down the streets, in the order, toward the square.

They had ten rows. Maybe less. The shape in the pool was drinking the city's water, and the sea was coming back into the city to give it more.

"We can make one more," said Kestrel.

"It's two," said Fenn. "The sea's in it now. The streets. I can't—" He stopped. He could hear it. The shape breathing in through the gates. And under it, behind it, the sea surging down the streets toward the gates in its own order. Two methods at once. Two sets of bells, ringing over each other. "I can't hear the one before. There's too many."

Brann put his hand on his shoulder. His good hand. Heavy, like a gate.

"You don't ring your bell," said Brann. "You ring the change." He was looking at the pool. "Listen for the change. Not the bells."

Fenn closed his eyes.

***

It was terrible. It was the hardest thing he'd ever done. Twenty-seven people crouched round him, behind a shield, with the sea coming. And him in the middle of them with his eyes shut, listening to two methods at once in a drowned city. The shape's breath through the gates. The sea's surges down the streets. Over each other. Under each other.

He stopped trying to hear the bells.

He heard where they swapped. Where one gate went before another, and then after it. The change. Not who rang. Where they moved. A pattern, the same pattern, coming up through all of it. Through the noise, the way a tune comes up through a crowd.

*One, three, two, five, four*.

He didn't decide. He didn't think he'd got it right. He just heard it, and it was the one before.

"*Now*," said Fenn.

Kestrel loosed.

He heard it go. He didn't see it. His eyes were shut. He heard the bowstring and he heard the water stop, all of it, everywhere. The shape in the pool and the sea in the streets. All of it, standing still in the drowned city for one breath, at slack water.

*Crack*.

And then a sound like a very big bell, a long way down, under everything. Ringing once.

Fenn opened his eyes.

His name was the sixth. He looked at it for a long time. He looked up at it. The way he'd always looked up at windows that came for other people.

It wasn't for other people.

***

The sea waited.

That was the thing. They came up the first street at a walk, all twenty, and the Ninefold seven, through the drowned city. And the sea didn't come. It was standing in the streets behind them, a man's height, grey, still. Waiting. Just as the window had said. Until the last of them was up the stair.

Then, behind them, in the bay, the whole sea let out its breath, and came in.

On the black sand, in the shallows, with the Quiet Oar's boats coming to take them off, Fenn sat down. He couldn't stand. His legs had gone.

Kestrel sat down beside him.

She had a sheet of paper out, folded small, the kind she wrote letters on. And a pencil. She was writing, on her knee, on the wet sand, with the sea coming in round them both. Fenn could see it. She didn't hide it.

*First. Again.*

She stopped. She looked at the sea for a long time. Then she wrote the rest.

*I didn't decide it.*

*— K.*

"Who's that to?" said Fenn.

"The tank," said Kestrel. "In the west." She folded it smaller. "He said go first."

***

The answer came on the Tuesday, at the top of the hundred and forty steps, folded small. Kestrel read it at the fire on the terrace and then held it out to Fenn without saying anything, the way she did now.

Three words.

*Good for you.*

*— I.*

Fenn looked at it. Then at her.

Kestrel had a pencil out already. She wrote on the bottom of his, under his three words, one of her own, and folded it to send back.

*Don't.*

Down on the black sand, in the evening, Harlan of Meridian was sitting with his twenty, crying. They'd made it on the next tide, third, after the Ninefold. He'd stood at the front the whole way down. He'd written to his guild's leader in the west with *THIRD. I WENT FIRST. IT WAS FINE.* And she'd written back one word, which he'd shown everybody on the sand, including people from guilds he'd never met. *Good*.

And at the foot of the cliff stair, by the harbour, Marisol and the Tidebound were going down into the bay for the first time. Not to win anything. Just to see the square, now it was quiet, and the pool, and the green stone. They didn't care about first. They lived here.

"You decided," said Kestrel, at the fire.

Fenn looked at her.

"No," he said. "I listened. To the one before."

"That's deciding," said Kestrel. "The good kind." She was quiet. "Somebody told me there was a good kind. Under a lake. I didn't believe him." She put her bow across her knees. "Good for you."

Fenn thought about it.

"*Don't*," said Fenn.

Kestrel turned and stared at him.

Then she laughed. Out loud, on the terrace, at the fire, in front of VANTA. Fenn had never once heard her do it in a year. Brann, across the fire, with his dented shield across his knees, looked at the two of them for a long time.

"That's two answers," said Brann. To nobody.
