Hyvred found out she was in the ten on Wednesday morning, from Kei, in the banner room, in front of everybody, and nearly walked out of it.

"Ice," said Kei, reading from his map. He'd drawn them on it, the ten, in a ring, with lines between. "Gabe. Kotori. Rachael. Deston. Suzu. Sheng Yang. Xav. Drew." He looked up. "Hyvred."

Hyvred had been sitting at the long table with her chin on her hand, not paying attention, watching the swamp guy sit in his chair at the end by the wall. He'd sat in it every day since Tuesday as if he'd been sitting in it all his life. She took her chin off her hand.

"*Me?*"

"You," said Kei.

"Why *me*? Tiger's thirty-three. Azac's a *wall*. Calvin goes ahead and comes back, that's *exactly* what you'd want in a—"

"Because it's under us," said Kei.

The banner room went quiet.

***

Ama Corrie had told them on Tuesday night, after the signing, in her dry voice, as if she were mentioning the weather.

"There's been a door under my stair since the fourteenth," she'd said. "Since the notice. I've been keeping it shut."

They'd all gone and looked. Forty-nine people crammed into the space under the stair to the eye, where Ama Corrie kept her broom and the chairs had been stacked for four hundred years. At the back of it, in the floor, where there'd been flagstones, there was a hatch. Iron, old, black. It had a ring in the middle of it, and round the ring, cut into the iron, seven lines going out from the middle like the spokes of a wheel. Like seven roads.

On the hatch, in the iron, very small, letters.

*A hall for ten answers only to a banner.*

And on either side of the ring, two iron hooks. The same as the hooks in the banner room. The same as the hooks in a ruined hall at the top of the world, where the old guild's banner had hung before they took it north.

"Crimson Ledger's three went in under three Halls where seven roads meet," Kei said now, in the banner room. "Down in the south. Their own rooms. They bought those rooms this spring. I always wondered why they wanted Halls where seven roads met so much." He put the map down. "This is why. There's one under every Hall like this one. Under the crossroads. Seven ways in."

"And she can see seven ways at once," said Drew, from the other end of the table, quietly.

Everybody looked at Hyvred.

She looked back at them. At forty-nine people, at a long table, under a banner with her own dot on it. And at the end of the table, by the wall, the swamp guy. He was looking at her the way he'd looked at her on a white terrace in a city with no name, right before he put his hand on her head.

She felt her face go hot.

"*Fine*," said Hyvred. "*Fine.* But I'm not standing next to *him*."

"You're the eyes," said Kei. "You stand where you can see. He's the front. You won't be anywhere near him."

"*Good*."

On the end of the table, very quietly, Bee said, "Breathe, Hyv."

***

They went down on Thursday at the second dawn, the ten, with the whole guild crowded into the Hall above to watch them go.

Bee carried the banner. She wasn't in the ten. She'd said *I'm a person with a pencil, I'm not going down a hole*. But she'd painted it, and nobody else was going to carry it. She took it down off its hooks in the banner room, rolled, and carried it to the stair, and down into the space under it. And she hung it on the two iron hooks on either side of the ring in the hatch, so that the night-blue fell across the iron with its gold dots and silver lines.

Nothing happened for a moment.

Then the seven lines cut into the iron round the ring began to glow. Silver-white, like the lines on the banner. Like the lines on the ceiling of the dome by moonlight. And the hatch opened. Not up. Down. It sank, slowly, into the floor, and became the top of a stair. A narrow stone stair, going down into the dark under the Hall of Roads.

Bee stood at the top of the stair with her hand still on the banner.

"It read it," she said, very quietly. "It read the *banner*."

"Somebody knew," said Higu, behind her, and her voice went funny.

The swamp guy went first. Obviously. He always went first. Then Gabe, then Drew. Then Hyvred, because she was the eyes, and the eyes went where they could see, and you couldn't see from the back.

She didn't look at him going down the stair in front of her. She looked at the stair.

***

It was a crossroads.

That was the first thing she saw, at the bottom. A round room, stone, low, with seven openings going out of it like the spokes of a wheel, under the seven roads. Seven tunnels, going away into the dark in seven directions. And in the middle of the room, on the floor, a ring of seven stones. Not herons. Just stones, worn smooth, round a flat stone in the middle with a star cut into it.

She could see down all seven at once.

That was the thing about her eyes, in here. She'd always seen everything, in the game, edge to edge. But she'd never stood somewhere and seen seven ways at once without turning her head, the way she did from the stair in the dome. Down here, in the dark, with her arrows in her quiver and her bow in her hand and her crosshair eye swinging, she could. Seven tunnels. Seven sets of shadows moving, a long way down each one.

"Seven ways in," she said. "Something in all of them. Coming."

"How far?" said the swamp guy. He didn't turn round. He was at the front of the room, by the northern tunnel, shield up.

"Different. North's furthest. South-east's closest. Forty paces. Low. Three of them."

He moved. Not to the north. To the south-east. Without looking at her. He just moved, three steps, shield up, to exactly where she'd said, and set his feet.

The three came out of the south-east tunnel low and fast, exactly where she'd said, and hit his shield.

*Left, forty paces, low.* She'd said that on the longest night to a boy with a crossbow on a bridge she'd never seen him on. She'd been calling things for other people since December. She'd never once called them for him. She hadn't known he'd just *move*. Without looking. Without asking. As if she'd said it and that was that.

She called the next one before she'd thought about it.

"*West. Twenty. High.*"

He moved.

***

It went well for a long time.

That was the trouble. Afterwards, sitting on the stair with her bow across her knees and her face hot, she thought that it had gone well for so long that she'd stopped watching the thing she should have been watching.

Seven tunnels. Things came out of all seven, in waves, never more than three at once. She called them. The front moved: the swamp guy, and Gabe two paces from Kotori, and Drew wherever she said *Drew*. Suzu went wide with the scythe and took whatever came out of the tunnel nobody else was in. Sheng Yang went in behind whatever was looking at the shield. Xav was a knife in the dark, the way he'd been in a drowned church in December, two notes, quick. Kotori rang on the eighth beat of Deston's count, Vigil, a little for everyone. Rachael stood two paces from Gabe and put her Mends where things were going to be. And Deston counted. Steady, under all of it, never louder than he needed to be.

And Hyvred, in the middle of the ring of seven stones, turning, slowly, with her bow, seeing all seven ways at once, calling.

"*North. Fifty.*"

"*East and south-west together. Twenty each. Gabe east.*"

"*Nothing in the north-west. Nothing. It's empty. It's been empty for—*"

She stopped.

It had been empty for a long time. The north-west tunnel. Nothing had come out of it since the start. Six tunnels with things in them, coming in waves. And one, the north-west, under the road she'd watched every evening from the eye. The road the nineteen had come up on Tuesday, with him in front. Empty. Dark. Nothing in it at all.

She looked down it with the crosshair. Properly, the way she looked at something when she really looked. All the way down.

Something was standing at the end of it.

Not coming. Standing. Very tall, very still, at the far end of the north-west tunnel, where it opened out. Holding something up. And as she looked at it, it looked back at her.

"*Ice*," said Hyvred.

She hadn't called him by his name all day. She'd called *front* and *left* and *Gabe* and *Drew*, and never once his name.

He turned round.

***

It came out of all seven at once.

That was what it did. It had been standing at the end of the north-west tunnel. Then it was at the end of all seven, the same tall still shape seven times, coming down seven tunnels toward the ring of stones at the same speed, from seven directions. Holding up seven of whatever it was holding. Seven lanterns, she saw, dark, like the Last Watcher's lantern in the Long Watch that she'd seen on his stream in April.

Seven of it. All coming.

"*Which one*," said Deston, very fast, the first time she'd heard him not count. "*Hyv, which one's real—*"

She looked. All seven. Edge to edge. She looked down all seven tunnels at once with the crosshair swinging, the way she'd looked at seven roads from the eye every evening for a month.

She couldn't tell.

They were the same. All seven of them. Same height, same walk, same dark lantern, same stillness at the top of each step. She looked and looked. There was no tell. No shadow that fell wrong. No dust that lifted where a real foot came down. She'd never not been able to see something, in here. Not once, since the first night, since she'd looked at a ceiling and seen all four corners and cried.

"*I can't*," said Hyvred. "*I can't tell, they're the same, they're all the—*"

They came out of the seven tunnels at once, into the ring.

The swamp guy took one on the shield. Gabe took another. Drew slipped one. Suzu, wide, took one across the middle with the scythe, and it came apart into nothing, and he said *not that one*. Sheng Yang went through another, and it came apart, and he said *not that one*. Xav's two notes went into a third, and it came apart too.

And the real one, wherever it was, swung its lantern.

She didn't see which one did it. That was the thing she'd never forgive. The lantern came round, from somewhere behind her, from the one she hadn't looked at. It hit the swamp guy across the back from the side, the side his shield wasn't on. Hard. The way the Last Watcher had hit him in the blue hall, the way she'd watched from her bed in Singapore.

He went down on one knee in the middle of the ring.

He didn't make a sound. He never did. He was on fifty, and he didn't make a sound.

Hyvred did.

She didn't know what sound it was. Something. Something she'd never made in her life, out loud, in the middle of a stone room under a round town. Kotori's Mend was already going onto him and Rachael's was coming and Gabe was moving. Six of the seven were still standing, and the real one was somewhere among them, and she couldn't *tell*.

"*Back*," said Kei's voice, from the top of the stair, from the Hall above. He'd been listening. "*Everybody back. Up the stair. Now.*"

They went back.

The swamp guy got up off his knee at twenty-two per cent with Kotori's Mend on him and went up the stair last. Of course he did. Walking backwards, with the shield, between the ring and the stair, while the six that were left stood in the ring of stones and didn't follow. They just stood there, holding up their dark lanterns, and watched them go.

The hatch rose up behind them and shut.

***

She sat on the stair inside the dome afterwards, on her own, with her bow across her knees, for an hour.

Not the bottom of the stair. The top, near the eye, where she sat every evening to watch the roads. She'd climbed all two hundred and twelve steps without stopping. She was sitting with her back against the wall and her face hot, not looking at anything.

She'd seen everything. In here she'd always seen everything. That was the one thing she'd had, since the first night, since the ceiling. In here she wasn't the girl who saw the world through a tube. She was the archer from the mill roof. She was the one who could see seven roads at once.

And she'd looked straight at the thing that hit him, and not seen it.

Somebody came up the stair.

She knew the walk. She'd known it since a terrace in a city with no name, since a square, a bow borrowed and given back grip first. She didn't look round.

He sat down on the step below her. Not close. Just there. He had his shield on his knees. He was at about eighty per cent now, she could see, from where she was sitting, at the edge of her eye, without looking. Kotori and Rachael had been at him for an hour.

He didn't say anything for a long time.

"Good calls," said the swamp guy.

"*Don't*."

"They were. Every one. Until the last."

"The last one was the one that *mattered*." Her voice went up. It always went up. "It came from *behind* me. I didn't *see* it. I can see *everything*, in here, I can see *seven roads*, and I didn't see the one that hit you. I looked straight at it. I *must* have looked straight at it, it was one of the seven, and I didn't—" She stopped. "You were at *twenty-two*."

"I know."

"You were on *fifty*."

"I know," said the swamp guy. "I knew exactly where it was. Left side. Low. Across the back." He shrugged, slightly, the shield moving on his knees. "That's what fifty's for."

"*Don't* make it a *joke*—"

"I'm not."

She looked at him then. At the side of his face, at the edge of her eye, without turning her head. She could do that out here too, now, sort of, blurry, for a week. In here she'd always been able to.

He wasn't joking. He was sitting on the step below her, looking up at the eye of the dome, at the sky. He was completely calm, the way he was always completely calm. And something about how calm he was made her want to hit him again, very hard. And also something else, which she was absolutely not going to think about on a stair, on a Thursday, at twenty-two per cent.

"They're the same," said Hyvred, more quietly. "All seven. I looked. There's no tell. Nothing. I've never not been able to see something in here."

"Then it's not a seeing thing," said the swamp guy.

She looked at him.

"Seven of it," he said. "Six aren't real. One is. You can't see which." He turned the shield over on his knees. "So you don't try to see which. You stand so it doesn't matter which."

"What does that *mean*?"

"I don't know yet," he said. "Dest will. He's been very quiet since we came up. He goes quiet when he's working something out."

He stood up. He looked down at her for a moment, on the step above. She thought, for one awful second, that he was going to put his hand on her head again. Right there, on the stair, with her face already hot.

He didn't. He just looked at her.

"You saw the north-west one standing at the end," he said. "Before anybody. Before it moved. You called my name." He paused. "You never call my name."

She opened her mouth. Nothing came out.

"Good call," said the swamp guy again. He went back down the stair before she could hit him.

She sat at the top of the stair to the eye with her bow across her knees and her face on fire. She waited until she was sure he was all the way down. Then she put her hands over her face and said, into them, very loudly, "*No.*"

From the bottom of the stair, in the Hall, she heard Bee laugh.
