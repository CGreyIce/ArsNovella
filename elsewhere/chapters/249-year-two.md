The stream had been a candle for a week, and three hundred thousand people were watching it.

Tif had it on the big screen in her room in Singapore, the way she'd had every stream on for four years. Curtains shut. The egg in the corner, humming, with Xyro in it; he was in the round town on the windows, keeping lamps, and she'd stayed out. Somebody had to. It was the fifteenth of November, a Wednesday, and at midnight it would be the sixteenth. And on the sixteenth it would be two years.

She wasn't missing it from inside an egg.

On the screen: a candle.

That was all. That was all it had been for a week, since he stepped past the twenty-second stone. He streamed POV, the way he always had. The Eye at his shoulder looking out of him. And out of him, past the edge, there was nothing. Not black; the stream wasn't black. It was just not anything. The encoder didn't know what to do with it. It came out as a sort of soft grey hum, nothing in it at all. Except, in the bottom of the frame, a small yellow flame. His candle. *Sedge*, on its twist of paper; she'd seen it the night Chernn gave it to him.

And his hand, sometimes, when the flame lit it. And a rope, going forward.

And sound.

***

The sound was the thing.

You could hear them. Nine people on a rope in the nothing. Their feet on ground you couldn't see. The rope creaking. And every few seconds, a small chime, like a spoon on a cup. Kotori's beat; Tif knew it from the longest night last year.

And over all of it, all night, every night: a voice.

Smooth, British, unhurried. Hako's. Narrating. *Here, in the absence of anything at all, we observe*—

She'd heard about Hako's voice for two years. She'd never really listened to it. You didn't, on the streams; he did it under his breath mostly, for himself. Now he did it out loud, all night, for the others, so they could hear where everybody was. And it came through on the stream, clear as anything, over the hum. Hour after hour.

People had started falling asleep to it.

She knew because they said so. In chat, at three in the morning, Singapore time, all over the world.

She'd thought about it all week. What it was, that three hundred thousand people would sit up all night watching a candle.

She'd known him four years. She'd been his only active mod for all of them. She'd made !dark on the first day, at four in the afternoon, when he cut the stream for the first time because he'd found a door. She'd thought it would get to ten.

She opened the dashboard.

*!dark: 423*

She'd known why people watched him, before. She'd known it on the first day, with twenty-one in chat. He did things. He walked into a fen and found the first world first. He fought a woman in silver on an oak floor and she said *it was like fighting a boss*. He pulled a crown out of the sky at the bottom of a lake and said *good for them*. He stood in front of a bell. He went fifty-one seconds with no rests in a cellar nobody saw. He smiled at Lucan on a bridge. She'd clipped that one. *LOOK*. Four million.

That was why people watched. Things happened.

Nothing was happening now. A candle, and a hum, and a rope, and a man narrating nine people walking through nothing. Three hundred thousand people. All night.

She didn't know why. She thought she was starting to.

***

She went to the studio's site at half past eleven and pulled up the Hall.

They'd said they would. Year Two, the same as Year One. *The Hall of First Clears*, every first clear in the second year of the game, in the order it happened. She'd read last year's to him on stream, all four hundred and twelve, from the bottom up, at the Hart. He'd stopped on *Sketch*, in the middle of the five, and said *oh*.

This year's was longer. Much longer. Eleven hundred and something. The game was bigger. The cities were open.

She scrolled to the top.

It went on for a long time. *here*, *here*, *here*, all the way down chat, three hundred thousand people typing *here* at midnight into the stream of a candle in the nothing. She didn't try to stop it. She wasn't sure she could have. She sat on the edge of her bed with her hands over her face and watched it go.

On the stream, in the soft grey hum, the candle stopped.

***

He'd read it. She could tell. He read chat on a little panel at the edge of his eye, the ghost panel, the same as always. Even out there. The panel was light; it was the only thing that was, apart from the candles. She'd wondered all week if he was still reading it.

He was.

The candle stopped moving forward. The rope went slack, then tight. Somebody behind him on the rope said *what*; Hyv, she thought. And then, very faint, under the hum, Hako stopped narrating.

And then his voice.

"Two years," said Ice.

Tif put her hands down.

"Hi, Tif," he said. To the nothing. To three hundred thousand people. To her, in a room in Singapore with the curtains shut.

She typed it before she could think.

On the stream, very faint, somebody laughed. Several somebodies. All down the rope, one after another, in the nothing. She could hear exactly where every one of them was. Hyv had said that, in a letter to Xyro, that Xyro had read her at breakfast. *When Hako talks about us we laugh and I can hear where we are.*

She could hear where they were. From Singapore. Nine laughs going away down a rope.

On the stream, in the nothing, Ice laughed. Out loud. She heard it go away down the rope. And Hako said, in the voice, *the tank appears to have been amused by a dispute over a chat log. The scientific community is divided.*

Tif put her face in her pillow and laughed until she couldn't breathe.

***

She thought about it after. At four in the morning, with the stream still going, and chat quieter. People asleep all over the world with a candle on their screens.

She thought about why.

It wasn't the things. It had never really been the things. The fen and the oak floor and the crown and the bell. Those were why people came. They weren't why people stayed.

People stayed because of *here*.

Because two years ago, twenty-one people had typed *here* into the chat of a man in a fen who said *hi* back to every one of them. Who answered the stupid questions out loud. Who never once asked for anything and was always, always there, at the front, so you'd know where he was. Who had a bus driver who typed *ok* every time he died, at lunch. And a mod who'd typed *first* when she was third. And never said anything about either of them except *hi* and *thank you*.

And now he was walking into nothing, on a rope, with a candle. And three hundred thousand people were sitting up all night with him. Not because anything was happening.

Because he was there. And they could hear where he was.

*It's not a void*, she'd typed. *It's past the edge.*

She thought she knew the difference now.

***

At a quarter to eight in the morning, Thursday, the sixteenth of November, two years exactly, they stopped.

She knew what was coming. She'd watched it every morning for a week. The rope going slack. Nine candles going down on the ground, one by one, in a ring. The stream's candle going down last, his, so the frame was suddenly low, at ground level, looking across a ring of small yellow flames at nine faces. Faint, lit from underneath. Thea and Gigi. Xola and Slep. Kotori with a tin. Hyv, looking at him. Hako. Suzu.

And then Suzu's hands, in the middle of the ring. Setting down a short thick candle with a little star on it. Lighting it from his own.

"*Set one to watch*," said Suzu.

She'd been waiting for it all night. She typed first. She always typed first.

It went on. All down chat. She didn't scroll. She let it go past.

On the stream, at ground level, across a ring of ten small lights in the nothing, Ice was looking at his panel. At the edge of his eye. She could tell from the way the frame was still. He was reading it.

He didn't say anything. He didn't need to. She'd known him four years.

Then the nine of them said it, on the stream, together, the way they did every morning. Out loud, into the nothing, round a ring of ten lights. A few seconds after three hundred thousand people had typed it.

"*So the rest may sleep.*"

And at eight, the egg took him. And the stream went to the end card she'd made two years ago, on the first day, the one he'd never changed. *Ice is in the fen. Back soon.*

He'd never been in the fen since the first month. He'd never asked her to change it.

She wasn't going to.

***

The egg in the corner opened at eight, at the same moment, with the sound it always made. Xyro sat up in it with his hair flat on one side and looked at her across the room.

She was sitting on the edge of the bed with her face wet.

"Happy two years," said Xyro.

"It's not *my* two years."

"It is," said Xyro. He climbed out of the egg and came and sat beside her. He always sat close. He loved talking about things, his interests, her, anything, and he talked to her about all of it. But he'd learned from Slep, on a road, to wait until nobody else had said it. He waited now. Then: "You've been his mod for four years. Two of them in here. That's your two years too."

She didn't say anything. She leaned on him.

"We said it too," said Xyro, after a while.

She looked up.

"At the long table," said Xyro. "At a quarter to eight. Every morning since they went past the edge." He was looking at the egg, not at her. "Kei started it. The first morning after. He stood up at the head of the table and said *they'll be making the ring now*. And then *set one to watch*. And all sixty of us said *so the rest may sleep*. At the same time as them. Without being able to see them." He was quiet. "Every morning. Eight mornings. Isa says it with us. From her chair."

Tif looked at the screen. At the end card. *Ice is in the fen. Back soon.*

"He doesn't know," she said.

"No," said Xyro. "Kei says don't tell him. Kei says it's not for him to know. It's for us to say."

She put her face in his shoulder and didn't say anything for a long time.

She went to bed at half past eight. In Singapore, on Thursday morning, two years exactly. With the egg in the corner, its lid up now, and the curtains shut, and Xyro asleep beside her inside a minute, the way he always was. And the stream ended, and the folder open on her second screen. *ice (do not delete)*.

She'd added one thing to it, before she lay down. Not a log. A screenshot. Chat, at seven forty-five, all the way down, *so the rest may sleep*, three hundred thousand times.

She named the file *year two*.

Then she lay in the dark, and thought about a ring of ten small lights in the nothing. And one in the middle, still lit, watching. A short thick candle with a star on it, that wouldn't go out by itself. On the ground, past the edge, with nobody there. And sixty people at a long table in a round town, saying a line at the same moment as nine people they couldn't see.

*Something has to put it out*, the window had said. Malcolm's. A year ago.

Nothing would, she thought. Not tonight. Not with three hundred thousand people having said the line, and sixty more who meant it.

She fell asleep.
