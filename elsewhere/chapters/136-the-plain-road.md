They left Fernwick on Monday at the first dawn, in a thin rain, and Sedge was on her steps.

She'd been on her steps when Ice came out of the reeds in November with a sack of silverleaf and mud to his knees. She'd been on them on the longest night, singing. She'd been on them every time he'd left since, and every time he'd come back. She was on them now, with her pipe and her shawl. The rain was dripping off the herbs that hung under her eaves, and she watched nine people come down the north walk toward her in the grey light with packs on.

"Swamp guy," said Sedge.

"Sedge."

"Round town."

"Round town."

She took her pipe out of her mouth and looked at him for a long time. At the shield on his back. At the eight behind him: Deston with the book, Suzu with the empty jar, Kotori with her bell, Sheng Yang standing, Gabe with his hammer, and the three new ones, who'd been on her steps a month and already felt like they'd been there longer.

"Mind the plain road," said Sedge. "It's flat. Flat roads make you lazy."

"I'll mind it."

"You won't. You'll walk it like it's a fen." She reached inside her shawl and took something out and held it out to him. A reed. A single long fen reed, cut at the root, the way she cut them on the raft gardens when she sang. It was folded over twice and tied in the middle with a bit of string. "For the boy," said Sedge. "Give him that. Tell him it's from the steps. He'll know."

Ice took it. It was light and green and smelled of the fen.

"I'll tell him."

"And tell him—" She stopped. She put her pipe back in her mouth. "You know what to tell him. I've said it twice. I'm not saying it three times. I'm old."

"*One day's here*," said Ice.

"That's the one," said Sedge, and went back inside, and shut the door. She did that, every time. She'd never once watched them walk away. He'd asked her why, in December. She'd said *you don't watch people go. You watch for them coming back. It's different.*

***

He looked back once, from the end of the causeway.

Not at the town. At the north walk, going away into the reeds. And past it, a quarter of a mile east, where you couldn't see anything from here but more reeds, and where nine grey stones stood in a ring round a warm pool. One of them was facing out.

*Set one to watch, so the rest may sleep.*

He'd never left the fen without somebody being there at night. Not since November. He'd always come back to the ring. Or Suzu had, or Deston, or he'd known exactly how many days it would be till he did.

He didn't know, this time. The round town, and then whatever came after the round town. A hall for ten. A banner. A room with a chair in it that an old woman had been sweeping under for four months.

He looked at the reeds for a long moment.

*It's good at it*, Suzu had said, in May, by the pool. *Let it.*

He turned round, and walked on.

***

Thea's six were at Brackenford, at the east bridge, where the plain road went out of town. They'd come down from wherever they'd been levelling to see them off. Ice hadn't asked them to. He hadn't told them when. Somebody had. Probably Tif, who'd always known his schedule better than he did.

They were standing on the bridge in a row, the way people stood to wave at a train. Thea at the end with her tower shield. Xola next to her, gills fluttering. Slep next to Xola, on her other side, obviously. Xyro, Tif with her ears forward, PenguChi with her spear, very still. And a sheep.

"*ICEY*," said Xola, from fifty yards away, at a volume that turned heads on the bridge. "*ICEY'S JOINING A GUILD*."

"Xola."

"*I'M GOING TO MISS YOU*."

"I'm going to Carrow Cross," said Ice. "It's eight days."

"*IT'S SO FAR*."

"You walked further than that to get here."

"*THAT'S DIFFERENT, I WAS WALKING TOWARDS YOU*," said Xola, and came across the last of the bridge and hugged him so hard Steadfast clanged on his back.

The others came. Tif first, with her ears all the way forward, who stood in front of him and looked at him for a moment. She didn't say anything. She'd been his head mod for six months and she was in a game now, and couldn't type in his chat. He could see her thinking about it.

"Behave," said Tif.

"I always behave."

"You *don't*. You go dark a hundred and twelve times." Her ears went flat. "Satayboy's going to run the commands. He's very loud. Tell chat to be nice to him."

"I'll tell them."

Kotori had come up behind Tif. They looked at each other, the two tea healers, and then Kotori said "*Sorr—*" and stopped and laughed. Tif laughed. Then they stood together at the side of the bridge talking very fast about bells until Rachael came and got Kotori by the sleeve.

PenguChi stood in front of him with her long spear, and looked at him slowly, all the way up and down.

"Penguin," said Ice.

"*Don't*."

"I didn't say anything."

"You said *penguin*," said PenguChi calmly. "I heard you. I'm going to kick you when you come back."

"I'll be ready."

"You won't," said PenguChi, and smiled, slowly, and stepped aside.

Slep nodded at him. That was all. Slep didn't need to say anything; he'd said it already, once, on a Landing in a fen, to Deston. *He's good people.* Patience the sheep came up behind Slep and tried to walk onto the plain road after Ice. Slep said "*Patience*," without raising his voice, and the sheep stopped, and looked at him, and stayed.

And Thea, last, at the end of the bridge, with the tower shield.

She didn't come closer. She stood where she was, and he stopped where he was. Not close. Just there, the way they'd stood at the end of a jetty in a fen a month and a half ago, before she'd sung.

"Go on, then," said Thea.

"You're not coming."

"Not yet." She looked past him at the plain road, going away east, flat and long, toward the round town. Her face did something small and complicated. "I'll come. When it's time. There's something I have to—" She stopped. "When it's time."

"No hurry," said Ice.

She looked at him.

"Higu says that," said Thea.

"Higu's right," said Ice. "She usually is. It's very annoying."

Thea laughed. The real one, the one from the jetty. Then she put her hand flat on his shoulder for a second, the way you'd touch something you'd been afraid wasn't there and found it was, and took it away again.

"Go on," she said. "Go and be in a guild. I'll see you in the round town."

***

Riverbank was on the plain road, a mile past the bridge, sitting on a milestone.

Eight of them, the way they'd been on the shingle in February and on the shingle on Thursday. Xav on the milestone itself with a pencil, drawing the road. Rui Xiang beside him with two knives, very still. Malcolm looking at the sky. John making small lights float off his fingers for no reason. Azac standing like a wall. Ryan sitting on his pack. Val with her wand across her knees. And Jun Liang, who saw them coming from a quarter of a mile away and stood up and shouted *OUR LEADER* at the top of his voice.

"He means Kei," said Xav, as they came up.

"I mean *ALL OF YOU*," shouted Jun Liang. "*EVERYBODY'S A LEADER. WE'RE IN A GUILD.*"

"We're not in it yet, Jun."

"*WE'RE WALKING TO IT. THAT'S THE SAME.*"

Xav got down off the milestone. He looked at Ice. Ice looked at him.

They'd walked out of Fernwick together in November, in the first week, three of them with Deston, before any of this. They'd split on a hill in December, when Ice had talked him into starting his own crew. *We'd see him around*, Ice had said. They'd seen each other around. In a channel, on a moor, at a gorge, on a riverbank. Never on a road, walking the same way for eight days.

"Hi," said Xav.

"Hi."

"You're signing second," said Xav. "I'm first. I said that in March."

"I know."

"I said you'd be annoyed about it."

"I'm not annoyed."

"You're a bit annoyed."

"A bit," said Ice, and Xav laughed, and put his pencil behind his ear. They started walking. Side by side, on the plain road east, with seventeen people behind them and Kei and Drew at the back. The way they used to walk out of a poly classroom nine years ago without needing to decide where they were going.

***

The plain road was flat. Sedge was right. It went east across a long green country of fields and hedges and small farms, with nothing much on it. Every few hours there was an inn, and every night they stopped at one. There were nineteen of them, which was more than any inn on the plain road had ever had at once. Every innkeeper looked at them coming up the road and went pale and started counting beds.

Ice found he didn't mind the flatness. He'd thought he would. He'd spent six months in a fen and on heron roads and at the tops of gorges, where every step meant something. On the plain road nothing meant anything much, and you could look round, and see who was walking next to whom.

He looked round a lot.

He saw Gabe and Azac walking together on the first afternoon, the two walls. They spent four hours talking about shields. The weight, the rim, the angle, the way you held the strap. Azac said *I'm a wall* and Gabe said *I'm an Anvil* and they argued for a mile about which was better. Neither of them won, and both of them enjoyed it more than anything that had happened to either of them all week.

He saw Calvin and Rui Xiang on the third day, the two who went ahead. They went ahead together, over every rise, and came back together. On the fourth day they started racing to the top of each rise and back, and on the fifth day Rui Xiang won for the first time and said "*ha*" very quietly. Calvin made him do it again.

He saw Dennis and Malcolm on the second day, walking together with their notebooks open. Dennis's notebook was the purse. Malcolm's were the IF/THEN books, eleven of them now, in which he wrote down what to do if anything at all happened. They compared pages for six hours.

"If it's still June when we get there," said Malcolm, at one point.

"It *is* June, darling."

"*If* it is," said Malcolm, "then—"

"It's June *now*. It's the first of July on Thursday. It's going to be June for two more days. It's a *calendar*, Malcolm, it's not a *weather* system—"

Malcolm wrote something down. Dennis wrote something down. They looked very pleased with each other.

He saw Rachael and Val, on the fifth day, walking a little apart from everyone. The two quiet ones. They didn't talk much. Val showed Rachael a light she could make with her wand, *lux*, small and white and steady, the first spell she'd ever done in the game, at an abbey, ninety-four per cent the first time. And Rachael showed Val a Mend, put on a scratch on Val's hand from a hedge. Val looked at the scratch closing and said, in her calm low voice, "Thank you." Rachael said "Don't make it weird." And they walked on together for the rest of the day.

He saw Kotori and John, who made lights, and Kotori, who rang a bell, find out they'd both been taught by old people who'd never explained why. He saw Suzu and Ryan walk side by side for a whole day without saying a single word, and look very satisfied about it. He saw Sheng Yang walk beside Jun Liang, quiet beside loud. Somewhere around the sixth day Sheng Yang started explaining Ender's Cut and Jun Liang listened to every word, very seriously, at a normal volume, which nobody had known he could do.

And he saw Deston and Kei at the back, every day, the two hubs. Deston counting things and Kei drawing them. Neither of them looking at anything in particular, both of them seeing everything.

Drew walked wherever he liked. Usually at the back. Sometimes with Ice, for an hour, not saying much.

"They're mixing," said Drew, on the sixth day, watching Calvin and Rui Xiang race to the top of a rise.

"They are."

"That's what it's for," said Drew. "A guild. Not the halls. Not the banner." He watched Gabe and Azac argue about rims. "This. People who didn't know each other walking next to each other until they do."

Ice didn't say anything. He watched Suzu and Ryan, walking together in perfect silence, and Rachael and Val, and Jun Liang listening.

"I said *not yet* for six months," said Ice, after a while. "Because I thought joining would change Moonlight Harvest. Make it something else."

"Has it?"

Ice watched his eight. Mixed in with Riverbank's eight, on a flat road, in the summer, walking east.

"No," he said. "It's just bigger."

"Big enough to hold the little groups," said Drew, "without them stopping being little." He smiled. "Niko said that. In January. On the first call."

The news came up the road with them, a bit at a time.

On Wednesday, Crimson Ledger's second ten wiped at the last door of their hall for ten, under a Hall where seven roads met somewhere in the south. That was two of their three. The third was still in. Nobody in the world had cleared one yet.

On Thursday, Kestrel posted. Sixteen names on the VANTA writ. Four to go. She didn't say anything else. She didn't need to.

On Saturday Hyvred messaged him, from the round town, at nine in the morning his time.

He looked at it for a long time, at the long wooden table in the inn, with Xav drawing beside him.

She'd said that in April. On a road in a fen, in capital letters. *I CAN SEE SEVEN ROADS AT ONCE.* He'd said *then you'll see me coming*. He'd been teasing her. He'd been dodging, the way he'd dodged everything she'd said all week.

He hadn't thought about it since. He thought about it now.

Xav, beside him, had stopped drawing. He was looking at Ice's face.

"Who is it?" said Xav.

"Hyvred."

Xav looked at him for a moment longer, with the pencil in his hand. Then he went back to drawing, and didn't say anything at all. Xav had known him for nine years and had learned, in that time, exactly when not to say things. Ice had never been more grateful for it.

***

On the last night, on the plain road, a day out from the round town, he went to the edge of the camp.

They'd stopped in the open. There was no inn for twenty miles in either direction, and nineteen people wouldn't have fitted anyway. They'd made a ring of lanterns on the grass, the way everybody made rings now, and lay down inside it in their blankets under the summer stars. Jun Liang was asleep. You could tell, because it was quiet.

Ice stood at the edge of the lantern ring and looked north.

He couldn't see anything. The plain road ran east. North was just dark fields and the edge of the world. But he knew what was there, somewhere, a long way up. A ruined hall halfway to the gold star, and past it, the Unlit Country. Kei had stood on a broken wall and looked at it. Higu's light had gone out in it.

*Set one to watch, so the rest may sleep.*

He'd stood watch on every night he could remember, in the game. On the plank, on the narrows, at the gorge, in the ring. He was good at it. He'd thought, for six months, that it was the whole of him.

He heard somebody come up behind him. He knew the walk.

"I'll watch," said Xav.

Ice turned round.

"Go and lie down," said Xav. He had his pencil behind his ear and a sheet of paper in his hand. "I'll watch. I'm not tired. I'm going to draw the stars anyway. I might as well draw them facing north."

Ice looked at him.

"You can't do it every night," said Xav. "Not now. There's nineteen of us. There's going to be a lot more than nineteen. Somebody else can face out." He sat down on the grass at the edge of the lantern ring, facing north, and started drawing. "Go on. You're the rest. Tonight."

Ice stood there for a long moment.

Then he went and lay down in the middle of the ring, between Suzu and Deston, with his shield beside him, not on his arm. He looked up at the stars over the plain road. Xav was a shape at the edge of the lanterns, facing out, with a pencil, keeping watch.

He slept.

In the morning, on the horizon, there was a round town, and seven roads, and a dome.
