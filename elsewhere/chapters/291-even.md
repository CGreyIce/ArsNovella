Malcolm had a clean page for the third bout, and he wrote *1 – 1* at the top of it, and then he didn't know what to write next.

He'd always known. That was the thing about being the one who said *it'll be March*. You always knew what to write next. You wrote the worst likely thing, calmly, and then you were right, mostly. And when you weren't, you crossed it out and wrote *not March* underneath. It worked for wicks and for roads and for nine people walking into the nothing. It had worked for the fifth fight, the draw, seventy minutes, both at one per cent and nine minutes of nobody moving. He'd shut the notebook on that one and said *it'll be March*, and been right, and they'd listened.

He looked down at *1 – 1* on the clean page and couldn't think of a single likely thing.

Suzu had won the first by sitting down. Xav had won the second by sitting down too, and then looking only at where Suzu was. There wasn't a worst likely thing for the third. There wasn't a likely thing at all.

So he wrote, under *1 – 1*, in his small careful hand:

*I don't know.*

He looked at it for a long time. He'd never once written that in a notebook in his life. He found he didn't mind.

***

The step was very quiet.

Fuller than it had been for any of the Dec fights. Everybody who'd come down for the first bout had stayed for the second, and more had come down in between. They'd heard the noise when Suzu laughed on the floor and come down the stair to see what it was. They were sitting on the steps above the step now, and on the stair itself, all the way up to the hatch. Leaning over the rail, packed in, very quiet.

At the top of the stair, Hobb had his slate against the wall. He had crossed off everything except three lines. *Sketch 2–1 · 3 to 1*. *Suzuyunn 2–1 · 8 to 1*. And at the bottom, very small, *EVEN · 40 to 1*, with one name beside it. Hobb was looking at that line as though it might bite him.

And in the corner by the hatch, behind Ama Corrie's counter, in the dark: Dec. Malcolm knew he was there now. He hadn't, in the first bout. He'd seen Xav look up at the start of the second and followed his eyes. He hadn't been able to stop looking since.

Dec hadn't moved. He was standing in the corner with his shield on his back, looking down at the ring, the way you'd look at a fire you'd lit and weren't sure would catch.

In the ring, Xav and Suzu stood three paces apart.

Neither of them sat down.

[ Bout three ]

"Begin," said Malcolm.

***

He tried to keep columns. He'd kept two columns in the fifth fight, *D* and *X*, and been very proud of them afterwards. Twenty-seven each. He'd written them down as they happened, every hit, and his hand had only shaken twice.

He gave up on the columns at about four minutes.

There were too many. Not too many hits; too many things. In the first bout, Suzu had done one thing: not be where Xav was looking. In the second, Xav had done one thing: look only at where Suzu was. Each of them had had one thing, and one thing had been enough for one bout.

In the third, they both had both.

Xav looking at where Suzu was. Suzu knowing he was, and so not going anywhere silly, just fighting. The scythe's long wide arcs, high and low, the snath short from no distance. The Glean taking a breath back off every cut, a soft green light along the blade. And Xav's two blades meeting it where it was, *tock*, again and again, and the other blade across whatever was open. And then Suzu, about every minute, doing something nobody would do. Lying down, sitting down, stepping inside an elbow. Just to see whether Xav had stopped looking.

He never had. Not once in the third bout.

And then Xav, about every minute, doing something Malcolm had never seen him do. Going early. On purpose. A breath ahead, the river, the old way. Just once in a while. Not to catch Suzu. To make Suzu think he'd started looking again, so that Suzu would go somewhere silly. And then *tock*. Blade into scythe, in the silly place, where Xav had been waiting.

Malcolm wrote it down. He didn't know what to call it.

*He's using the early one to make Suzu do the lazy one*, he wrote. *Then he's on time for it.* He looked at that. He crossed out *lazy*, and wrote *least effort*. It seemed fairer.

Twenty minutes.

Malcolm had stopped writing. He was just watching.

He'd known Xav since poly. He'd watched him draw for years on the backs of things, and he'd watched him lose to Dec five times in this room, and nearly win the sixth. He'd sat by a ditch full of moonbells with him in November for an hour and ten minutes, teaching him to hold still. He thought he knew everything Xav could do.

He'd never seen him like this.

It wasn't the Dec fights. In the Dec fights Xav had always been chasing. You could see it on him, all the way through, even in the draw. A man a long way behind somebody, getting closer, never quite there. It had been beautiful and it had been hard to watch.

This wasn't chasing. Neither of them was behind. They were both just there, in the middle of the room, three paces apart and then no paces and then three again. Doing everything they had, and having it answered.

Suzu was smiling. Not the millimetre. All of it, the whole time now, his long silver hair all over the place and his face open. Like a man who'd been asleep in the sun for two and a half years and had just woken up and found it was the best day of his life.

Xav was smiling back.

On the step, Kotori was crying. Very quietly, at the edge of the silver lines, with two empty cups. Malcolm didn't think she knew.

Thirty-one minutes.

Malcolm knew where it was going about a minute before it got there.

He couldn't have said how. He wasn't Hyvred; he didn't see everything. He wasn't Dec. He was a pessimist with a notebook who'd learned, in the fifth fight, what it looked like when two people got to the end of everything they had at the same time. And he could see it now. Both of them, at about ten per cent. Both of them slower. Both of them at the end.

Then they did the same thing at the same moment.

He saw it. He'd see it every time he closed his eyes for a week. Suzu came in with the wide row at knee height, the long flat arc, the way he'd started every bout. Except it wasn't at knee height. It was higher, much higher than anybody would cut, at the ribs. A silly height. Nobody cut there with a scythe. It was where Xav wasn't looking, because nobody would ever look there.

Xav wasn't looking there. He was looking at where Suzu was.

And Suzu was in the stroke, all his weight in it, at the end. Open.

They both went.

The scythe across Xav's ribs, at the silly height, where nobody looked. Xav's right blade across Suzu's shoulder, where Suzu was, on time. The same moment. Not a breath between them. Malcolm heard both of them land as one sound.

***

They stood there.

Three paces apart. Both at one per cent. Xav with both blades out, Suzu with the scythe at the end of its stroke. The Seven Below so quiet that Malcolm could hear the lamp at the top of the stair.

He knew what came next. He'd seen it before, in January. Nine minutes of nothing. Two people who both knew that whoever moved first would lose, standing there until somebody on the step said *it'll be March*.

He'd been the somebody, in January. He'd sat there for nine minutes before he said it. Thinking about how long it would go. Being right about it.

He didn't wait this time.

He didn't have to. He'd written *I don't know* at the top of a clean page, and he'd been right about that too. He shut the notebook. He stood up on the step, which he never did.

"*Even*," said Malcolm.

He said it into the Seven Below, loudly, in his ordinary calm voice, before either of them could decide anything. Both of them turned their heads and looked at him. Xav at one per cent, and Suzu at one per cent, three paces apart.

"Even," said Malcolm again. "I'm calling it. Now. I'm not waiting till March." He opened the window with a hand that wasn't shaking at all, for once. "One each. And the third one level. That's even."

***

At the top of the stair, Hobb the turnip carter made a sound like a man stepping on a rake.

Then the room went off.

Malcolm didn't hear most of it. Gabe, obviously. Leaka on her feet. RX saying *EVEN, it's EVEN, he SAID it was even, he BET on even* to anybody who'd listen. Dennis, at the top of the stair beside Hobb, saying *darling, darling, pay the man* very gently. Hobb was looking at his slate with his chalk in his hand, at the bottom line. *EVEN · 40 to 1*. One name.

In the ring, Xav sat down on the floor. So did Suzu. Facing each other, three paces apart, cross-legged, both of them breathing like men who'd run a long way.

Kotori came in with two more cups. Malcolm didn't know where she'd got them. She gave one to each and went back to the edge of the silver lines and wiped her face.

Then Dec came down the stair.

He came down through everybody. Through the people on the stair and the people on the steps above the step and the step itself. They moved for him without being asked, the way people did. He came down into the Seven Below and walked across the floor to the ring and stopped at the edge of it. Between the two of them, sitting on the floor.

Xav looked up at him.

"You knew," said Xav.

"Yes," said Dec.

"*How* long?"

"Since I've known him," said Dec.

Xav looked at Suzu. Then back at Dec.

"In October," he said. "The first one. In this room. If he'd been in the ring instead of you. He'd have beaten me."

"Yes," said Dec.

"Easily."

"Yes."

"Then *why didn't he*?" said Xav. "For a year and a half. He was in the corner. By the hatch. Every time. He could have fought me any time he liked." He was laughing, a bit, and something else. "Why didn't he?"

Dec looked down at Suzu, sitting on the floor of the ring with his cup and his scythe across his knees, his long silver hair all over his face.

"He's lazy," said Dec.

"It was effort," said Suzu.

***

Xav laughed so hard he had to put his cup down.

When he could speak again, he looked up at Dec from the floor.

"So I don't get you," he said.

"If you beat him," said Dec.

"I *tied* him."

"That's not beating," said Dec.

Xav stared at him for a long time. Then he put his face in his hands and laughed again, helplessly, on the floor of the Seven Below. Suzu, across from him, smiled. All of it.

"Next time," said Xav, through his hands.

"Next time," said Suzu.

"*You*," said Xav. "Next time *you*. Not him. I'm not even—" He looked up at Dec. "I'm not even thinking about you. I'm thinking about *him*." He pointed at Suzu with his whole arm. "He lay *down*. He *sat down* in a fight and *won*. He cut me at *rib height* with a *scythe*. Who *does* that?"

"Nobody," said Suzu. "That's why."

Dec stood at the edge of the ring and looked at the two of them. Malcolm saw his face. It was doing something Malcolm had seen once before, in the corner by the hatch, at the start of the second bout. A man who'd waited a long time to see something, and was seeing it, and didn't want to miss any of it.

"Good," said Dec. To Suzu.

Suzu looked up at him.

"That's mine," said Suzu.

"I borrowed it," said Dec.

***

Afterwards, the two of them sat against the north-east wall, under the mark. Not Xav and Dec, the way they had in September and January. Xav and Suzu. Shoulder to shoulder, with their cups, not saying anything. Dec had gone back up the stair to the corner by the hatch, as though that were his place now. Hyvred was beside him in the dark with his arm.

Malcolm sat on the step with his notebook open on his knee.

At the top of the clean page: *1 – 1*. Under it: *I don't know*.

He looked at it for a while. Then he wrote under it, in his small careful hand:

*Even.*

And under that, after a long time, smaller:

*Not March. Not anything. I didn't know, and I was right.*

He thought that was probably the best thing he'd ever written in a notebook.

Val was writing.

On the step below him, with her satchel open and a sheet on her knee. She'd brought a blank one down for the first bout, Malcolm had seen. She'd sat with it on her knee through all three and hadn't written a mark. Now she was writing fast, dashes and dots and three kinds of mark and a fourth he'd never seen her use. A small circle with nothing in it.

"What's that one?" said Malcolm.

"Where he isn't," said Val, not looking up. "I've never needed a mark for it before. Everybody's somewhere." She drew another circle. "He's mostly circles."

At the top of the sheet she'd written his name. *Suzuyunn.* Her first page on somebody she'd never once seen fight, until tonight.

***

Hyvred came down the stair at the end, with Dec's arm, and stopped at the wall.

She looked down at Suzu, against the wall, with his cup. He was still awake then, just. She looked at him for a long time, edge to edge, the way she looked at things she was about to call.

"I can call you now," said Hyvred.

Suzu looked up at her.

"I saw how Xav did it," she said. "You don't look where you'll be. You look where you are. And then you've got nowhere to go." She was frowning, the good way. "I could call that. I think. If you were in a hall. If there was something to call."

Suzu thought about it. Like a cat.

"Try," he said. "Some time."

"I will," said Hyvred. And Malcolm saw Dec, beside her, look at her with an expression he'd only ever seen on Dec's face in a fight.

***

Xav came and sat on the step beside Malcolm, after, for a minute. He had his sketchbook open to a page Malcolm hadn't seen. A man sitting cross-legged on a floor, smiling, with his hair pushed back. Drawn fast, and right.

"What now?" said Malcolm.

"Again," said Xav. "And again. Him." He looked at the wall, where Suzu was. "I was going to spend the summer thinking about Dec. I'm going to spend it thinking about rib height."

"It'll be—" said Malcolm, out of habit.

"Don't."

"—June," said Malcolm. "I was going to say June. The next one." He looked at his notebook. "I've stopped doing March."

Xav looked at him for a long time. Then he put his arm round Malcolm's shoulders, on the step, the way he'd done to RX at the bottom of a lake once and never to Malcolm. Malcolm sat very still and let him.

***

Up at the top of the stair, Hobb was counting out forty silver into Suzu's hand, one at a time, with an expression of great suffering. Suzu took it all, and thanked him. Then he walked across the Seven Below to RX on the step and poured all forty into RX's hands.

RX stared at it.

"For the ears," said Suzu. "Forty. On Thursday." He looked at RX. "You taught him."

RX looked at the forty silver in his hands. He looked at Suzu. He looked at Xav against the wall.

"I *did*," said RX. "I *taught* him. Forty *times*. In the *ear*." He closed his hands on the silver. "Nobody ever *says* that."

"I said it," said Suzu. And went back to the wall, and sat down beside Xav, and went to sleep. Sitting up, with his cup in his lap, almost at once. Because it was a waste not to.
