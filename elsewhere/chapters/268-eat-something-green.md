The lion had shrunk.

Leaka held it up in the back room of the Hart, by the ears, and looked at it. A year ago it had gone over two people. Now it would just about go over a goat. The red cloth had gone pink and stiff. The gold paper had come off in the fountain and never come back. The basket that had been its head had gone soft on one side, so the whole face leaned. Bee's painted eyes looked up at her out of it, slightly crossed. Sad. Like a dog that knew it had done something wrong.

"It's *ruined*," said Leaka.

"It went in a fountain," said Curse, from the doorway. "In January. You were in it."

"*We* were in it."

"I was in the back end," said Curse. "I couldn't see. You said *straight on*." He came in and looked at the lion. "You said *straight on* about four times, quite loudly, and then we were in the fountain."

"It was *straight on*. The fountain was *in the way*."

"Yes," said Curse. "That's what fountains do."

Leaka put the lion down on the table, very gently, because it had been a good lion, and looked at it for a while.

"We're making a new one," she said.

"I thought you'd say that."

"A *proper* one. With a jaw that *moves*. And eyes that *shine*. And it is *not* going in the fountain."

Curse looked at her the way he did. Calm, and fond, and not believing a word of it.

"Okay," he said.

***

She did it properly. That was the thing nobody expected. Everybody thought she was all noise. She was all noise, mostly. But when she decided to make a thing, she made it.

She did the body herself. Tailoring was her skill, and she was good at it, and she didn't care who knew. Red cloth from the dyer's on Spoke Street, the deep kind that didn't go pink. Gold thread for the edges. A long fringe down the back, in strips, that would move when the lion moved. She sat up in the back room of the Hart for four nights with the cloth across her knees and a needle in her mouth, singing, badly, to herself.

She asked Milli for the jaw.

"Leather," said Leaka. "So it opens and shuts. So it can *eat* things."

Milli looked at her across the round market. Gentle, tall, with his cat's-paw stamp on the strap of every bag he'd ever made.

"What's it going to eat?"

"*Green*," said Leaka.

Milli thought about that for a moment, and then he laughed. Because everybody knew. Leaka told the story about once a week. *Eat something green*. In capitals. At the top of the chat, every night, for years, to a man in a swamp with a sack of herbs who never did.

"I'll do you a jaw," said Milli.

She asked Hana for the eyes.

"Not paint," said Leaka. "Paint goes in fountains. I want them to *shine*."

Hana took her down the north tunnel the next evening, to the cold place where the old stone met the new, and dug. Excavation was Hana's skill, and Jewelcrafting after it. They came up with two stones out of the deep rock, dull and grey, the size of eggs. Hana sat at the long table for two nights with them, cutting, while Milli watched her. On the third night she put them in Leaka's hands.

They were gold. Not yellow; gold. With a light in them, right at the back, like the light in Kei's lamp at the eye.

"*Oh*," said Leaka, and burst into tears, because she did that. Hana hugged her, and Milli hugged both of them, and then Jared came in and said *y'all*, and that was the end of that evening.

***

She asked Curse for the word.

Lions had a word on their foreheads. Hana had shown her pictures, out there, last year, on her phone. A character, in gold, right in the middle of the face, above the eyes. Hana said it meant *king*. Three lines across and one down.

"You do words," said Leaka. "Do that one."

Curse looked at the picture on her phone for a long time, out there, on the call. He was in Australia, and it was nearly midnight there, and he looked tired. He was smiling anyway.

"I can't read it," said Curse.

"You don't have to *read* it. You have to *write* it."

"Odette said a label makes a thing remember what it is." He was quiet. "It's not my word. If I write a word I can't read, the lion won't know what it is. It'll just be lines."

"So *learn* it," said Leaka.

He did.

He asked Hana. Then Kotori. Then, because Kotori said *ask him, he grew up with it*, he asked Ice. Leaka found the two of them at the long table on Thursday night: CGI with a brush, very carefully, and Curse beside him with his inkwell, watching. Three lines across. One down, through the middle.

"Why's the middle one shorter?" said Curse.

"It's not shorter," said Ice. "The bottom one's longer. So it stands up."

Curse looked at it for a long time.

"So it stands up," he said.

He wrote it on the lion's forehead in gold on the Friday, with the fine brush he'd brought from Merrowgate in a tin. He did it once, slowly, without stopping. Three lines across. One down, through the middle. The bottom one longest. So it stood up.

Leaka didn't cry that time. She screamed. Which Curse said was worse.

***

She asked CGI last.

She'd been saving it. She found him on the rim of the fountain on Saturday night at the second dusk, with his sword across his knees. Hyv was beside him with her arm through his, and her head on his shoulder, and her tail round his ankle, as if she'd been grown there. They were looking at the market. Hyv did that now, everywhere, in front of everybody. Leaka thought it was the best thing that had ever happened in the round town.

"Icey boi," said Leaka.

He looked up. He'd gone pink already. He'd been pink for about a month. She didn't think he even noticed any more.

"Hi, Leaka."

"Last year," said Leaka, "the lion went in the fountain."

"I heard."

"You weren't *here*. You were walking to *Masmark*. On your *own*. In *January*. I screamed *CGI SHOULD BE HERE* out of the *head*. Everybody heard it. Tif put it in a *clip*."

"I saw the clip."

"*This* fountain," said Leaka, and kicked the rim of it. "This *exact* fountain." She pointed at him. "It went in because Curse couldn't *see*. He was in the back end, bent double, holding on to my belt. And I said *straight on*. And the front of a lion and the back of a lion weren't doing the same thing at the same time."

He looked at her for a moment. She saw him get it before she'd said it. He always did.

"Tempo," said Ice.

"*TEMPO*," said Leaka. "You did it on a *hill*. With a *stone*. Nine people pulling at once on a *BOOM*. Xola told me." She leaned in. "The lion's two people. Front and back. They need to step at the *same time*. Newish has the drum, but the drum's for *dancing*. Somebody's got to call the *steps*. So the back end knows where the front end's going. *Before* it gets there."

"Okay," said Ice.

Leaka stared at him.

"You said *okay*," she said. "You didn't even *ask* what for."

"You told me what for."

"I told you about *half*!"

"It was enough," said Ice.

Beside him, with her head on his shoulder, Hyv said, without opening her eyes, "He's very boring. I love it."

Leaka screamed. Two people at the chestnut stall dropped their chestnuts.

***

They practised every night for a week. At the second dusk, in the round market, round the fountain.

It was terrible at first. Leaka in the head, with Hana's gold eyes looking out in front of her and Milli's leather jaw in her hands. Curse in the back end, bent double, holding on to her belt, seeing nothing but the inside of a red lion. Newish on the steps of the Hart with her drum. And Ice standing on the rim of the fountain with his sword in one hand and his shield in the other, calling the steps.

Not with his voice. With the shield.

He tapped the rim with the flat of his sword. *Tik*. Small. And that was a step. *Tik*. Another. *Tik-tik*. Two, quick, to the left. Curse, in the back end, couldn't see a thing. But he could hear the rim of a shield from anywhere in the market. And the shield was always a breath ahead of where the lion was going. So by the time Leaka's front feet went left, Curse's back feet were already going left. Because he'd heard *tik-tik* before she'd even decided.

"*How* does he know where I'm going?" said Leaka, from inside the head, on the third night. "*I* don't know where I'm going."

"He's been watching you," said Curse, from the back end. "For about ten minutes. Then he knows."

"*That's cheating*."

"It's Lucan's," said Curse.

On the fifth night they went all the way round the fountain three times without stopping. On the sixth they went up the steps of the Hart and down again. On the seventh night Leaka did the thing she'd seen on Hana's phone. The lion reared up on its back legs, Curse holding her by the waist and lifting her, and its head went up high. And it looked out over the round market with gold eyes and its jaw open.

*Tik.* And it came down. Both ends at once.

The whole market clapped. Forty people. Ama Corrie, in the south door, with her broom, didn't clap. But she didn't go back in, either.

"*Hm*," said Ama Corrie.

***

On the last night, after practice, Isa called her over.

She was in her chair by the north door, under the grey blanket, with her stick across her knees. She'd watched every practice through the open door of the Hall, across the market. Leaka didn't know how much she could see from there. Not faces. Shapes, maybe. A red shape going round a fountain and a man on the rim going *tik*.

Leaka knelt by the chair, sweaty, with the lion's head under her arm, so Isa could see her properly.

"Will it eat?" said Isa.

"At the end," said Leaka. "Somebody holds out the green. On a string. And the lion eats it. For luck. For the year." She grinned. "I've been telling people to eat something green for *years*. Nobody ever *does*."

Isa looked at the lion's head under her arm for a long time. At Hana's gold eyes. At the word on its forehead that stood up.

"In my mother's day," said Isa, "nothing on the hill ate anything green. There wasn't any. Stone and black water and heather that never flowered. For three hundred years." She was quiet. "And now there's a thorn in the water at two hundred and seventeen. In leaf. With fruit on it. In January."

"I heard," said Leaka. "Gigi grew it."

"*Hm*," said Isa. She put out her thin old hand and touched the lion's nose, very lightly, the way she'd touched the jar. "Who holds out the green?"

Leaka hadn't thought about it. She'd thought Hana would. Or Ama Corrie, if Ama Corrie could be got out of the door.

"Whoever wants to," said Leaka.

"*Hm*," said Isa, and closed her eyes. And Leaka knelt there by the chair for a while longer, with the lion's head under her arm, and didn't know she'd just been told.

***

Out there, on the Sunday, Leaka booked a plane.

She did it in the afternoon, her time, on her phone, sitting on the end of her bed. She took a screenshot of it. She sent it to Hana with no words. Just the picture: a flight, and a date in April.

Hana didn't answer for four minutes.

Then: *you're COMING*

*obviously i'm coming you absolute muppet*

*LEAKA*

*what. it's in april. it's a WEDDING. you said come*

*i didn't think you'd*

*i'm COMING HANA. i'm going to cry the whole time. i'm going to cry on the plane. i'm going to cry at you IN PERSON*

There was a long gap.

Then a picture from Hana. Just her hand, on a table somewhere, holding her phone, shaking. And Milli's hand, on top of it.

Leaka sat on the end of her bed and cried, which she did, about everything, all the time. It was a feature.

***

On the Monday, it was the eve.

Out there, everybody who had a new year had it, somewhere, at a table, with their own people. And Leaka on the other side of the world, in the afternoon, with nothing to eat a reunion dinner with but a sandwich, because it wasn't her new year. But it was theirs, and she'd been in it last year, and it had felt like hers.

So in here, after, they did it again.

Maigo did the dinner, like last year. Long trestle tables carried out of the banner room into the round market. Rice and chicken and fish, and long noodles for long life, and no pork in any of it, because there'd never be pork in anything Maigo made. Sixty-two people and a sheep, and Isa in her chair at the end, under Ama Corrie's blanket.

And CGI. At the table. Between Hyv and Gabe.

Leaka hadn't thought it would get her. It got her.

She'd screamed *CGI SHOULD BE HERE* out of a lion's head a year ago, into a fountain, on the other side of the world from her own body, at the top of her lungs. And here he was. At the reunion dinner. Being fed a dumpling by Hyv, off her own chopsticks, in front of everybody, pink to the ears, and letting her.

"*CGI IS HERE*," screamed Leaka. At the table. With her mouth full.

He looked up.

"I'm here," said Ice.

"*YOU'RE HERE*," said Leaka, and cried into Maigo's noodles. Curse, beside her, patted her on the back. Jared, across the table, said *y'all*, and passed her a napkin, which was the second thing that worked.

***

The lion came out of the Hart at the first morning of the year.

Newish on the drum. The whole round town on the steps and the rim of the fountain and the roofs. Chernn's candles in the windows. Hana's six hundred lanterns waiting in their crates for the pole. And the lion, new, red and gold, with a fringe down its back that moved and a word on its forehead that stood up. With Leaka in the head and Curse in the back end, holding on to her belt.

And on the rim of the fountain, with his shield: CGI.

*Tik*.

They went.

Round the market. Past the chestnut stall. Up the steps of the Hart and down again. The fringe went like water. The gold eyes caught every lantern. The jaw opened and shut on the drum. And the back end of the lion went exactly where the front end went, at exactly the same time, every step. Because a man on a fountain rim was tapping it a breath ahead, so that Curse knew where Leaka was going before she did.

Round the fountain. Once. Twice.

Leaka could see it, out of the gold eyes. The water. Right there, at her left foot, the edge of it. Where they'd gone in last year.

*Tik-tik*. Right.

They went right. Both ends at once. Not in.

The round town made a noise like a lot of people letting out a breath they'd held for a year.

And at the top of the south steps of the Hall of Roads, in the south door, in her chair, which Suzu had carried out: Isa.

She had a stick. Not her walking stick. A long thin pole, the kind you'd hang a lantern on. And on the end of it, hanging from a red string, a bunch of green leaves. A whole lettuce, from the market, round and fresh. With a red packet tied to its stem.

Leaka had told her. Whoever wants to.

She held it out.

The green. On a string, on the end of a pole, at the top of the south steps, in the hands of the oldest person in the round town. Held out to a lion.

*Eat something green*.

Leaka couldn't see much, after that. Her eyes had gone. She heard *tik* and went up the steps, and Curse went with her, every step, at the same time. At the top she heard *tik-tik-tik*, very fast, and knew what it meant. She reared up, and Curse lifted her by the waist, and the lion stood up on its back legs at the top of the south steps in front of Isa Corrie.

And Leaka opened Milli's jaw, and took the green.

The whole round town screamed. All of it. The roofs, the steps, the fountain. Gabe was the loudest. Then Leaka, inside the lion's head, with a lettuce in her mouth and the red packet in her teeth.

Isa laughed. Out loud. The dry small laugh, like a stick breaking.

"*Hm*," she said. "It ate it."

***

They didn't go in the fountain.

Not on the dance. They came down the steps and round the market one more time with the green in the lion's jaw, and the whole way round, *tik*, the lion went where it was going, and nobody went in anything.

Then Leaka took the head off. She stood at the rim of the fountain with it under her arm, in the first light of the new year, red-faced and soaked in sweat and laughing. Curse came out of the back end, blinking, and stood beside her.

"We didn't go in," said Curse.

"*No*," said Leaka.

"We did it properly."

"We *did*."

He looked at her. She saw him see it coming. Too late.

"It's *tradition*," said Leaka.

She took hold of his hand and jumped into the fountain.

He came with her. He always came with her. They went in together, both ends at once, the head and the back, into the cold water of the round market's fountain on the first morning of the year. And came up laughing so hard neither of them could get out.

The round town screamed again.

On the rim of the fountain, with his shield, CGI was laughing too. Not the small open one he did in fights. A real one. Leaka had heard it maybe twice in years of his chat. She sat in the fountain with the water up to her waist and the lion's head bobbing beside her and listened to it.

"*CGI*," she shouted, from the water. "*You're LAUGHING*."

"You jumped *in*," said Ice.

"It's *TRADITION*."

"It's *one year*."

"It's *two* now," said Leaka. "That's tradition. Two's tradition. Ask Ama Corrie."

In the south door, with her broom, Ama Corrie looked at the fountain for a long time.

"*Hm*," said Ama Corrie. Which, everybody agreed afterwards, was a yes.
