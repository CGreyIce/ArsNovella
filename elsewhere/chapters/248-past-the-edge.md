*Here*, said the voice in Hako's head, *in the absence of*—

And stopped.

It had never stopped before. Not once. Not since Otterbank, the first day, waist-deep in the shallows below the jetty with his spear, narrating an otter. Not on the longest night, at the first mile, with the dark coming down the road. Not at the top of the bell tower with the Hour lifting its hammer. The voice had always gone on. Smooth, British, unhurried. The voice of a man in a hide with a long lens, watching something enormous come down to a river to drink.

That was how he looked at things. You narrated a thing and then you could look at it. Even if it was frightening. Especially then. The voice made it something you watched instead of something that happened to you.

He stepped past the twenty-second stone and there was nothing to look at.

And the voice stopped.

***

He'd known it would be dark. Isa had said. *Not dark. Nothing.* He'd thought he understood. He'd thought it would be like the Seven Below with the lamp out, the way Kotori had taught them all October. Black, but with things in it. The stream, the silver lines through your boots, the square of grey at the top of the stair.

It wasn't like that.

There was nothing under his feet. Not nothing; there was ground. He could feel it. Heather, short and dry, and stones in it. But he couldn't see it, or anything. He looked down and there was nothing there to see, not even black. Just not-there. His own hand, held up in front of his face, wasn't there either. Only the candle.

*Old Tess*, on the twist of paper. Lit from Thea's lantern at the twenty-second stone, the last thing he'd seen. A small yellow flame on the end of nothing. He knew it was in his hand because he could feel it in his hand. He couldn't see his hand. He could see the flame.

And the rope.

He could feel the rope. Round his waist, under his arm, over his shoulder. Hand-twisted, straw-coloured, from a rope-walk in Masmark. Taut, ahead of him, going forward into nothing toward Xola. Taut behind him, going back into nothing toward Slep. He held onto it with the hand that wasn't holding the candle. And he walked.

And there was nothing to narrate.

***

The first night, nobody said anything for four hours.

Hako counted them, afterward. He was the one who counted, out here, it turned out. RX counted in the round town. Out here there was nothing to count but hours and steps, so he counted those.

Four hours of walking on a rope in the nothing, with a candle in his hand. Not saying anything. Not hearing anything. Except the small sounds of nine people walking, and the rope creaking very slightly where it went through hands. And every so often, very faint, from somewhere ahead, a chime. Like a spoon on a cup.

*Ting.*

Kotori's beat. *Mend.* Every few seconds, on somebody. Whether they needed it or not. So everybody would know where everybody was.

He'd learned it in October, in the Seven Below, with the lamp out. He'd been good at it. In October.

Out here he heard the chime and didn't know where it came from. There was no wall for it to come back off. No floor. The sound went out from wherever Kotori was and didn't come back, and he couldn't tell if it was ten paces away or a hundred.

He walked, and held the rope, and his candle, and didn't say anything. And the voice in his head was quiet, for the first time since Otterbank. And he found that he was very, very frightened.

***

"Hako," said somebody, ahead.

Hyv. Two people ahead on the rope. Or three. He couldn't tell.

"Hako, talk."

He didn't say anything. He didn't think he could.

"*Hako*," said Hyv. Her voice was doing something. "I can't hear where you are. Kotori's beat goes out and doesn't come back. There's no edges. I can't—" She stopped. "Talk. Please. Anything. The voice. Do the voice."

He opened his mouth.

Nothing came. The voice wasn't there. There was nothing to look at, and he'd only ever had the voice for looking.

"I can't," he said. In his own voice. Small. "There's nothing. I can't do it about nothing."

There was a long silence on the rope.

Then, from the front, from a long way ahead, from the first loop of the rope, Ice.

"Then do it about us," he said.

***

Hako stood still on the rope.

He thought about it. He thought about Otterbank, and the shallows, and the otter on the jetty that had looked at him the first day as if it knew. He'd narrated it because it was there. And then the river things, and the trappers, and the town. And then the road of lights, and the Lamp Watch, and Tiger and Maigo. *Conservation efforts*. He'd narrated whatever was in front of him.

There was nothing in front of him.

But there was a rope. And nine people on it.

*Here*, said the voice in Hako's head. Very faintly. As if it had been a long way off and was coming back. *In the absence of anything at all*—

"Here," said Hako, out loud.

His voice did something. He let it.

"Here," he said again. The voice. Smooth, British, unhurried. Coming out of his mouth into the nothing, and going out from him, and not coming back. "In the absence of anything at all, we observe a line of nine travellers. Roped together. Proceeding north into a region so devoid of features that the cartographers of the round town have declined to draw it."

On the rope ahead, somebody laughed. Xola.

"At the front," said Hako, "the tank. A solitary creature, typically found at the front of things. Here observed at the front of nothing, which he appears to find, if anything, rather restful."

"*It is*," said Ice, from a long way off.

"Behind him," said Hako, warming up now, the voice coming back into him like water into a dry channel, "the bearer. Carrying on his back a box of black iron containing a light that must not be seen. He has not spoken in four hours. This is, for him, a normal amount."

"*Good*," said Suzu.

"And behind *him*—"

"*Ting*," said Kotori, and laughed.

***

He talked for the rest of the night.

All of it. Four more hours, until the egg took them at eight. He narrated every one of them, over and over, in order and out of it. The healer, maintaining her beat with the diligence of a metronome and the warmth of a kettle. The one with the horns, whose remarkable eyes are of no use here whatever, and who is consequently listening harder than any creature in recorded history. The one with the lamp and the one with the thorn, who have recently been observed holding hands, a development the scientific community has been awaiting for some eighteen months.

"*Hako*," said Thea, somewhere, laughing.

"The one with the trident and her mate, who walks on whichever side she is not," said Hako. "A pair-bonded couple of considerable devotion. Note the way he waits until nobody else has spoken before—"

"*Hako*," said Xola.

"—before he speaks," said Hako. "And then usually says the one thing nobody else said."

"*Thank you*," said Slep, from behind him, with great dignity.

And Hyv, ahead, said:

"I can hear where you are."

Hako stopped talking.

"I can hear where everybody is," said Hyv. "When you talk about them. They laugh, or they say your name, or they say *good*. And I can hear where it comes from." Her voice was shaking. "Kotori's beat goes out and doesn't come back. But you're talking, and they're answering. And the answers come from somewhere." She was quiet. "Keep talking. Please. All night. Every night."

Hako didn't say anything for a moment.

"*The one with the horns*," he said, in the voice, very gently, "*has made a request. The narrator is happy to oblige.*"

***

They stopped at eight, every morning, when the egg was going to take them. You couldn't not stop. Twelve hours, the pod said, and then you came out wherever you were. And wherever they were, out here, was nothing.

Isa had told them what to do. On the north steps, at dusk, in October, with Suzu on the step below her. *Set one to watch, so the rest may sleep.*

So every morning at a quarter to eight, they stopped. They stood in a ring on the rope, nine of them, close, facing in. Each of them put their candle down on the ground in front of them, so there was a ring of nine small flames in the nothing. And in the middle, one more.

Not one of Chernn's. One of Malcolm's.

Kei had given them twelve, at the north door. Twelve kept wicks, from the hundred and sixteen Malcolm had made in December a year ago, with Tamsin's jar. *It does not go out by itself. Something has to put it out.* Chernn had dipped them, the twelve, into twelve short thick candles. With a mark on each one, a little star, so you'd know which they were.

Suzu set one in the middle of the ring. He lit it from his own, the best one, Mother Dipp's. And said, very quietly, the thing Isa had taught him, though it wasn't dusk.

"*Set one to watch*."

And the nine of them said the other half together, in the nothing, round a ring of ten small lights.

"*So the rest may sleep*."

And at eight, the egg took them.

***

Hako came back the first night expecting the worst.

He'd lain in his pod out there, all day, in Vietnam, thinking about it. Nine candles in a ring in the nothing, and nobody there. Twelve hours. They'd burn down. Chernn's burned eight, nine. They'd be out by the time anybody came back. And the dark, which couldn't see the jar in its black box, would see nine little lights go out one by one. And then one more, in the middle, still lit, on its own, on the ground, in the nothing.

He came out of the dark at eight in the evening on the rope, where he'd been. And looked down.

The ring was there. Nine candles, burned down to stubs, cold. Out.

And in the middle, one.

Lit. Short, thick, with a little star on it. A small yellow flame, standing straight up, in the nothing. Exactly as tall as it had been when Suzu lit it. As if nobody had been gone at all.

*It does not go out by itself.*

"*Oh*," said Hako. Not in the voice. In his own.

"It watched," said Suzu, beside him, very quietly.

***

They walked like that for seven nights.

Out of the egg at eight in the evening, onto the rope. Candles lit from the watch. Walking. Hako talking, every hour, all of them, in the voice. Kotori's beat. Hyv listening. Ice at the front, saying very little, except *left* sometimes, when he felt the ground go a certain way under his feet. And at a quarter to eight in the morning, the ring, and the watch, and the words.

*Set one to watch. So the rest may sleep.*

Seven mornings. Seven of Malcolm's twelve. Every one still lit when they came back.

Hako counted the steps. Somebody had to. He counted them under the narrating, the way RX counted under everything, a second voice in his head under the first. By the seventh night he had a number. He told nobody what it was. He didn't know what it meant. It was just how far.

He talked.

He talked about Ice walking round a goose, in January, on his own, and the clip that went everywhere. He talked about Otterbank, and the otter, and how he'd thought it knew. He talked about Tiger and Maigo, his own three, on the road of lights, at home, keeping the first mile. And how much he missed them, which he said in the voice, so it would sound like a joke, and nobody laughed.

He talked about the Hour, at the top of the bell tower, lifting its hammer. *And so the tank, having caught a bell, sits down.*

"*I did sit down*," said Ice, from the front.

"The narrator stands by his account," said Hako.

***

On the fifth night, something went past.

Hyv heard it first. Of course she did. She'd been listening harder than anybody, for five nights. She stopped on the rope, and the rope went tight, and everybody stopped.

"*Quiet*," she said.

Hako stopped talking.

Nobody moved. Nine people on a rope in the nothing, with nine candles, very still. And the black case on Suzu's back, in the middle, not showing anything.

Then Hako heard it too.

Not footsteps. Not breathing. Something like the sound the lake had made under the island, when the crown went out. A long low sound, everywhere at once. And under it, closer, a lot of small sounds. Like reeds moving in a wind there wasn't. Like a great many tall thin things swaying, very slowly, all together.

The Unlit.

Hako had seen them on the longest night. Hundreds. Tall and thin and swaying, coming up out of still water and moor pools, leaning toward lights. He'd stood at the first mile with Tiger and watched them come.

He couldn't see them now. There was nothing to see. But he could hear them. All round. Going past. Not toward the rope. *Past*. North to south, or south to north, he couldn't tell. A great many of them, swaying, in the nothing, going somewhere.

And as they went past, he heard them turn.

Not toward him. Toward the candles. He could hear it, the swaying changing. Leaning. A great many tall thin things in the nothing all leaning, very slightly, toward nine small flames on a rope.

And then toward the middle of the rope. Where Suzu was.

"*Nobody move*," said Ice. Very quietly. From the front.

***

Nobody moved.

Hako stood on the rope with *Old Tess* in his hand and didn't breathe.

He heard them lean. All of them, the whole long swaying sound, bending toward the middle of the line. Toward a black iron case with a black iron star on it, that showed nothing. That let nothing out, not even the light.

They couldn't see it.

He could hear them not seeing it. That was the strangest thing, afterward. He could hear the swaying go uncertain. Lean in, and stop, and lean in again. Like somebody turning their head toward a sound in the next room and not being sure they'd heard anything.

Something very close to him, on his left, swayed past. So close he felt the air move on his face. Cold. It leaned toward his candle, toward *Old Tess*, a small yellow flame on the end of nothing. It stayed there for a long moment, leaning.

*It's a candle*, thought Hako. *It's just a candle. It's not what you want.*

The thing leaned away.

And went on. With the others. Past. The long swaying sound going away into the nothing, slowly, all together. Until it was very faint. And then not there.

***

Nobody said anything for a long time.

"It looked at my candle," said Hako, at last. In his own voice.

"Mine too," said Gigi, behind him somewhere, very small.

"It looked at all of them," said Hyv. "And then at Suzu. And then it didn't know." She let out a breath. "Chernn said. In October. *It'll see two hundred and eleven little lights going north, and no middle. It'll think it's a road.*"

"It thought we were a road," said Thea.

"It thought we were a road," said Hyv. "That's all. Nine candles on a rope. A road." She was quiet. "And it went to look for the middle somewhere else."

Hako stood on the rope and found that his hands were shaking. The candle in one, and the rope in the other. He couldn't see them shake. He could feel them.

"*Here*," he said. In the voice. It came out cracked. He kept going. "*Here, in the absence of anything at all, we observe nine travellers who have just been mistaken for a road. They are taking it, on the whole, rather well.*"

Xola laughed. It was a wet laugh. Then Kotori. Then, from the front, Ice, out loud. And Hako heard where every one of them was.

"*Go on*," said Hyv. "*Please.*"

He went on.

***

On the seventh morning, at a quarter to eight, they made the ring.

Nine candles on the ground, close, facing in. Suzu lit the seventh of Malcolm's twelve in the middle and said *set one to watch*. And the nine of them said *so the rest may sleep*. And then, before the egg took them, there were a few minutes. There always were. Nine people sitting round a ring of ten lights in the nothing, waiting for eight o'clock.

Hako looked at the ring.

Ten small flames, in the not-there. Nothing round them. Nothing under them. Nothing above. Just ten points of yellow, in a ring, and nine faces he could see now, a little. Lit from underneath, very faint, by their own candles. Thea and Gigi, close. Xola with her head on Slep's shoulder. Kotori with a tin of tea in her lap. Suzu with the black case on his back. Hyv with her face turned toward Ice, listening to where he was.

And Ice, at the north side of the ring, facing out. Of course.

*Here*, said the voice in Hako's head. Very quietly. Not for anybody. Just for him.

*In the absence of anything at all, we observe the only thing there is.*

He didn't say it out loud. He didn't need to. He looked at the ring of ten lights and the nine faces round it. And for the first time since he'd stepped past the twenty-second stone, he could look at something without being frightened.

The egg took him at eight. The last thing he saw was the watch, in the middle, standing straight up.
