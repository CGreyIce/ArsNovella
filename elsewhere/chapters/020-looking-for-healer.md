The board outside the Adventurers' Hall was the size of a barn door, and half of it was people looking for healers.

Ice stood in front of it with his hands in his pockets and read it from top to bottom, and felt something he'd felt in every MMO he'd ever played. It was a small, sinking, familiar feeling. He'd hoped it would be different here.

*LF HEALER, LV 10+, SERIOUS ONLY.*

*Need healer for river runs. Will pay 20 silver per hour. NO TIME WASTERS.*

*LOOKING FOR A HEALER WHO ACTUALLY HEALS (last one stood in front of the boar)*

*healer pls. we are nice. we have snacks*

*WTB healer. PM CL_Recruit_02. Paying in real money.*

There were other notices. People selling things, mostly: *Rat pelts, 1c each, bulk*, which made him feel a pang of sympathy. People offering to carry things across the fords for copper. A long careful notice in four languages about a lost dog that turned out to be an NPC's dog, posted by the NPC. But under and over and around all of it, pinned three and four deep, were the same three words in thirty different handwritings.

*Looking for healer.*

"Everyone took a sword," said Xav, reading over his shoulder. "Of course everyone took a sword. Nobody wants to stand at the back for twelve hours."

"I would," said Deston.

"You've got a book that makes everyone better at swords. That's different."

"It's a bit different," Deston admitted.

There was a notice down in the bottom corner, half covered by a newer one. It said simply, in neat small writing, *healer, lv 10, available most days*. Somebody had already scrawled across it in red, *TAKEN*.

***

Xav wrote the notice.

Of course he did. He bought a sheet of paper and a stub of pencil from the clerk inside the hall, an elderly man with very small spectacles who seemed to regard the whole world as a filing error. He sat down on the bench outside and wrote for a long time, crossing things out. Then he showed it to them.

*MOONLIGHT HARVEST seeks one healer.*
*Level 10 or above.*
*Must be able to stand still.*

"Must be able to stand still?" said Deston.

"Every healer I've watched this week runs around," said Xav. "They panic and run toward whoever's hurt, and then the thing that hurt them hurts *them*. We want someone who stands still."

"Can I add something?"

Xav handed him the pencil. Deston wrote, under it, in his big round handwriting: *Kind people only.*

Xav looked at it. "That's going to get us a lot of people who think they're kind."

"Everybody thinks they're kind. I want the ones who are." Deston handed the pencil to Ice. "Dec? Anything?"

Ice looked at the notice. *Must be able to stand still.* He thought about it, and crossed out *still* and wrote above it *somewhere*.

*Must be able to stand somewhere.*

"Somewhere," said Xav.

"It's not about standing still. It's about standing in the right place. If you're in the right place you can move all you like." Ice handed back the pencil. "We'll know it when we see it."

They pinned it up in the only gap on the board, between *healer pls. we are nice. we have snacks* and *WTB healer. PM CL_Recruit_02*. Ice looked at that one a moment longer than he needed to.

***

Twenty-three people answered by sunset.

He'd thought there'd be three. He'd forgotten, briefly, that he was on a needle in the middle of the square, and that a hundred and sixty people were watching his stream. Some of them were in Brackenford. Some of them had friends in Brackenford. Within an hour of the notice going up, people started coming over to the bench where the three of them were sitting with bread and Ice's last bottle of Good draught, and asking, in a dozen accents, *is this the Moonlight Harvest one? The one with the swamp guy?*

They took them out to the weir to try.

The weir was below the town, where the three rivers had come together and the one big brown river poured over a long stone step into a pool full of churning white water. Under the falling water, among the rocks, lived things the Brackenford people called *mudwolves*: long low grey things, slick and fast, like otters that had grown up angry. They came up out of the pool in threes and fours. There was an old stone footbridge across the river just below the weir, narrow, with a low wall on each side. One person wide, nearly.

It was the best narrow place Ice had seen since the fen. He loved it immediately.

The test was simple. Ice stood in the middle of the bridge with his shield. Xav and Deston stood behind him, the way they always did. The healer stood wherever they liked. Ice banged his sword on his shield, three or four mudwolves came up out of the pool, and they all watched what the healer did.

The first one stood right behind Ice, so close the shield's edge clipped her on the backswing. The second ran forward to heal Xav and was knocked off the bridge into the pool by a mudwolf he hadn't seen. He came up spluttering and swearing in German and was very gracious about it afterwards. The third healed beautifully, and talked the entire time about his stream and how great it would be if they did a collab, and asked twice whether Ice could say his channel name on stream. The fourth was very good, and very fast, and Ice watched her for a whole fight and thought *maybe*. Then Xav leaned over and said quietly, "Her name was CL_Field_11 last week. She's changed it. Check the Tally history." She had. She smiled politely when they said thank you, and left, and didn't look back.

By the twentieth, it was dark, and the mudwolves were getting bored, and so was Ice's chat.

They went back to town. They ate. They didn't say much. Deston fell asleep sitting up on the bench and logged out like that, because it was nearly midnight in Singapore and his exam was in two days. Xav logged out half an hour later, after a long silence and one word, "Tomorrow." Ice sat on the bench in the dark in Brackenford, outside the Adventurers' Hall, under the notice that said *Must be able to stand somewhere*, and looked at it for a while.

Then the window told him he'd been here nearly twelve hours, and he went.

***

She came on Monday morning, at about half past nine, when they'd nearly given up.

They were sitting on the bench again. There'd been two more since dawn, and neither was right, and Deston had to log out at noon to revise. They were talking quietly about whether it would be worth going back to Fernwick to look, or whether they should ask on the forums, or whether they should just go with the fourth one from yesterday, the very fast one, and keep an eye on her.

"Um," said a voice. "Sorry."

Ice looked up.

The girl standing in front of the bench was small. Small enough that he had to look down a little, sitting down. She had black hair cut level with her jaw, with a fringe that came right down to her eyebrows, and a round face, and very round dark eyes that were looking at the three of them, and at the notice above them, and back, with an expression of enormous worry. She was in a pale blue robe that came to her ankles, the kind you got from the cloth merchant on the east bridge when you'd saved up a little, and she held a short white staff in both hands like a person holding the strap on a bus. A small brass bell was tied to the top of the staff with a blue ribbon. It rang, very faintly, every time her hands moved, which was often.

Over her head, it said *Kotori*. Under that, nothing. No party.

"Sorry," she said again. "Is this—are you still looking? For a healer? I saw the paper." She pointed at the notice, as if they might not know where it was. "I'm level ten. I'm not very good. Sorry. I mean, I'm—I heal people. I'm a healer. But I'm not very good at it, probably. I just thought I'd ask. Sorry."

There was a short silence.

"That's four sorrys," said Xav, not unkindly.

"Oh! Sorry," said Kotori, and then went bright red, and put her face behind her staff, and the bell rang.

Deston started laughing. He couldn't help it, Ice could see. It wasn't at her, it was the way you laugh at a very small cat walking into a door. And then Kotori started laughing too, a beat behind, behind her staff, a soft surprised giggle that she was clearly trying to stop and couldn't.

"Hi," said Ice. "I'm Ice. This is Kai, and Sketch."

"Hi," said Kotori, from behind the staff. Then she lowered it, and looked at him properly for the first time, and her face changed. "Oh. Are you—are you the one on the stone? Ice. Number seven?"

"Yeah."

"Oh! Congratulations." She said it completely sincerely, the way you'd congratulate a neighbour on their new car. "That's really good. I'm—" She checked something in the air. "I'm not on the stone."

"Most people aren't," said Ice. "Do you want to come to the weir?"

***

He knew within about forty seconds.

She didn't stand where any of the others had stood. When they reached the bridge, and Ice walked to the middle and set his feet, and Xav and Deston fell in behind him, she didn't follow them onto it. She stopped at the near end and looked at the bridge for a long moment. She looked at the pool, at the falling water, at the rocks. Her eyes moved slowly over everything, the way Ice's did when he walked into a new place, and the way Xav's did. Then she walked off the end of the bridge, down the bank, along the river wall, and stepped up onto an old stone mooring post that stood out of the shallows a few metres to the side, below the bridge and a little upstream.

It was about the size of a dinner plate. She stood on it with her feet together, holding her staff, like a heron.

Ice looked at where she was. Then he looked at the pool, where the mudwolves would come up. He looked at the lines they'd take up the bank to the bridge, the way they'd come every time yesterday, twenty times.

None of those lines went anywhere near the post. The water round it was too fast. Anything coming out of the pool would have to come across the current to reach her, and nothing had, all day yesterday. And from up there, a little higher than the bridge, she could see all three of them, the whole length of the bridge, from end to end, at once.

"Ready?" he called.

"Sorry, yes!" called Kotori, from her post. "Ready! Sorry."

He banged his sword on his shield.

***

Four mudwolves. They came up out of the white water in a rush, grey and slick and fast, the way they had twenty times yesterday, and hit the bridge.

He turned the first. Xav went round behind it, Deston counting him his second, *Let the MANY move as ONE*. The second came at Ice's shins, and he turned it into the low wall. The third and fourth came together, at the same time, the way they did when they were clever. One went high and one went low. He got the slope on the high one, but the low one got past the edge of the shield and its teeth went into his calf. A hot hard staple at fifty per cent, the bite he knew from a hundred rats.

And before the pain had finished arriving, there was a bell.

A single small clear ring, from the side, from the mooring post. Warmth went into his leg where the teeth had been, like stepping into a bath, and the red bar that had just dipped came back up. It didn't even finish dipping. It came back as it was going down, so that for half a second it hesitated, and then simply wasn't hurt any more.

He hadn't seen her move. He looked across, very quickly, between strikes. She was standing on her post with her feet together, exactly where she'd been, her staff held out in front of her with the bell swinging, and she was already looking at Xav.

Xav took a bite on the shoulder, coming round the back of the third mudwolf. Before it had stopped stinging, there was the bell again.

Deston didn't take anything at all. He never did. But at one point a mudwolf, turned by Ice's shield, went off the bridge into the shallows on the far side, and came up out of the water facing Deston's back. The bell rang before it had even started to move, a different note, higher. A soft pale shimmer of light came down over Deston's shoulders like a coat. When the mudwolf hit him, it slid off the shimmer the way a blow slid off Ice's shield.

Ice stood in the middle of the bridge with the grey dust blowing away off the water, and read *sorry the one on Ice was late*.

It hadn't been late. It had been early. It had landed on him before he'd finished being hurt. He'd been healed by a lot of people in a lot of games over a lot of years, and he'd never once had it land like that.

He turned round. She was standing on her post, looking at him anxiously over the top of her staff.

"How did you know to stand there?" he said.

Kotori looked at the post under her feet, as if she'd only just noticed it.

"Um," she said. "It looked like a place where nothing would go?" She thought. "And I could see everyone from it. All three of you. If I stood on the bridge, I'd only see Kai's back." She looked worried. "Was it wrong? Sorry. I can stand on the bridge."

"No," said Ice, and his voice did something he hadn't told it to. "No. Don't stand on the bridge."

They did three more fights. In the third, a mudwolf came up out of the shallows right by her post, across the current, which nothing had done all day. Kotori looked at it, said "Oh! Sorry," and hit it on the nose with the bottom of her staff. The bell rang. The mudwolf looked so surprised it went back into the water.

"Did you just apologise to a mudwolf?" said Xav.

"It was rude not to," said Kotori. "It didn't do anything. It just came up."

They sat on the river wall afterwards with their legs over the water, the four of them, and Ice found out about her the way you find out about people in games, sideways, in bits.

She was in Singapore. Deston said "*So* am I!" so loudly that a man on the bridge turned round. She'd grown up in California, near Los Angeles, and moved to Singapore with her family when she was sixteen, because of her dad's work. She was at university there. Her exams had finished on Friday. She had a whole holiday, and nothing to do in it, and that was why she was here.

"It was my dad's," she said, about the pod. "He bought it on launch day, because everybody was talking about it. He used it twice and got really motion-sick." She looked a little apologetic on his behalf. "So he gave it to me. He had to send it back to the company to get it, um—re-fingered? Re-registered. To me. It took three days. He was very sad about it."

"Re-fingered," said Deston, delighted.

"That's not what it's called," said Kotori, going red again. "Sorry."

She didn't stream. She didn't know who Ice was before she saw the stone. She'd seen the notice that morning and liked that it said *kind people only*. She'd picked the staff on the first day because the room had been quiet and the rain was nice and it seemed like a gentle thing to carry. She'd become a healer more or less by accident, because the first person she met in her beginner town had been hurt, and she'd wanted to help. She didn't know that healers were rare. She didn't know that what she'd done on the post was rare. She said, twice, that she wasn't very good.

Ice made a joke about the mudwolf she'd apologised to. Something about it filing a complaint with the Adventurers' Hall, and the clerk with the small spectacles regarding it as a filing error. Xav snorted. Deston laughed.

Kotori laughed about a second later, a soft surprised giggle, as if the joke had taken a moment to walk over to her and knock.

***

He asked her before Deston had to go.

"We're a party," he said. "Moonlight Harvest. There's three of us here, and one more coming, he's in another town, catching up. We do weird things. We go dark. We fight more things than we should, on purpose, in narrow places. And there's something—" He stopped. Not on stream. "There's something we want to do, in about ten days, that needs five people who are good. We're four."

Kotori looked at him, and at Xav, and at Deston, with her round dark eyes and her staff held in both hands.

"Me?" she said. "But I'm not—"

"You're very good," said Xav.

She looked at him. Xav had said it the way Xav said facts. It sat there in the air between them.

"Oh," said Kotori.

"We noticed," said Deston.

"I'm going to keep doing it."

"We'd be worried if you stopped," said Ice.

She laughed again, a beat late. Then she reached out and touched the window, and the bell on her staff rang.
