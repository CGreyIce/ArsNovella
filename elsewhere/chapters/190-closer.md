Rui Xiang counted them twice, because that was what he did, and he got eight both times, and that was the problem.

"Eight," he said. "There's eight of us on the step. Xav said seven."

"Xav said *Riverbank*," said Malcolm, beside him. "Riverbank's eight. Xav's one of them. He's down there." He pointed with his stick, past the bottom of Ama Corrie's stair, through the open hatch, into the round pale room of the Seven Below. "So there's seven of us on the step. And Gabe."

RX looked along the step. Malcolm. John with his mirror. Azac with his enormous shield across his knees, eating bread. Ryan, saying nothing. Val with her wand and her book. Jun Liang, humming. And at the very end, much too big for the step, Gabe. He'd come because, he said, he'd watched the first one forty-one times on a phone and he was not missing the second one for anything.

"Gabe's not Riverbank," said RX.

"Gabe's *Dec's*," said Azac, with his mouth full. "It counts."

"It doesn't count. That's not how counting works."

"It's how *Gabe* works," said Gabe, comfortably, and nobody argued, because it was true.

RX counted again anyway. Eight. He felt better.

***

He'd been chasing Dec for six years.

Not in this game. Before. In a rhythm game nobody in the round town had ever heard of, where you clicked circles on a screen in time with a song. Dec had been the best of all of them, by miles. He'd played everything on the mod that hid the circles a second before you clicked them, as if that were normal. RX had chased his scores for six years. He'd sat up at three in the morning in his room in Singapore with a song on loop, fifty times, a hundred, trying to beat a number Dec had set in an afternoon without really trying.

He'd never beaten one. Not once. He'd got close. He'd got so close, sometimes, that he'd thrown his mouse across the room.

He knew exactly what it felt like to be half a breath behind somebody for six years.

So when Xav had come up out of the Seven Below in October with seven per cent and a headache, and said *twenty-six minutes*, RX had known. He'd looked at Xav's face and known it, before Malcolm said anything. He'd seen that face in the mirror at three in the morning, a hundred times. The face of somebody who'd just found out how far.

And now it was the second week of January, and Xav had said *downstairs*, and they were on the step.

***

Dec came across the Hall at twenty past eight with his shield on his back and the lamp in his hand, and Ama Corrie opened the hatch for him herself.

"Again," she said.

"Again," said Dec.

"Mind the third step."

"I always mind it."

"You always mind it," agreed Ama Corrie. "That's why I say it. So you'll keep minding it." And she went back to her counter.

He came down the stair past them. RX moved his knees. Dec stopped on the step below, the one above the loose one, and looked at the eight of them.

"You came," he said.

"Xav said we could," said RX. "He said Riverbank. I said is Gabe Riverbank and he said Gabe's *Dec's*, and Azac said—"

"It's fine," said Dec. "Thank you for coming." He looked at Gabe. "You too."

"Forty-one times," said Gabe. "On my phone. I'm not missing this."

Dec nodded, as if that were a perfectly reasonable thing to say. Then he went on down into the Seven Below, minding the third step. And at the bottom, before he went out into the round room, he put his hand up to the little floating light at his shoulder and touched it.

"*Not for them*," said RX, under his breath, because he'd been told about the first one so many times he knew it by heart.

"Not for them," agreed Malcolm.

***

Xav was already in the middle of the room.

He was standing in the ring where the seven silver lines met, with his two blades out and down. His back foot was set a little behind the other, on a mark nobody could see. RX knew it was there anyway. He'd watched Xav put it there every morning at first dawn for three months, on a chalk mark in the ring in the market, with the pigeon watching.

Dec walked out and stopped ten paces off. Lamp down by the wall. Shield up, sword down.

"Whenever," said Xav.

"Whenever," said Dec.

Nobody moved.

It was worse than RX had thought. He'd heard about the thirty-one seconds in October. Nobody moving, nobody deciding. He'd thought it would be like the start of a race, tense, a few seconds. It wasn't. It went on. Thirty seconds. Forty. A minute. The two of them standing in the silver light ten paces apart, perfectly still, and the room so quiet RX could hear the stream in the north tunnel a quarter of a mile away.

"What are they doing?" whispered Gabe.

"Waiting," said Malcolm.

"For *what*?"

"For the other one to decide," said Malcolm. "And neither of them's going to."

RX looked at their feet. He couldn't help it. Xav's back foot, on its mark, a breath behind. And Dec's. Dec's had been doing it for a year, Xav said: since a stone in a fen, waiting for things to come up out of the water. The same. Two back feet. Both waiting a breath, so the step could go either way.

*He stole it*, RX thought, about Xav. *He stole it and now they've both got it and nobody can go.*

At one minute and forty seconds, Dec went.

Of course he did. He always did. RX saw it this time. He'd spent three months looking at Xav's sketchbook over his shoulder in the Hart, so he knew what to look for. Dec's weight went forward. The step began. The back foot stayed.

And Xav didn't move at all.

That was the new thing. RX would remember it afterward more than anything else in the fight. In October, Xav had answered. Half a breath behind, but answering. Now he didn't. Dec's step began, and Xav stood there. Not deciding, the way he'd taught Kestrel at the ring in the market. Not reading the step, not guessing which way the back foot would let it go. Just standing in the middle of the room with his blades down, holding the whole room in his head.

Dec's back foot came round.

Left.

And Xav was already there.

Not because he'd guessed. RX was sure of it afterwards, though he couldn't have said how. Because he hadn't. He'd let Dec decide, and then gone with it, at the same moment. The way Kestrel had learned to shoot where Xav *was* instead of where he'd be. The way the two of them had stood in the ring in the market at first dawn for two weeks while a pigeon slept.

The left blade went in under the shield's rim and came out again.

"*One*," said RX.

He said it out loud. He hadn't meant to. It came out of him the way the numbers came out when he was counting.

Down in the room, Dec stepped back. He looked at his side, where the line was. Then he looked at Xav, and something happened to his face. RX was too far away to see it properly. But he'd known Dec since they were seventeen. He'd seen that face once before, years ago, the night RX had finally got within a thousand points of one of his scores.

*There you are.*

***

It went on for forty-one minutes.

RX counted every one of them. He counted the minutes on the little clock in the corner of his window, and he counted the hits out loud, and after a while the others counted with him. Malcolm first, under his breath. Then John. Then Azac, with his mouth full. Then Gabe, much too loudly. Val didn't count. She was writing it all down in her book.

It wasn't like October. He'd been told about October, every second of it, by Xav and by Gabe and by Kestrel. In October it had been two people going round and round each other very fast, Dec first, Xav half a breath behind.

This was slower.

They didn't go round. They stood, mostly. In the middle of the room, close, five paces, three, sometimes one. Moving very little. A step, and a step, and a long time with nobody moving, and then something so fast RX couldn't see it, and then stillness again. It was like watching two people play a board game where every move took a minute to think about and a quarter of a second to make.

Dec still went first. Always. RX watched for it, because he'd been told and because he wanted to see if it was true. It was true. Every exchange, Dec began it. A step, a turn of the shield, a shift of weight. But Xav didn't answer him any more. Xav went *with* him.

"*Two*," said RX, at six minutes. A touch on the thigh.

"*Three*," at eleven. The shoulder, above the shield.

"*Four*," at fourteen, and RX's voice went up, because four was more than October. Four was more than anybody.

And Dec changed.

RX saw it. He didn't know what it was at first. A wrongness, the way you notice a wrong note in a song you know. Dec had been going first. He was still going first. But at fourteen minutes he stopped going first *quickly*. He started going first *late*.

He'd step, and then wait. Not on the back foot. On the step itself. Half a breath, with the step already begun, before he finished it. And Xav, who'd been going with him, had to wait too. Because you couldn't go with something that hadn't decided yet.

"*Oh*," said Malcolm, beside RX. Very quietly. "Oh, that's *mean*."

"What?" said Gabe. "What is it?"

"He's deciding later," said Malcolm. "He's doing Xav's thing. The thing Xav taught Kestrel. At the ring." He watched for a moment. "He was there. Every first dawn. He walked past them every morning on the way to the tunnel."

"*He stole it*," said John.

"He *borrowed* it," said Gabe, with enormous pride. "He always says he borrows things."

Down in the room, Xav laughed. Out loud, in the middle of the fight, at five paces, with a blade in each hand. It went away down the seven tunnels.

"*You stole that*," said Xav.

"I borrow things," said Dec.

"*You steal them*."

"You stole mine first," said Dec. And went first, late, and Xav had to wait for it, and the shield's rim came round into his shoulder.

At twenty minutes, the window came up that everybody was waiting for.

RX felt the step go tight under him. Everyone on it had seen the October fight, or had it told to them. The pull. The cistern filling for nineteen minutes and then all of it at once, Xav going across the floor of the Seven Below on his back like a man hit by a wave from the wrong direction.

Xav saw it too. RX saw him see it. He saw him go a little further off, to the edge of a sword's length, the edge of a pull. The way he'd stayed out of reach of it for four minutes in October.

And Dec didn't use it.

He didn't use it at twenty minutes, or at twenty-five, or at thirty. The number kept climbing, every blow he turned going under the surface. Four thousand. Five. Six. And he never let it go. He just carried it, in the corner of the window, getting bigger, the whole time.

"Why isn't he using it?" said Gabe.

Malcolm was quiet for a long time.

"Because he doesn't have to," he said. "Look at Xav."

RX looked.

Xav was being careful. He'd been careful the whole fight, but now he was careful in a different way. He was staying just out of reach. Not going in as close as he had at fourteen minutes. Every time he went in, he came out again a little faster than he needed to. Because somewhere in the corner of every window in the room there was a number that said six thousand, and getting closer to Dec meant getting closer to that.

Dec had never invited him once. RX realised that at about thirty-one minutes. In October, at the end, Dec had lowered his shield and opened a door, and Xav had gone through it laughing. This time there'd been no door. Not one. Dec hadn't given anything away all fight. He'd just stood there with six thousand under the surface, and made Xav decide, every time, whether it was worth it.

"*Five*," said RX, at thirty-three minutes, and his voice cracked. Xav had decided it was worth it. A line across the back of Dec's sword hand.

"*Six*," at thirty-four.

"*Seven*," at thirty-six. And then, very fast, "*Eight*." Two in a row, in and out, inside and away before the pull could come. Dec at forty-four per cent, then thirty-nine. Xav at twenty.

The ninth was at thirty-nine minutes.

RX saw it coming. He'd watched this kind of thing for six years from the wrong side. He knew what it looked like when somebody was about to get within a thousand points. Xav went in on the left without deciding. The obvious place, the place everybody went, the place Dec would least expect. And for a quarter of a breath he was there, inside the shield, both blades going in.

"*Nine*," said RX.

And Dec was already turning.

Not away. Not with the shield. He turned the way he'd turned on the first night in the fen, a year and two months ago, with a leech coming up out of the black water. The way that had gone in a forty-second clip that had changed everything. The back foot going round, the world tilting very slightly. And the sword coming down from over his shoulder in the one cut he'd done more times than anything else in his life.

He didn't let the Undertow go. RX would think about that for weeks. Seven thousand eight hundred and eighty, sitting there under the surface, and Dec didn't spend a single point of it.

He used the Falling Cut.

It skittered over the silver lines and into the mouth of the north-east tunnel. And the sword came back round, at the end of the cut, and rested very lightly on the side of Xav's neck.

Xav stood in the middle of the ring with one blade in his left hand and the other a long way off in the dark. He had four per cent and a sword at his neck. He looked at Dec for a long time.

"Yield?" said Dec.

Xav didn't answer straight away. He was breathing hard. RX could see his chest going. He was looking at Dec's window, the corner of it. At the number still sitting there under the surface, never spent.

"You didn't use it," said Xav.

"No."

"You carried it the whole time."

"Yes."

"*Why*?"

Dec thought about it, with the sword still resting on Xav's neck.

"Because you were watching it," he said. "The whole fight. Every time you went in, you were watching it. You were thinking about it." He shrugged, very slightly, so as not to move the sword. "I didn't have to use it. You were already paying for it."

Xav stared at him.

Then he laughed. The same laugh as at fourteen minutes, out loud, down all seven tunnels. He put his head back and laughed at the plain stone ceiling of the Seven Below. It went on for a while.

"Forty-one minutes," he said, when he'd stopped.

"Forty-one," said Dec.

"Nine."

"Nine," said Dec. "You were counting?"

"*RX* was counting," said Xav. "He's been doing it out loud the whole time. Like a lunatic."

Dec looked up at the step for the first time. At RX. At all eight of them.

"Thank you," he said, to RX.

RX didn't know what to say. So he didn't say anything, which hadn't happened to him in about four years.

Down in the room, Xav looked back at Dec.

"Closer," he said.

"Yes," said Dec.

"Yield," said Xav.

Dec took the sword away.

***

RX thought about it for a long time afterward, on the walk up the stair, and through the Hall, and at the Hart.

He thought about a rhythm game, and three in the morning, and a song on loop a hundred times. About getting within a thousand points, and throwing a mouse across a room. About six years of being half a breath behind somebody who'd set the number in an afternoon without really trying.

He'd never beaten one of Dec's scores. Not once.

But he'd got closer. Every year, a bit. And Dec had never once told him to stop. He'd never said *you'll never get there*. He'd never minded being chased. He'd seemed to like it.

He looked across the Hart at Xav, at the corner table, with his left hand still flexing where the Falling Cut had come down on his blade. He had his paper sketchbook out already. He was drawing a back foot and a step that waited half a breath before it finished.

"Xav," said RX.

Xav looked up.

"Nine," said RX.

"I know."

"October was three."

"I know," said Xav. And then, after a moment, very quietly, so only RX heard it: "It'll be March."

"It's always March," said Malcolm, from the next table, without looking up from his stick.

Xav laughed. And went back to drawing. And RX, who'd been chasing somebody for six years and knew exactly what it looked like, watched him do it for a long time, and didn't say anything. Then he got up, and went to the bar, and got two cups of the Hart's terrible tea, and put one down in front of Xav without a word.

Xav looked at it.

"What's that for?"

"It's what you do," said RX. "When somebody's chasing. Somebody brings them tea." He sat down. "I didn't know that till just now."

Xav looked at him for a long time. Then he drank it.

"It's terrible," he said.

"I know," said RX, and counted the Hart's lamps, twice, and got eleven both times. And felt better.
