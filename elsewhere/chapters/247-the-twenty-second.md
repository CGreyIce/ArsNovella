Thea had set the last lantern at the edge of the world in September a year ago, and she'd never told anybody what she'd seen past it.

Nothing. That was what she'd seen. She'd stood at the twenty-second stone past the old hall at dusk with a lantern in her hand. Gigi was a pace to her left, not close, near. Eight others were behind them on the line, each at a stone with a lantern of their own. She'd set hers in the little hollow on the north face of the stone and lit it, and looked up.

And past it, there'd been nothing.

Not dark. She'd seen dark. She'd stood at the north door of the round town on the longest night with a lamp in one hand and a shield on the other arm and watched the dark come down the road like a tide. That was dark. It had edges. It moved. You could see it coming.

Past the twenty-second stone there'd been nothing to see coming. The road just stopped. Not ended; stopped, as if somebody had drawn it on a page and then not drawn any more. And past where it stopped, the world didn't go dark. It just didn't go on.

She'd looked at it for a long time. Then she'd turned round and walked back down the line of lanterns with the others, and nobody had asked her what she'd seen. And she hadn't said.

She was going to walk into it in five days.

***

They went out of the north door at the first dawn on Friday, the third of November.

The whole round town was there. Of course it was. On the north steps, and in the Hall behind, and on the roofs of the houses at the top of the north road. Moonlight Harvest with Gabe at the front, crying already. Hana's five. Bee, with her wings folded and her face very still, at the front of the steps, as close as she could get to Hyv without being in the way. Aurelie in plain blue, small, with Vane behind her. Kei with his map. Isa, carried out in her chair by Tiger and Maigo, on the top step. With her root hands in her lap and nothing in them, for the first time in ten months.

And in the little window at the top of the north door, a candle. Lit.

Kite had come round from the south gate. He stood in the north door with his hands at his sides, the way he stood in the south one. Wendy had started asking people on the way out too, the longest night before last. Kite asked on the way out now.

He asked them one at a time.

Suzu, with the black case on his back, said *Kotori*. Kotori said *Suzu, and the tea*. Ice said *her*, and looked at Hyv, and Hyv went red to the horns, and Bee on the front step made a sound. Hako said *everybody, in a manner of speaking*. Xola said *Slep* and Slep said *Xola*, at exactly the same moment, and Xola hit him.

Then Hyv. *Him. And the beat.* Kotori's face did something.

Then Thea.

"Who'd stand next to you?" said Kite.

She'd answered it before. On the south steps, in April, coming home from Masmark. *Xola, and the thorn on my left.* Xola was in another three, this time. Xola was going north on a rope with Slep and Hako, three people further down the line.

"The thorn on my left," said Thea.

Kite looked at her. Then he looked at Gigi.

Gigi was a pace to Thea's left. Not close. Near. Where she'd been for a year and a half, since a gate in July and *there's a chair* and *whenever you want, or never, never's allowed*. She had her staff in her hand and a candle in her left pocket with *Bramble* on it.

"Who'd stand next to you?" said Kite.

Gigi didn't answer straight away. Thea didn't look at her. She couldn't. She looked at the north road going away up the moor. Ninety-four lamps on the backs of grey stones, leaning.

"Thea," said Gigi.

***

Thea had heard her name in Gigi's voice a lot of times. In the game, in the first winter, before. In a hundred calls, a long time ago, before any of it went wrong. At a gate, saying *hi*. At a gap on the longest night, saying *lie* while Thea said *turn*. In a hall at the bottom of a lake, saying *it's allowed*.

She'd never heard Gigi say it as an answer to that question.

She didn't turn round. If she turned round her face would do something in front of the whole round town, and Bee, and Isa, and Kite in the door.

"Fair," said Kite.

***

The north road was the north road for four days.

She knew it. Everybody did. They'd walked it in September a year ago, the ten of them, lighting the twenty-two. And again every September since, and the Lamp Watch had walked it in threes all the year between, keeping the ninety-four. Grey stones every mile, a black cage on the back of each, a flame in every cage. Moor on both sides, heather, the occasional pool. The rings, every twenty-three stones, where the old guild had camped three hundred years ago going north.

It wasn't the same road now.

The lamps were leaning. She'd heard about it all autumn, from Tiger and Hako and the Lamp Watch, and seen it from the roof. She'd never walked under it. The first ten were straight. By the twentieth she could see it if she looked. By the fortieth, she didn't need to look. Every flame in every cage on the back of every stone, bending north. Toward the edge. Toward where the road stopped. As if something up there were breathing in.

Hyv walked beside her, on her right. Gigi a pace to her left. Ice at the front of all nine, the way he always was, with Suzu and Kotori behind him.

"They're leaning toward *us*," said Hyv, on the second day.

Thea looked.

They were. The flames. Not just north. The ones they were passing leaned, very slightly, as the nine went by. Toward the middle of the line, where Suzu walked with a black iron case on his back.

"It can't see it," said Hyv. Very quietly. "Ina said. Nothing gets out. Not even the light." She was looking at the flames, all of them, edge to edge. "But they can feel it. The lamps. They know something's going past."

"Lamps aren't the dark," said Thea.

"No," said Hyv. "But they've been on this road a year. Leaning toward wherever it is." She looked north. "And now it's going past them."

***

On the fourth day, they came to the old hall.

It sat where the road came over a ridge, on the north side, in the heather. A ruin. Walls, mostly, some of them shoulder-high, some fallen. A doorway with no door. A long room with no roof, open to the sky, with stone benches round the walls. Kei had found the black book here, the first time, two Septembers ago. The old Roll, with three hundred years of Isa's mothers in the back of it. There'd been a banner on the far wall once; you could still see where it had hung. A ghost of a star on the stone, fainter than the stones round it, where three hundred years of sun hadn't got.

They camped in it. Ice said *here*, and they did.

Thea sat on a stone bench in the roofless hall at the second dusk and looked at the ghost of the star on the far wall.

She'd been here twice. In September a year ago, with the ten, on the way to the twenty-two. And on the way back, the same night, very late, in the dark, with Gigi a pace to her left. She'd sat on this same bench then, too. Gigi had sat on the far end. Neither of them had said anything. The others had gone to sleep in the corner, out of the wind. And she and Gigi had sat on the two ends of the same stone bench in an old hall with no roof, looking at a star that wasn't there. Till the third dawn. Not talking.

It had been the most they'd said to each other in a year.

Gigi came and sat on the far end of the bench.

Thea didn't look at her.

The others were in the corner, out of the wind. Kotori with a kettle on a little fire. Hako narrating it in the voice, *and here we observe the healer in her natural habitat*. Ice and Suzu at the doorway with no door, looking north. Hyv between them, looking at everything.

"Same bench," said Gigi.

"Same bench," said Thea.

***

They didn't talk. Not then.

Thea wanted to. She'd wanted to for a year and a half. Since the gate in July, and *later, can we talk*, and *whenever you want, or never*. Since a gap on the longest night, saying *turn*. Since the bottom of a lake, *it's allowed, you told me I could*. Since every Sunday at the long table with Gigi two chairs down, not close, near.

She didn't know how to start. Out there, a long time ago, it had been dark between them, and hard, for a long time. And then she'd left. And Xola had held her up until she could stand. And afterward neither of them had known how to start. Not in the game, not anywhere.

She looked at the ghost of the star on the wall.

"Tomorrow," said Gigi. "At the edge."

Thea turned her head.

Gigi was looking at the wall too. At the star that wasn't there. Her face was very still in the last of the light.

"You've wanted to for a year and a half," said Gigi. "I know. I've watched you want to. Every Sunday." She was quiet. "I've wanted to too. I didn't know how to start either."

Thea didn't say anything. She couldn't.

"Tomorrow," said Gigi. "At the twenty-second stone. Where you set the lantern. Before we go past it." She looked at Thea at last. "Isa says past it there's nothing. You can't see anything. You walk by a candle and a rope." She almost smiled. "I'd like to have said it while I can still see your face."

***

The twenty-two ran from the old hall to the edge, every fifty paces.

Thea knew them all. She'd walked them with a sack of lanterns. The boy with the map, the boy with the sack, the girl with the lamp, the girl with the thorn. Isa had watched them from the edge, she said, a year later, on the night she came home. *A girl with a shield who set the last one. With the thorn on her left. Not close. Near.*

They were all lit. They'd been lit since that September. The Lamp Watch came up every month to fill them. Little lanterns in little hollows in the north faces of twenty-two stones. Going up from the old hall over the last ridge to where the road stopped.

Leaning. All of them. Hard.

Ice stopped the nine at the twenty-first. He didn't say anything. He just stopped, and they all stopped behind him, on the rope they'd tied that morning at the old hall. Hand to hand, the rope man's rope from Masmark. A hundred fathoms, straw-coloured, coiled over everybody's shoulder in turn.

He looked back down the line. At all of them. Then at Thea.

"Ten minutes," he said.

Thea looked at him.

"Hyv says you need ten minutes," he said. "At the last one." He didn't look at Gigi. "Take them."

He went and sat down on the twenty-first stone with his back to the north and his shield on his knees. And Hyv sat beside him. And Suzu and Kotori. And Hako and Xola and Slep. Seven people on a rope, sitting down in the heather at the twenty-first stone, looking very carefully at the old hall behind them.

Thea untied herself from the rope.

Gigi untied herself.

They walked the last fifty paces together.

***

The twenty-second stone was taller than the others. It always had been. Old, grey, with a five-line star cut into its south face, worn nearly smooth. And on its north face, in the hollow, a lantern. Hers. The one she'd set, a year ago September, with the thorn on her left.

It was lying nearly flat. The flame. In its little glass. Pointing north, like a compass needle, into the nothing.

Thea looked past it.

It was the same. Exactly the same. The road stopped. Past the stone, past the lantern, the heather went on a few paces and then just didn't. Not dark. Not black. Just not there. As if the world had been drawn up to here and then somebody had put the pencil down.

Gigi stood beside her. Not a pace to her left. Beside her.

Neither of them said anything for a long time.

"I'm sorry," said Gigi.

***

Thea didn't move.

She'd never heard it. Not once. In all of it, out there, a long time ago, when it was dark between them. Not after, in the game, at a gate, at a gap, at the bottom of a lake. She'd waited years to hear it. She hadn't known she was waiting until she heard it, at the edge of the world, with her lantern lying flat beside them.

Gigi said all of it.

It took a while. Thea wouldn't tell anybody what she said. Not Xola, not Hyv, not the long table. It was theirs, the way Hana and Milli's day was theirs. Out there and in here, a long time ago and now. Except that it was about blame. About how Gigi had blamed her, every time, for everything, whether she'd done well or badly. And Gigi had known it for a long time, and hadn't said, because she didn't know how to start.

Thea didn't say anything until she'd finished.

Then she did.

That took a while too. She wouldn't tell anybody what she said either. Except that at the end of it, she said the thing she'd never been able to say. Not *it's fine*; it hadn't been fine. Something truer than that, and kinder, that she'd been carrying since a gate in July and *later, can we talk*. And she put it down at the twenty-second stone, beside the lantern.

And then they stood at the edge of the world and laughed. Both of them. At the same time. At the two of them, who'd neither of them known how to start, for years. And had walked four days up a road of leaning lamps to the place where there was nothing, so they could.

***

"I can still see your face," said Gigi.

"For about another minute."

"Then I'll look at it," said Gigi. And did. In the last of the light from a lantern lying flat in a hollow in a stone.

Thea let her. She didn't look away. She thought about a corridor in Malacca, that Hyv had told her about, on a call. A door at the end of it. And somebody standing in it, saying *okay, look*, and standing still so they could.

"Not near," said Gigi, at last.

Thea looked at her.

"Isa said *not close, near*," said Gigi. "The night she came home. About us. At the twenty-two." She was looking at the lantern. "I don't want to be near. Not past here. Not in the nothing." She swallowed. "Close. Can it be close?"

Thea put out her hand.

Not far. Just out, to her left, where the thorn was.

Gigi took it.

***

They walked back down the fifty paces to the twenty-first stone holding hands. Seven people on a rope in the heather looked up at them very carefully, and then very carefully away.

Xola didn't look away. Xola stared. Xola had held Thea up for years, out there, when it was bad. She'd known about all of it, all of it, and never said a word to Gigi, because it wasn't hers to say. She looked at their hands for a long time.

Then she put her face in Slep's shoulder and made a sound Thea had only ever heard her make once. At a jetty, in the game, a long time ago, when Thea sang.

"*Finally*," said Xola, into Slep's shoulder.

"That's Rachael's," said Slep.

"*I'm borrowing it*."

Ice, on the twenty-first stone, didn't say anything. He looked at Thea and Gigi holding hands. Then at Hyv beside him, who had both hands over her mouth. Then at the rope.

"Tie on," he said.

They tied on. Thea, and then Gigi, close, on her left. Not a pace off. The rope between them so short their elbows touched.

***

They went past the twenty-second stone at the second dusk.

Ice first. Of course. On the rope's first loop, with his shield on his arm. He stood at the stone for a moment with his hand on its south face, on the worn star. Then he stepped past it, past the lantern lying flat, into where the heather stopped.

And was gone.

Not gone. The rope was still going, taut, ahead. But Thea couldn't see him. One step past the stone and he wasn't there. Not dark. Just not there, the way the world wasn't there.

Suzu next, with the black case on his back. Then Kotori, with the tea. Then Hyv.

Hyv stopped at the stone. She looked past it for a long time, with her eyes that saw everything. Thea watched her face. And saw it do something it had never done in the game. Not once, in two years.

It saw nothing.

Hyv took a candle out of her pocket. A lumpy one, honest, with *Pip* on its twist of paper. She lit it from the lantern in the hollow, Thea's lantern, lying flat. It caught. A small yellow flame, in her hand, going nearly flat too, pointing north.

"Somebody small should go first," said Hyv. To nobody.

She stepped past the stone.

Thea saw the candle go. One step, two. A small yellow point going away from her into nothing, getting smaller. Not because it was going away. Because there was nothing round it to show how far. Just the flame, on its own, in the not-there.

Then it was her turn.

She looked at the twenty-second stone. At her lantern, lying flat in its hollow. A year and two months ago she'd set it here, with the thorn on her left, not close, near, and looked past it at the nothing and not told anybody.

Gigi's hand found hers on the rope.

"Close," said Gigi.

"Close," said Thea.

She stepped past the stone.
