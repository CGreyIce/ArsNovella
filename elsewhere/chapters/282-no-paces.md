Gabe had been loud about everybody for eleven years, and he'd never once been loud about himself, and he'd never noticed until the Sunday.

He noticed at the long table. At the second dusk, the day after Deston and Chernn's day. Sixty-one chairs and a stool and everybody in them, and Deston and Chernn at the end with their hands under the table. Chernn had a dish in front of her, wrapped in a square of cloth. The stub of a two-wick candle. It had burned all the way down on the milestone and she was going to send it to Mother Dipp for the windowsill.

Gabe was in his chair. The one he always had, halfway down, on the window side, where he could see the door and everybody coming through it and say *HI* to them. He'd had it since the first winter.

Rae was in the chair beside him.

Not the chair she always had. Rae's chair was across the table and two down, by Dennis. She'd had it since she signed. And on the Sunday she came into the banner room at the second dusk and walked down the long table past her own chair. She picked up the one beside Gabe's, which was Tiger's, and moved it. Tiger, without being asked, moved to Rae's. Then she sat down beside Gabe. Not a pace off. Not at his back. Beside him. Her shoulder against his.

No paces at all.

And Gabe, who'd screamed at every single person in this room who'd ever done anything like that, said nothing.

He couldn't. His mouth wouldn't. He sat there with his hands on the table and Rae's shoulder against his, and his face got hot. It went up from his collar to the top of his ears and stayed there. He looked at the table. He looked at his hands. He looked at anything except the sixty people who had all, at exactly the same moment, stopped talking.

***

Hyv screamed.

Of course she did. She'd been quiet for two years, and now she was the loudest person in the round town. She stood up at the long table with her hands flat on it. She looked down it at Gabe and Rae with her eyes that saw everything, edge to edge. And she *screamed*.

"*GABE*."

Gabe flinched.

"*GABE'S PINK*," screamed Hyv. "*LOOK AT HIM. HE'S PINK. RAE MOVED HER CHAIR AND HE WENT PINK*."

"I'm *not*—"

"*You've screamed at me every day since January*," said Hyv. Her voice went up and up. "*Every DAY. At the long table. On the STAIR. In a PILLOW, out there, Ice SAID. The AUNTIE next door KNOCKED.*" She pointed at him down the long table. "*And now RAE moves her CHAIR and you've gone PINK and you're not saying ANYTHING*."

The whole long table went off.

Leaka screamed. Xola screamed, and then Thea put her hand over Xola's mouth and screamed into her own. Dennis said *darling* several times, getting louder. Jared said *y'all*. Kotori said *oh*. Suzu said *good*. And somewhere in the middle of it, Gabe heard Ama Corrie at the banner room door say *hm*. In a tone that, if she'd been anybody else, would have been a laugh.

Gabe put his face in his hands.

"You've gone pink," said a voice beside him. Not Rae's. The other side.

Dec. With his chair pulled round, looking at him, with an expression Gabe had never once seen on him in eleven years. It was the face Dec made in a fight, right before he did something nobody had seen before. The small, sudden one.

"I'm not *pink*," said Gabe, into his hands.

"You're pink," said Dec. "I've been pink since January. I know what it looks like from the inside."

"He's been pink since December," said Rae.

Gabe took his hands off his face and stared at her.

She was sitting beside him, at no paces, very calmly. She was looking down the long table at sixty people screaming, with her ordinary patient expression, and her staff across her knees.

"Since the north door," said Rae. "The longest night. When I came the last pace." She didn't look at him. "You've been pink since then. Nobody noticed, because you were busy being loud about everybody else."

Gabe opened his mouth. Nothing came out. For the first time in his life, in the game or out of it, he had absolutely nothing to say.

"There," said Rae. "Now they've noticed."

***

He didn't say anything about it for the rest of the night. He couldn't work out why.

He'd thought about it on the walk north, at the back, with PenguChi. About being loud. He'd been loud about Dec for eleven years. *That's my best friend*, to anybody, whether they'd asked or not. Because Dec wasn't going to be loud about himself, and somebody had to be. He'd been loud about Deston and Chernn, about Hana and Milli, about Hyv and Dec. About everybody. He'd screamed into a pillow out there and the auntie had knocked on the wall.

And now there was a thing that was his. Rae, at no paces. And he didn't want to be loud about it. At all.

He didn't understand it. He sat at the long table with her shoulder against his and didn't understand it. When the egg came at eight he went out still not understanding it.

***

Out there, in the kitchen at Melaka Raya, he sat on the counter with his kopi and waited for Dec to put the kettle on.

Dec put the kettle on. He didn't say anything. He did the thing he did, where he stood at the counter with his back to you, making tea, and let you say it or not.

"I don't want to be loud about it," said Gabe.

Dec didn't turn round.

"About Rae," said Gabe. "I've been loud about everybody. For years. I was loud about you at Hyv's *gate*. I was loud about Deston for twenty-two months about a *sticker*." He looked at the kopi. "And now it's me and I don't want to say anything. I just want to sit there. With her. At no paces. And not *say* it."

The kettle clicked off. Dec poured. He came and leaned on the counter beside Gabe with his cup.

"Hyv was quiet for two years," said Dec. "About me. She kept it inside. Like her tail round a chair leg. So nobody could say it was silly." He drank his tea. "And then on a hill she decided she didn't want to be quiet any more. So she isn't."

"I know. She *screamed* at me."

"She's allowed," said Dec. "And you're allowed not to."

Gabe looked at him.

"It's the same thing," said Dec. "The other way round. She's loud because she was quiet too long. You're quiet because you were loud too long." He put his cup down. "Both's allowed. Thea told me. In a group chat. *If you want to. It's allowed.*" He looked at Gabe. "It doesn't say which way."

Gabe didn't say anything for a long time.

"Deston said it," he said, at last. "Last night. About out there. *Some things you keep.*"

"Yes."

"I never kept anything," said Gabe. "In my whole life. I tell everybody everything. About forty times."

"You kept the door," said Dec. "Twice. All night." He picked up his cup again. "You didn't tell anybody about that. Not really. You said *I held*. Two words. And then you cried at a flame on a hill for forty-one minutes and only ever told Suzu why." He drank. "You keep things, Gabe. You just keep the big ones."

Gabe looked at him for a long time over the kopi.

Then he got down off the counter and hugged him. Dec stood there with the cup in his hand, the way he did, and let him. Then he put his free arm round Gabe and held on, for a bit.

"Don't scream into the pillow," said Dec. "The auntie's had a long week."

"I won't," said Gabe. "I'm *keeping* it."

***

On the Tuesday, they went down the north tunnel. Gabe and Rae, and nobody else.

He hadn't asked her. She'd come to the hatch at the second dusk with her staff and stood there. He'd been going down on his own, for the levels, the way he did when he couldn't sleep. She hadn't said anything. She'd just come down the stair behind him. Not a pace behind. Beside, as far as the stair let her.

Down past the cold place where the old stone met the new. Into the deep workings, where the Delvers came up out of the rock in fours.

They came up in five.

[ Delver · Lv 62 ] ×5

Bigger than the ones Dec fought. Grey stone shoulders, arms like pillars, faces that were only cracks. They came out of the walls of the tunnel on both sides at once, grinding, and the floor shook.

Gabe planted the Anvil.

He knew how to do this. He'd been doing it in doors for two years. Shield up, feet set, shoulder in. The first Delver hit him like a cart full of rocks hitting a wall, and he gave nothing. The second came round his left, low, for his legs.

He'd have taken that one. On the shin. He always did. At fifty per cent pain, the way Dec kept it, because Dec kept it there. He'd have taken it and gone down to seventy and got up.

It didn't hurt.

He felt it land. A stone fist the size of his head, on his left shin, hard enough to crack it. And under it, already there before the fist arrived, a warmth. As though the bone had been mended a breath before it was broken.

He looked round. Rae was behind his left shoulder, a pace off. Her staff was down, the end of it glowing, pointed not at him but at the place where his shin had been going to be. She'd seen the Delver go low before it went low. She'd mended the place before it was a place.

"*Hit it*," said Rae.

He hit it. The Anvil's rim, sideways, into the crack that was its face, the way Salty had taught him in three weeks of turns into his shield. It went over backwards into the third one. The third one swung at his head. He ducked it, and felt warmth on the back of his neck where the swing would have landed if he hadn't. Already mended. Just in case.

"You're *cheating*," said Gabe, laughing, swinging.

"I'm *watching*," said Rae. "There's a difference."

The fourth one came for her. Not for him. Round him, to the back, where the healer was. They always did that. Everything went for the healer.

He'd never once got there in time. Not for anybody. He was a door; he held where he was. He didn't go.

He went.

Off his feet, sideways, a step and a half, the Anvil up. It wasn't where he was. It was where she was going to be hit. He got there a breath before the Delver did. It hit the Anvil instead of her, and the shock went down his whole left side into the floor. And he didn't move.

[ Anvil · Hold · *where she was going to be* ]

Rae looked at him over the rim of the shield, a pace off, with her staff.

"You *went*," she said.

"You were going to be there," said Gabe. "I could see it." He was laughing. He couldn't stop. "I could *see* it, Rae."

The fifth one came up out of the floor between them. They took it apart together. Him holding. Her mending where he was going to be held. In about forty seconds, without saying anything at all. He'd never fought like that in his life. Two people, both of them a breath ahead, not of the thing they were fighting. Of each other.

They stood in the deep tunnel afterwards among five piles of grey stone, breathing.

"That's new," said Rae.

"That's yours," said Gabe. "I borrowed it."

"You can keep it," said Rae.

***

Mother Dipp's letter came on the Wednesday.

Chernn read it out at the long table at the second dusk. She hadn't meant to. She'd opened it at the counter, and started laughing, and then crying, and Kotori had made her sit down. And the whole long table had gone quiet, waiting. And Chernn had looked up from it and seen sixty people waiting, and said *oh, all right*.

Nobody said anything for a while.

Then Deston, at the end of the long table, made a sound that Gabe had never heard him make. A sort of very small laugh that went wrong halfway through and turned into something else. He put his face in Chernn's shoulder, enormous, at the long table, in front of everybody, with his ears the colour of the banner. And Chernn put her arm round him and looked at the letter and didn't say anything.

"It *sat down*," said Leaka. Very quietly, for Leaka. "For somebody who wasn't *there*."

"It's a goose," said Kei.

"It's a goose that *considered*," said Leaka.

Gabe didn't scream. He wanted to. He'd have screamed at it a week ago, at the top of his lungs, at the long table. A goose sitting down five days away in the middle of a green for two people it couldn't see. He'd have stood up.

He didn't stand up. He sat there with Rae's shoulder against his, at no paces, and felt his eyes go. And let them. Quietly.

Under the table, without looking at him, Rae took his hand.

***

On the Thursday, Deston found him on the north steps.

Deston sat down beside him. Too big for the step, both of them. The two biggest people in Constellation on one step, which was meant for one person and a lamp. Deston had his book under his arm. He didn't open it. He hadn't opened it since the milestone, Gabe had noticed. He just carried it.

"You were loud about me," said Deston. "For twenty-two months. About a sticker."

"I know. I'm sorry."

"Don't be sorry," said Deston. "You were right." He looked up the north road. "I'd still have that sticker on the door if you hadn't been. I'm slow. I read the line in the book and wait for it to work." He was quiet. "You don't wait. You shout. And then things happen."

"I shouted at a *goose*," said Gabe. "Out there. In my head. For about a week."

"It sat down."

"It sat down," said Gabe. "For you. From five days away." He wiped his face. "I wanted to scream about it. At the long table. I didn't."

"I saw."

"I don't know why," said Gabe. "Dec says I keep the big ones. He says it's allowed. To keep them." He looked at his hands. "I never kept anything, Deston. I've been loud about everybody for eleven years."

Deston didn't say anything for a long time. He did that. He was slow.

"You were loud about me," said Deston, "so I'd hear it. When I couldn't. Every day for twenty-two months. *Ask her. Ask her. Deston, ASK HER*." He looked at Gabe. "That's not loud. That's holding a door. You were holding it open. So I'd go through."

Gabe stared at him.

"Now somebody's holding one for you," said Deston. "Rae. At no paces." He put his hand flat on the cover of his book, the way Isa did with things. "So you don't have to shout. You can just go through."

"Slow," said Gabe.

"Slow's allowed," said Deston. "Ask Chernn. Slow is how a candle burns all the way down."

***

On the Friday, the last Friday in April, he went and stood in the north door.

He didn't know why. Nobody was coming. Nobody was going. The north road was ninety-four flames standing, and past them the twenty-two, and past them the edge. And past the edge a dark country with green coming up in it, and a hill in the still water with a flame on it, and three hundred and one stones. It was spring all the way to the middle.

He stood in the north door because it was his. He'd held it twice. On the longest nights, all night, with the dark leaning. He'd never once stood in it on an ordinary night just to stand there.

It stuck when he opened it. It always did.

He stood in it, at the second dusk, with the Anvil on his arm, looking up the north road at the ninety-four flames. The little window at the top of the door had a square of red under it, upside down. *Arrived*. Curse had said that was what it meant. *It came*.

Rae came and stood beside him.

Not behind him. Not at his back. She came across the Hall of Roads from the long table and stood in the north door beside him. The north door was two shields wide. It had always been two shields wide. On the longest night, he'd thought it was so two people could hold it. Back to back.

It was so two people could stand in it. Side by side. Looking out.

"Nothing's coming," said Rae.

"I know."

"You're holding it anyway."

"I'm not holding it," said Gabe. "I'm just standing in it." He looked at the ninety-four flames going away north. "I've never just stood in it."

Rae didn't say anything for a while. She stood beside him in the north door with her staff, looking up the road.

"Mends where it's going to be," she said, at last. Which was her. Her whole way of fighting. She healed the place somebody was going to be hit before they were hit there, so that when it came, it was already mending. "That's what I did. On the longest night. I came the last pace because I could see where you were going to be." She was quiet. "You were going to be at no paces. With me. I just got there first."

Gabe looked at her.

He didn't say anything. He didn't scream. He didn't stand on anything, or tell the long table, or send anybody a message in capitals, or say *that's my* anything to anybody.

He put his arm round her. In the north door, at the second dusk, with nothing coming. And kept it.

From the long table, very faintly, through the open banner room door, somebody screamed. Hyv, probably. Gabe didn't look round.

"She'll do that every time," said Rae.

"I know," said Gabe. "It's allowed."
