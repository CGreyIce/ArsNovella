Kite kept the gate the way Wendy had kept it, because Wendy had told him how, and he'd never in his life been told how to keep anything.

You stood in it. That was the first thing. Not on the steps, not beside it. In it, in the south gate of the Hall of Roads, with the round market below you and the fountain going, and the road coming in round the curve from the south. You stood with your hands at your sides, where people could see them. Wendy had said that. *So they know you're not holding anything.*

Then, when somebody came up the steps to sign, or went down them to leave, you asked them.

*Who'd stand next to you?*

And whatever they said, you said *fair*.

And then, Wendy had said, on the last morning, with her pack on and the ten waiting at the bottom of the steps to walk to Masmark: *And then you sit on the steps with them after. That's the bit people forget.*

He'd kept it since February. He hadn't forgotten that bit once.

***

Half the guild had gone south on the seventeenth of April, and the round town was very quiet.

He'd asked every one of them. Standing in the gate at the first dawn, with the sun coming up over the east road, twelve of them on the steps with their packs on. Kei and Higu. Gigi. Thea and Xola and Xyro and Slep. Hako with his spear. Rafi with the big black sword that hummed. Bee, with her wings folded under her pack straps. Hyvred. And the swamp guy, at the front, with his shield on his back and a dent in it the shape of a crown.

They'd said what they always said. It didn't matter. You asked anyway. That was what the gate was for. Not to find out. To make them say it out loud, at the edge of the town, so they'd hear themselves.

The swamp guy had said *her* and looked at Hyvred, and Hyvred had gone red to the horns and hit him. And Kite had said *fair*. Then he'd sat on the steps with all twelve of them for a quarter of an hour, saying nothing, until Kei stood up and said *right*.

Then they'd gone. Down the south road, round the curve, fourteen days to a lake the size of a country.

And the round town was half empty, and the gate was very quiet. And Kite stood in it every morning anyway, with his hands at his sides.

***

He liked it quiet. He'd never have said so. He didn't say much.

The banner room was half empty in the mornings. Moonlight Harvest at the near end, eight of them and Chernn, without the first of the nine. Gabe talking. Rachael deciding who'd be helped first. Deston with his book. Suzu on the stool by the north door, beside Isa Corrie's chair, with the jar on the table in front of them both. Hana's five by the window. Tiger and Maigo on the road of lights. Drew at the forge, or on the road with Tiger, or at the forge again.

And the long table half empty. Ama Corrie sweeping under all the chairs anyway, every Sunday, the sat ones and the empty ones. Under, not round.

He'd been in Constellation since the founding, fourteen months. He still didn't know where to sit. He'd taken to sitting on the south steps in the evenings, just outside the gate, where he could see the road. It seemed the right place for him. Half in, half out. Wendy had said that was fine. *Some people are doors*, she'd said. *That's a thing you can be.*

He'd thought about that a lot.

***

On the fourth of May, at the second dusk, somebody came up the south road and stopped at the bottom of the steps and didn't come up.

Kite saw her from the gate.

She stood where the south road came into the round market, by the old milestone with *CARROW CROSS* on it and seven roads cut into the top. Where the market opened out and you could go any way. She didn't move. She just stood there, in the last of the light, at the crossroads, with her hands at her sides.

*Ash likes crossroads*, he'd said once. To Kei, a long time ago, on a call he'd made from a hedge on the north road at two in the morning. *At dusk.*

He knew her by how she stood. You didn't run with somebody all of the first winter without knowing how they stood. The weight on the left, the right hand loose, near the hilt, not on it. Two short curved blades on her back, crossed. A dark coat. And on the collar, where a little dark bird had been stitched, a patch of darker cloth and some loose threads.

She'd cut it off.

Kite stood in the gate for a long time and looked at the loose threads on her collar.

He'd cut his off in an hour, the winter before last, with his skinning knife, sitting in a ditch. He'd done it badly. You could still see where.

She'd done it badly too.

***

He didn't go down.

He thought about it. He stood in the gate with his hands at his sides and thought about it for the whole of the second dusk, while the light went and the lamps came on round the market. The baker's window. The blue door. The cookshop on Spoke Street. And out on the north road, behind the town, ninety-four lamps on the backs of grey stones, facing the wrong way.

She didn't come up. She stood at the milestone till it was dark, and then she sat down on the base of it with her back against the stone, and stayed there.

He knew what that was too. He'd sat at a milestone on the north road for six days, once, the winter before last. Not coming closer. Waiting to find out if he was allowed.

Gigi had come out on the sixth day with a cup of something horrible. *It'll fall down. Drink that. You look terrible.*

Kite went into the Hart.

Kotori was in the kitchen, at the kettles, with Chernn dipping candles in the corner. She looked up when he came in. He didn't come in much.

"Something horrible," said Kite. "And good for you."

Kotori looked at him for a long moment. Then she looked past him, out of the kitchen window, across the dark market to the milestone at the bottom of the south road, where somebody was sitting.

She didn't ask. She reached for a pot.

***

He took it down at nine.

He walked down the south steps and across the market with the cup in both hands. Past the fountain, past the chestnut stall, shut, past the ring of nine dark flagstones east of the fountain where people settled things. He stopped in front of the milestone.

She looked up at him.

He'd left the winter before last. He'd seen her twice since. Once at the stone cross on the moor, the February before last, when the swamp guy put her on the ground and let her get up again. *Not today, then.* Once on a road, from a long way off, at a crossroads at dusk, the week after. She'd seen him too, and turned and gone the other way. She looked the same. Calm, narrow face. Older round the eyes.

"Kite," said Ash.

"Ash."

He held out the cup.

She looked at it. She didn't take it.

"What is it?"

"Horrible," said Kite. "Good for you. Kotori made it."

"Who's Kotori?"

"The healer," said Kite. "The one who says sorry."

Ash looked at the cup for a long time. Then she took it. She held it in both hands without drinking it, the way you'd hold something in case it was a trick.

Kite sat down on the cobbles beside the milestone. Not too close. A sword's length. He put his bow down across his knees, unstrung.

Neither of them said anything for a long time.

***

"Nightjar's gone," said Ash at last.

Kite didn't say anything.

"In March," said Ash. "When the cities opened. Nobody pays for roads any more. Nobody walks the roads. Everybody who matters is at fifty, in a city, behind a wall." She turned the cup in her hands. "The money went. And when the money went, everybody went after it. Twelve of us, in January. Four in March. In April, one." She drank, finally. Her face did something. "That's horrible."

"Yes."

"Is it good for you?"

"Yes."

She drank some more.

"You don't leave Nightjar," said Ash. "You get left." She said it flatly, the way she'd said it to him on a road the winter before last, before the four of them killed him. "I said that to you."

"I remember."

"I got left," said Ash.

Kite looked at the market. At the lamps in the windows. At the fountain, and the steps going up to the gate, and the gate, empty, with nobody in it.

"Why are you here?" he said.

Ash didn't answer for a long time.

"I watched him," she said. "Every night. For a year. The swamp guy. You told them that. On the call." She wasn't looking at Kite. "I watched him walk round a goose. I watched him sweep a room. I watched the gate in January. I watched him pull a crown out of a lake and say *good for them*." She put the cup down on the base of the milestone. "I was paid to watch him. Somebody wanted his days. And then nobody was paying, and I kept watching."

"Why?"

"I don't know," said Ash. "I wanted to know what it was."

"What what was?"

She turned her head and looked at him. Her face was very still.

"That," she said, and looked up the steps at the gate.

***

He didn't tell her to come up.

It wasn't his to say. That was the thing about the gate. You asked, and you said *fair*, and you sat on the steps after. But you didn't say yes. Kei said yes. Or the long table did, round the fire on a Sunday, the way they had for Kite the winter before last, arguing about whether to trust him. Higu for, Maigo against, Salty saying nothing. And Gigi, in the end, who'd brought him the horrible cup, saying *he walked toward a light. That's three*.

It had been Wendy's count. *That's three. It's not fourteen. It's three.*

"Kei's in Masmark," said Kite.

"I know. I watched them go." Ash picked the cup up again. "Twelve. On the seventeenth. You asked them all and sat with them."

"You were here?"

"On the east road," said Ash. "In a hedge."

Kite almost laughed. He didn't.

"He'll be gone till August," he said. "Maybe longer. It's fourteen days each way."

"I've got time," said Ash. "I haven't got anything else."

***

She came back the next dusk. And the next.

She didn't come up the steps. She came up the south road at the second dusk and sat on the base of the milestone with her back against the stone and her blades across her knees. She stayed till the third dawn. Then she went away down the south road, Kite didn't know where. And came back at dusk.

He took her a cup every night. Kotori made it without being asked, by the third night. She'd have it ready on the end of the counter when he came in, in a cup with a chip in the rim. On the fourth night there were two cups.

"For you," said Kotori. "You're sitting out there too."

Kite looked at the second cup.

"Thank you," he said.

"You're welcome," said Kotori, and didn't say sorry, and went back to the kettles.

***

People noticed. Of course they did. The round town noticed everything.

Gabe noticed first, on the second night, because Gabe noticed things loudly. He came out onto the south steps at the second dusk with his shield and his enormous face and stopped dead. "*Is that*—" he said, and Rachael put her hand over his mouth and turned him round and walked him back inside.

Tiger noticed on the third night, coming in off the road of lights with Maigo. He stopped at the bottom of the steps and looked at the milestone for a long time. He didn't say anything. Tiger had stood still in the middle of the first mile on the longest night saying *not yet* while the dark came down the road. He knew what it was to stand somewhere and not go.

Drew noticed on the fourth.

Drew came across the market from the forge in the evening with soot on his hands. He saw Kite sitting on the cobbles a sword's length from the milestone with two cups, and Ash on the base of it. And he stopped.

Kite looked up at him.

Drew had held the road for him once. On the first longest night, on the Waymeet road, with Kite coming at a run out of arrows and three Unlit behind him. Drew had stepped out and stood in the road and held it. Kite had never been sure why. He'd asked, once. Drew had said *you were running toward the light*.

Drew didn't say anything now. He came across the cobbles and sat down on Kite's other side. Close. Close enough that their shoulders touched. He put his elbows on his knees and looked at the fountain.

Ash looked at him across Kite.

"You're the one who stays," said Ash. "On the keystone. In the lake."

"Yes," said Drew.

"I watched it."

"Lots of people watched it."

"I watched it eleven times," said Ash.

Drew thought about that.

"Okay," he said.

And the three of them sat at the bottom of the south road in the dark, saying nothing, with the fountain going, until the third dawn.

***

Ash looked up at him.

"Nobody," she said. "You know that. That's the rule. That was always the rule."

"Fair," said Kite.

He sat down. He gave her the cup.

***

On the sixth night, Chris came down the steps with a book.

Not a real book. His window, held open in front of him, the way Chris walked round with it open half the time. Every town's top hundred he could find. The public death log, every name, every day, back to the beginning. He'd kept it since the first month. Nobody had asked him to. He said somebody should know.

He stopped in front of the milestone. He looked at Ash for a long time with his pixel sunglasses pushed up on his head. Then at Kite.

"You asked me once," said Chris. "How many. For you. The winter before last, at the long table. You said you'd stopped counting. I said *fourteen. From the public death log*." He looked at Ash. "Do you want to know yours?"

Ash didn't say anything for a long time.

"Yes," she said.

Chris looked at his window. He scrolled. He didn't have to scroll long; he'd looked already. Kite could tell from his face.

"Three hundred and eleven," said Chris.

Nobody said anything.

The fountain went. A lamp went out in a window on the east side of the market and came on again.

"I knew it was a lot," said Ash. Very quietly.

"It's a lot," agreed Chris.

"I can't give them back."

"No," said Chris. He shut his window. He was quiet for a moment. Then he said, very carefully, the way Kite had never once heard Chris say anything: "Nor could he. Kite. He couldn't give his back either. Fourteen." He put his sunglasses down over his eyes. "Wendy said three. When he walked toward a light, and told Kei where you'd be, and shot an arrow out of the air. She said *that's three. It's not fourteen. It's three.*" He looked at Ash through the sunglasses. "Start with three. That's what I'd do."

And he went back up the steps and into the Hall without another word.

Ash sat on the base of the milestone and looked at the place where he'd been.

"Who's that?" she said.

"Chris," said Kite. "He counts things."

***

He wrote to Masmark again on the seventh night. Not to Kei. He'd never done it before. He didn't know if he was allowed. He did it anyway.

It came back the next morning. Three lines. The swamp guy didn't write long letters either.

[ Letter · from Ice → Kite

— Ice ]

He read it to her that night, at the milestone, at the second dusk, with two cups.

"He says *not today*," said Kite.

Ash went completely still.

Then she laughed.

Kite had never heard her laugh. Not once, all the first winter. Not on any road, at any crossroads, at any dusk. It was a small, surprised sound, as if somebody had pushed it out of her. She put her hand over her mouth. Her shoulders went. It went on for quite a long time.

"*Not today*," said Ash, through her hand.

"He said you'd know."

"I said it to him," said Ash. "At the stone cross. He put me on the ground and let me get up. He said *tell whoever's paying you that every name on that road has friends on it*. And I said *not today, then*." She took her hand away. Her eyes were wet. "*We'll have your day another day, swamp guy.* I said that too. In the fen. The first time."

"He remembers," said Kite.

"Of course he remembers," said Ash. "He remembers everything. I watched him for a year. He remembered the name of a goose."

***

On the tenth night, Kite asked her again.

He didn't plan it. He just stood up off the cobbles at the second dusk, with the cup in his hand. He looked down at her on the base of the milestone with her blades across her knees and the loose threads on her collar. And the question came out of him the way it came out of him in the gate, every morning, to everybody.

"Who'd stand next to you?"

Ash looked up at him.

She didn't say *nobody*.

She didn't say anything for a long time. The fountain went. The lamps were on all round the market. The baker's, the blue door, the cookshop. And up the steps, the gate, with nobody in it. Kite had been sitting out here for ten nights instead of standing in it.

"You," said Ash.

Kite didn't move.

"Not because you'd say yes," said Ash quickly. "I know you can't. I know it's not yours. I watched you not say it for ten nights. I'm not asking for a yes." She stopped. "You asked who. That's who."

Kite stood at the bottom of the south road with a chipped cup in his hand and looked at her.

He thought about Wendy, in the gate, the last morning. *And then you sit on the steps with them after. That's the bit people forget.* He thought about a milestone on the north road, the winter before last. A cup of something horrible. Gigi saying *it'll fall down*. The long table round the fire on a Sunday, arguing. Drew in the road, holding it.

"Fair," said Kite.

He sat down again. A sword's length.

Then, after a while, a bit closer.

***

He wrote to Kei that night. The longest letter he'd ever written. Four lines.

[ Letter · from Kite → KeiKatamori
she said me
i said fair
she's not asking for a yes
she'll be here when you're home
— Kite ]

Kei wrote back at the third dawn.

[ Letter · from KeiKatamori → Kite
Then she'll ask me when I'm home.
And it might be no.
Tell her that.
And then sit with her anyway.
— K.
(Higu says hello. Higu says Gigi says: is she drinking it.) ]

He told her. All of it. *It might be no.*

Ash looked at the cup in her hands, the chipped one, nearly empty.

"I know," she said. "I'll ask him anyway."

Kite looked at her.

"Tell Gigi," said Ash, "it's horrible. And yes."

***

He went back up the steps at the third dawn. He stood in the gate with his hands at his sides, the way Wendy had told him, the way he'd done every morning since February. Except the ten mornings he'd been sitting at the bottom of the road instead.

The sun came up over the east road. The round town woke up. Moonlight Harvest went out onto the high fells, Gabe at the front, talking. Tiger and Maigo went out onto the road of lights. Drew went to the forge. Kotori opened the Hart. Ama Corrie came out onto the step of the Hall with her broom and looked at him over her spectacles.

"You were out there," she said. "All night. Ten nights."

"Yes."

"Sitting."

"Yes."

Ama Corrie looked past him, down the steps, across the market, to the milestone at the bottom of the south road. Somebody was still sitting on the base of it, with her back against the stone and two blades across her knees, asleep.

"Hm," said Ama Corrie.

She went back inside. Kite heard her through the open door, sweeping. Under, not round.

He stood in the gate. Down at the milestone, Ash woke up and looked round. The way you do when you've fallen asleep somewhere you didn't mean to, and aren't sure, for a moment, where you are. She looked up the steps at the gate. At him.

He raised his hand. Not a wave. Just his hand, open, where she could see it, so she'd know he wasn't holding anything.

After a moment, at the bottom of the road, she raised hers.
