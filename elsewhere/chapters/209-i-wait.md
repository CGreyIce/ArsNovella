Xyro waited until nobody else had said anything, and then he said it.

"Tif says go."

They were in the Crownless Hall at eight o'clock on the fourth night, nineteen of them, at the top of the stair that went down through the floor of the dais. The king was on his throne above them with his head bowed, asleep or waiting. The seven lamps were still burning on their posts, kept. And nobody had said anything for about a minute, because Kei had just read out the board.

[ The Crown Below · the race
VANTA — the deep · kept · *at the Crown*
Sable Court — out · fallen (11 of 20)
Meridian — the deep · kept
Constellation — the Crownless Hall · kept
The Open Hand — out ]

"VANTA's at the crown," Kei had said. "Already. They went in at eight. They're there."

And then nobody had said anything.

Xyro had waited. He didn't, usually. He'd never been a waiter; he'd been the one who said things first and then wished he hadn't. But he'd learned it from Slep, on a road in July, at the back of the line, with blisters. *I wait until nobody else has said it.* He'd been practising ever since. If you waited until everybody else had said what they were going to say, then what was left was usually the one thing nobody had said. And that was usually the thing that needed saying.

Nobody had said it. So he did.

"Tif says go," said Xyro.

Everybody looked at him.

"She's watching," said Xyro. "Out of the egg. On Ice's stream. She messaged me before I went in." He thought about how to say the rest. "She said *VANTA's at the crown and you're going to want to stop. Don't stop. Go.*" He paused. "She said *it's not about first*."

There was a silence.

Then Hyvred laughed. Not much. A breath.

"*Tif*," she said.

"Tif," agreed Xyro.

Ice, at the front, at the top of the stair, looked back at him. He looked at Xyro for a long moment, the way he looked at doors. Then he nodded. Once.

"Tif says go," said Ice. And went down.

***

He'd gone into the egg that night in Tif's room in Singapore, the way he usually did. She'd been at her desk with the lights off and Ice's stream on the big screen and chat on the small one. A cup of bitter tea going cold. She hadn't gone in for the race. She was forty-eight; you needed fifty for Masmark. She'd said she didn't mind. She'd said *somebody has to mod*.

"Xy," she said, as he was lying down.

He looked at her over the edge of the egg.

"You're going to want to stop," said Tif. "Tonight. In the deep. VANTA's at the crown already. The board says. Everyone's going to see it and think *we've lost*, and want to stop." She didn't turn round from the screen. "Don't stop."

"Okay."

"It's not about first," said Tif. "It never was. Not for him." She was quiet for a moment. "I've watched him for five years, Xy. Every stream. Ten people and a swamp. He's never once played to be first. He plays to be *there*." She turned round at last and looked at him, in the light from the screen. "So be there. With him. All the way down. Whatever the board says."

He looked at her for a long time. He'd known her two years. He'd never been any good at knowing when to say things. She'd always known.

"I'll tell them," he said. "If nobody else does."

"Somebody will," said Tif. "Probably you. You've been practising."

He laughed. Then the lid came down, and he went in, and her message was already in the corner of his window, waiting for him. She'd written it while he was lying down. *VANTA's at the crown and you're going to want to stop. Don't stop. Go. it's not about first.*

***

He'd known Ice for two years, through Tif. Not well. Tif knew him well. Tif had modded for him for four years and typed *first* when she wasn't, and called him *idiot*, and cried at Year One into her arms at her desk with Xyro sitting on the floor beside her chair. Xyro had known Ice through all of that. Through the way Tif talked about him. Like a brother who'd never once asked for anything and needed looking after anyway.

He'd never fought next to him. Not properly. Not until the island.

He went down the stair under the Crownless Hall behind Salty and Wendy. His bow was in his hand and Tif's message was in the corner of his window, where he could see it. And he thought: *it's not about first*.

He didn't know what it was about yet. He'd wait. He'd find out.

***

The deep was the longest.

Kestrel had been right. Kei said it on the way down, quietly, to nobody. A hundred and six, and forty, and sixty-six, and everybody had thought that was the top, the eye, the two hundred and twelve. And then there was the deep. And the deep went down and down. Further than the other three put together, a long winding stair in the rock under the bottom of the lake. Past three kept places where VANTA had been before them, their lamps still burning in the cages. Past things that came up out of pools in the side passages, and had to be held while the line went by.

They held them. In threes. Nobody alone.

It took four hours.

At the second of VANTA's kept places, an hour down, he got it wrong.

It was a side passage off the stair, with a pool at the end of it. Still water, black. Things came up out of it while the line went past, and Kei put Xyro and Wendy at the mouth of the passage, the two bows, to hold it. *Three*, Kei said, and put Jun Liang with them with his hammer.

The first thing that came up out of the pool was small. Low, flat, ink on the floor. Xyro loosed at it before it was halfway out of the water. Before he'd thought. The way he'd always done everything, before Slep.

The arrow went in. The small thing came apart. And behind it, rising out of the pool where the small thing had been, much bigger, tall and thin and swaying, came the thing the small one had been in front of. Already out. Already coming. With Xyro's arrow spent and his hand going back to his quiver too late.

Wendy's four arrows went past his ear. *Let go.* Four, into the tall one, before it was three steps out of the water. It went back down.

Xyro stood in the mouth of the passage with his empty bow and his heart going.

"You went early," said Wendy. Not unkindly. She was already nocking again.

"I know."

"The little one's always first," said Wendy. "Something always comes behind it. You wait for the second one." She loosed at something else. "Hester taught me that. In Ashcombe. A year ago. I froze for a whole night and then I learned to let go." She glanced at him. "You've got the other problem. You let go too soon."

"Slep said wait," said Xyro.

"Slep's right," said Wendy. "Slep's always right. It's very annoying." She loosed again. "Wait for the second one."

He waited for the second one. All the way down the passage, all the way through that kept place, he waited. The little ones came, and he let them come, and Jun Liang hit them with his hammer, singing. And when the big ones came up behind them, he was ready, and loosed, and it went in.

[ Bow · Loose · Grade 79% ]

It was the best grade he'd ever had.

"Better," said Wendy, at the end of it.

He thought about Tif, out of the egg in Singapore, watching on Ice's stream with her tea going cold. *It's not about first.* He thought about Slep on the road, at the back of the line, beside him. *Somebody had to be on that side too.*

He thought: *wait for the second one.*

At midnight, at the third dawn, which they couldn't see from under a lake, they came out of the bottom of the stair into a cavern. And stopped.

It was the bottom of the lake.

Not under it. *At* it. Xyro looked up, and up, and there was the lake. The whole of Great Lake Masmark, held up above them like a ceiling of black water. Not by rock. By nothing. A roof of water, standing still, so high you could barely see it, and so heavy you could feel it. Fish swimming in it, very small, very far up. And at the very top, so far it was only a faint shimmer, the lights of the city on the surface. A ring of gold, wavering.

Below it, in the middle of the cavern, a pool. Black, and very still, and very big. The size of the round market.

And above the pool, in the air, the crown.

***

It was light.

That was all it was. A ring of light, gold, the size of a cart wheel, floating above the black pool in the middle of the bottom of the lake. Turning, slowly. Very bright. The brightest thing Xyro had ever seen in the game. Brighter than the forge, brighter than the seven lamps, brighter than anything. It was so bright that everything in the cavern leaned toward it.

He felt it. His feet. His bow. His whole body, very slightly, leaning toward the crown over the pool.

[ The Drowned Crown · Lv 56 ]

[ *Everything that falls in the lake comes here.* ]

"*Don't*," called Hyvred. Sharp, at the front. "*Don't go to it. It pulls. Everything. Toward the pool. Hold your feet. Everybody, hold your*—"

And the pull came.

It came all at once, like a tide going out. Not wind; nothing touched him. But the whole floor of the cavern tilted. Not really. It just felt like it. Every one of them, all nineteen, sliding toward the black pool and the crown above it, a step, two steps, before they knew they were moving.

"*Anchor*," said Ice.

He didn't shout it. He said it the way he said everything. And he planted his feet on the rock at the edge of the cavern and *didn't move*. And the pull went past him like water past a stone. Xyro saw it. Saw the pull bend round Ice the way the stream bent round a rock in the north tunnel.

"*Behind him*," called Kei. "*Everybody. Behind Ice. In his lee. NOW.*"

They went. Nineteen people sliding across the floor of the bottom of a lake into the shelter of one man standing still. Like ships into a harbour.

The pull stopped. For a moment.

Then it came again.

***

They were six minutes into it when the window came.

Xyro would remember that afterward more than anything. He'd tell Tif about it, after, on the floor beside her chair, at four in the morning. Not the crown. Not the pull. The window.

They'd been fighting for six minutes. Holding, mostly. The crown didn't fight like a thing. It pulled, and pulled, and every time it pulled, things came up out of the black pool beneath it. Drowned things, tall and thin and swaying. And they had to be held off while everybody stayed in Ice's lee. Thea and Gigi on the left, *thorn, Turn*. Salty and Azac on the right, a black sword and a wall. Xav and RX and Ryan going round the edges, fast. Bee drawing shields. Kei calling from the middle, with his drops, three at a time, steadying whoever was lowest.

Xyro was at the back with Wendy, the two bows. He was loosing at the things from the pool. And waiting. Waiting for the crown to come low enough to hit, because it was too high, turning in the air above the pool, and nobody could reach it.

Six minutes in, it came.

[ WORLD FIRST
The Crown Below has been cleared for the first time in Elsewhere.
It is the first great door of Masmark to be cleared in this world.
Cleared by: VANTA
Kestrel · Brann · Ottie · Mags · Ren ]

And fifteen more names after that. Xyro didn't read them. Nobody did.

[ This message has been shown to every traveller in Elsewhere. ]

The cavern went quiet.

Not the fight. The fight went on. The crown kept turning and pulling, and the things kept coming up out of the pool. But all nineteen of them, for one second, stopped. You couldn't not. Every traveller in the world had just stopped, all at once, to look at nothing.

*VANTA.*

Kestrel. First. At last. After four times second.

Xyro looked at the front. At Ice, standing at the edge of the cavern with his shield up and the pull going round him like water round a stone. At sixty per cent, with Hyvred two paces behind him calling.

He'd read it too. Xyro could tell. His head had moved, just slightly. The way you'd move your head to read a window in the corner of your eye.

And Xyro waited to see what he'd say.

He waited. Everybody waited. Nineteen people at the bottom of a lake, fighting a crown of light, waiting to see what Ice would say about being second.

"*Good for them*," said Ice.

And went left, because Hyvred had called it.

***

Somebody laughed. Xyro thought it was Wendy, beside him. A short surprised laugh, out loud, in the middle of a fight. Then Salty, which was rarer. Then Thea, on the left. And then all of them, nineteen people laughing at the bottom of a lake with a crown of light trying to pull them into a pool.

"*Good for them*," said Gigi, under her breath, to her thorn, like a prayer.

"*Good for THEM*," shouted Jun Liang, from the right, and hit something with his hammer, delighted.

"*He means it*," said RX, counting the things coming up out of the pool. "*He means it. He always means it. Twenty-three. Twenty-four.*"

Xyro didn't laugh. He was practising waiting.

He looked at Tif's message in the corner of his window. *It's not about first.*

And he understood.

It wasn't. It never had been. Not for Ice. Not for any of them. He looked round the cavern at the bottom of the lake. Nineteen people from a round town nobody had heard of, holding each other in the lee of one man standing still. Laughing because he'd said *good for them* about somebody else's first, and meant it.

It had never been about first. It was about who was in the lee.

He nocked an arrow.

***

At forty minutes, the crown came lower.

It had been turning above the pool the whole fight, too high, pulling. But it had been pulling for forty minutes now. And the pull didn't work. Every time it pulled, the nineteen were in Ice's lee, and Ice didn't move. And the things it sent up out of the pool were held, in threes, and went back down. So it came lower. Slowly. Turning. Brighter. As if it wanted to see why nothing was coming.

"*It's coming down*," called Hyvred. "*It's lower. Ten feet. It's— it wants him. It wants Ice. He's the only thing it can't move.*"

"*Let it*," said Ice.

He was at forty-one per cent. Xyro could see his window. He'd been standing still at the edge of the cavern for forty minutes with the pull going round him, and every pull went *under the surface*. Every one of them held.

Eleven thousand.

Xyro had never seen a number like that. Nobody had. On the longest night, Bee said, it had been nine thousand, and he'd pulled a lake into a stream with it.

"*It's coming*," called Hyvred. "*Five feet. It's going to try to take him. It's going to pull just him, all of it, at once. It's*—"

The crown came down over the pool toward Ice. Turning, so bright that Xyro had to squint. Low, now. Low enough to hit. And everything in the cavern leaned toward it, harder than before. Xyro's feet slid on the rock. Wendy grabbed his arm.

He didn't loose.

Everybody else did. Wendy beside him, *let go*, four arrows. Val's light from the left. Malcolm's. Xav going in under it, fast, both blades. Salty's black sword coming round, the length of a man. All of it at the crown, now that it was low enough, all at once.

And Xyro waited.

He didn't know why. He'd tell Tif afterward that he didn't know why. His arrow was nocked and his bow was drawn and the crown was low, and everybody else was loosing at it. And he didn't. He held. Not the way Kestrel held, at the top of the draw, with a decision so big everybody froze. The other way. The way you hold your breath at the bottom of a lake, waiting for somebody else to come up first.

He was watching Ice.

Not the crown. Ice. At the edge of the cavern, with the crown coming down at him and his shield up and eleven thousand under the surface. And his feet. Xyro had heard Drew talk about feet. He'd never understood it. He watched Ice's feet now.

The back foot was waiting.

A breath.

*He's going to pull it*, Xyro thought, quite clearly. *He's going to let it get close, all the way, and then pull it the rest of the way down. Off itself. Into him.* And then for one moment, after the pull, it'll be on the ground. Low. In front of him. Not turning.

*And everybody will have already loosed.*

He kept waiting.

The crown came down. Three feet over the pool, then two, then over the rock at its edge, toward Ice. So close now its light was on Ice's face. On his shield. On Hyvred, two paces behind him with her bow up, calling.

"*Ice*," said Hyvred.

Not calling. His name.

The back foot came round.

Xyro waited.
