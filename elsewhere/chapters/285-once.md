Ice had been at the far end of every room he'd ever been in, and he'd never once minded.

It was the best place. You could see everybody from the far end. Who came in, and who went out, and who was sitting on their own. You could see the door. You could see where everybody was going to be before they got there. Nobody looked at the far end of a room, mostly. That was the other good thing about it. You could be in a room your whole life and nobody would notice you were there until afterwards. They'd scroll back through the list and go *oh. Ice was here. The whole time.*

He'd been in everybody's chat like that for years. He'd been at the back of a war like that for two, at the front of it, which was a different kind of back. He'd been at the far end of the long table in the banner room, in the first of the nine chairs, for two and a half years.

He'd never once stood up at it.

He was going to, at a quarter to eight. He'd said so. *Once.*

***

It was the last night of April. A Monday.

He went down the north tunnel at the second dusk, because he couldn't sit still, and Hyv came with him because she always did. Down past the cold place where the old stone met the new, into the deep workings. She didn't hold his arm in the tunnel. She called.

"Four. Two high, two low."

He went first. The river, the slope, the cut on the beat. Lucan's, and Salty's, and the fen, which was his. He didn't think about any of it. The Delvers came apart in the dark round his feet, and her arrows went through the joints of their stone shoulders, and that was that.

[ Lv 66 ]

He stood in the deep tunnel and looked at the window for a while.

Sixty-six. In November he'd been fifty-nine, on a hill, in the dark's own water, with eight people round a flame. Seven levels since the longest night. Most of them hadn't come from anything. The game had just counted things. A walk home. A draw. A boom on a shield so twenty people pulled at once. A stone the right size.

"You're thinking," said Hyv, behind him.

"Yes."

"About tonight."

"Yes," said Ice.

She came up beside him. She didn't ask. She'd asked on Saturday, on the north parapet, once, and he'd said *soon*, and she hadn't asked again. She'd said it was the strangest thing about being loud. You said the thing once, out loud. And then you didn't have to keep saying it, because it was said.

She took his arm with both hands. In the tunnel, in the dark, with nobody to see.

"You're very boring," said Hyv.

"I know."

"I love it."

"I know," said Ice. And went pink in the dark, where nobody could see, except her. She could always see.

***

He sat on the south steps of the Hall of Roads for most of the night.

Not at the far end of anything. In the middle of the steps, where people came up and went down. Kite was in the gate behind him, with his hands at his sides, asking *who'd stand next to you* of everybody who came. Two people came up the steps while Ice sat there. One said *nobody yet*. Kite said *that's an answer*, which was Aurelie's. The other was the thin young man with no guild, from the milestone in January. He had a guild now. Ice didn't know which. He said, very fast, very badly, *the ones I came up with*. And Kite said *fair*, which was Wendy's.

Ice watched the round town.

He'd done it before. Sat on a step and watched a town go by. But he'd never done it like this, knowing he was going to stand up at the end of the night. It made everything very clear. The way a room goes clear when you know you're going to say something in it.

The middle pole by the fountain. Green ash, bare, with its seven ropes going out to nothing. The gold eye at the top of it, which Alice had cut in a week at the long table, catching every lamp in the market. Jared had said it would stay up. Ama Corrie had said *it's in the way*, and then *it can stay*.

The forty-one squares of red on forty-one doors, a bit faded now. One upside down, under the little window at the top of the north door. *Arrived*.

The yard behind the Hart, where a door stood on its own in a frame, on two hinges with stars on them. Milli had made it with his own hands, against the grain and then with it. It didn't go anywhere. Alice walked through it every evening anyway, from one side of nothing to the other, and Milli came after her.

The Hart's kitchen window, where Chernn was dipping, with Deston on the stool beside her. Nine days married, out there. Still on the stool. Still holding the wick-rod.

The north door, where Gabe was standing. Not holding it. Just standing in it, with the Anvil on his arm, looking up the north road at the ninety-four flames. Rae was beside him. Not behind. Beside. The door was two shields wide. Gabe had told him, on Saturday, out there, in the kitchen, with the kettle on, what the two shields were for. He'd said it as though it were the biggest secret in the world. Ice had said *I know*, and Gabe had said *how*, and Ice had said *I've seen you two in it*.

And at the top of the dome, at the eye, on the *Somewhere* bench, beside Kei's lamp, a small shape in a grey blanket, looking north.

***

Hyv laughed at that one, out loud, on the step, so that two people at the chestnut stall looked round.

"*He falls over less*," she said. "Lucan of Sable Court. *Less*."

"He'll be ready by July," said Ice.

"Will *you*?"

He thought about it. Properly. He'd never once been ready for anything in his life before he did it. He'd just done it, and found out afterwards.

"I'll be there," he said. "That's the same thing."

***

At half past seven, he went into the banner room.

Everybody was there. It was the end of the night, the end of April, a Monday. Sixty-one chairs and a stool, full. Kei at the head with his map. Higu beside him. Alice and Milli at her five by the window. Jared beside them, where Alice had pulled his chair out with her foot a fortnight ago and he'd kept it. Leaka and Curse. Thea and Gigi, close. Xola and Slep. Kotori, with the kettle. Suzu, with the empty black case under his chair, where he kept it now. Salty and Wendy. Chernn and Deston, from the kitchen, smelling of wax. Gabe and Rae, from the north door, at no paces. Drew and Kite and Ash, three. PenguChi, with Patience the sheep lying on her feet. Dennis with his ledger. Tiger. Maigo. Ina. Hako. Bee. Everybody.

Isa was in her chair by the banner room door. Suzu had carried her down from the eye. Ama Corrie was in the doorway behind her, with her broom.

He sat down in the first of the nine. Hyv sat beside him. She didn't sit on his knee. She'd sat on his knee at the long table every night for three months. Tonight she sat beside him, on her own chair, with her hands in her lap and her tail round her own ankle, and didn't look at him.

She knew. Of course she knew. She saw everything.

Out there, in Singapore, on the end of a bed, Tif would be watching. With the stream on the big screen and three alarms set anyway.

At a quarter to eight, Kei stood up.

***

Ice stood up before Kei could say it.

He hadn't planned how. He'd thought about it all night on the south steps. And he'd found that the only way he could do it was the way he did everything. Not deciding. Just going, on the beat, before anybody else had.

He stood up at the far end of the long table, in the first of the nine chairs. Sixty-two people and a sheep looked at him.

Nobody said anything. Kei, at the head of the table, with his mouth open to say *set one to watch*, closed it. And sat down again, slowly, without taking his eyes off Ice. Kei had never once in two and a half years not said the line at a quarter to eight.

The banner room was so quiet he could hear the fire.

"Somebody asked me once," said Ice, "who'd stand next to me."

He didn't say it loudly, at first. He said it the way he said everything. Plainly. To the room.

"Lucan. On a beach at the bottom of a lake. A year ago. And I said *Hyv*." He could feel his face going. He let it. "I said it quietly. On a beach. To one person. I didn't know anybody else heard."

Down the long table, Thea put her hand over her mouth.

"Somebody did," said Ice. "She ran into the lake. Up to her knees." He looked down the table. "Thea saw."

"I *saw*," said Thea, through her hand.

"I've been quiet my whole life," said Ice. "About everything. I've been at the far end of every room. I didn't know I was allowed to be anywhere else. Somebody told me it was allowed, in January, a year ago. In a group chat. And I went to a gate. And she said *me*. And I said *I know*." He swallowed. "And then I didn't say anything loud about it for a year and a half. Not once. Not to anybody."

He turned round.

Hyv was sitting beside him with her hands in her lap. She was looking up at him. Edge to edge, close, with her eyes that saw everything. Red to the horns. Not saying anything.

He'd never seen her not say anything. Not in three months.

"She's been loud about me," said Ice. To the room. But looking at her. "Every day. Since the old hall. At this table. On the stair. On the stream, into the camera. Under a roof, against a green pole, in front of everybody. *Mine*. *Come here*. *You're very boring and I love it*." He heard Gabe make a noise at the far end of the table and stop himself. "She was frightened every time. For weeks. Her hands shook. I didn't know. She told me on Saturday."

Hyv's hands, in her lap, had started shaking again.

"So I'm saying it once," said Ice. "Loud. In front of everybody."

And he said it loudly. For the first time in his life. In a voice the whole of the banner room heard, and the Hall of Roads through the open door, and Ama Corrie at the counter, and Isa in her chair. And three hundred thousand people through a small eye at his shoulder, and one of them in Singapore on the end of a bed.

"*She's very loud*," said Ice. "*And I love it*."

***

Nobody made a sound for about three seconds.

Then Hyv stood up.

She didn't scream. Everybody thought she would. He thought she would. She stood up out of her chair, very fast, red past the horns, with her hands shaking. She looked at him from about a foot away with her mouth open and nothing coming out of it at all.

So he did the rest of it.

He put his hand on her face, the way she'd put both of hers on his at the old hall in January, in front of eight people, at a quarter to eight. And he said it. The word she'd said first, that had made her nearly sick. Loudly. So the room would hear.

"*Mine*," said Ice.

***

The banner room went up.

He'd heard it go up before. When Milli said *we've picked a day*. When Rae moved her chair. At the old hall, when Xola screamed so long it went away over the heather. This was louder than all of those at once.

Gabe screamed. Obviously. He stood up and screamed and then looked at Rae and sat down again and screamed sitting down. Leaka stood on her chair. Xola screamed into Slep's shoulder. Slep said *Xola* once and gave up. Thea and Gigi both had their faces in their hands. Alice was crying. Milli was holding her. Jared said *y'all*, very loudly, for the first time ever. Kotori dropped the kettle. Suzu said *good* and then, after a moment, something Ice had never heard him say. *Finally*. Which was Xola's.

Dennis said *darling* about forty times.

PenguChi, at the back of the room, with the sheep on her feet, said nothing at all. But she lifted her spear, a little.

And in the doorway, Ama Corrie, with her broom, looked at the two of them in the middle of it for a long time.

"*Hm*," said Ama Corrie.

Then: "*Well*."

Then, after a moment, quieter, so that only the people nearest the door heard it, and Isa: "*About time*."

***

Hyv still hadn't said anything.

She was standing in front of him at the far end of the long table, with his hand on her face, red past the horns, shaking. In the middle of sixty-two people screaming. Her eyes were doing the thing he'd seen them do once, on the north parapet, when he'd said *it's allowed*. They'd stopped looking at everything. They were only looking at one thing.

"You went *first*," said Hyv. At last. Very faintly.

"Not at everything," said Ice. "At that."

"You said you'd do it *once*."

"I did it once."

"You did it *twice*," said Hyv. Her voice was coming back. "*She's very loud and I love it.* And *mine*. That's *twice*."

Ice thought about it.

"The second one was yours," he said. "I borrowed it."

Hyv stared at him for a long time.

Then she hit him on the arm. Hard. The way she'd hit him in the gate of the round town a year and a half ago, twice. *That's for knowing. That's for allowed.*

"*That's for twice*," said Hyv.

And then she put both hands flat on his chest. She pushed him back, not hard, against the wall of the banner room behind the first of the nine chairs. And kissed him. In front of everybody. The way she always did, going first. Except that this time, for once, she wasn't.

He'd gone first. She was answering.

The banner room, which had nearly stopped, went up again.

***

When it had stopped, properly, which took a while, Kei stood up again at the head of the long table.

He looked down it at all of them. At Ice, standing against the wall at the far end with Hyv's arms round his neck, and at the rest of them. He didn't have his map out. He hadn't had it out all night.

"Set one to watch," said Kei.

And sixty-two people and a sheep said it. All at once, in the banner room, at a quarter to eight in the morning at the end of April:

"*So the rest may sleep.*"

Hyv said it loudest. Of course she did. Into his shoulder. He said it with her. Not loud. Just with her. At the same time, not a breath late.

And somewhere a long way north, at the bottom of the sky, past the ninety-four and the twenty-two and the edge and the dark country coming up green, a flame stood straight up over a jar on a hill. Three hundred and one stones in the still water.

Watching itself.

***

Out there, in the kitchen at Melaka Raya, the phone was going before the egg had finished opening.

He went and put the kettle on first. Then he picked it up.

*!loud*, Tif had written. *new command. "once. (tif says)"*

Then:

*you idiot*

*you absolute idiot*

*i'm crying so much xyro came out of the egg again*

*he says hi*

*he says it's about time*

*everybody says it's about time*

*chat typed MINE for eleven minutes*

*i didn't slow mode it*

*i didn't want to*

He stood in the kitchen with the kettle going and read it twice. Then he wrote back.

*thank you for being there*

There was a long gap.

*always*, Tif wrote. *idiot.*

Gabe came in. He was red-eyed. He sat on the counter with his kopi and looked at Ice and didn't say anything. For once. For a long time.

Then he said, very quietly, "You were *loud*, Dec."

"Once," said Ice.

"You were loud *about* somebody," said Gabe. "In eleven years I've never once heard you be loud about anything." He wiped his face. "I'm keeping it. I'm not telling anybody. I'm keeping it."

"Okay," said Ice.

The phone went again. Not Tif.

*come here*

He looked at it for a long time. In the kitchen, in the grey morning, with the kettle clicking off and Gabe on the counter.

*i'm out here*, he wrote. *out there. it's morning.*

*i know*, wrote Hyv. *come here anyway. tonight. the eye.*

*okay*

*you were so loud*

*once*

*do it again*

He thought about it. Properly. The way he did.

*not yet*, he wrote.

There was a long gap. He could see her typing, and stopping, and typing.

Then:

*good*

*that's suzu's*

*i borrowed it*

He put the phone face down on the counter and made the tea. Two cups. He gave one to Gabe. Then he stood at the kitchen window, in Malacca, in the grey light of the first of May, and drank the other. He thought about a bridge in Masmark in July, and a man who'd learned the slow one by falling over less. About a draw that would be a fight again in May. About a stone the right size in the still water, and a hawthorn that was going to flower in the right month for the first time in three hundred years.

He'd been at the far end of every room his whole life.

It turned out you could stand up at it once. Out loud. And the room didn't fall down.

It held.
