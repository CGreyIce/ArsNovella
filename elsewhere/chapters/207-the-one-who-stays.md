Drew watched feet. He'd always watched feet.

He'd learned it a long time ago, before the game, hosting tournaments. Lobbies full of people waiting for a match to start, and him at the front with a headset, running it. You learned, doing that, that you couldn't tell anything from faces. Faces lied. Faces did what people wanted them to do. But feet didn't. Feet were honest. A man who was about to leave a lobby shifted his weight toward the door a full minute before his face knew it. A woman who was about to lose her temper planted her heels. A team that was going to win stood with their feet pointing the same way.

So he watched feet, and in fifteen months in the game it had never once let him down.

On the second night in the Crown Below, at the bottom of the second flight, he watched twenty pairs of feet come off the last step into the Long Gallery. And he knew, before anybody said anything, that somebody was going to have to stay.

***

The second flight was forty steps.

RX counted them out loud, the way he counted everything, and Kei told him to stop, and he counted them under his breath instead. Forty. Down from the kept place where they'd logged out the night before, through a black door with a crown on it, into the rock.

"A hundred and six," said RX. "And forty. Like the round town."

"Like the round town," said Hyvred.

"So the next one's sixty-six," said RX. "And then it's done."

Nobody said anything. Drew looked at Hyvred's feet, at the front, beside Ice's. She'd shifted her weight back. Just a little. The way you did when somebody said *and then it's done* and you didn't think it was.

At the bottom of the forty there was a gallery.

He'd never seen anything like it. Nobody had. A long straight corridor, so long you couldn't see the end, going away under the lake in a dead straight line. The floor was square stone tiles, each as wide as a man was tall, a hundred of them, two hundred, going away into the dark. The walls were black rock. And the ceiling was glass.

Not glass. Something like glass. Clear, and very thick, and above it, the lake.

Drew looked up. Everybody did.

The whole of Great Lake Masmark was above them. Green and dark and very deep. Fish swimming in it, slow, silver, looking down. And far above that, at the very top, at the surface, wavering, lights. The lights of Masmark, all the way round, a ring of gold on the water. The lanterns of a city at night, seen from the bottom of a lake.

"*Oh*," said Bee, very quietly.

Drew looked down again. At the floor. At the tiles.

They weren't all the same.

***

"*Don't step on the dark ones*," called Hyvred.

Drew had seen it a breath before she said it. The tiles went in a pattern. Most were grey, the same pale grey as the stone in the Seven Below. But every so often, scattered down the gallery in no order he could see, a tile was darker. A shade, no more. If you weren't looking at the floor you'd never have seen it.

He was always looking at the floor.

"*They drop*," called Hyvred. "*I can see under them. There's nothing under them. Water. A long way down.*"

Kei looked at the gallery for a long time with his map on his arm.

"We go on the grey," he said. "One at a time. Hyv calls every tile. Ice first."

"*Ice first*," said Hyvred, and Ice stepped onto the first grey tile.

It held.

They went down the Long Gallery under the lake one at a time, in a line, on the grey. Hyvred calling every tile. *Grey. Grey. Left one. Skip one. Grey.* Ice first, then the others behind him in order, stepping exactly where he'd stepped. It was slow. It was so slow. But it worked. The lake above them, the fish looking down. The lights of Masmark wavering at the top of everything. The only sound Hyvred's voice and twenty pairs of feet on stone.

Drew went near the back. He always went near the back. The edge, Kei called it. The one who watched the line from behind, so nobody fell off the end of it without somebody seeing.

He watched their feet. All of them. Ahead of him, going down the gallery in a line. He knew every pair by now.

Ice's, at the front. Never hurrying, never stopping. The back foot waiting a breath on every tile, so it could go either way.

Hyvred's, just behind. Light, quick, always pointing where she was looking.

Thea's, solid, and Gigi's beside them, a pace off. They'd come to step the same, the two of them, over the months, without noticing. Xola's, between, protective. Salty's, enormous, careful. Wendy's beside his. Xav's, dancing. Kei's, in the middle, steady as a map.

He'd watched them all for fifteen months. He knew what all of them would do.

So he knew, at about the hundred and fortieth tile, when the gallery started to fall, which one of them was going to try to stay.

***

It started behind them.

A sound, first. A long low groan in the stone, like the bellows in the drowned forge when they'd first moved. Then a crack, sharp, very loud in the quiet. And Drew turned round, at the back of the line, and saw it.

The tiles behind them were going.

Not the dark ones. All of them. Grey and dark together. One after another, from the bottom of the forty steps, coming down the gallery toward them. Dropping into the black water underneath, one at a time. *Crack. Splash.* *Crack. Splash.* Like a row of dominoes. Getting faster.

"*RUN*," called Hyvred. "*It's coming. All of it. Behind. RUN. Grey only, grey only, GREY*—"

They ran.

Down the Long Gallery under the lake, in a line, on the grey, with Hyvred calling it at the top of her voice. *Grey, grey, left, skip, GREY*. Twenty people running on stone tiles with water a long way under them and the lake above, and behind them the floor falling in. Getting closer.

Drew ran at the back. He watched feet. He watched the floor. He heard the tiles going behind him, *crack, splash*, closer, and closer.

And ahead, at the front, the gallery ended.

Not in a door. In a gap.

A gap in the floor, three tiles wide, where there were no tiles at all. Just black water a long way down. And on the far side of the gap, the floor went on, and a door, black, with a crown on it. And in the middle of the gap, on its own, one tile. Standing up out of the water on a stone pillar like a stepping stone. Grey. The only way across.

Ice reached it first.

He stopped on the edge of the gap. He looked at the single tile on its pillar. Drew watched his feet. They stopped, and planted. The weight went back. And then forward. Onto the edge. Toward the tile.

Drew knew what it meant before Ice did. He'd been watching feet all his life.

***

"*It's a keystone*," called Hyvred. "*The tile. In the middle. It's holding the far side up. If nobody stands on it, the far side drops too. All of it. The door.*" Her voice cracked. "*Somebody has to stand on it. While everybody crosses. And then*—"

She didn't finish. She didn't have to.

*And then the floor behind them comes down the gallery, and reaches the gap, and the keystone goes too. With whoever's on it.*

Twenty-four hours. Locked out. A quarter of a level. And out of the race.

Ice had already stepped toward the edge.

Drew saw his back foot come round. The weight going forward. Ice was going to step onto the keystone and stand on it, with his shield. And everybody would go past him, across, to the far side. And then the tiles would come down the gallery and the keystone would go, and Ice with it. He'd do it without thinking, the way he did everything at the front. Because that was what the front was. Because somebody had to, and it was usually him.

And Kei, in the middle of the line, was opening his mouth. Drew saw it. To say *not always you*. To say *Ice, no*. Kei had said it on the stair in September, and at the long table, and in the netloft. And Ice was going to step onto the tile before Kei got the words out. Because Ice always moved first. Before you'd decided. Before you'd said anything.

Drew moved first.

***

He didn't know he was going to. That was the thing he'd tell Tiger afterward, at two in the morning, in Australia, on a call. He didn't decide it. He'd been watching Ice's feet for fifteen months. He knew exactly when the back foot would come round. He knew the breath it waited.

So for the first time in fifteen months, he went in the breath.

He went past the whole line. Fast, the way Counter went, *Slip*. Low, on the grey, past Xav and Kei and Thea and Gigi and Salty and Hyvred. Past Ice, whose back foot was just coming round. And he stepped off the edge of the gap onto the single tile on its pillar in the middle of the black water, before Ice's foot came down.

The tile held.

Behind him, the far side of the gap shuddered. Groaned. And held. Because somebody was standing on the keystone.

"*Drew*," said Ice.

Drew didn't turn round. He stood on the tile with the black water a long way down on every side, and the lake above, and the lights of Masmark wavering at the very top of everything. He looked at Ice on the edge of the gap, with his foot still in the air.

"You're the front," said Drew. "I'm the edge."

"*Drew*—"

"The edge stays," said Drew. "That's what it's for." He planted his feet on the tile. "Go. *Go.* Everybody. *Now.* It's coming."

It was coming. The tiles behind them going, *crack, splash*, a hundred paces back, eighty, sixty.

Ice looked at him. For one second. Drew saw his face do something he'd never seen it do in fifteen months.

Then Ice went.

He stepped off the edge, past Drew on the keystone. Across the gap, onto the far side. He went without stopping, the way he went without turning his head when Hyvred called. Because somebody had called it, and he trusted the call. On the far side he turned round and stood with his shield up at the edge of the gap, and said, quite loudly, the way he never said things:

"*ACROSS. EVERYBODY. NOW.*"

And they came.

***

They came past Drew on the keystone, one after another, fast. Stepping off the edge onto his tile beside him, and off it onto the far side. Twenty people across one tile in the middle of a lake, with the floor coming down behind them.

Hyvred, first, with her face white. She looked at him as she went past and didn't say anything, and her tail touched his arm.

Thea. Her shield nearly knocked him off. Gigi right behind her, a pace off, and as she stepped past him onto the far side she said, very quietly, "*Good thorn*." He didn't know why. He'd never been thorn. It was the nicest thing anybody had ever said to him.

Xola. Salty, enormous, careful, the tile tipping under the weight of him and Drew holding it level with his feet. Wendy, who said *okay?* and he said *okay*. Xav, dancing. RX, counting the people going past out loud, because he couldn't help it. *Nine. Ten. Eleven.* Malcolm. John. Azac with his shield, who stopped on the tile beside him for a moment and put his hand on Drew's shoulder and said, "*Wall*." And went.

*Fifteen. Sixteen.*

The tiles behind him were thirty paces back. Twenty.

*Seventeen. Eighteen.* Bee, with her wings out, flicking a Shield onto him in grey pencil as she went past, which wouldn't help, and he loved her for it. Xyro, quiet, who looked at him and nodded once, the way Ice nodded.

*Nineteen.*

Kei.

Kei stopped on the keystone. Right beside him. The tiles were ten paces back.

"*Drew*," said Kei.

"Go, Kei."

"You're not—"

"I'm the one who stays," said Drew. "Tiger said. At the gate. In September." He looked at Kei. "*Who'd stand next to you?* Tiger said *Drew*. And I said *the one who stays*." He smiled. He couldn't help it. "I meant Tiger. I always meant Tiger. He keeps the lamps." He looked at the tiles coming. Five paces. "Turns out it was me too."

Kei looked at him for a long, long moment.

Then he went across. And on the far side, he turned round and took out his map. In the middle of the gap, on the white, at the exact place where Drew was standing on his tile, he drew a dot.

***

The last tile went at the edge of the gap behind him.

*Crack. Splash.*

And the keystone went.

He felt it go under his feet. The pillar it stood on giving, the stone tipping. He didn't jump. There was nowhere to jump to. He stood on it as it went, the way he'd stood at the edge of everything for fifteen months, watching.

Across the gap, on the far side, nineteen people stood at the edge and watched him go. He could see their feet. All of them, planted, pointing at him. The same way.

*A team that was going to win stood with their feet pointing the same way.*

Ice was at the front of them. His shield up. His feet planted on the very edge, as close as you could stand without going over. He was looking at Drew.

Drew raised his hand.

Not a wave. Two fingers. To the side of his eyebrow. He'd seen the clip from the ferry, on Ice's stream, in February. A man with a crossbow, and a salute from a game with a black sky.

"*o7*," said Drew.

He saw Ice's face do something. Then the keystone went down into the black water, and Drew went with it.

***

He woke up in his egg in Australia at a quarter past four in the morning, in the dark, with the lid down.

He lay there. He didn't open it. Fifty per cent pain, in a body that didn't hurt. He could still feel the cold of the water. He could still feel the tile going under his feet.

He'd stayed.

After a while he opened the lid and sat up and found his phone on the floor by the egg. There were messages. A lot of them. The guild call, going mad. Kei, one line: *thank you*. Hyvred: *DREW. DREW. YOU IDIOT. THANK YOU.* Thea: a lot of crying faces. Gigi, one word, which he read several times: *good*. Salty: *okay?* Azac: *wall*.

And Ice.

That was all. Two characters. A man saluting.

Drew looked at it for a long time, in the dark, in Australia.

Then he rang Tiger.

He knew Tiger would be up. Tiger was always up when Drew was in the game. He'd say he wasn't, and he was. It was four in the morning in Australia, and Tiger should have been asleep, and wasn't.

Tiger picked up on the first ring.

"I stayed," said Drew.

There was a pause.

"I know," said Tiger. "I watched. On Kei's stream." His voice was odd. "You held it forty seconds. Nineteen of them across."

"Was it enough?"

"They're through," said Tiger. "The door. The third flight. Sixty-six steps. They're in." He paused. "Without you."

"Good," said Drew.

There was a long silence on the line.

"*The one who stays*," said Tiger, at last. Very quietly. "You said that about *me*. At the gate. In September. In front of *everybody*."

"I know."

"And then you went and did it *yourself*."

"I know," said Drew. "I didn't decide it. I just went." He lay back down in the egg with the lid up and the phone against his ear, and looked at the ceiling. "I've been watching feet for fifteen months, Tiger. His. Everybody's. I knew he was going to go. I knew the exact breath. And I thought, *not this time*. Not him. Not always him." He was quiet. "So I went in the breath."

Tiger didn't say anything for a long time. Drew could hear him breathing, a long way off across Australia, in the dark.

"Come home," said Tiger. "When the lockout's done. Come home to the round town."

"I can't. They'll need me in Masmark. After."

"*Not yet*," said Tiger.

Drew laughed. It surprised him. He lay in his egg in the dark at four in the morning, with the cold of a lake still in his bones, and laughed. *Not yet.* Tiger at the first mile on the longest night, a mile out in the dark, keeping the last lamp on the road while everyone shouted at him to come in.

"Not yet," agreed Drew.

"I'll keep the lamps," said Tiger. "You keep the edge."

"Okay."

"Drew."

"Mm."

"You stood still," said Tiger. "On the tile. While it went. You didn't jump. I watched." His voice went very quiet. "You stood still the whole way down."

"There was nowhere to jump to."

"That's not why," said Tiger.

Drew didn't answer. He didn't need to. Tiger had been watching him for fifteen months too.
