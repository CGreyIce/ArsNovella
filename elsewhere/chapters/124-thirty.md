Gabe had said *thirty* on a rise by a headless heron in March, and meant it, and then spent two months finding out exactly how far thirty was.

It was a long way. It was a long way with three people who'd started at one. Rachael had never played a game like this in her life. Calvin couldn't stand still. Dennis thought every fight was a business decision and every business decision was a fight. He loved all three of them. He'd have walked to the end of the world for all three of them. Some days in May he felt like he had.

But by the second week of May they were in the White Hills, and the White Hills were where thirty lived.

***

It was chalk country. Long, smooth, bare green hills going away to the south in waves, with the white showing through wherever the grass was thin. On the sides of the biggest hills, cut into the turf so the white chalk showed underneath, there were figures. Giants. A man with a staff, a hundred yards tall. A horse with too many legs. A woman with her arms up, as if she were holding something over her head. Very old, the locals said. Older than the towns. Somebody had to go up every seven years and scour them, or the grass grew back and they disappeared.

At night they got up.

[ Chalkwalker · Lv 31 ]

Not the big ones on the hills. Smaller ones, man-sized, made of the same white chalk. They came up out of the hillsides after dark, where the turf was thin, and walked the downs in straight lines, very slowly. They didn't care about you unless you got in their way. If you got in their way, they hit you with a chalk fist, and you remembered it.

Gabe got in their way on purpose, every night, for two weeks.

That was the job. Get in the way, Stand In, take the fist, let the hammer heat, swing. Let the three of them hit it from behind while it was looking at him. Over and over, all night, until the sun came up at midnight and the chalkwalkers went back into the hills and lay down.

"You're going to break," said Rachael, on the third night, at two paces, watching his health.

"I'm an Anvil," said Gabe. "I don't break. I get hit and things get a shape."

"That's a *class description*, Gabe. It's not a medical opinion."

"It's been working."

"It's been working because I've been *healing you*." She put a Mend on him without looking, the way she did now. Not where he'd been hit. Where he was going to be. She'd learned that somewhere. He didn't know where. "Stand still."

He stood still. He'd learned that, too.

***

She was always two paces from him.

He'd noticed in April, on the Scalds, after Calvin woke the Kettle. He hadn't said anything. He didn't say anything now. But he noticed it every night. When the chalkwalkers came in their straight lines over the downs, Gabe went forward into their way, and Rachael came with him. Not close. Never close. Rachael didn't do close; everybody who'd ever known her knew that, and nobody tried. Exactly two paces. Where his Stand In could reach her if something came for her. Where she could see his health and his feet and the thing in front of him, all at once.

He'd stood two paces from Kotori for three months. He'd stood two paces from Rachael for two. *Who stands two paces from you?* Calvin had asked him, on a hot ridge in April. *Nobody*, he'd said. *That's the job.*

He'd been wrong. He hadn't known it then. He knew it now. He didn't say so. Some things you didn't say. You just stood still and let them stay true.

***

Calvin went ahead and came back. Every time.

He did it the way other people breathed now. Over every rise on the downs, every night. Look, and come back at a run, and stop two paces off, and say what was there. *Three walkers, straight line, east to west. Don't stand in the middle.* *One big one, asleep in a chalk pit. Don't.* *Nothing. Nothing at all. It's beautiful.* He came back so fast he overshot sometimes and had to come back from the other side. He'd told everybody about that, too.

"I'm not even running ahead any more," he said, on the fourth night, coming back from a rise with his spear over his shoulder. "I'm running *back*. That's the new thing. I go ahead *in order to* come back. Gabe. Gabe, that's different."

"It's different," Gabe agreed.

"I think that's growth."

"Don't push it, Cal," said Rachael.

***

And Dennis kept the purse.

He'd kept it since a clerk with a pen behind each ear wrote *Moonlight Harvest* at the top of a charter in April. In a little notebook from Mistbath, in very neat letters, he wrote down every coin that came in and every coin that went out. Chalkwalker dust sold well; it was good for something in Alchemy. Calvin's hides sold well. Dennis's cut stones, from Excavation and Jewelcrafting, sold best of all, because Dennis wouldn't sell them for less than they were worth. Then he'd talk the buyer into thinking they were worth more.

At the top of the first page of the little notebook, the same line was still there. *Owed to Declan: 150 gold.* And under it, very small: *He says it isn't.*

Gabe saw him look at it every night, before he logged out. Dennis read his inventory every night like a bank statement. Then he'd turn back to the first page of the notebook and look at that line for a long moment, and close it.

"You could just let it go," said Gabe, on the fifth night. "He means it. He doesn't want it back."

"I know he doesn't," said Dennis.

"Then—"

"That's not the point, Gabe." Dennis put the notebook away. "The point isn't what he wants. The point is that I said I would."

Gabe looked at him. Dennis, who'd played the same game with him and Deston for years on a screen, and had always been the one with the money. Who'd always paid his share of everything, exactly, to the cent, and been oddly fierce about it. Gabe had always thought it was about money.

He was starting to think it wasn't.

***

They made thirty on the twenty-sixth of May, at half past one in the morning, on a hilltop under the chalk woman with her arms up.

It wasn't a chalkwalker that did it. It was the thing the chalkwalkers walked away from. Calvin had found it a week ago, asleep in a chalk pit, and come back and said *don't*. For a week they hadn't. Then on Wednesday night Calvin came back from the rise above the pit and said, very calmly for Calvin, "It's awake."

[ The Scourer · Lv 33 · elite ]

It was the thing that came every seven years to scour the giants. Tall and white and bent over, with a long chalk blade like a scythe, for cutting turf. And this year, apparently, nobody had come to do it. So it had come itself.

It came for the chalk woman. Straight up the hill toward her, with the blade.

They got in its way.

Gabe went first, because he was the Anvil. He planted himself on the slope between the Scourer and the chalk woman and put the shield up.

"*Stand In*," he said, and the blade came down.

He felt that. All the way down. At thirty he felt it, which was halfway to being Dec, which had been the whole point. He felt it in his arm and his back and his knees, and he stayed up.

Rachael's Mend was already on him. Before the blade landed. Of course it was.

"*Gild*," said Dennis, and the Scourer looked at Gabe as if he were the only thing on the hill worth cutting. As if he were made of gold. It turned all the way round from the chalk woman to Gabe, and hit him again, and again, and every time it hit him the hammer got hotter.

And Calvin went round it. Ahead, and back. Round its far side in the dark and back again, fast, with the spear, where it wasn't looking, over and over. Coming back to two paces every time, saying *left side's cracked*, *it's slower on the left*, *go left*.

So Gabe went left.

The hammer came round, white-hot from every blow he'd taken. It hit the Scourer on its cracked left side, under the arm, and the whole tall chalk thing staggered sideways down the slope.

"*Now*," said Rachael, at two paces, and put everything she had into him at once.

He swung again.

Nobody said anything for a moment. They stood on the hill under the chalk woman with her arms up, breathing hard. The Scourer lay in pieces on the slope below them, white on the grass.

Then Calvin said "*THIRTY*," at the top of his voice, so loud a chalkwalker three hills away stopped walking for a second.

Dennis sat down on the grass and opened his notebook and wrote something in it, very neatly, and closed it again. He was smiling.

And Rachael, at two paces, looked at Gabe's health bar. Thirty-one per cent and climbing. Then she looked at him.

"You're an idiot," she said.

"I'm an Anvil."

"You're an *idiot*. You stood in front of a thing with a *scythe*." She put another Mend on him, unnecessarily. Her hands weren't quite steady. "Thirty."

"Thirty," said Gabe.

He thought about a rise by a headless heron in March. *Thirty. I'll bring you three more.* He'd said it to Dec, on the way south, alone, with a hammer.

He got his messages up.

*We'll be here.* Gabe looked at that for a while, on a hilltop in the White Hills at two in the morning, with three people he'd walked a long way for, two paces away.

Then he put the hammer on his shoulder.

"North," he said.
