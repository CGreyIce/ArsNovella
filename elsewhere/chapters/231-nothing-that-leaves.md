The fish man had it on the cart at the second dawn, on the ice, between a pike and a heap of small silver things.

Ice saw it from the bottom of the north-west road. You couldn't not see it. Long and curved and single-edged, the steel gone dark with water, the hilt wrapped in black with a thread of silver at the end. Lying on the fish man's ice under the striped awning, among the fish, as if it had been caught.

He stopped.

"Yours?" said the fish man.

"His," said Ice.

The fish man looked at it with his boot face.

"Came up in the nets," he said. "This morning. Off the mouth of the third stream, where it comes out into the lake." He wiped his hands on his apron. "Everything that falls in the lake comes up somewhere. Sooner or later. Three hundred years, I've had swords in my nets. Boots. A bell clapper, once." He looked at Ice. "Never had one that was still warm."

Ice looked at the blade for a long time.

*Nothing that leaves the bridge comes back onto it.*

"Can I take it to him?" he said.

The fish man considered him.

"It's not mine," he said. "It came up in my nets. That makes it the lake's." He picked it up off the ice, carefully, by the hilt, the way he'd pick up anything that might still be sharp. And held it out across the cart. "The lake says take it."

***

Sable Court's Hall was on the north-east rim, above the water. Black stone, like VANTA's, but narrower, taller, with no banner at all. Just a silver thread painted down the door from top to bottom.

He knocked.

The door opened. One of them, in black. Ves, the one who'd said *again, tomorrow, on the bridge*. She looked at him. Then at the blade in his hands. Her face did something.

"Lucan," she said, over her shoulder. Not loud.

He came. Moving, the way he always moved. Down a stair at the back of the Hall, through a room full of people in black who all stopped what they were doing. To the door.

And stopped.

He looked at the blade. He looked at it for a long time.

"It went in the stream," said Lucan.

"It came out in the lake," said Ice. "The fish man had it. On his ice. Between a pike and some small silver things."

Lucan stared at him.

"*Nothing that leaves the bridge comes back onto it*," he said.

"This isn't the bridge," said Ice.

He held it out. Hilt first, across his forearm, the way you'd give somebody a sword you'd taken off them, if you were giving it back properly.

Lucan didn't take it.

He stood in the door of Sable Court's Hall with twenty people in black behind him, all perfectly still. And looked at the tank holding his sword out to him. And he started to laugh. Not the short surprised one. Longer. With his hand over his face.

"You walked it here," he said, through his hand.

"Yes."

"Across the city. From the north-west harbour. On a Sunday."

"It's a nice walk," said Ice.

Lucan took his hand away from his face. He took the sword. He held it in both hands, the dark wet steel, the black hilt with the silver thread, and looked at it.

"Thank you," he said.

"Thank the lake," said Ice. "The fish man says it's the lake's."

***

Lucan walked him back to the bridge. He didn't have to. He did.

They walked along the north-east rim and round onto the north terrace, the two of them, in the morning. Not saying much. People stopped when they saw them. Of course they did. Half of Masmark had been on this terrace twelve hours ago, watching them fight. Now they were walking along it side by side, the tank with his shield on his back and the river beside him with his sword. Talking about the weather.

Lucan didn't stop moving. He never would. But Ice noticed he moved less. A little.

They stopped at the west end of the bridge over the third stream. The water going under it white and loud, the way it always had. Nobody on it. The old stone, worn in the middle.

"The river," said Lucan.

Ice looked at him.

"At the start," said Lucan. "You moved like me. A breath ahead of me. For seven minutes." He was looking at the bridge, at the middle of it, where they'd stood. "Where did you learn that?"

Ice thought about it.

He'd told Vane on the harbour wall, at the third dawn. *He doesn't know that.* And Vane had said *he'll work it out*. Lucan was very good. He'd have worked it out, eventually.

But he'd asked.

"Here," said Ice. "On this bridge. In July." He nodded at the north terrace above them. "Every night, on the way back from the Undercliffs. I stopped up there and watched you train. Twenty people doing the cellar at you. And you going round it." He paused. "I learned it off you. While you were learning the rain off your own."

Lucan went completely still.

Not a little. All of him. On the end of the bridge, with his sword in his hand, the way he'd gone still on this bridge in March when he heard *Kei says*.

"*Off me*," he said.

"Yes."

"You learned the river off *me*. In a *month*. From a *terrace*."

"You're very good at it," said Ice. "It was easy to see."

Lucan looked at him for a very long time.

"Nobody's ever learned anything off me," he said. Very quietly. "Not in the wind game. Not here. I've been the best at it for years and nobody's ever—" He stopped. "They watch. They can't do it. They say *he's a river* and they go round me, or they try to stand still." He looked at Ice. "Nobody's ever done it back."

"I borrow things," said Ice.

***

Lucan laughed again. And then he didn't. He looked out at the lake, at the mouth of the third stream where it went out into the water and the fish man's nets had found his sword.

"I'll ask again," he said.

"I know."

"Not soon. Next year. I need—" He shook his head. "I need to learn the slow one. And I can't. I've got the wrong arms. I tried this morning, in the Hall, with Ves. I fell over." He was almost laughing again. "I need to learn how to be early for something that doesn't care if I'm early."

"That's hard."

"It's the hardest thing I've ever heard of," said Lucan. "I'm going to love it." He looked at Ice. "Next year. On this bridge. Properly."

"Ask me properly," said Ice. "Next year."

"I will," said Lucan. "You know I will."

He held out his hand. Palm up, the way Ice had held out his on the bridge, last night, at six per cent, with a sword gone into the stream.

Ice took it.

***

They went home on Monday.

Not all of them. The netloft's eight stayed, the way they had in April: Riverbank, keeping the room, with the second banner on its hooks and the red cloth on the second hook beside the rope. Xav at the long table with Val's new page in front of him. The bridge, start to finish, twenty-seven minutes in dashes and dots, with three different kinds of mark on it now. Xav had been looking at it since Saturday night.

"September," said Xav, at the door, when Ice came to say goodbye. "Wherever you are. I'll walk."

"The round town's a long way."

"I walked it before," said Xav. "In the first winter. With the eight." He looked at the page in his hand. "I can walk it again to lose to you."

"You might not."

Xav looked up.

"You might not lose," said Ice. "You've got three to learn now. And a page." He looked at the page, at the three kinds of mark. "That's more than Lucan had."

Xav's face did the thing it did, between being hit and being given a present. He didn't say anything. He put the page inside his coat, flat, against his chest. The way Suzu carried the jar.

***

Wendy gave the netloft door to Rafi.

She did it the way she'd given the round town's gate to Kite in February. Ice wasn't there for that; he'd been fourteen days down the south road with a goose. But he'd heard. *And then you sit on the steps with them after. That's the bit people forget.*

She stood in the netloft door with her pack on, on Monday at the first dawn, with Salty behind her with Right Size on his back. And Rafi in front of her in the lane with his big black sword. And she took her hands out of her pockets.

"Who'd stand next to you?" said Wendy.

Rafi thought about it. He always thought about things. He wanted to be fair.

"Everybody who comes to the door," he said.

Wendy looked at him for a long time.

"That's the job," she said.

She stepped out of the doorway, into the lane. And Rafi stepped into it. Into the netloft's one door, the one that stuck, at the end of a lane off the north-west harbour of the biggest city in the world. And put his hands at his sides, where people could see them.

"Stand in it," said Wendy. "Not beside it. In it. Hands where they can see them. Ask everybody. Whatever they say, say *fair*." She looked at him. "And then sit on the step with them after."

"The bit people forget," said Rafi.

Wendy's face did something.

"Who told you that?"

"Kite," said Rafi. "In a letter. In May. He said you told him. He said it's the most important bit and nobody ever remembers." He looked down the lane at the harbour. "I'll remember."

Wendy hugged him. Hard. Rafi went red to the ears and stood there in the doorway being hugged with his hands still at his sides, where people could see them.

"Boss," he said, over Wendy's shoulder, to Ice. "I'm a door."

"You're a good door," said Ice.

***

Fourteen days, back up the road they'd come down in April.

He walked at the front. Of course he did. Kei beside him with his map, the new sheet with Masmark on it and the north-west harbour and the Undercut in pencil. Hyvred on his other side, close. Behind them, the rest: Higu, Gigi, Thea, Xola, Xyro, Slep, Bee, Hako. Salty and Wendy at the back, the two of them, not quite holding hands.

He hit fifty-seven on the third day, on the long rise out of the crater, fighting nothing very much.

[ Level up · Lv 57 ]

It came for the bridge, he thought. The game did that sometimes. Gave you the level for a thing a few days after the thing, when you'd stopped expecting it. He looked at the window for a while, walking.

"Fifty-seven," said Hyvred.

"Fifty-seven."

"It's for the bridge."

"I think so."

"It's late," said Hyvred.

"It's allowed," said Ice.

She hit him.

***

On the fifth day, the goose.

It was standing in the middle of the road where it always stood, on the long straight stretch past the ferry, where the road went between two fields with nothing in them but goose. The same one. Or one exactly like it. Looking at all thirteen of them coming toward it with deep suspicion.

Kei stopped. He looked at his map. He'd drawn the goose on it in February, on the way down, a small round shape on the road with one word beside it: *don't*.

"*Don't*," said Kei.

"I'm not going to," said Ice.

He walked round it. He'd walked round it in January, on his own, the first time, and Tif had clipped it and it had gone everywhere. *HE WENT DARK AND IT DIDN'T MATTER.* He'd walked round it again in April with the ten. He walked round it now. Off the road, into the field, a wide slow curve, not too close. The goose turned its head to watch him go.

Hyvred walked round it with him.

Not behind him. Beside him. The same curve, the same distance, her hand in his. The goose watched them both. Then, very slowly, with enormous dignity, it sat down in the middle of the road.

"It *sat down*," said Bee, behind them.

"It's never sat down," said Kei, writing on his map.

"It's never seen two," said Hyvred.

***

They came round the curve into the round town on Monday the fourteenth of August, at the second dusk.

The south road, round the last bend, and there it was. Carrow Cross. The round market and the fountain and the Hall of Roads with its dome, the eye at the top gold in the last of the light. Seven roads going out of it like the spokes of a wheel. And the whole round town on the steps, the way it always was for anybody coming home.

Moonlight Harvest by the fountain. Gabe at the front with his shield and his enormous face, already crying. Deston beside him with Chernn, and on Chernn's hand, in the game, nothing. Ice noticed that. Nothing on her hand in here. Because the ring was out there, on her real one, and in here was where they'd met. Where there'd been a candle.

Suzu on the steps near the top, out of the way. Kotori beside him with a pot. Hana's five by the fountain. Tiger and Maigo and Drew. Niko's four, Chris with his window open, counting. Ina at the forge door with soot on her hands. Everybody.

Kite in the gate, with his hands at his sides.

And at the bottom of the south road, at the milestone with *CARROW CROSS* on it and seven roads cut into the top, a woman sitting on the base of it with two short curved blades across her knees. And the bird cut off her collar, badly. Watching them come.

Ice stopped at the milestone.

She looked up at him.

He'd put her on the ground at a stone cross on the moor, the February before last, and let her get up. *Not today, then.* She'd said that. And *we'll have your day another day, swamp guy*, in the fen, the first time.

She'd been sitting at this milestone since May. Fourteen weeks. Every dusk.

"Not today," said Ice.

Ash looked at him for a long time.

Then something happened to her face that Kite would describe to him later, in a letter of four lines. He'd never once seen it happen, Kite wrote, in all the first winter.

"Not today," said Ash.

***

He went up the steps.

Kite was in the gate. He'd been in it all day, Suzu said later. Since the first dawn, when Kei's letter came saying *Monday*. He stood there now with his hands at his sides and looked at Ice at the bottom of the steps, with Hyvred beside him and eleven behind them, dusty, slow, from fourteen days on the road.

"Who'd stand next to you?" said Kite.

Ice looked at him.

He thought about a bridge. About a terrace, and a harbour wall at the third dawn, and Vane saying *I was next to you*. About a cellar, and a ladder, and Salty saying *your hips are wrong*. About a doorway at the end of a long corridor, and a cup put down without looking.

He thought about the war. Twenty people at the front, and nobody beside, and never once turning round.

He turned round.

On the bottom step of the Hall of Roads, with the whole round town above him. He turned all the way round and looked back down the south road. At the eleven behind him, dusty from fourteen days. At Kei with his map and Bee with her pencils and Salty and Wendy not quite holding hands. At the milestone at the bottom of the road, with a woman sitting on the base of it who'd been waiting fourteen weeks to find out what it was.

At Hyvred, beside him.

He turned back.

"Behind me," said Ice. "And beside me. And in front." He looked at Kite. "All of them."

Kite looked at him for a long time.

"That's three answers," said Kite.

"I know," said Ice. "I've got three now."

Kite's narrow face did something. He looked down, very fast, at his own feet. And then up again.

"Fair," he said.

***

Kei went down to the milestone afterward, at the third dawn, with two cups. Ice watched from the steps. He didn't go down. It wasn't his to say.

Kei gave her a cup. He sat down on the cobbles beside the milestone, a sword's length off. The way Kite had, fourteen weeks ago. And they talked for a long time. Ice couldn't hear what they said. He didn't try.

At the end, Kei got up. He said something short. Ash looked up at him and nodded once.

Kei came back up the steps past Ice with the empty cups.

"Well?" said Ice.

"Not yet," said Kei. "She asked properly. I said not today. Nobody signs on the day they come in." He looked back down the road at the milestone. "I said ask me again on the longest night. Here. In the round town. With everybody." He paused. "If she still wants to, after that, she'll know what it is."

"What did she say?"

"She said *I'll be here*," said Kei.

"Will she?"

Kei looked at him.

"She's been here fourteen weeks," he said. "Every dusk." He went on up the steps with the cups. "Hm."

***

He went in at last, through the Hall, to the banner room.

It was full. Sixty-one chairs, and most of them sat in. And the first of the nine, at Moonlight Harvest's end, empty, waiting. With a cup already on the table in front of it, still steaming, because Kotori had seen him coming up the road.

At the far end, by the north door, Isa Corrie was awake in her chair. With the jar on the table in front of her, glowing through the clay, and a row of Chernn's candles lit beside it. She looked at him come in. Her bright black eyes.

"You came back," she said.

"Yes."

"With the girl with the horns."

"Yes."

Isa looked at him for a long time.

"You're different," she said.

"I turned round," said Ice.

Isa Corrie folded her root hands on her blanket and looked at him. Then at Hyvred, beside him. Then at Suzu, on the stool beside her chair, where he'd sat every afternoon all summer with the jar.

"*Hm*," said Isa.

He sat down in the first of the nine. He put his feet on the rung of the next chair, the way he always did. Hyvred sat down beside him, in the chair that had been Bee's. He held out his hand, without looking, for the cup.

Kotori put it in it.

At the far end of the room, Ama Corrie looked up from the window seat, where she'd been sitting with her broom across her knees. Waiting, Ice thought. For whoever came in last.

"You're slow," said Ama Corrie.

He looked at her.

She'd never said it. Not once, in a year and a half. *Mind the third step. Again. Hm.* She'd never once said *you're slow*.

"I was up a bridge," said Ice.

"I heard," said Ama Corrie. "Everybody heard." She got up off the window seat with her broom. "Sit still. It's Monday. I'll do the chairs anyway."

And she started down the long table. Under, not round.
