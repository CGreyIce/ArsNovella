The first time Hyv said *mine* out loud, at the old hall in January, she'd thought she was going to be sick.

Nobody knew that. She hadn't told anybody. Not Xola, who'd screamed. Not Thea, who'd put her hands over her face. Not the swamp guy, who'd gone pink from the collar up and said *you're going to be like this now*. She'd said *I've been like this for ages. Inside. Now I'm going to be like it outside. Get used to it.* Very loudly, red to the horns, with her chin up.

And then she'd taken his arm with both hands. That way nobody would see them shaking.

She'd been loud in fights her whole life. *LEFT. THE LEFT ONE'S AWAKE.* Up a stair into Rook's Hall. That was different. That was calling. Calling wasn't about you. It was about where things were. You saw everything and you said where it was, and everybody went where you said, and nobody looked at *you*.

*Mine* was about her.

It had been about her every single time since. At the long table. On the stair. In the round market, across forty people, *come here*. On the stream, into his eyes, three hundred thousand people. On the green pole, under the roof, in front of everybody. Every time she'd been sure, for about a breath, that somebody was going to laugh. Or look at her funny. Or say *it's silly*. Or take it.

Nobody had.

Not once. In three months. Not one single person had laughed at her for being loud about him. They'd screamed, mostly. Gabe had screamed into a pillow out there and the auntie next door had knocked on the wall. Ama Corrie had said *hm* about nine times, in nine different ways. Leaka had cried. But nobody had laughed. Nobody had said *silly*. Nobody had taken anything.

She'd stopped waiting for it, somewhere in March. She didn't know when. She'd just noticed one day at the long table, with her chin on her hand, saying *you're very boring and I love it* across a plate of Maigo's noodles. Her hands weren't shaking.

They hadn't been for weeks.

***

On the Tuesday she was at the forge with Ina, fletching.

Ina didn't fletch. Ina made arrowheads, at Old Joss's anvil, in black iron that didn't shine. Hyv fletched them, at the bench by the window, with goose feathers from Tallow Green that the tallow man had brought in a bag on the cart, *from the goose, it doesn't need them*. They'd worked like that every Tuesday since March. Ina at the anvil, Hyv at the bench, not talking much.

The swamp guy came past the window at the second dusk. Going somewhere. He didn't look in.

"*Come here*," said Hyv. Not loudly. Through the open window.

He stopped. He turned round. He came in.

He stood in the doorway of the forge with his shield on his back, looking at her, waiting to be told why.

"Nothing," said Hyv. "I just wanted you to come here." She held up an arrow. "Look. Goose."

He looked at the arrow. He looked at her.

"It's a good arrow," he said.

"I *know*."

He went pink and went out again. Hyv went back to fletching, grinning.

At the anvil, Ina had stopped hammering. He was looking at her through the smoke with his oni mask pushed up on top of his head.

"You've got him trained," said Ina.

"I haven't *trained* him," said Hyv. "He comes because he wants to."

Ina thought about that for a long time with the hammer in his hand.

"That's worse," he said. And went back to the star he was bending.

***

The letter came on the last Saturday in April, at the second dusk, folded very small, the way Sable Court's letters always were. Black wax. A silver thread through it.

The swamp guy read it at the long table. She read it over his shoulder with her chin on it. She did that now. Every letter. He'd stopped noticing. He'd once asked her, at the start, *do you want to read it first?* and she'd said *no, I want to read it with you*. And he'd gone pink and said *okay*. Now he just held it a bit lower so her chin fit.

[ Letter · from Lucan (Sable Court)
Tank.
I can do the slow one now. Badly.
I fell over about four hundred times. Ves counted.
July. The bridge. Properly.
I'm asking. This is me asking.
She can watch.
— L. ]

Hyv read it twice.

She read the last line three times.

*She can watch.*

***

She knew what it meant. She'd known since the shingle under Masmark's lake, a year ago, at the bottom of the Crown Below. Drew and Lucan had sat on the shingle all night, after. And Lucan had looked across it at the swamp guy and said *tank, who'd stand next to you?*

And the swamp guy had said *Hyv*.

One word. A year ago. Before the hill. Before Singapore. Before the old hall and *mine*. Before he'd ever once said *we're together* out loud. He'd said it to the best swordsman in Masmark, on a beach at the bottom of a lake, in front of everybody. *Hyv*. As though it were the most obvious thing in the world.

And Lucan had said *then she can watch. When I ask you. On the bridge. Properly. When you're ready.*

She'd been on the far side of the shingle with Thea. She'd heard it. She'd gone so red that Thea had asked if she was ill. She'd said *no* and run off along the beach and stood in the lake up to her knees for ten minutes.

She'd never told him she'd heard.

"*She can watch*," said Hyv, now, into his shoulder.

"Yes," said the swamp guy.

"He means me."

"Yes."

"He remembered," said Hyv. "From the shingle. A year ago." She'd gone hot all over, the way she used to, the old way, before the old hall. "You said *Hyv*. On the shingle. When he asked who'd stand next to you."

The swamp guy turned his head and looked at her.

"You heard that," he said.

"I heard *everything*," said Hyv. "I always hear everything. I see everything. I heard you say *Hyv* to Lucan of Sable Court on a beach at the bottom of a lake a *year ago*. And I ran into the *lake*." She was laughing and her face was burning. "Up to my *knees*. Thea thought I was *ill*."

He looked at her for a long time.

"You never said," said the swamp guy.

"I was *quiet* then," said Hyv. "I was quiet about everything." She put her chin back on his shoulder, on the letter. "I'm not quiet now."

***

She wasn't.

She stood up.

She hadn't decided to. She just found she was standing, at the long table, at the second dusk, with Lucan's letter in one hand and the other on the swamp guy's shoulder. And sixty-one people and a sheep looking at her, because when Hyv stood up now, something was about to happen.

"*Everybody*," said Hyv.

The banner room went quiet.

"*He's fighting Lucan*," said Hyv. Loudly. To the long table. "*In July. On the bridge in Masmark. Properly. Lucan asked. This is Lucan asking.*" She held up the letter. "*And I'm going to WATCH*."

Gabe screamed. Then stopped, and looked at Rae, and went pink, and sat on his hands.

"*It says so*," said Hyv. "*It says SHE CAN WATCH. That's ME. He said HYV on a BEACH a YEAR AGO when Lucan asked who'd stand next to him and I ran into a LAKE*—"

The long table went off. All of it.

Xola was screaming *I KNEW IT, I KNEW SHE RAN INTO THE LAKE FOR A REASON*. Thea had her face in her hands, shaking. Leaka was standing on her chair. Drew, at the far end, who'd been on the shingle that night with Lucan, put his hand over his eyes and said *oh no* very quietly. Ama Corrie at the banner room door said *hm* so loudly it was nearly a shout.

And the swamp guy sat at the long table with his ears gone pink, looking up at her. With an expression she'd only ever seen on him once before. On a bench under a tree in Singapore, when she'd pushed him back against it and gone first.

"*And I'm going to SCREAM*," said Hyv. "*On the bridge. The whole time. Every hit. Lucan's going to have to fight him with me SCREAMING*."

"He knows," said the swamp guy. Quietly, from his chair.

"*How*?"

"It says *she can watch*," said the swamp guy. "He's heard you."

Hyv stared down at him.

Then she sat down on his knee. At the long table, in front of everybody, the way she sat on the north parapet at the eye. With her arm round his neck and her tail round his ankle and Lucan's letter crumpled in her other hand.

"*Mine*," said Hyv. Into his ear. Not loudly. Just for him.

Her hands weren't shaking at all.

***

The window came while she was sitting there.

Not for her. For all of them. It came up in front of every face at the long table at once. Sixty-one people and a sheep looking at the air in front of them, the way travellers did. Ama Corrie said she'd stopped wondering what at in the first month.

[ Constellation · Lv 61 ]

[ *For a stone, a roof, and four hundred and thirty-one windows.* ]

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"Sixty-one," said Kei, at the head of the table. He looked at it. He didn't have his map out, for once. "We were fifty-seven at Christmas."

"Four levels," said Salty. "For a *stone*?"

"For the spring," said Kei. "All of it." He looked down the long table. "The stone. Jared's roof. Chernn's windows. The lion that didn't go in the fountain and then did." He paused. "The game counts things. It counted those."

"It counted the *lion*?" said Leaka.

"It says *a roof*," said Curse, beside her. "It doesn't say lion." He looked at the window. "I think the lion's in it, though. Somewhere."

Hyv looked at the window from the swamp guy's knee. *For a stone, a roof, and four hundred and thirty-one windows.* The game hadn't counted a single fight. Not the fell-hounds at the sixty-first stone. Not the Delvers, or the draw in the Seven Below. Not Lucan's bridge, which hadn't happened yet. It had counted a stone in the water, and lanterns, and candles in windows.

The quiet things.

"*Hm*," said Ama Corrie, at the door. And went off to sweep something.

***

Thea caught her on the way out of the banner room. Just in the doorway, with Gigi a step behind her, close.

"I knew," said Thea.

Hyv looked at her.

"On the shingle," said Thea. "A year ago. You said *no, I'm not ill* and ran off down the beach and stood in the lake up to your knees for ten minutes. You were red to the horns. I could see it from the shingle, in the dark." She almost smiled. "I'd heard him too. *Hyv*. To Lucan. I was right there."

"You never *said*."

"You were in the lake," said Thea. "And then you came out and sat on the far end of the shingle with your knees up and your tail round your feet. And didn't say anything to anybody for the rest of the night." She looked at Hyv. "You'd have died. If I'd said."

Hyv thought about it.

"I'd have died," she agreed.

"So I didn't," said Thea. "I waited. Somebody told me that. You wait till nobody else can, and then you say the thing." She glanced back at Gigi. "Slep. In the game. At a fire."

"You waited a *year*."

"I waited till you said it," said Thea. "At the long table. Standing up. Loud." She put her hand on Hyv's arm for a moment. Not the way Hyv held the swamp guy's. The way you'd touch the arm of a friend you'd known a long time and watched get braver. "I've known him a very long time, Hyv. Since before any of this. I've watched him be quiet about himself for years. Everybody has." She was quiet. "Nobody's ever been loud about him. Not like you. Not ever."

"Gabe," said Hyv.

"Gabe's loud *for* him," said Thea. "That's different. Gabe's been holding a door." She took her hand back. "You're loud *about* him. Like he's a thing worth saying out loud. He's never once heard anybody do that."

Hyv didn't say anything. She couldn't.

"Thank you," said Thea. Quietly. And went back into the banner room with Gigi, close. Hyv stood in the doorway of the banner room for a while on her own before she went to find him.

***

At the end of the dusk, when the long table had stopped screaming, she took him up to the eye.

Up the two hundred and twelve, holding his arm, backwards, counting. At the top, Isa was on the *Somewhere* bench with Kei's lamp, under the grey blanket, the way she was every dusk.

Isa stopped her on the way past. Not with words. With her hand, out of the blanket, held up a little. Hyv stopped.

"You're the one who sees everything," said Isa.

"Yes."

"And you've gone loud." Isa was looking at her face, close, the way she had to. "Ama Corrie says. She says you stood on the long table." A pause. "On it, she said. Not at it."

"I stood *up*," said Hyv. "At it. I didn't stand on it. Leaka stood on her *chair*."

"*Hm*," said Isa. She looked north for a moment, at the gold point, past both of them. "My mother used to say the lamp was the loudest thing on the hill. It had been saying *here* for three hundred years. Every night. All night. To anybody who'd look." She looked back at Hyv. "Loud's just a lamp that says where something is. So people can find it."

Hyv didn't say anything.

"You see everything," said Isa. "You always have. And now you say where it is." She put her hand back in the blanket. "Good. Somebody who sees everything should say so."

The swamp guy, beside Hyv, holding her hand, said nothing. But she felt his fingers go tighter.

"Go on, then," said Isa, and looked north again.

They went round to the north side, where there was only the parapet, looking up the road.

She didn't sit on his knee. Not this time. She sat beside him. On the parapet, side by side, the way the man who carried water had sat with Isa's mother's grandmother across a stride of black water. Deston had told her. Next to.

The gold point at the bottom of the north. The hill, with three hundred and one stones.

"I was so frightened," said Hyv. "At the old hall. The first time. Saying *mine*."

The swamp guy looked at her.

"I nearly threw up," said Hyv. "I held your arm so nobody could see my hands shaking." She looked north. "Every time, for weeks. I thought somebody would laugh. Or say it was silly. Or take it." She was quiet. "That's what happens. When you're loud about something you love. Somebody takes it."

"Nobody took it," said the swamp guy.

"Nobody took it," said Hyv. "Three months. Nobody laughed. They screamed. Gabe screamed so much the *auntie next door* knocked. But nobody laughed." She put her hand on the parapet between them. "My hands stopped shaking in March. I didn't notice when."

He put his hand on top of hers.

"You said it was allowed," said Hyv. "In February. Here. You said *it's allowed, be loud*. Nobody had ever told me." She turned her hand over under his and held it. "And you were right. And I've been loud for three months. And nothing bad happened. Not once."

"I know," said the swamp guy.

"So I want you to," said Hyv.

He went still.

"Once," said Hyv. "Be loud. About me. In front of everybody." She looked at him, edge to edge, close. "You go first in fights and last at everything else. You went first once, in February. On my forehead. I couldn't speak for an hour." She was grinning. "I want you to go first again. Loud. Once."

He looked at her for a long time. She could see him thinking about it. Properly. The way he did. As if she'd asked him something hard and simple.

"Once," said the swamp guy.

"When?"

"When it's the right time."

"It's *always* the right time," said Hyv.

"Then soon," said the swamp guy.

***

He didn't do it that night. She hadn't thought he would. He'd said *soon*. And he was slow; she'd known that for two years. He'd walked round a goose, once, rather than make it move. He'd stood in a gate when she said *me* and told her he'd known since the step, and she'd hit him for it. *That's for knowing.*

She didn't mind. That was the strange thing. A year ago she'd have minded so much she'd have run into a lake.

She sat beside him on the north parapet at the eye with his hand in hers and looked at the gold point at the bottom of the sky. She thought about a bridge in Masmark in July, and a man who'd learned the slow one by falling over four hundred times. About standing at the end of the bridge and watching. And screaming. Loudly, the whole time, every hit, in front of half a city. Because she was allowed.

At a quarter to eight, on the parapet, they said it with everybody. *Set one to watch. So the rest may sleep.* Hers came out louder than his. It always did now.

And then, right before the egg, the swamp guy leaned over and said in her ear, very quietly, so nobody else would hear:

"*Mine*."

Just once. Not loud. Just for her.

She went red to the horns. She couldn't help it. She didn't say anything. She couldn't.

That wasn't the loud one, she knew. That was the practice.
