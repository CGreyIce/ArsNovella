Hyv saw everything, and for three weeks she'd been very carefully not looking at one thing.

She'd seen the board in the banner room in June. *The notch · the second dawn out · early*, in pencil, at the bottom, in Kei's hand. She'd seen the swamp guy at the forge with Ina, from across the market, and Ina's face. She'd seen Hana's face at the long table go white and then red. She'd seen Milli turn round on the stair. She'd seen him at the back of the walk every afternoon for fourteen days with PenguChi, carrying something he didn't know how to set down. She'd seen his hand go to his coat on the bridge, after, at thirty-four per cent, and stop there.

She'd seen Gabe's face all day yesterday. Like a man who'd swallowed a lantern.

She'd seen all of it. She'd decided not to look.

*Is that allowed?* she'd asked PenguChi, at the back of the walk, on the twelfth night. *To see something. And not look at it. So somebody else can show you.*

*Thea said everything's allowed*, PenguChi had said.

So she hadn't looked. For three weeks. The hardest thing she'd ever done, harder than being loud. She'd sat on his knee at the long table and on the north parapet at the eye and on a wall on the crater road. She'd held his arm and looked at everything in the world except the one thing.

He woke her in the dark before the dawn with one word.

"Hyv."

She opened her eyes.

***

He was crouched beside her by the fire. The camp was asleep round them, fifty-seven people under coats on the flat place below the rim. Suzu asleep sitting up against a rock. Kotori under a blanket. The fire low.

Behind him, the road went up the last switchbacks to the notch. The sky over the rim was just starting to go grey.

She looked at him. At his face, in the last of the firelight.

It wasn't doing nothing, very carefully. It wasn't doing the small sudden thing. It was doing what it had done on the bridge at fifty minutes, after the *BOOM*, in the drawing Xav had given her. The one in her coat, against her chest. Just there. Looking at her. Not knowing.

She didn't say anything. She got up.

He held out his hand.

Not his arm. His hand. She'd held his arm with both hands for seven months, since the old hall in January, everywhere, on every stair and road and bench. So nobody would see her hands shake, at first. And then because she wanted to. She'd never once just held his hand to walk somewhere.

She took it.

They went up the road.

***

At the bottom of the last switchbacks, where the road left the camp, PenguChi was sitting on a rock with her spear across her knees and Patience lying beside her. Facing up the road. At the back of everybody.

She didn't look at them as they went past. She didn't say anything. She just sat there, the way she sat at the back of everything, so that nobody could come up the road behind them without going past her first.

Hyv looked at the back of PenguChi's head as they went past, and nearly cried, and didn't.

Up the switchbacks. Steep, at the end, the way the last flight of the two hundred and twelve was steep. Sixty-six steps, the hardest ones, with the wind. He went first. Of course he did. Holding her hand behind him, without looking back, at exactly the height her hand would be.

She didn't need to see where he'd step. She knew. She'd always known where he'd be.

That was the thing she'd never told him. She could call everybody, because she saw where they'd be. She could call him best of all. On the hill, round the inside of the ring, a breath ahead of every gap. In the north tunnel, on the stair, on the road. She'd always known exactly where he'd be.

Except now. Up this road, in the dark, to the notch. She didn't know what he'd do there.

She'd decided not to.

***

The notch.

Two black walls of rock either side of the road, open to the sky. A gap at the top of the crater rim, wide enough for about four people to stand in it if they stood close. And in the middle of it, sitting, facing west, the way it had sat for two weeks: the goose.

It turned its head and looked at them as they came up the last of the road into the notch. Grey and white and large, with an expression of permanent enormous offence.

It didn't get up.

The swamp guy walked past it, round it, the way he always walked round geese. To the east side of the notch, where the road went over the top of the rim and down into the crater. He stopped there. He turned round, with his back to it, and looked at her.

And behind him, below, a long way below, was Masmark.

***

She'd seen it at dusk. Coming in, two weeks ago, from the other side. The lake gold in the evening and a thousand lights coming on round it.

She'd never seen it at dawn.

The lake was dark. Flat and dark and enormous, the size of a country, going away east from the bottom of the crater wall to the edge of the world. Round it, all the way round, the ring of the city, still lit. Thousands of small lights, the night's lights, still burning. And down the inside of the crater wall from notches like this one, from every direction, dozens of roads, lit. All of them coming down to the same place.

In the middle of the lake, small, the island. And the tower.

And at the far edge of the world, at the far side of the lake, the sky had gone from grey to gold.

The sun came up.

She watched it come. She couldn't not. It came up over the far edge of the lake, a long way off, and the light came across the water toward them. Not all at once. In a line. A long gold edge sliding across the dark lake from the far side, the way the tide went out at Saltreach, a pace at a time. The top of the island's tower caught it first. Gold, very small, in the middle of the dark. Then the island. Then the water round it.

And as the gold came across the lake, round the shore, the city's lights went out. One by one. Not because anybody put them out. Because the day had come and they weren't needed any more. Thousands of them, going out in a ring round the lake as the sun came across it. As if the whole of Masmark were blowing out its candles because the morning had arrived.

The roads down the crater wall went gold, one after another.

The light came up the crater wall below them. Up the switchbacks they'd come down two weeks ago and up last night. Up the last of the road. Into the notch.

It reached the swamp guy's back, standing at the east edge of the notch with Masmark going gold behind him. It went round him and lit the two black walls. And the goose, sitting in the middle. And her.

She stood in the notch in the first sun with the whole of Masmark going gold below, and the man she loved standing in front of it, looking at her. And she knew it was the most beautiful thing she'd ever seen. She saw everything. She'd seen a lot.

He'd known it would be.

He'd come here alone, a year and a half ago, at dusk, at the end of fourteen days. And looked at it, and thought, *I'll come back here*.

***

He took something out of his coat.

Not the small thing. Something else, first. Long and thin. An arrow.

She knew it before she saw it properly. Two grey feathers and one white. Winter had re-fletched it a long time ago with a white feather off the moor, *so he can find it in the quiver, in the dark*. Niko had breathed something into it, a breath of a cold place at the end of a tunnel. *Hold it up and it'll lean.* *Which way?* *Home.* And she'd held it out to him on her palm at a gate in the round town on the morning he left for Masmark. *So you know which way.*

He'd stood here with it. In this notch, a year and a half ago, at the end of fourteen days, alone. He'd held it up on his palm and it had leaned. Back. North and west, over his shoulder, the way he'd come. To a cold place at the end of a tunnel. Where she'd sat four paces off every morning, then two.

He held it up on his palm now.

In the notch, in the first sun, with Masmark behind him.

It turned.

Slowly. On his palm, the way a compass needle turns. Not north and west. Not back over his shoulder the way they'd come, to the round town, to the tunnel, to the cold place.

It turned until it was pointing at her. A foot away. And stopped.

[ *Remembers a place* ]

[ *Home.* ]

Hyv looked at it for a long time. At an arrow on his palm, pointing at her.

"It used to point at the tunnel," said the swamp guy. Very quietly. "When I stood here before. It leaned back the way I'd come. To where you were." He was looking at it. "It doesn't lean back now."

"No," said Hyv. Her voice didn't work properly.

"It leans home," said the swamp guy. "Niko said. Home was the tunnel. Where you sat." He looked up from the arrow, at her. "It's not a tunnel any more."

***

He put the arrow away. Back in his coat, carefully.

Then he took out the small thing.

Wrapped in a square of red paper. Curse's. She knew it; there was a fold in the corner where Curse folded all of them. He unwrapped it on his palm, where the arrow had been.

A ring.

Black. Not shining. Iron, the dark's own, out of the fourth tunnel, quenched in the dark stream the way Right Size had been. She knew whose hands had made it. She'd seen Ina's face through the forge door in June. And set into it, small, a gold stone. Not yellow; gold. Cut with a lot of small faces, so that wherever she looked at it from, it caught the light from somewhere else. The sun coming over the lake. The gold on the black walls of the notch. And at the back of it, deep in, a light of its own, like the light at the top of the green pole in the round market, that Hana had cut.

[ *a ring* · *black iron, quenched in the dark* · *a stone that sees from wherever it is put* ]

[ *Made by: Inaxho · Hanarisu* ]

[ *Carried by: Ice* ]

She'd seen everything. She'd seen Ina's face, and Hana's, and Gabe's. She'd seen the board. She'd known for three weeks.

She hadn't known it would be this.

***

He didn't kneel. She'd heard Deston had knelt, on a green by a pond, with forty-three candles behind him. The swamp guy didn't kneel. He stood in front of her in the notch, in the first sun, at the edge of the crater, with Masmark gold behind him. He held the ring on his palm between them, the way he'd held the arrow.

He went first.

He'd always gone first in fights and last at everything else. She'd told him so, on the heather, with a dead fell-hound at his feet. *You always go first in a fight. And last at everything else.* She'd always gone first, at everything else. At the gate, *me*. On a bench in Singapore, pushing him back against it. At the old hall, *mine*. Against a green pole under six hundred lanterns. On a bridge, last week, in front of half of Masmark. She'd always gone first, because he never would.

He went first.

"Hyv," said the swamp guy.

She couldn't say anything.

"Will you marry me," he said.

It wasn't a question, the way he said it. It wasn't a statement either. It was the thing in between. The thing you said when you'd said it and were waiting.

"Not just here," he said. "Not just in here." He was holding the ring very still. "Out there too. In real life. All of it." He was quiet. "I mean both."

***

Nobody had ever asked her for anything she loved.

That was what she thought, afterwards. Not in that moment. In that moment she didn't think anything. She stood in the notch with her hands shaking, the old way, the way they'd shaken at the old hall in January the first time she said *mine*. She looked at him. At his face in the first sun, not knowing what she'd say. Waiting, the way he waited for everything. On the back foot, a breath.

*You just say it. And then you wait. And somebody answers.*

He'd said that, on a bench at the eye, in August a year ago. To her, about Singapore. About asking for a day.

She'd answered then.

She answered now.

"Yes," said Hyv.

It came out very quietly. Barely a voice. Not the loudest thing in Masmark. The quietest thing she'd ever said.

Then she heard it. Her own voice, saying it, in the notch. And something in her that had been quiet her whole life, about everything she loved, because if you were loud somebody would take it, turned over.

"*YES*," said Hyv.

Loud. Into the notch, into the first sun, at the top of the crater wall. It went off the two black walls and out over the rim and down the inside of the crater. It went down the switchbacks and the dozens of roads and out across the lake going gold, toward a city putting out its candles. The loudest thing she'd ever said. Louder than *mine*, louder than seventy-four minutes on a terrace.

"*Yes. Here. And out there. In real life. All of it. Both.*" She was crying. She didn't care. "*YES*."

***

He put the ring on her finger. His hands were shaking too. She'd never once seen his hands shake. Not at the hill, not on the bridge, not in the dark with twelve gaps breathing.

It fit. Of course it did. Ina had asked Hana, Hana had asked somebody, somebody had asked Thea, who'd measured Hyv's hand against her own on the north road in May without Hyv noticing. Hyv found all that out later. She didn't care about any of it then.

She looked at it on her hand. Black iron that didn't shine. A small gold stone that saw from wherever it was put.

It caught the sun coming over Masmark and held it.

She looked up at him.

"You went *first*," said Hyv.

"Yes."

"You *never* go first. Not at anything but fights."

"I know," said the swamp guy. "I wanted to. Once." He was looking at her, at her face. Pink right up to the ears, in the first sun. "You always go first. I wanted you to have one where you didn't have to."

Hyv looked at him for a long time.

Then she put both hands flat on his chest.

She pushed him back. Not hard. Against the black rock wall of the notch, on the east side, with Masmark gold below. And kissed him. The way she always did. Going first.

Except she wasn't, this time. He'd gone first. She was answering.

It was the best answer she'd ever given anybody.

***

She didn't know how long they stood there. A while. The sun came up over the rim. The lake went from gold to blue. Masmark's lights were all out.

At some point she noticed the little eye at his shoulder. The one that looked out of him, for the stream. It had closed.

[ Ice has gone dark ]

He'd done it before he woke her. She hadn't seen. She'd decided not to look.

"You went dark," she said, into his shoulder.

"Yes."

"Three hundred thousand people."

"It was ours," said the swamp guy.

***

Behind them, in the middle of the notch, the goose stood up.

Hyv heard it. She turned round. It had been sitting there two weeks, facing west, waiting. It stood up now, all at once, with enormous dignity. It shook out its wings, once. Grey and white and large. It looked at the two of them, at the east edge of the notch, for a long moment.

Then it turned round and walked off. West. Down the road, toward home. In no hurry at all.

"It's going *home*," said Hyv.

"It's seen two," said the swamp guy.

She laughed so hard she had to hold on to him.

***

They came up the road at about the first hour. All of them.

PenguChi first, for once. She came up the last switchback with Patience and stopped at the top of the road, at the edge of the notch, and looked at them. At Hyv's hand. PenguChi didn't say anything. She lifted her spear. A little.

Then the rest. Fifty-seven people coming up the last of the road into the notch in the morning sun, because PenguChi had got up off her rock and they'd known that meant they could.

Gabe got to the top and saw Hyv's hand, and screamed.

He'd kept it for a day. In here and out there. He screamed for about a minute, at the top of the crater road, at the sky, with Rae holding him up at no paces. The whole of the inside of the crater must have heard it. It was the best scream Hyv had ever heard.

Then everybody.

Leaka on a rock. Xola, who'd been in a group chat called *operation tail* a year and a half ago, screaming *IT WORKED, IT WORKED, IT WORKED*. Thea with both hands over her mouth. She'd measured Hyv's hand against her own on the north road in May. Gigi close beside her. Hana, crying, who'd cut the stone. Suzu, who'd known, saying *good*, and then *finally*, which was Xola's. Dennis counting, twice, and getting it right. Kei with his map under his arm, very carefully not looking at the pencil at the bottom of it.

Hyv held up her hand so they could all see it.

The gold stone caught the sun and held it.

"*HE ASKED*," screamed Hyv. Over all of them. The loudest thing in the notch, at the top of the crater, over Masmark. "*HE WENT FIRST. HE ASKED. AND I SAID YES*."

And beside her, against the black wall of the notch, pink to the ears in front of fifty-seven people and a sheep, the swamp guy didn't say anything.

He didn't have to. He'd said it. Once. Out loud.
