Tif had learned to heal by watching a girl on a mooring post.

She hadn't known that was what she was doing. In December she'd just been modding. She sat at her desk in Singapore with his stream on the second screen, counting things, timing people out, being the head mod. And in the corner of the picture, on a post in a fen, there'd been a small girl in a pale blue robe with a white staff and a brass bell. She'd been ringing it a moment before anybody got hit.

Every time. Every single time. Ice would turn a blow off his shield, and the warmth from the bell would already be there, waiting for it. As if the girl on the post had seen the blow coming before Ice had.

Tif had watched her do it for five months. She'd never thought about it as learning. She'd thought about it as watching.

Then she'd come into the game, and picked up a staff, and found out that watching something for five months and doing it were two completely different things.

***

She'd been late all week in Millrace. Ringing the staff after the claw hit, not before. After Thea fell over, not before. Her hands knew what to do and her hands were half a second slow, every time, and her ears went flat every time and she couldn't make them stop.

Then on Wednesday she'd been early. All of Wednesday. And the game had given her a class with *early* in it, and PenguChi had said *I was watching*, and Tif had gone pink to the tips of her ears in a way she hadn't known ears could.

She was early again on the river road.

They left Millrace on Thursday, all six of them, walking south and east along a river that came out of the bottom of the mill pond and went on, wide and brown, toward the fens. Thea in front with the tower shield. Slep and Xola beside each other. PenguChi at the back, slowly, watching. Xyro walking backwards to look at things. And Tif, in the middle, with her staff.

And Slep's sheep, which had followed them out of Millrace and wouldn't go home.

On Friday, on a bend in the river road, something came out of the reeds.

[ Reed Stalker · Lv 12 ]

It was long and low and green-brown, and it came out of the reeds at Thea's back. Not her front, where the shield was. Her back, where nobody was looking. Tif saw it before anyone. She'd been watching everybody's backs all week. She didn't know when she'd started doing that. She thought it was probably December.

She saw it coming, and where it was going to land, before it got there.

She rang the staff.

*Kindle.*

The warmth went into Thea a full second before the Reed Stalker's jaws closed on her shoulder. When they closed, Thea was already mending. She staggered, and turned, and didn't go down. The shield came round, the way a millwright had taught her, and sent the next lunge to the left, where Slep was. And then it was over.

Thea stood on the river road breathing hard with her hand on her shoulder and looked back at Tif.

"You rang before it bit me," said Thea.

"I saw it," said Tif.

"Before it *bit* me, Tif."

"I know." Tif looked down at her staff. Her hands were shaking. "I learned it off Kotori. On the post. In December. I didn't know I was learning it."

Xyro picked her up off her feet in the middle of the river road and spun her round. Tif shrieked and hit him with the staff, gently, and didn't mean it at all.

***

They came into the old fen on Sunday at dusk.

Tif knew where they were before Thea said it. She'd known for an hour. The river road came down off a low hill into the reeds. The reeds went on for miles, grey-green and silver in the evening light, with the black water between them. There was the causeway, raised up over the water on old stones. There were the lamps, being lit one at a time along it by a man with a pole. She'd watched that man light those lamps on a screen a hundred times. And at the end of the causeway, a town on stilts over black water. The Landing. The long north walk on its posts, going away into the reeds.

Fernwick.

She stopped walking.

Everybody stopped walking, because she had. They stood on the causeway in the dusk, six people and a sheep, and looked at it.

"Tif?" said Xyro.

"I've seen it," said Tif. Her voice didn't sound like hers. "Every night. Since the first night. On a screen." She looked at the lamps, and the stilts, and the black water. "It's real. It's—"

She didn't finish. She didn't have to. Xyro put his arm round her, and she let him, and they stood on the causeway until the lamp-man reached them. He looked at them, six strangers and a sheep, and touched his hat, and went on.

Somewhere, Tif thought, thirty thousand people were typing. She couldn't see it. For the first time in five months she had no idea what his chat was saying.

She found she didn't mind.

***

Thea went first. That had been the plan since Saturday on the step in Millrace. *We're going to surprise Ice.*

They walked along the Landing in the dusk, past the eel man mending a net. He looked up at them, and at the sheep, and went back to his net. Past the people who still came every evening to buy cabbage tea for luck. To the end of the north walk, and the ladder, and the steps over the channel. Tif knew those steps. A boy with a black sword used to sit on them.

There was light in the window of the house on the stilts. There was the sound of something being ground. There was the smell of herbs, so many herbs, the smell that Tif had imagined through a screen every night for five months and never once got right.

Thea stood at the bottom of the steps with the tower shield on her back. Tif saw her take a breath.

Then she went up and opened the door.

***

He was at the table.

Of course he was. Sitting in the chair that was his, under the herbs, with a dropper in his hand and a row of stoppered draughts in front of him. Across from him an old woman with a pipe was grinding something. At the hearth a small girl in a pale blue robe was stirring a pot. The fire was going and the rain had just started on the roof.

He looked up.

Tif saw his face. She'd seen his face every night for five months, in a corner of her second screen. She'd never seen it do this. It went completely still. The dropper stopped halfway to the glass. He looked at Thea in the doorway with a tower shield on her back, and didn't say anything at all, for a long time.

"Surprise," said Thea.

Her voice shook, a little. Only a little. Tif doubted anybody but Xola noticed.

Ice put the dropper down.

"Hi, Thea," he said.

It was just two words. He said it very quietly, the way you'd say something you'd been waiting a long time to say and had decided not to make a fuss about. Thea, in the doorway, nodded, once, and her eyes went bright, and she didn't cry. Tif thought that was probably the bravest thing she'd ever seen her do.

Then Xola came through the door behind her at a run and nearly knocked the herbs off the beams.

"*ICEY*."

She threw her arms round his neck from behind, chair and all, gills going like flags. Ice made a noise Tif had never once heard on stream. She thought it was probably a laugh he'd been keeping somewhere.

"Xola."

"*SURPRISE*."

"Yes."

"Were you *surprised*?"

"Yes."

"*How* surprised?"

Ice thought about it, with Xola hanging off his neck.

"I dropped the moonbell," he said.

Across the table the old woman took her pipe out of her mouth and looked at the six of them in her doorway, and the sheep on the step behind them.

"Swamp guy," said Sedge. "Is that a sheep?"

"Yes," said Slep, from the back.

"Is it *yours*?"

Slep thought about it.

"It might be," he said.

***

Tif hadn't been able to say anything yet. She was standing in the doorway behind everybody, with her staff, with her ears turned all the way forward. She was looking at the small girl in the pale blue robe at the hearth, with a white staff leaning against the wall beside her and a brass bell on a blue ribbon.

The girl had turned round from the pot. She was looking at Tif. At the ears, and the staff, and the green eyes.

Ice looked from one of them to the other. He didn't say anything. He just moved his chair a little, so they could see each other properly.

"Kotori," he said. "Tif."

Kotori looked at her for a long moment.

"You're the mod," she said. "The one who counted. The *!dark*."

"Yes."

"You counted my healers," said Kotori. "Before I was there. *Healers tried: twenty. Healers kept: zero.*" Her face did something. "And then *one*."

Tif hadn't known Kotori knew that. She'd never thought Kotori would have watched the VODs. She stood in the doorway and felt her ears go pink again.

"I watched you," said Tif. It came out all at once, the way things did when you'd been carrying them a long time. "On the post. Every night. You ring before. You ring where it's going to be. I watched you do it for five months and I didn't know I was learning it, and then I came in and I couldn't do it, and then on Wednesday I *could*. And on the river road on Friday a thing came out of the reeds at Thea's back and I rang before it bit her. And I'd learned it off you. I'd learned it all off *you*."

Kotori stood at the hearth with her stirring spoon in her hand and didn't say anything at all.

Then she said, "Sorr—"

She stopped. Tif watched her stop. Watched her close her mouth on the old word and take a breath and find a different one, the way somebody had taught her to, a long time ago, in a ring.

"Thank you," said Kotori.

"*You're* welcome," said Tif. "I mean. *Thank* you. I mean—"

Sedge took her pipe out of her mouth.

"Two of you," said Sedge. "Lord help us." She nodded at the hearth. "Kettle's on. Brew something."

So they did.

They stood side by side at Sedge's hearth while the rain came down on the roof and the others crowded round the table behind them. Kotori showed Tif the cabbage tea, the one for luck, that tasted of cabbage. Tif showed Kotori the calming one, the one Brewing had given her in Millrace, that tasted of nothing much and made you breathe slower. They made both, in the same pot, because the pot was the only one. Kotori said that was a terrible idea and did it anyway.

It tasted of cabbage, and it made you breathe slower.

Sedge leaned over from the table, looked into the pot, and moved it two inches to the left, without saying anything.

"*Fair*," said Kotori, delighted. "She only moves it when it's going to be good."

Behind them, at the table, Ice was sitting with Xola still half hanging off his chair. Thea sat across from him in Sedge's other chair with the tower shield leaning against her knee. They weren't saying very much. Tif turned round from the hearth with a cup in each hand and looked at the two of them. At Ice, with his face doing the thing it had done when Thea opened the door. Not quite stillness any more. Something under it.

She took him a cup.

"Welcome home, Tif," said Ice, taking it.

Tif looked at him for a moment. At the chat she couldn't see, at the edge of his eye.

"Behave," she said.
