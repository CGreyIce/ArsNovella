Out there, it had been Saturday. It was theirs. Gabe had been there. That was all anybody in the round town was ever going to hear from Deston about it. He'd decided that on the green, a long time ago, by the pond, on his knees. Some things you kept.

In here, it was night.

He opened his eyes at the long table in the banner room, in the chair beside Chernn's, where he'd logged out a day and a half ago with her hand in his under the table. Her hand was in his under the table now. She'd come in a breath before him. She was looking at him.

"Hi," said Chernn.

"Hi," said Deston.

The banner room was empty.

He'd thought it might be. She'd said *small*. She'd said *nothing, please*. And the round town had listened, the way it listened to Chernn, because she so rarely asked for anything. Sixty-one chairs and a stool, all empty, pushed in. The banner on its two hooks under Ama Corrie's stair, with sixty-two dots on it, gold. One lamp, low.

And in the window of the banner room, the one that looked out over the round market, a candle.

Tall, white, plain. Thin sweet Tallow Green wax. Standing in a dish on the sill. With a twist of paper beside it, unwrapped, flat, where somebody had smoothed it out so it could be read.

*Old Tess.*

***

They went out through the Hall.

Ama Corrie was at her counter. Of course she was. The Roll was open. There was a candle on the counter beside it, lit. Ama Corrie didn't have a window by her counter, so she'd put it on the counter, where the Roll could see it.

She looked at the two of them for a long time as they came out of the banner room.

"You're late," said Ama Corrie.

"We were out there," said Chernn.

"I know where you were." She looked at their hands. "*Hm*." Then she went back to the Roll, and didn't sweep anything. Deston thought that might be the kindest thing she'd ever done.

The south door was open.

They went out onto the top of the south steps and stopped.

***

Every window in the round town was lit.

Not a roof. Not lanterns. Not red and gold. Just windows. Every one. All the way round the round market and down every street and every road going out. Small and yellow and steady, one candle in each. The Hart's front windows and its back one, the one with ideas. The forge's. Every window on Spoke Street. The cookshop where Xola worked the fires. The dyer's. The chestnut man's shed, which only had one window and it was very small. The blue door at the end of Spoke Street, which had a window beside it, with a candle in it.

Four hundred and thirty-one.

The round town was quiet. No crowd in the market. Nobody on the fountain rim. Nobody at the foot of the green pole, whose gold eye at the top was catching every one of the four hundred and thirty-one and holding them.

Just windows. With candles in them.

And in every window, behind every candle, somebody.

He saw them as his eyes got used to it. In the Hart's front window, Gabe. Sitting on the sill inside, with his face pressed against the glass, crying, obviously. And Rae beside him, at no paces at all, with her hand on his back, looking out at them very patiently. In the window above the forge, Ina and Old Joss, side by side, Joss with his pipe. In the cookshop, Xola, waving both arms, and Slep behind her, not waving, which was the same thing. In a window on the first floor of a house on Spoke Street, Dennis. He saw Deston see him and mouthed, through the glass, very clearly, *darling*.

Kotori in the Hart's kitchen window, where Chernn dipped. Leaka and Curse in a window over the dyer's, Leaka with both hands over her mouth. Hana and Milli in a window of the Hart, Milli's arm round her. Jared and Azac. Kei and Higu. Tiger. Wendy and Salty. Thea and Gigi, close. Drew and Ash. Kite, in the window beside the gate, because that was the nearest one to where he stood every day.

In the window of the banner room, behind them, where they'd come from: Ice. With Hyv. Both of them had come in after Deston and Chernn went out, quiet, and stood there. Hyv had her arm through his and her chin on his shoulder. Ice wasn't doing anything. He was just there, at the window, with the candle, looking out.

The old woman at the blue door. The chestnut man. The dyer. People Deston didn't know, who lived in the round town and had been there before the guild and would be after. Each of them in a window, behind a candle somebody in Tallow Green had dipped for a stranger.

Nobody came out.

That was the thing. Nobody came out. Nobody screamed, not even Leaka. Nobody ran down the steps. They'd all been told *small*. They'd all been told *honest*. And they were all doing exactly that. Sitting in their windows, behind their candles, looking out at two people on the top of the south steps.

Being there. Not being loud about it.

Chernn's hand had gone very tight in his.

***

"They're all in their windows," said Chernn.

"Yes."

"All of them. Every window."

"Yes," said Deston.

"I said nothing." Her voice had gone. "I said small. Please. I said I didn't want a—"

"It's not a roof," said Deston. "It's just windows."

She looked at him.

"You said honest," said Deston. "This is the most honest thing I've ever seen." He looked down at the round market. Four hundred and thirty-one yellow squares in the dark, and a face behind every one. "Everybody we know. In their own window. With a candle from somebody they don't know. Not coming out." He swallowed. "Just so we'd know they were there."

Chernn put her face in his shoulder.

He looked over the top of her head at the round town, at all of it, lit. He thought about the green. About the pond, and the forty-three candles behind him, and the two flames in front. And Mother Dipp shutting her door so loudly the whole village heard. That was the custom; everybody stayed in. And then the whole village coming out in a ring with candles after Chernn said *yes*, and the goose sitting down.

They hadn't come out this time.

They'd stayed in. Every one. In their windows.

It was the same custom, he realised. The other half of it. You stayed in while it was being asked. And when it had been answered, you lit a candle in your window, so they'd know you knew.

***

They walked down the south steps into the round market. Slowly, the two of them, hand in hand.

Nobody came out. They walked across the market past the fountain and the green pole, past every lit window. And at every window, the person inside did one small thing.

Gabe put his hand flat on the glass. Rae beside him lifted hers, a little. Xola blew them a kiss, enormous. Dennis mouthed *darling* again, more firmly. Ina nodded. Old Joss raised his pipe. Leaka took her hands off her mouth for one second, and then put them back on, because otherwise she'd have screamed. Kotori held up a cup. Hana and Milli, in the Hart's window, held up one candle between them, two hands round it.

Nobody said anything. Deston walked through the round town with Chernn's hand in his, in the light of four hundred and thirty-one windows. And he'd never heard anything so loud in his life.

***

At the bottom of the south road, at the milestone, there was a light.

Not in a window. The milestone didn't have a window. It was a grey stone with a worn base, at the edge of the round market, where the south road began. Where Kite had sat beside it for months, the first winter, before he came any closer. Where Ash had sat on its base all autumn, waiting. Where, Kite said, a thin young man with no guild had sat in January, and then come up to the gate to ask him.

On the base of the milestone, a candle.

Short. Thick. Two wicks. Standing in a dish. One wick lit.

And beside it, on the base of the milestone, under the grey blanket: Isa. With Suzu sitting on the cobbles at her feet, a sword's length off.

Chernn stopped.

"Isa," she said.

"You came home," said Isa. "In whatever way it is you come home. Through whatever door it is." She was looking at the candle, close, where she could see it. "But the road is the road. My mothers said. Whoever's out, it's the road that brings them in. Whatever road it is." She moved her old hand in the blanket. "So it goes on the road. At the bottom. Where people wait."

"It's lit," said Deston. "One wick."

"One's mine," said Isa. "For whoever was out. That's the line." She looked up at them. "The other one's yours."

***

He knew what she meant. He'd done it before. On a green by a pond, at dusk, on his knees, with forty-three candles behind him. One candle, two wicks. Light both, and then ask. And the one asked answers before it burns down.

Nobody was asking anything. It had all been asked. It had all been answered. Out there, and before that, on the green.

Chernn knelt down at the milestone. She took a spill out of her apron, where she always kept one. She lit it from Isa's wick and held it to the other.

It caught.

Two flames. One candle. Side by side, on the base of the milestone at the bottom of the south road.

[ *It will burn all the way down.* ]

Chernn sat down on the cobbles in front of the milestone. Deston sat down beside her. Suzu, at Isa's feet, moved over a little to make room, the way Kite had moved over once for Aurelie. Nobody had ever moved over for Kite. It was the kind of thing that got passed on.

They sat there, the four of them, in front of a two-wick candle on a milestone, with four hundred and thirty-one windows lit behind them.

***

Isa didn't sleep. Not for a long time. She sat on the base of the milestone with the blanket round her and watched the two flames, close.

"Did any of them," said Chernn, after a while. "Your mothers. On the hill." She stopped. "Did any of them have somebody?"

Isa was quiet for so long that Deston thought she hadn't heard.

"Three," said Isa. "In three hundred years. That I know of." She moved her hands in the blanket. "You couldn't, mostly. Somebody had to keep the lamp. You couldn't leave it. And nobody came out there. Nobody knew where it was." She looked at the flames. "But three times, somebody came anyway."

"Who?"

"My mother's grandmother's was a man who carried water," said Isa. "From the edge. Up the stones. Every week, for forty years. He never once came inside the ring. That was the lamp's, and hers. He'd stop on the last stone, the crooked one of that year, and sit there. And she'd sit on the hill. With the stride of water between them." She was quiet. "And they'd talk. All night. Across it."

Chernn didn't say anything.

"Forty years," said Isa. "Across a stride of water. My mother said they were the happiest two people she ever heard of." She looked at Chernn, close. "He never once asked her to leave the lamp. And she never once asked him to come inside the ring. They just sat. Next to it."

"Next to," said Deston.

"*Hm*," said Isa. "You've got a word for it. In your round town. I heard it." She looked at the two of them, side by side on the cobbles. "That's what they were. My mother's grandmother and the man who carried water. Next to. For forty years."

She closed her eyes then. She didn't sleep, Deston thought. Not yet. She just sat there with her eyes closed and her face toward the two flames, the way she sat on the roof at dusk, looking north at a light she couldn't see.

***

He felt it about an hour later. The Accord.

He'd had it with one person since the beginning. Since the chalk hill behind Tallow Green, the first week, with the little white hares that bit ankles. He'd stood behind her and sung the line, the way you'd hum along with somebody in a kitchen. And she'd moved before the hare got there, on the count, and stopped, and said *was that you?* It had been with her ever since. A thin warm thread between them, under everything, in every fight.

He felt it settle now. At the milestone, with nothing to fight. Like a candle that had been burning a long time, finding the bottom of itself and burning steady.

[ *It no longer needs the line.* ]

He looked at the window for a long time. Then at the book under his arm, which he'd carried every day for two and a half years.

He didn't open it. He just put his hand flat on the cover, the way Isa put her hand on things.

Chernn, beside him, without looking, put hers on top.

***

Somebody came down the south road from the market, a long time later. Quiet, with a shield on his back and two cups.

Deston knew who it was before he looked. He'd known Dec since before the game, since a long time before the game. He'd walked behind him at Lv 5 down a narrow place banging a sword on a shield, reading *Let the many move as one* in a voice that wobbled because he was trying not to laugh. He'd moved to Malacca, into the unit opposite, and peeled the *DIJUAL* sticker off its door to frame.

Dec didn't come close. He sat down on the cobbles a sword's length off, the way Kite sat, and the way Suzu sat. He held out one of the cups.

"Tea," said Dec. "Kotori's. From the window."

Deston took it.

"You're slow," said Dec.

"Forty-three candles," said Deston.

"I know. I counted." Dec looked at the two flames on the milestone. "Took you long enough."

"You wrote that. In July. A day late."

"I was up a tower," said Dec.

Deston looked at him over the cup. Dec, with his shield on his back, sitting on the cobbles a sword's length off, looking at a two-wick candle. Hyv wasn't with him. He'd left her at the banner room window with her chin on her hands, Deston found out later, and said *I'll be back*, and she'd said *go*. She'd known he'd want to come down on his own.

"Thank you," said Deston.

"For what?"

"Coming down." Deston was quiet. "Being slow. All of it. You were slow first." He looked at Chernn, beside him, watching the two flames with her chin on her knees. "I learned it off you."

Dec didn't say anything for a while.

"Good," he said. Which was Suzu's. "That's the good kind."

He stayed about ten minutes. Then he got up and went back up the south road toward the market, toward the banner room window, where Hyv was waiting with her chin on her hands. Deston watched him go. Somewhere near the fountain, under the green pole, somebody in a window very nearly screamed and was stopped. Deston thought it was probably Gabe. He thought Dec had probably gone past the Hart's window, and Gabe had seen his face.

***

The candle burned all the way down.

It took all night. Past the third dawn. Past the third dusk. Into the grey before the first dawn. Two flames on the milestone getting shorter, side by side, never going out. Chernn watched it the whole time with her chin on her knees. Deston watched her watch it.

Behind them, one by one, the windows went out. Not all at once. One at a time, as the night went on, as each candle burned down, the way candles do. The chestnut man's first, because it was smallest. Then the cookshop. Then the forge. Then one by one, all the way round the round market. Four hundred and thirty-one lights going out one by one. Not because anybody blew them out. Because they'd burned all the way down.

The last window in the round town to go out was the banner room's. *Old Tess*.

And then just the milestone. Two flames, very short, in a dish.

At a quarter to eight, out there, the two-wick candle on the milestone burned down to the bottom. Both flames together, at the same moment, went out.

Isa was asleep. Suzu was asleep, sitting up, with his back against the milestone. Chernn wasn't. She picked up the dish with the stub in it, still warm, and wrapped it in the square of cloth Mother Dipp had sent it in.

"For the windowsill," she said. "At the end of all the others."

"Both," said Deston.

"Both," said Chernn.

From up in the round town, from the long table, faint, through the open south door, he heard Kei say *set one to watch*. And sixty people and a sheep, quietly, so as not to wake anybody, said *so the rest may sleep*.

Deston and Chernn said it at the milestone. Quietly too.

The egg took him at eight with her head on his shoulder and the stub in her lap. The last thing he saw was the gold eye at the top of the green pole in the round market. Catching the first sun, and holding it.
