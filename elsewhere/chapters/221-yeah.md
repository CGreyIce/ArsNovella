Ryan said *yeah* a lot, and people thought it meant he wasn't listening.

It didn't. It meant *yeah*. It meant he'd heard, and he'd thought about it, and he didn't have anything to add. Most of the time there wasn't anything to add. People said a thing, and it was true, or it was a good idea, or it was going to happen anyway. And you could say a lot of words back, or you could say *yeah*. He'd never seen the point of the words.

Xav understood. Xav had known him nine years, since poly, and Xav had never once asked him to say more than he wanted. Declan understood too. Declan said *okay* the way Ryan said *yeah*. They'd worked that out about each other a long time ago, and never needed to talk about it.

So when Kei stood at the bottom of the stair inside the Bell Above with his map case and looked round at the twenty of them and said, "Ready?", Ryan said:

"Yeah."

And meant it.

***

The stair went up round the inside of the tower wall, very steep, into the dark.

He'd never seen anything like the inside of the tower. Nobody had. From outside, from the island, it was a plain grey thing with a bell on top. Inside, it was huge. Bigger than the outside, which didn't make sense, but nothing in the game made sense if you looked at it too hard. The stair went round and round the wall, up and up, and the middle was hollow, all the way to the top. And down through the hollow, from somewhere so far above you couldn't see it, hung ropes. Hundreds of them. Thick, old, swaying very slightly.

And the bells.

They were everywhere. In the walls, in the floor, above and below. Small ones, ringing, high to low, one after another. Then two of them swapping. Then two more. Never the same twice.

Val had heard it. Of course she had.

She'd sat on the rocks on the north side of the island for three hours on Thursday with her ear against the stone. She hadn't said a word to anybody. Then she'd come back to the netloft and sat at the long table with her book. And Ryan, who'd been sitting across from her eating a fish, had watched her write it down. Dashes and dots, the way she'd written the cellar. The order of the bells. Every change.

"What is it?" he'd said.

"A tempo," Val had said, without looking up. "The whole tower's a tempo." She'd kept writing. "Somebody built a building that rings."

"Yeah," Ryan had said.

***

"The steps move," said Hyvred.

They were twenty steps up. Ryan felt it before she said it. The step under his left foot went down, very slightly, a finger's width. And the one ahead of it came up.

On the beat. On the change.

"Every step's on a bell," said Val. She was halfway up the line with her book open in one hand and her wand in the other. "Listen. When the treble and the second swap, their steps swap. Up and down." She listened. "Next change, the second and the third."

The step under Ryan's right foot dropped.

He was already off it. He didn't know how. He'd just heard Val say *the second and the third*, and heard the bells, and his feet had gone. The way they went when something came at him in a fight, before he'd thought about it. Onto the next step up, which had just come up to meet him.

"Oh," said Ryan.

Behind him, Azac with his enormous shield went down a finger's width with a step and said a word the game didn't translate.

"Listen to the one before," said Val. Very calmly, in the voice that made people trust her. "Don't look at the steps. Listen to which bells just swapped. The next change is always the pair after."

"*Always*?" said Gigi.

"It's a method," said Val. "It's written down. It goes all the way round, every bell with every other bell, and comes back to the start." She held up her book. "I wrote it down. On the rocks. It's in here."

Kei looked at her for a long moment. Then at the book.

"Val calls the changes," he said. "Hyv calls everything else."

"Yeah," said Ryan.

***

They went up.

It took the whole of the first night. Not because it was far. Because you could only move on the beat. You stood on your step and waited for Val to say the pair, and you listened for the bells, and on the change you went. Twenty people going up a stair that breathed, one change at a time.

Val called. *Treble, second. Third, fourth. Second, third.* Never loud. Never hurried. She'd started reading it out like a line, Ryan noticed, after the first hour. Not just saying the bells. Saying them with a tempo, so you could hear the change coming in her voice before it came in the walls.

Hyvred called everything else. *Rope coming in on the left. Gap at forty. Ryan, there's a loose one under you, the next change it goes.* She'd never stopped calling in the cellar until she couldn't. Here she didn't stop at all. Two voices going up the tower, one slow and even, one fast and edge to edge. And Declan at the front, saying nothing, going up on every change as if he'd been climbing this stair his whole life.

The board came up at about the fourth hour, in the corner of everybody's window. The one the Rim Hall put up for races.

"Meridian's at the first quarter," said Kei. "Already."

"They're fast," said RX.

"They knew," said Hyvred. She was looking at the board, edge to edge. "They went straight up. On the first night. Nobody fell." She was quiet for a moment. "Somebody told them about the bells."

Ryan looked down the line at Val.

Val was looking at her book, not at the board. Her face was doing something small at the corner of her mouth.

"Kestrel," said Val.

"*Kestrel* told *Meridian*?"

"I saw her," said Val. "On Thursday. On the rocks. She walked round to the west side and talked to Aurelie for about a minute and walked back." She turned a page. "I didn't hear what she said. But afterward Aurelie went and put her ear on the wall. For an hour. With one of hers who kept nodding."

Kei was quiet for a long time.

"And VANTA's resting," he said.

"*Resting*," said Xola. "On the first night. On the *stair*."

"She said she would," said Declan, from the front. It was the first thing he'd said all night. "In March. *We rest first.*" He went up a step, on the change. "She meant it."

"Yeah," said Ryan.

***

The first quarter was the ringing chamber.

They came up off the stair at the end of the first night into a round room. The whole width of the tower, with a wooden floor and a low ceiling with holes in it. Twelve holes. And through every hole a rope came down, thick, old, with a woollen grip near the bottom striped red and white and blue.

And on every rope, a Ringer.

Ryan had never seen anything so beautiful. Twelve figures in a ring, bronze, green with age, taller than Azac, in long bronze robes. Each with both hands on a rope. Pulling. Not fast. On the beat. Down, and the rope went up through the ceiling, and somewhere above a bell rang. And down. And the next one. Round the ring, high to low, and then two of them swapping. Their bronze faces turned very slightly to watch each other, the way people do when they're keeping time.

[ The Ringers of Masmark · Lv 56 · twelve ]

[ *Nothing that stops ringing stays up.* ]

"Oh," said Gigi.

"*Nothing that stops ringing stays up*," said Malcolm slowly. "That's—"

"That's the stair," said Val. "If the ringing stops, the stair falls." She looked at the twelve bronze Ringers, pulling their ropes, round and round. "And we have to get past them."

The Ringers noticed them.

All twelve heads turned at once. Not stopping. Still pulling, still on the beat. But looking. And the one nearest the stair, the tenor, the biggest, took one hand off its rope, and reached for them.

The ringing faltered.

Ryan felt the floor go. Not much. A shudder, under his feet, through the whole tower.

The tenor's hand came back to its rope. The ringing went on. The floor steadied.

"*If they stop to fight us*," said Malcolm, "*the stair—*"

"The stair falls," said Val. "With us on it. And everybody behind us."

Nobody said anything.

***

Kei looked at the map. Then at the Ringers. Then at Val.

"Can we ring it?" he said.

Val looked at him.

"Twelve ropes," said Kei. "Twelve of us. Somebody takes each rope when its Ringer goes down. And keeps it going. On the beat. While the rest fight the next one." He was very calm. "You call it. Like the stair. We ring it ourselves."

"We don't know *how*," said Gigi.

"Val knows," said Kei.

Val was very still for a moment.

"I know what it sounds like," she said. "I don't know how to *pull* it."

"Then we'll learn on the way," said Declan.

He went first. Of course he did.

He went across the wooden floor to the tenor, the biggest, the one that had reached for them. He stood beside it with his shield up. The Ringer looked down at him with its bronze face, still pulling. And on the next change, the moment its rope went up through the ceiling and its hands were empty for half a breath, Declan hit it.

Not hard. The shield rim, into the bronze robe, where it would turn it. And the Ringer turned, the way he'd meant it to. Off its rope, toward him, both hands reaching.

The rope swung free.

"*Ryan*," said Hyvred.

He didn't think about it. He heard his name and he went. Across the floor, under the Ringer's reaching arm, and caught the rope on its way down. Both hands on the striped grip. It nearly took him off his feet; it was heavy, and it was going up again. He let it go up with him, onto his toes, and then pulled. Down.

Somewhere above, the tenor rang.

On the beat. Late, by half a breath. But on.

The floor steadied.

"*Again*," said Val. "*Pull on my count. Now. Now. Hold, it's a change— now.*"

He pulled.

***

It took six hours.

They took the Ringers one at a time, in the order Val said, because some bells could be rung by a person who'd never rung before and some couldn't. Declan turned each one off its rope with the shield, on the change. Salty and Azac and Jun Liang held it. Xav and Gigi and Xola and Thea and the rest brought it down. And while they did, somebody took the rope. RX, on the treble, counting under his breath. Then Hako on the second, narrating. Then Bee, then Slep, then Malcolm. One by one, round the ring, the bronze Ringers went down. And people took their places. Until Ryan was standing in a ring of twelve with eleven others, every one of them on a rope, pulling, while Val stood in the middle with her book and called it.

Badly. They rang it badly. Hako kept ringing early. Bee couldn't reach the grip without jumping. Xyro rang the fifth twice in a row by mistake and the whole floor shuddered and everybody screamed at him.

But it didn't stop.

And Ryan, on the tenor, the heaviest, the first one, found that he loved it.

He'd never done anything like it. You pulled, and far above you a bell rang. You couldn't see it. You couldn't see anything but the rope going up through the hole in the ceiling and the person across the ring from you, whose bell came after yours. You watched them, and they watched you, and you kept time. Not by looking at anything. By listening to the one before.

Fenn had said that, apparently. On VANTA's terrace. Kestrel had told Xav and Xav had told everybody. *I'm just listening to the one before.*

He pulled. Somewhere above, the tenor rang. On the beat. Not late any more.

"*Last one*," said Kei.

The last Ringer, the eleventh, went down under Salty's black sword. And Val, in the middle of the ring, looked up from her book with her face lit. Ryan had never seen it like that.

"Rounds," said Val. "Everybody. Rounds, and then *stand*."

They rang rounds. High to low. All twelve, one after another, the long falling line, the way the tower had started.

And on the last stroke, the tenor, Ryan's, everybody let go.

The ropes went up through the holes in the ceiling and didn't come down.

"Yeah," he said, very quietly, to nobody.

***

"Third quarter," said Kei. "They're two ahead."

"Two *quarters* ahead," said RX.

"They didn't ring it," said Val, looking at the board. "They can't have. They'd have taken as long as us." She was quiet. "They must have found another way. Round the ringers. Or through." She looked at her book. "They'd planned it. Two hundred people, drawing the tower, for a month. And then Kestrel told them about the bells, and they knew where the stair moved. They went straight past."

"We rang it," said Gigi.

"We rang it," agreed Kei.

Nobody said *and it cost us*. Nobody needed to. They'd spent six hours on a floor pulling ropes, and Meridian had gone past in one. And somewhere a long way up, twenty people in deep blue were two quarters closer to the bell.

"Up," said Declan.

"Yeah," said Ryan.

***

The second quarter was where the stair stopped.

It just stopped. They came up round the wall above the ringing chamber, on the beat, all night, with Val calling. And then about halfway up, the steps ended. There was a gap in the wall where the stair should have gone on, a long one, all the way round the curve of the tower. Nothing but dark and the hollow beside them, and the ropes hanging down through it.

And on the far side of the gap, a long way round, the stair started again.

"It's fallen," said Hako, in his documentary voice. "At some point in the last thousand years, the stair, unable to support the weight of its own history—"

"*Hako*."

"Sorry."

Kei looked at the gap for a long time. Then at the ropes.

"Those," said Hyvred. She was looking at the hollow, edge to edge. "The ropes go all the way up. Past the gap. To the next landing." She looked at Val. "Are they bells?"

Val listened.

"Yes," she said. "Every one. Every rope in the hollow's a bell." She went pale. "If you pull on one, it rings. If you climb one, every time you put your weight on it, it rings."

"And if it rings out of order—"

"The method breaks," said Val. "And the stair—"

"Falls," said Kei. "Yes."

They looked at the ropes for a long time. Hundreds of them, hanging in the dark, swaying very slightly.

Ryan looked at them too.

He didn't think about it much. He thought about it a bit. He thought about a rope going up through a hole in a ceiling, and the weight of it, and how it took you onto your toes on the way up. How you had to let it, and then pull. He thought about listening to the one before.

"Val," he said.

She looked at him.

"Which one's next?" said Ryan. "In the method. After the one that just rang."

Val looked at her book. Then at the hollow. Her finger went down the page.

"The fourth," she said. "Then the seventh. Then the second." She looked up. "Why?"

"If I climb the fourth on the fourth," said Ryan, "and the seventh on the seventh. And so on. All the way up." He took his hands out of his coat pockets, which he hadn't done all night. "Then it rings in order. Doesn't it."

Nobody said anything.

"That's insane," said Xola.

"Yeah," said Ryan.

***

Declan looked at him.

Ryan had known him nine years. He'd seen that look before. Not often. The look Declan gave somebody when they'd said a thing nobody else had thought of. As if he'd just noticed there was somebody there worth noticing.

"Can you?" said Declan.

Ryan thought about it.

"Yeah," he said.

"Okay," said Declan. And then, to Kei: "Let him."

Kei looked at Ryan for a long time. Then at Val.

"Call it for him," he said. "Only him. Slow."

Val came to the edge of the gap with her book.

Ryan went to the edge of the stair, where the hollow was, and the ropes hung down in the dark. He found the fourth. Val pointed; he'd never have known. Thick, old, rough. He put both hands on it.

"*Now*," said Val.

He put his weight on it.

Somewhere far above, very faint, a bell rang. On the beat.

"*Hold. Hold. The seventh, on your left. Reach— now.*"

He let go of the fourth and swung, and caught the seventh. And put his weight on it. And far above, the seventh rang.

"*The second. Above you. Climb, climb— now.*"

He climbed.

***

He climbed for an hour. Rope to rope, across the hollow and up, on the beat, with Val's voice coming up out of the dark below him like a line being read. *The ninth. Now. Hold. The third, on your right. Hold— now.* He never looked down. He never looked at the ropes. He listened to the one before, and to Val, and went when she said. Behind him, the bells rang, one after another, in order. Every change. Never the same twice.

He thought about nothing very much. He thought it was the best thing he'd ever done.

At the top of the gap, where the stair started again, he let go of the last rope on the last beat and stepped onto stone. He stood there in the dark, breathing hard, with his hands raw.

Then he took a coil of line off his belt, the long one Hako had made out of something he'd skinned, and tied it round a post. And threw the other end back across the hollow into the dark, where twenty lamps were.

Somebody caught it.

"*Ryan*," said Hyvred, from very far below. Her voice was doing something. "*Ryan, you rang it, you rang the whole thing, all the way up, in ORDER*—"

"Yeah," said Ryan.

They came across one at a time on the line, with Val calling. Declan last, with his shield on his back, at the end of the line where the weight was. He stepped off onto the stone beside Ryan and stood there for a moment. He looked at him, the same way.

"Good," said Declan.

Ryan didn't say anything. He didn't need to. He put his hands back in his coat pockets, raw as they were, and they went up.

***

"At the bell," said Kei.

Nobody said anything.

"They're at the top," said Kei. "They'll be fighting it tomorrow. When they come back in." He looked at the board for a long time. "We're a quarter behind."

"Twenty minutes, maybe," said RX. "If they're slow at the top. If we're fast in the last bit."

"They won't be slow at the top," said Hyvred. "They've planned the top. They've planned everything." She was looking at the board, the whole of it, edge to edge. "They're going to do it by the book. Carefully. Nobody falls. And they're going to be first."

She didn't sound angry. Ryan noticed that. She sounded like somebody reading out what was going to happen.

Declan was quiet for a long time.

"What's at the top?" he said.

"The bell," said Val. "And something that rings it." She looked at her book, at the last page, where she'd written what she'd heard from the rocks on Thursday, with her ear against the stone. "The small bells are a method. They go all the way round and come back. But underneath them, every so often. Not on the method. On its own." She stopped. "Something big. Something very slow. A great big *boom*. Like a door. Like a heartbeat." She looked up. "It's the great bell. Something's ringing it. Under everything. On its own tempo."

"What tempo?" said Declan.

"Slow," said Val. "Very slow. And then— I don't know. Getting faster, I think. Toward the end of the hour I listened. Like it was running out of time."

Declan didn't say anything for a long time.

Ryan watched him. He'd known him nine years. He'd watched him in a classroom, and on a stream, and in a cellar, where he'd sat on the old net-makers' steps and heard fifty-one seconds with no rests in them at all. He knew what Declan's face looked like when he was thinking about a tempo.

"Okay," said Declan.

He didn't say anything else. He didn't need to.

"Yeah," said Ryan.
