There were two hundred and twelve steps up to the eye, and Hyv held on to his arm for every one of them.

He had to go up sideways. The stair was narrow, a stone spiral inside the dome's wall, and it hadn't been built for two people side by side. It had been built for one person at a time, carrying a lamp. So he went up it sideways, with his shield on his back and Hyv on the step above with both hands round his arm, going up backwards.

"You'll fall," said Ice.

"I won't."

"You're going up backwards."

"I can *see*," said Hyv. "I can see the step behind me. I can see everything." She went up another, backwards, holding on. "Sixty-one."

"You're counting them."

"So the steps know you're going up," said Hyv. And grinned at him, upside down from the step above, in the lamplight. With her horns nearly touching the curve of the wall.

He'd carried a pole up these steps in August, for Alice. The bottom end, the heavy one, with Jared at the top, both of them sweating. He'd been up and down them a hundred times. He'd never once been up them sideways, being counted.

"Two hundred and twelve," said Hyv, at the top.

And didn't let go.

***

The eye was the round hole at the very top of the dome, open to the sky. A ring of stone round it, wide enough to walk on, with a parapet. And on the south side, a bench. Room for two. Made of a pole that had held, with *Somewhere* written under a scar. It was Alice's bench. Hers and Milli's. Kei's lamp sat at one end of it, the one he'd put there the night after the longest night so the hill could see the round town.

Nobody sat on the bench except Alice. Everybody knew that.

So Hyv didn't sit on the bench. She walked him round the ring to the north side, where there was only the parapet. A low stone wall, knee-high, looking north. Up the road, past the ninety-four, toward a gold point at the bottom of the sky that was the hill.

"Sit," said Hyv.

He sat on the parapet.

She sat on his knee.

Not next to him. On him. Sideways, with her legs over his, and her arm round his neck, and her tail round his ankle, very firmly. Then she put her head against his and looked north, at the gold point, as if this were a perfectly ordinary way to look at things.

Ice went pink. He felt it go right up to his ears and stay there.

On the far side of the eye, with his map on his knee, by his lamp, Kei looked up.

"I'm working," said Kei.

"Work over there," said Hyv.

Kei looked at her for a moment. Then he picked up his map and his lamp and his pencil, got up, and went round the ring to the far side of the far side. He sat down again without a word and went on working.

Ice thought it was the funniest thing he'd ever seen. He didn't laugh. He was too pink.

***

She'd always been quiet.

Not in fights. In fights she was the loudest person he knew. *LEFT. THE LEFT ONE'S AWAKE. NOT THAT ONE.* Up a stair into Rook's Hall. But everywhere else she'd been quiet, the whole two years. She saw everything and said almost none of it. She'd sat four paces off at the hatch every morning for six weeks, then two, and said nothing. She'd said *me*, at the gate, the day he left for Masmark, one word, and then nothing for a long time after.

Since the old hall she said everything.

She said it at the long table. On Monday, with sixty people round it, he'd been explaining to Drew how you angle a shield so a thing slides off it. Drew fought off his shield. He wanted to know how it slid. He'd been at it for about ten minutes. The rim low, the slope, letting the weight turn it. And Hyv had been sitting beside him with her chin on her hand, watching him do it. And when he stopped, she'd said, out loud, at the long table, to everybody:

"You're very boring and I love it."

There'd been a silence about the length of a breath.

Then Gabe had screamed. Not a word. Just a scream, from the far end of the long table, with his face in his hands. Leaka had screamed *JARRED. JARRED DID YOU HEAR*. Jared had said *y'all*, very quietly, to his soup. Drew had gone very still and looked at his shield. And Ama Corrie, coming past the banner room door with her broom, had stopped, and looked in, and said:

"*Hm*."

And gone on.

***

She said it in the round market. She'd call across it, across the fountain and the ring of nine stones and the chestnut stall and forty people, at the second dusk. Not *Ice*. Just:

"*Come here*."

And he'd go. He didn't know why. He'd be at the smithy with Ina, or at the Hart with Suzu, or halfway up the north road with Tiger. And from the market, *come here*, and he'd put down whatever it was and go.

"He *WENT*," said Leaka, on Thursday, at the chestnut stall. Very loudly, to the whole round market. "*JARRED*. She said *come here* and he *WENT*."

"I saw," said Jared.

"He's *never* gone! He's never gone *anywhere* anybody said! He goes where *he's* going and everybody *follows him*—"

"He went," agreed Jared, and gave Leaka a chestnut, which was the only thing that ever worked.

She said it on the stream.

He streamed every night. His own eyes, his own POV, the way he always had. Three hundred thousand people, since the hill. On Friday, at the well, he was talking to Kotori about the tea when Hyv came into frame from the left. She put her face right up to his, so close the whole stream was horns and dark hair and two eyes. She looked straight into his eyes, which meant straight into the camera, and said:

"*Mine*."

And walked out of frame again.

He didn't read the chat. He never did, while he was in. But he knew what it would say. He could feel it, the way you could feel weather.

***

She said it out there too. Only by message, but she said it.

The phone went on the kitchen counter on Saturday morning, in Malacca, at a quarter past eight. He'd just come out of the egg and put the kettle on. Gabe was in the doorway with his kopi.

*come here*

Then, a moment after, a pin. A little map. The eye, on the roof of the dome. In the game.

*i'm out here*, he wrote. *out there. it's morning.*

*i know*, wrote Hyv. *come here anyway. tonight. 8. the eye.*

*okay*

*good. you're very boring. i love it.*

He looked at it for a long time with the kettle going.

"What," said Gabe, from the doorway.

"Nothing."

"You've gone *pink*," said Gabe. "In *real life*. At *eight in the morning*." He came across the kitchen and looked over his shoulder at the phone before Ice could turn it over. He read it. Then he put his kopi down very carefully on the counter, and walked out of the kitchen, and into his own room. He shut the door. And screamed into a pillow, loudly enough that the auntie next door knocked on the wall.

He came back out about five minutes later. Red in the face. He picked up his kopi off the counter where he'd left it and stood in the doorway again, holding it, not drinking it.

"Dec," said Gabe.

"Yes."

"Eleven years," said Gabe. "I've been loud about you for eleven years." He looked at the kopi. "On every stream. In every chat. At every gate. *That's my best friend*. To anybody. Whether they'd asked or not. Somebody had to be. You weren't going to be loud about you. You'd just stand there being okay." He swallowed. "I thought it was always going to be just me."

Ice didn't say anything. The kettle clicked off behind him.

"It's not just me," said Gabe. "Now." He was going red again, in a different way. "Somebody else is being loud about you. Out loud. In front of everybody. At the *long table*." He wiped his face with the back of the hand that wasn't holding the kopi. "I'm so *glad*, Dec. I'm so glad it's not just me."

"You were first," said Ice.

"I *know* I was first," said Gabe. "I'll *always* be first. I've got eleven years on her." He drank the kopi at last, all of it, in one go, the way he did when he didn't know what to do with his face. "She's catching up *fast*, though."

"She is."

"She sent you a *pin*."

"She did."

"To the *eye*," said Gabe, and went back into his room, and screamed into the pillow again. The auntie knocked twice.

***

On the second Tuesday, they went down the north tunnel together. Just the two of them, the way they used to, for the levels. Down past the cold place where the old stone met the new, into the deep workings, where the Delvers came up out of the rock in fours.

She didn't hold his arm in the tunnel. She called.

"Four. Two high, two low. The low ones first. The left high one's slow."

He went first. Shield, rim low, the slope. The two low ones hit it and slid. He cut one on the beat. Her arrow took the other through the joint of its stone shoulder. The high ones came down off the wall, and he was already where the slow one was going to land. The river. Lucan's. He'd had it so long now he didn't think of it as borrowed.

[ Lv 65 ]

He stood in the deep tunnel with his sword down and his shield up and the four Delvers in pieces round his feet, at ninety-one per cent.

"You went first," said Hyv. From the dark behind him, where she'd been calling.

"You said the low ones first."

"I said it, and you *went*." She came up beside him. She took his arm again, both hands. In the tunnel, in the dark, with nobody to see. "You're *so* boring," she said. "I love it."

He looked down at her.

"You keep saying that," said Ice.

"It keeps being true."

***

He asked her at the eye. On the Wednesday after, at the second dusk, on the north parapet. She was on his knee again, with her arm round his neck and her tail round his ankle, looking north at the gold point at the bottom of the sky. Kei was on the far side of the far side, working.

"Why now?" said Ice.

She didn't pretend not to know what he meant. She never did. She saw everything.

She was quiet for a long time.

"I've never been loud about anything I loved," said Hyv. "Not ever. Not out there. Not in here, before." She was looking north, not at him. "I'd keep it. Inside. Like the tail round the chair leg. You don't let it show. And then nobody can—"

She stopped.

"Nobody can what?" said Ice.

"Say it's silly," said Hyv. "Or look at it funny. Or take it." She was holding on to his neck a bit tighter. "I loved the bow for months before I told anybody I was any good. I loved calling before anybody knew I could. I loved *you*—" She went red, to the horns, in the dusk. "For *ages*. Before I said *me*. At a gate. One word. And then I didn't say anything else for *months*."

"I know."

"And on the hill, at the north stone," said Hyv, "I said I'm always going to go first. And then I thought, but I'm always going to be *quiet*, too. Even going first. I'll go first and then be quiet about it." She looked at him at last. Edge to edge, close. "And I didn't want to be. Not about you. Not any more."

Ice didn't say anything for a while.

He thought about a group chat called *operation tail*, a year ago, in January. Three people in it. Messages on a counter, face down and then face up, for a week. *it's about hyv.* *you're not a rock.* And then Thea, on her own, at the end. *if you want to. it's allowed.*

He hadn't known, until somebody wrote it, that he'd thought it wasn't.

"It's allowed," said Ice.

Hyv went very still on his knee.

"That's Thea's," she said.

"I borrowed it. Somebody told me, once. Nobody had ever told me before." He looked at her. "So I'm telling you. It's allowed. Be loud."

She looked at him for a long time. Her eyes did something he'd never seen them do. They stopped looking at everything, and just looked at one thing.

"You'll go pink," said Hyv.

"I'll go pink."

"Every time."

"Every time," said Ice.

She put her face in his neck. She didn't say anything. For once in a fortnight, she was quiet. He held on, on the north parapet at the eye, with the gold point at the bottom of the sky. Kei, on the far side of the far side, very carefully did not look up from his map.

***

The letter from the east came on Thursday.

Not to Constellation. They weren't a guild over five hundred; they were sixty-two, and a sheep. It came to Ice, from Kestrel, folded very small, the way her letters always were. Kei read it out at the long table at the second dusk, because Ice asked him to.

*There's a third door. I said spring. It's spring on the first of March. A city on the east coast, the fiftieth level, called Saltreach. White cliffs. A black harbour. A tide that goes out a mile, twice a day. And under the bay, when it's out, a drowned city with a great door in it. They're calling it the Low Tide.*

*VANTA's going. Sable Court's going. Meridian's sending twenty. There are four new guilds there already, on the coast, that nobody's heard of.*

*Come and lose to me.*

*— K.*

Kei put the letter down.

"Who wants to go east?" said Kei.

Nobody said anything.

It wasn't the silence of people who didn't want to. Ice could feel it. It was the silence of sixty people who'd each got something else, at home, in the spring, and were waiting to see if they were the only one.

Suzu said it first. He said things first now.

"Spring," said Suzu. "I'm carrying a stone."

"I'm carrying it *with* him," said Salty. "It's a big stone."

"April," said Alice, at the far end. Beside her, Milli said *April*, and went red.

"April," said Chernn. And Deston, beside her, said nothing at all, and looked at the ceiling, and his ears went the colour of the banner.

"I'm cutting a pole," said Jared. "For Chinese New Year. And then for April." He looked at Alice. "Y'all need a roof."

Kei looked down the long table for a long time.

"We're staying home," said Kei.

Ice wrote back that night. On the bench at the eye, with Hyv reading over his shoulder, which she did now, without asking, with her chin on it.

*We're home this spring. Somebody's carrying a stone. Somebody's getting married twice. Go first.*

*— I.*

"*Twice*," said Hyv. "Two *different* people."

"I know."

"You wrote it like it's the *same* people."

"Kestrel will know," said Ice.

***

He did it on the Friday. At a quarter to eight.

He hadn't planned it. He'd thought about it, a bit, since the parapet. *It's allowed. Be loud.* And he'd thought that if he was going to tell somebody a thing was allowed, he ought to be able to do it himself. At least once.

They were at the long table, all of them, for the words. Kei stood up at the head of it, the way he did now, every morning. Hyv was beside him, with her hands round his arm.

Before Kei could say anything, Ice turned round.

He took her face in his free hand, the way she'd taken his at the old hall. And he kissed her. On the forehead, between the horns. Quickly, in front of sixty-two people and a sheep, at a quarter to eight in the morning.

And let go.

Hyv stared at him.

She didn't say anything. She'd gone red. Not to the horns; past them, somehow. Her mouth was open. For the first time in a fortnight, for the first time since the old hall, she had absolutely nothing to say.

Gabe screamed. Obviously.

Leaka screamed. Rae put her hand over her eyes. Ina dropped a spoon. Kotori said *oh*. Suzu said *good*. And Ama Corrie, at the banner room door with her broom, said:

"*Hm*."

And then, after a moment, very quietly, in a voice Ice had never heard her use: "*Well*."

"You went *first*," said Hyv. Finally. Very faintly.

"Not at everything," said Ice. "At that."

He was pink. He could feel it. Right up to the ears, the whole of his face, in front of everybody, at the long table.

He didn't mind at all.

Kei, at the head of the table, waited until everybody had stopped screaming. It took a while.

"Set one to watch," said Kei.

"*So the rest may sleep*," said sixty-two people and, very nearly, a sheep.

Hyv said it into his shoulder. Loudly.
