Gigi had never been close to anybody on purpose, and she was finding out it took practice.

Near, she knew. She'd been near for a year and a half. A pace to the left, at a gate, at a gap, at the twenty-two. Near was easy. You stood where you could see somebody and they could see you, and you didn't touch, and nobody had to say anything. Isa had named it on the night she came home. *Not close. Near.* And Gigi had heard it from across the banner room and thought, *yes. That's what it is. That's all it can be.*

Then the twenty-second stone. *I'm sorry*, she'd said. First. At the edge of the world. And Thea had let her say all of it. And then said hers. And Gigi had said *close, can it be close*, and Thea had put out her hand.

Close was different.

Close meant your elbows touched on the rope. It meant sitting against the third stone of a ring on a hill with your shoulder against somebody's, for hours, and not moving away. It meant being looked at, in the light now, in the gold light from the middle, by somebody who'd decided to look. And not looking away yourself.

She was bad at it. She'd been bad at it for two weeks.

She was getting better.

***

The hill was different in the light.

They'd come up it in the nothing, on a rope, behind a candle. Forty paces of grit and cold moss they couldn't see. Now there was the middle, standing over the jar on the flat stone. And the hill came out of the dark round it, and stayed.

It was small. Smaller than the round market. A low round hump of grey stone and moss in the middle of a black lake, with twelve stones round the top. A flat one in the middle. You could walk all the way round the ring in about a minute. Gigi had. Many times. In the first week she walked round it every night, slowly, touching every stone, because she couldn't believe it was there.

And past the ring, down the slope, the still water.

You could see it now. Black, flat, going away from the foot of the hill in every direction to the edge of the light. And past the edge of the light, dark. Just dark. Ordinary, with depth in it. The middle lit the water, a little, all the way round, so it shone very faintly, like a black mirror. And in it, upside down, the hill. The ring. The flame.

And going away south across it, toward the edge they'd come from, the stones.

Three hundred. In a line, curving, left and then right, a step apart. You could see the first fifty or so, from the top of the hill, in the light. Little grey islands in the black. The rest went off into the dark. Gigi had stood at the south side of the ring on the second night and counted the ones she could see, out loud, the way Suzu had. And Hako had stood beside her and counted with her, in his own voice, not the other one.

At two hundred and seventeen, there was something that wasn't a stone.

***

She went down to it on the fifth day.

Not all the way. You couldn't; it was a long way out, past where the light went. She went down to the foot of the hill and stood at the edge of the still water, on the heather, with her staff. And looked out along the line of stones into the dark. Where it was.

She couldn't see it.

But she could feel it. That was the thing about thorn. You didn't stop feeling it when it was out of your hand. She'd grown hedges round the old gate at Briarwick in the first winter. Round the round town's wall on the longest night. Up through cobbles, through stone, through floors. And she could always feel them after, a little. The way you can feel your own hand in the dark without seeing it.

She could feel the knot of hawthorn at two hundred and seventeen, out in the still water. Where a stone should have been. Where a woman who kept a lamp had been ill one winter, a long time ago, and missed a spring.

It was alive.

She'd thought it wouldn't be. She'd thought it would just be a knot, a thing, a step. Wood, black and wet. She'd grown it out of nothing, in the still water, in the dark, with her staff in the water and her word. She'd thought it would hold for long enough for nine people to cross and then just be wood.

It was alive. She could feel it. Growing. Very slowly. In the cold, in the black, in the still water that was the dark's own water. Putting out something.

She stood at the edge of the water with her staff and couldn't breathe.

***

"What is it?" said Thea.

She'd come down behind her. Of course she had. Close. Gigi hadn't heard her. She'd been feeling the thorn.

"It's growing," said Gigi.

Thea looked out at the dark.

"The step," said Gigi. "The one I made. At two hundred and seventeen. It's alive. It's—" She didn't know how to say it. "It's putting something out."

Thea didn't say anything for a long time.

"Can we go and see?"

"It's a long way. In the dark. Past the light."

"We've done the dark," said Thea. "Three weeks of it." She held out her hand. Not far. Just out, to her left. Where the thorn was. "And it's not nothing any more. It's just dark."

Gigi looked at the hand.

Two weeks ago, at the twenty-second stone, she'd taken it, and it had been the hardest thing she'd ever done. She took it now. It was still hard. It was a bit less hard.

***

They went out along the stones together. With *Bramble* and *Hollin*, two of Chernn's candles, lit from the middle. Gigi in front, because it was her thorn. Thea behind, close, on the same stone sometimes when the stones were wide enough. Counting, out loud, both of them, the way Suzu had. So the stones would know them.

*One. Two.*

Past fifty, the light from the middle went. And it was dark. But just dark. The candles lit the stones in little circles, and past them, the black water. And above, the dark. And that was all it was.

*A hundred and fifty.*

Gigi stopped.

At the edge of the candlelight, to the left, on the still water. Something standing. Tall and black and thin. Swaying, very slightly. The way the Unlit swayed on the longest night, coming up out of moor pools, leaning toward lights.

It wasn't leaning.

It was standing on the water a few paces off, very still for an Unlit, and it was looking past them. Not at their candles. North. Back along the line of stones toward the hill. Toward the middle, standing over the jar, a long way behind them now, very small.

Looking at it.

Not looking *for* it. *At* it. The way you look at a fire in a hearth you've finally found your way home to.

"It's not looking for anything," said Thea. Very quietly.

"No," said Gigi.

The Unlit stood on the still water and looked at the middle for a long time. And then it sank. Slowly, without a sound, back into the black water, the way the shape had at a hundred and eighty. The water closing over it so flat you'd never have known.

They didn't say anything about it. They went on.

*A hundred and fifty-one.*

***

At two hundred and sixteen, Gigi stopped again.

She held *Bramble* out, low, over the water. The way Suzu had held the best candle, to look for the next stone.

And there it was.

The knot of hawthorn. Black, wet, twisted, as thick as a man's body, its top just breaking the still water a step on. Where she'd grown it. It looked the way it had looked three weeks ago, in Suzu's light. A knot of black thorny wood in the black.

Except for one thing.

At the top of the knot, on a thin black twig that hadn't been there three weeks ago, there was a flower.

One. Small. White. Five petals, the way hawthorn has. Open. In December, in the dark, in the still water past the edge of the world, a flower.

Gigi made a sound she'd never made in her life.

***

She knelt on the two hundred and sixteenth stone, in the cold, with *Bramble* in her hand. And looked at the flower. She couldn't stop looking at it.

Hawthorn didn't flower in December. It flowered in spring. In May; *may blossom*, people called it, out there, in old songs. She'd grown hawthorn in the game for two years. Round gates and walls and through cobbles. It had never once flowered. You didn't grow it for flowers. You grew it for thorns.

It had flowered.

Here. In the gap. In the place where a spring hadn't been.

"It's a spring," said Thea, behind her.

Gigi turned her head.

Thea was standing on the two hundred and fifteenth with *Hollin* in her hand, looking at the flower over Gigi's shoulder. Her face was doing something. Gigi had known that face a long time. Out there, before. In the dark of it, a long time ago, when everything was hard between them. And at a gate, and a gap, and the bottom of a lake, and the twenty-second stone. She'd never seen it do this.

"The spring that wasn't," said Thea. "The woman who kept it was ill. She couldn't walk out with a stone. So there wasn't a spring that year. Just a cross on the edge of the flat stone." She looked at the flower. "And you grew a step. In the gap. And it's flowered." She swallowed. "It's the spring. It's just late."

Gigi couldn't say anything.

"Three hundred years late," said Thea. "But it came."

***

They sat on the stones for a long time. Gigi on the two hundred and sixteenth, Thea on the two hundred and fifteenth, close, their knees nearly touching across the gap of black water between the stones. Looking at the flower in the light of two candles.

"I didn't know it could," said Gigi, at last. "Flower. I never grew it for that."

"Why did you grow it?"

Gigi thought about it.

"For the thorns," she said. "To keep things out." She looked at her hands. "That's all I ever grew. Round gates. Round walls. Things to keep things out." She was quiet. "I was good at it. Everybody said. *Good thorn.*" She almost laughed. "I kept everything out. For years."

Thea didn't say anything.

"Out there too," said Gigi. "Before." She made herself say it. Close meant saying things. "I kept you out. I blamed you for everything and I kept you out with it. Like a hedge." She looked at the flower. "I said that at the twenty-second stone. I know. I'm saying it again. Because I didn't know you could grow it for anything else."

"You grew it for a step," said Thea.

"I grew it so nine people could get across."

"So it's not just for keeping things out," said Thea.

Gigi looked at the flower for a very long time.

"No," she said.

***

They went back to the hill at the third dawn, along the stones, counting. Thea in front this time. Close.

Gigi didn't pick the flower. She'd thought about it. She'd wanted to; she'd wanted to take it back to the ring and show everybody. And put it on the flat stone beside the jar. She didn't. It wasn't hers. It was the spring's.

She told them, though. At the fire. Everybody.

Hyv made a sound and put her face in her hands. Kotori said *oh* very quietly and made more tea than anybody needed. Hako, in the voice, said *the first recorded flowering of hawthorn in the still water, in December, in the gap of a missed spring. The scientific community is* — and then stopped, and didn't finish, and said in his own voice, *I'm going to go and look tomorrow*.

Suzu, at the flat stone, by the jar, said nothing. But he went to the rim of the flat stone, where the notches were. To the cross, and the notch he'd cut beside it with Kotori's knife, with the little thorn under it.

And beside the thorn, with the same knife, very small, he cut a flower. Five petals.

***

Ice didn't say anything either.

He was at the north side of the ring, the way he always was. Facing out, with his back to the middle. He'd been there most of every night for two weeks. Not on watch, exactly. Nobody had asked him to watch. He just stood there, at the north, looking at the dark. The way he'd stood at the front of everything for two years.

Gigi went and stood next to him. She didn't know why. She'd never stood next to him on purpose. He'd been the swamp guy, the one at the front. She'd held a gap alone on the first longest night, two years ago, and he'd been a long way off in a fen holding something else. She'd never once stood beside him for anything.

He didn't look round.

"It flowered," she said.

"I heard."

"In the gap. The spring that wasn't."

"I know," said Ice.

She looked at the dark. North. Past the ring, past the still water. Where it was just dark, now, with edges, and depth. Not leaning.

"The longest night's in five days," she said.

"Yes."

"Isa says it forgets. On the longest night. Even with the middle lit. It comes south all at once, the way it always has. Because that's what it does." She'd had a letter from the round town that morning. From Ama Corrie, in Ama Corrie's hand, very small, the way she wrote in the Roll. *Isa says. On the longest night it gathers at the middle before it goes. Keep it lit.* "It comes here first. To look at it. Before it goes south."

"Yes," said Ice.

"All of it."

"All of it," said Ice.

She looked at him. At the side of his face, in the gold light from behind him.

"Are you frightened?" she said.

He thought about it. She watched him think.

"I'm at the front," he said. "And there's a ring behind me. Twelve stones. Nothing comes inside." He was quiet. "And eight people. And a flame that's been lit three hundred years." He looked north. "And a hawthorn in the gap that flowered in December."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the answer," said Ice. "I've never been at the front of anything with that much behind me."

***

Thea sang that night.

She hadn't sung since the jetty. In the game, a year and a half ago, at a jetty at dusk. Everybody had cried. Xola had cried so much she had to sit down. Thea had never sung again. Not on any stream, not in the round town, not on the longest night. Gigi knew. She'd been near for a year and a half, and listening, and Thea hadn't sung once.

She sang now. On the hill, in the ring, at the second dusk, sitting against the third stone with Gigi's shoulder against hers.

Not loud. Not to anybody. To the middle, maybe, standing over the jar. Or to the dark outside the ring, which wasn't leaning. Or to the still water, and three hundred stones, and a white flower at two hundred and seventeen.

Gigi didn't know the song. She didn't think it had words. Or it had words in a language the game didn't bother to translate. It went up, and round the ring, and came back off the twelve stones the way the bell had come back off the walls of the tower in Masmark. Soft.

Nobody moved while she sang. Kotori stopped with the pot in her hands. Hako stopped narrating. Xola put her face in Slep's coat. Hyv, by the fire, with her eyes that saw everything now, in the light, closed them. Suzu, at the flat stone, put his hand on the edge of it, on the notches.

At the north side of the ring, Ice didn't turn round.

But Gigi saw his shoulders go down. All the way. The way they did, Hyv said, when he finally put something heavy down.

***

When it was over, nobody said anything for a long time.

Then Thea leaned her head on Gigi's shoulder. Close. Not asking. The way you'd lean on a wall you knew was there.

Gigi didn't move away.

She'd thought, at the twenty-second stone, that close would always be hard. That she'd have to decide it every time. Put out her hand, take Thea's, not move away. Every time, on purpose.

She sat against the third stone on a hill in the still water with Thea's head on her shoulder. And found she hadn't decided anything. She just hadn't moved.

*Practice*, she thought. *That's all it is. Like thorn.*

You grew it to keep things out, for years. And then one day you grew it for a step. And it flowered.
