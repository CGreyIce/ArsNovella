The swamp guy came out of the south door with a pack on his back, and every single person in Constellation stopped breathing, and Hyvred could see all of them doing it.

That was the thing about her eyes, in here. Edge to edge. All of it, at once. She couldn't not see. She stood at the bottom of the south steps with her bow on her back and an arrow in her hand, two grey feathers and one white. And she could see the whole round market, every face in it, without moving her head.

Gabe by the fountain, with his shield on, looking like a man about to be sick. Deston beside him, very calm, with his hand on Gabe's arm. Kotori with a pot. Suzu doing nothing, which meant he was doing a lot. Riverbank on the steps, RX counting under his breath, Xav with his sketchbook shut on his knee. Hana and Milli with their hands on each other's arms. Leaka with both fists in her mouth. Kei on the top step with his map, very still. Higu beside him. Gigi at the side, a pace off from Thea. Thea and Xola holding hands so hard she could see their knuckles go white. Bee behind her, a little to the left, with her wing out.

Winter at the side with Niko and Newish and Chris. Winter looking at her. Not at him. At her.

*Because it's short enough*, Winter had said. She'd heard it. Of course she'd heard it. She heard everything too, in here, if she was listening.

And at the top of the steps, coming down, the swamp guy.

***

He came down slowly. He always came down steps slowly. A shield on his back and a sword on his hip, and the pack, the big one, for fourteen days of road. His hair did the thing it did. The teal streak, the one from his model, caught the first light coming down the east road. His face didn't do anything. It never did.

He looked at all of them. She saw him look. All of Constellation, sixty people, silent, on the steps and round the fountain at the first dawn on a Monday, for no reason anybody had told him. She saw him notice. She saw him not know why. She saw him look at Gabe, and Gabe look at the fountain very hard, and Deston look at the sky.

Then he looked at her.

At the bottom of the steps, by Wendy's gate, with an arrow in her hand.

He didn't stop walking. He came on down. Eleven steps. Ten. She counted them. She'd counted the two hundred and twelve so many times she counted every step now without meaning to.

Six. Five.

Her tail had gone round her ankle three times. She could feel it. She didn't care.

He came down the last step and stopped at the bottom, in front of the gate. In front of Wendy, who was standing where she always stood, with her hands in her pockets. And in front of Hyvred, a pace to the side of Wendy, with the arrow.

Wendy took her hands out of her pockets.

"I ask on the way out too, now," said Wendy. "Since the longest night." She looked at him. "Who'd stand next to you?"

And the swamp guy opened his mouth.

***

"*Me*," said Hyvred.

She said it before he could. She hadn't known she was going to say it until it was out. It wasn't in the one breath. It wasn't anything they'd practised by the stream. It just came out of her, the way *left* came out when something was coming down a slope. Before she'd decided, because there wasn't time to decide.

The round market was so quiet she could hear the fountain.

The swamp guy looked at her.

He'd shut his mouth. He was looking at her the way he looked at a door nobody had opened. She'd watched him look at doors for a year, on stream, in the fen and the drowned church and the Seven Below. She'd never once had him look at her like that.

She couldn't stop now. She'd started. If she stopped now she'd never start again. Winter's voice in her head, flat, on the edge of the stream at midnight. *You wait a long time. And then you don't.*

So she said the one breath.

"I like you," said Hyvred. "I've liked you since you were ten people and a swamp. Since before I was in the game. I'm not going to hit you." She held out the arrow. "So you know which way."

And she stopped. Because that was the one. *Don't change it. Don't add anything. Don't take anything out.*

She stood at the bottom of the south steps of the Hall of Roads with sixty people watching. Her arm was out, with an arrow on her flat palm. Two grey feathers and one white. Her face was so hot she thought it might actually be on fire, in here. Her tail was round her ankle four times. Her eyes, the ones that saw everything edge to edge, all at once, were looking at one thing. Only one.

***

He put his hand up to his shoulder.

To the little floating light there. The Eye. The thing that let a hundred thousand people see out of him, every day, for a year. She'd watched through it since it was ten people. And he touched it, once.

It went out.

[ Ice has gone dark ]

She stared at the place where it had been.

"*Not for them*," said RX, on the steps, very faintly, as if he couldn't help it.

The swamp guy looked at her. At her face. At the arrow on her palm.

"I know," he said.

Hyvred's brain stopped.

"You—" she said. "You *know*?"

"I've known for a while."

"You've *KNOWN*?" Her voice went somewhere it had never been before. Up. A long way up. "*How long is a while?*"

"Since the step," said the swamp guy. "Outside the banner room. *No*." He thought about it. "Maybe before. I'm slow about some things."

"*SINCE THE STEP*?" That was September. That was *four months*. "Then *why didn't you*—"

"I didn't think I was allowed."

She stopped.

The whole round market had stopped. She could see all of it, edge to edge. Sixty people, and nobody moving. At the side, Thea had let go of Xola's hand and put both hers over her mouth. Xola's gills had gone flat, the way they almost never did, and she was crying.

"*Allowed*," said Hyvred.

"Somebody told me it was," said the swamp guy. He didn't look at Thea. He didn't need to. Hyvred saw Thea, at the side, go very red behind her hands. "So."

He looked down at the arrow on her palm.

Then he took it. Gently, the way he took the cup Kotori brought him every night. Not by the point, by the middle of the shaft. He turned it over and looked at it. At the scratch from the rock, a finger's length from the tip. At the fletching. Two grey feathers and one white.

"So I can find it," he said. "In the dark."

"Winter did the feather," said Hyvred. Her voice had gone very small. "Niko did the rest. It remembers the cold place. Where we sit. If you hold it up a long way off, it leans. Toward—"

"Here," said the swamp guy.

"Yes."

He put it through the strap of his pack, over his shoulder, where he could reach it. Where he'd see the white feather every time he turned his head.

Then he looked at her.

"I like you too, Hyv," said the swamp guy.

***

She'd tell Bee about it afterward, on a call, at four in the morning. She'd tell it about forty times, and Bee would listen to all forty without once saying *you've told me this*. She'd tell Xola, and Xola would scream. She'd tell Thea, and Thea would cry, and then say *I knew it, I knew it, I KNEW it*, and then cry again.

She'd never tell anybody what it felt like. She didn't have the word. Niko was right. Some things you couldn't say on an edge.

She hit him.

She didn't mean to. She hadn't hit him since the step, since the night the ten came home in September. She'd stopped, and not known why, and then known. And now she hit him. Her fist went into his shoulder, the one with the shield strap, the way it had every time he came back from anywhere for five months, without her ever deciding to.

"That's for *knowing*," said Hyvred.

He didn't move. He never moved when she hit him. He looked at her.

She hit him again.

"That's for *allowed*."

"Okay," said the swamp guy.

And then, because she was impulsive, because she'd always been impulsive, because she'd waited a year and Winter said you didn't wait any more, she stepped forward. One step. Off the paving and into the space in front of him, where there wasn't any space. She put her arms round him, pack and shield and all, as far as they'd go, and her face into the front of his armour.

She'd never done that. Not to anybody. Not in the game.

She felt him go still.

She felt him not move for a second. Two. The way he went still when something came at him and he was deciding where to be. And she thought, very clearly, with her face in his armour, *oh no. Oh no, I've got it wrong. He doesn't*—

His arms came round her.

Not fast. Carefully. The way he held the lamp out to the side so the light would go where you put your feet. One arm round her back, over her bow. The other hand coming up, slowly, and resting on her head. Between the horns. The way it had once, in a dungeon, the day she'd hit him twice and he'd patted her head and she'd said *mrp* and hidden behind Bee's wings for an hour.

"*Mrp*," said Hyvred, into his armour.

"I know," said the swamp guy, very quietly. "It's allowed."

***

The round market went off like a firework.

She didn't see all of it. For the first time in a year in the game, she didn't see everything edge to edge. She had her face in the front of somebody's armour and her eyes shut. She heard it, though. Everybody heard it.

Gabe first. Of course Gabe first. A noise from the fountain like a man who'd held his breath for a week and been allowed to stop. "*DEC!*" And then something about SimpleMMO that nobody understood. Xola screaming. Not words. Just screaming, with joy, on and on. Leaka screaming with her, *ICEY BOI*, *ICEY BOI*, until Curse put a hand on her arm and she screamed into his sleeve instead. Higu laughing. RX shouting *I COUNTED THEM, I COUNTED THE STEPS, IT WAS ELEVEN*, which was true and meant nothing.

Thea crying. Hyvred could hear her. Laughing and crying at the same time, the way she'd done on a bridge in July, and saying, over and over, *I knew it, I knew it, I knew it*.

And under all of it, the drum. Newish, at the side, had taken it off her back and started to play. *Tempo.* Steady, on the beat, the way it went on the high fells. Not loud. Just there. As if the round market needed something to keep time to.

Hyvred stood at the bottom of the south steps with her arms round the swamp guy and his hand between her horns. And she let the whole round town go off round them, and didn't look at any of it.

She'd see that window later, on Tif's clip. She'd cry at it in her room in Singapore at five in the morning, with the lights off. She'd watch it eleven times.

***

Wendy let it go on for a long time. Wendy was very good at letting things go on.

Then she cleared her throat. Loudly. The way she did at the gate when somebody had been standing in front of it too long.

Hyvred opened her eyes. She let go. She stepped back, one step, onto the paving. Her face was so hot she thought it would never be cool again. The swamp guy let her go, slowly, his hand coming off her head last. He was looking at her. His face was doing something.

She'd never seen it do anything. Not once, in a year. She'd seen the corner of his mouth go, sometimes. She'd seen him laugh out loud on the floor of a tunnel once, at Xav. She'd never seen *this*.

It wasn't much. You'd only have seen it if you'd been looking. She was looking. She was the one who saw everything.

He'd gone pink.

The swamp guy. Pink. In front of sixty people and a fountain.

"*Oh*," said Hyvred. "Oh, you've gone *pink*."

"Yes," said the swamp guy.

"You *never* go pink."

"I know."

"Xola said you went pink in *POV*. On the VOD. When I— at the stream—" She stopped. "*You've been going PINK*?"

"Apparently," said the swamp guy. "I can't see it."

Hyvred laughed. Out loud, at the bottom of the south steps. A real one, the kind she'd only ever done on stream before, at something stupid. It went up into the dome and the pigeons went up off the eye.

At the gate, Wendy looked from one of them to the other.

"Okay," said Wendy. "So that's who'd stand next to you." She looked at the swamp guy. "Fair?"

"Fair," said the swamp guy.

"Then the gate's open," said Wendy. And she stood aside.

***

He didn't go straight away.

He stood at the open gate with his pack on and the arrow through the strap, and looked at all of them. Constellation, all sixty, on the steps and round the fountain. Still making a noise, though it had gone softer now, the way a crowd goes softer when the thing it's been waiting for has happened.

He looked at Gabe.

"Tonight," he said. "The Thornway."

"I can tank," said Gabe. His voice was very thick. "I can *tank*, Dec. Go."

He looked at Kei, on the top step.

"I'll tell you what it's like," he said. "Masmark."

"You'd better," said Kei.

He looked at Thea, at the side, red-faced, with her hands still over her mouth. He didn't say anything to her. He didn't need to. He nodded, once. And Thea took her hands away from her mouth and nodded back. Hyvred, who saw everything, saw it. Something between the two of them that was very old and very simple and had nothing to do with her at all, and was about her anyway.

Then he looked at Hyvred.

"Fourteen days," he said.

"I know."

"Then you're fifty."

"I'm *forty-seven*."

"You'll be fifty by March," said the swamp guy.

"*February*," said Hyvred.

He looked at her for a long moment. Something happened at the corner of his mouth.

"February," he agreed.

And he went out of the gate, down the south road, into the first light, with the pack on his back and a white feather over his shoulder. He walked slowly, the way he always walked. He didn't look back.

She watched him go.

She watched him all the way to the curve of the road, where the round market ended and the fields began. All the way to the first milestone, a mile out, where the road turned east. She could see him the whole way, edge to edge, a small figure on a grey road with a shield.

At the milestone, he stopped.

He didn't turn round. He reached up over his shoulder and took the arrow out of the strap, and held it up in front of him, flat, on his palm. The way she'd held it out to him at the gate.

She saw it lean.

Back. Toward the round town. Toward the Hall of Roads, and the north tunnel under it, and the cold place at the end where they sat. Toward her.

He looked at it for a while. Then he put it back through the strap, and went on.

***

His first letter came that night, at the second dusk, while she was in the banner room trying not to look at the door every thirty seconds and failing. Bee had stopped pretending not to notice about an hour before.

[ Letter · from Ice
day 1. 23 miles
there's a goose on the road that won't move
it leans the right way
the arrow. not the goose ]

She read it four times.

Then she wrote back. It took her nineteen minutes. She wrote eleven different letters and deleted ten. Bee timed it.

[ Letter · from Hyvred
the goose is probably a boss
don't fight the goose
day 1 here: 47.4. february. i said february
also you went PINK ]

She sent it before she could change it. Then she put her face down on the long table, on her arms, beside Bee's, and laughed into her sleeve. Bee, without looking up from her drawing, moved one wing over her. Not a little this time. All the way.
