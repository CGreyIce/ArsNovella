The south-west road under the road smelled of hawthorn.

Gigi noticed it on the first step down. Bee had hung the banner on the hooks and the seven lines round the ring had lit, and the iron had sunk away into a stair of light. Drew went down first, and Thea after him with her great shield on her arm. Then the rest, one at a time. And as Gigi stepped off the last stair of light into the round room at the bottom, with its seven tunnel-mouths and its ring of stones round a star, she smelled it. Coming out of the south-west tunnel on a cold draught.

May blossom. Bitter and sweet at once, like almonds and something underneath that wasn't almonds. She'd have known it anywhere. She'd woken up in it.

"That's the Briarwick road up there," she said, before she could stop herself. "The south-west. It's hedges all the way. Our hedge is three hundred years old."

Nobody answered. She hadn't expected anyone to. She'd said it to the tunnel, really.

But at the front, at the mouth of the tunnel, Thea turned her head. Just a little. As if she'd heard.

***

Drew had put them in an order. He always did.

Thea at the front, with the shield. Drew at her left, half a pace back, where he could slip in and answer whatever came at her shield side. Salty at her right with his greatsword on his shoulder, not saying anything, because Salty never said anything in a tunnel. Then the second rank: Xola and Slep on the right, the trident and the glaive. PenguChi on the left with the long spear. And Gigi in the middle, behind Thea, with her hawthorn staff, where she could see everything in front and lay thorn across it. Behind her, Tif with her staff and her ears, and Niko and Tiger at the back, Tiger already bored.

"Behind Thea," Drew had said to Gigi at the hatch, quite casually, as if it were nothing. "Middle. You can see the whole front from there."

Gigi had looked at him. He'd looked back. Neither of them had said anything. He'd put her directly behind Thea on purpose. Of course he had. Drew didn't do anything that wasn't on purpose. She'd decided at the hatch, standing there with her staff, not to make it into a thing.

It was very hard not to make it into a thing.

She walked behind Thea for an hour down the south-west road. The tunnel was round and old, with roots coming through the ceiling. Hawthorn roots, she was sure of it now, black and twisted, the roots of the hedges on the road above. A faint line of light ran along the floor where the Seven Below had sent it when they kept the hall. And she watched Thea's back.

She couldn't help it. That was the trouble. It was the first time in her life she'd been behind Thea for an hour with nothing to do but walk. And she watched her. The way Thea held the shield, not braced, loose. The way she walked on the balls of her feet. The way her head turned a fraction at every sound, left, right, listening. The way she put her hand up, once, to touch a root that came down low across the tunnel, as if checking it was there.

Gigi watched all of it. And under the watching, very quietly, like a pot starting to simmer, she felt the old thing begin.

*She's holding the shield too low.* *She's too far forward.* *She'll get caught on that root.* *Why's she stopping there?* *That's not where you'd stop.*

None of it was true. Gigi knew that. She could see it wasn't true. Thea was holding the shield exactly where it needed to be, for a Turn. She'd been taught by someone who knew what they were doing. Thea was good. She was better than good. She'd been at the front of a line for ten weeks and it showed in everything.

And still the old thing simmered away under Gigi's ribs, looking for the wrong. Out of habit. The way your hand goes to a light switch in a house you've moved out of.

She held her staff tighter and walked.

***

The first Delvers came at the second bend.

There were three of them, pony-sized, with shovel hands and no eyes and a pink star of feelers on the end of the nose, twitching. They came out of the walls. That was what Delvers did. You'd be walking along a tunnel and the wall would bulge and crack, and a mole the size of a pony would come out of it at you with its hands going like spades.

[ Delver · Lv 31 ] ×3

Thea took the first one on the shield and Turned it right into Salty. Salty's greatsword came round once, silently, and it was over. The second came out of the wall on the left, and Drew was there. *Slip*, *Answer*: the step aside and the cut back that Drew did better than anybody Gigi had ever seen. The third came out of the floor right under PenguChi's feet. PenguChi, who'd been holding for nine seconds, put the spear straight down into it without looking.

Gigi didn't do anything. There was nothing to do. Three Delvers, from three directions, and nothing to lay thorn across.

She stood behind Thea with her staff and felt useless, and furious about feeling useless. Then she was furious about being furious. That was the old thing again, in a different coat.

"Good," said Drew, mildly, to nobody. And they went on.

***

It went on like that for two hours. Delvers out of walls. A root-thing that came down out of the ceiling like a rope with teeth, which Thea Turned into the wall and Niko pinned there with one spoken word, *Hold*, so that it hung there twitching while Xola put the trident through it. A nest of small blind things in a side chamber that Tiger skimmed into and out of so fast that by the time anyone else got there they were dead.

[ Skimmer · Skim (Advanced 91%) ]

Gigi laid thorn twice. Once across a side-tunnel, so a Delver coming out of it had to come bent double and slow. Once in front of Tif, when a root came down at her from behind. Both times it was right. Both times it held.

Both times, she noticed, she'd been watching Thea instead of the thing she laid it for, and only just caught it in time.

She didn't say anything about that. Nobody noticed. Probably.

Except that after the second time, as they walked on, Xola dropped back half a pace from the second rank, until she was walking almost level with Gigi on the right. Not next to her. Near.

She didn't say anything. She didn't look at Gigi. She just walked there, with the trident on her shoulder and her gills moving slowly in the cold tunnel air.

Gigi didn't know what it meant. She didn't ask.

***

The chamber at the end of the south-west road was round, and very big, and full of holes.

Drew had told them about it at the hatch. It was the reason Kei kept putting tens down this road. Two had gone down already, and neither had got past the end. *There's something at the far end*, Drew said. *She comes out of everything at once.*

It was the size of the Hall of Roads' floor, nearly. A dome of packed earth and stone, with hawthorn roots coming through the ceiling in great black ropes, and the floor dropping away in a shallow bowl. And all round the walls, at every height, there were holes. Dozens. Round black burrow-mouths, each one big enough for a pony to come out of, some low at the floor, some high up near the roof. And one, the biggest, straight ahead on the far side, at the bottom of the bowl, where the line of light on the tunnel floor ran into it and stopped.

"There," said Drew quietly. "That's hers."

They stood at the mouth of the tunnel and looked.

Nothing moved. The chamber was silent. You could hear the roots creak. You could hear Patience, who wasn't there, which was to say you could hear Slep not saying anything about Patience, who was upstairs with Ama Corrie and had been very upset about it.

"When we go in," said Drew, "she comes out. And so do all of them. Every hole. That's what happened to the last two tens. They stood in the middle, and things came out of every hole at once, and they couldn't hold the middle." He looked at Gigi. "So we don't let them come out of every hole."

Gigi looked at the holes. Thirty, forty of them.

"You want me to lay thorn across all of them," she said.

"All but one," said Drew. "Her one. The big one. We leave that open. Everything that wants to come at us has to come out of that one hole, one at a time, bent double." He looked at Thea. "And you stand at it."

Thea looked at the big black hole at the bottom of the bowl. Then at Drew.

"And Turn them," she said.

"Left to Slep," said Drew. "Right to PenguChi. Salty on the next one. Me on whatever gets past. Xola, Tiger, Niko, anything that comes out of the walls if the thorn goes. Tif on everyone." He looked from Thea to Gigi and back. "It only works if the thorn's there before they come. And it only works if the Turn's there when they do. One of you's late, it all comes apart."

Nobody said anything.

"Decide," said Drew. "Every time."

***

It came apart.

Gigi would go back over it that night, again and again, on the roof, with Higu's hand under hers. She'd never be sure exactly when it started to go. But she knew why.

They went in. Thea went down the bowl at a run with the shield up, to the big hole at the bottom. Gigi stood at the top, in the tunnel mouth, where she could see every burrow at once, and started laying.

*Lie.*

[ Lay · Grade 81% ]

The thorn came up out of the earth across the first hole like a hedge growing in fast motion. Black and tight and full of spines. Then the second. Then the third, the fourth, round the wall, low holes first, then high ones, a word for each, *lie, lie, lie*. Her staff moved and the hawthorn smell filled the chamber. And deep in the earth, all round them, something woke up.

You could feel it. A shudder in the floor. Then the sound of digging. In every hole at once, coming closer.

Gigi laid faster. Twelve holes. Fifteen. Twenty. Her hands knew this; she'd laid thorn across a counter with something coming over it. Twenty-five. Thirty. The thorn spreading round the chamber wall in a black ring, hole after hole.

And at the bottom of the bowl, Thea was standing at the big hole with her shield, alone. Her weight was on the balls of her feet, ready. The digging was coming up the hole toward her.

Gigi looked at her.

She didn't mean to. She was laying the thirty-first. She glanced down, once, at Thea. And the old thing came up under her ribs like a hand. *She's too close to it.* *She's standing wrong.* *She'll be knocked flat.* *She always does this.* And Gigi's eyes stayed on Thea for one second too long, looking for the wrong.

The thirty-second hole was high up on the left wall. She laid it late.

Not very. Half a second. But half a second was all it took. The thorn came up across the hole just as the first brood-thing burst out of it. It was a blind, clawed thing like a Delver, smaller and faster, and it came through the half-grown hedge before it closed. Then another. Then four, out of that one hole. And they didn't go to the big hole at the bottom where Thea was, because they didn't have to. They came down the side of the bowl at her from above and behind, while she was facing the wrong way.

[ Burrow-brood · Lv 30 ] ×6

"*Thea, left!*" shouted Drew.

Thea turned. She was quick, she was so quick now. She got the shield round and took the first one. But the big hole at her front chose that moment to open. Up out of it came something enormous. Pink-nosed and blind, with hands like ploughshares, and a hide like a hillside.

[ The Burrow Mother · Lv 34 · Elite ]

It came out of the hole straight into Thea's back.

She went down. The shield went one way and Thea went the other, and three of the brood were on her before she'd hit the ground. Gigi heard her make a sound. She saw her health in the party window go from green to yellow in one long lurch.

[ AltheaVT · 41% ]

And in the half-second after that, standing at the top of the bowl with her staff, Gigi heard herself start to say it.

"You were—"

*You were in the wrong place.* It was right there. It came up out of her like water out of a spring, as easily as it ever had. *You were too far forward. You were facing the wrong way. You were—*

She heard it. She heard the first two words come out of her mouth into the chamber, and she heard where they were going. And she stopped.

She stopped dead, with her mouth open and the sentence half out of it. For a second, the whole chamber seemed to stop with her.

"*My fault*," said Gigi. Loud. Louder than she'd ever said it. "That was me. I laid it late. *I was watching you instead of the holes.*"

Down at the bottom of the bowl, flat on her back with brood on her and the Burrow Mother rearing over her, Thea turned her head and looked up at Gigi.

Just for a moment. Across the whole chamber. With her health at forty-one percent and something with ploughshare hands coming down at her face.

Then Drew was there. *Slip*, *Answer*, and the Mother's hand came down on stone instead of Thea. Salty's greatsword took two brood off her at once. Tif rang something on her so bright it lit the chamber. And Xola came down the side of the bowl, not running, *pouring*. She went down it the way water goes down a slope, and hit the brood on Thea's legs with the trident and took them off her.

"*Out!*" said Drew. "Everybody out! Back to the tunnel! *Now!*"

They went out. They dragged Thea between them, Xola under one arm and Slep under the other. The Burrow Mother sank back down into her hole as if she'd never left it. The brood went back into the walls. The thorn across thirty-one holes withered slowly and went to dust.

***

They sat in the tunnel mouth, ten of them, in the light from the line on the floor, breathing.

Thea was at fifty-eight percent and climbing, slowly, under Tif's hands. She had a long scratch down one side of her face and her hair was full of earth. She was sitting with her back against the tunnel wall and the shield across her knees. She wasn't looking at anybody.

Gigi was sitting on the other side of the tunnel. As far away as it was possible to be in a tunnel.

She was shaking. She was furious with herself, and frightened, and so ashamed she could feel it in her teeth. And under all of it, very small, there was a kind of wonder. She'd heard herself stop. She'd heard the old thing come out of her mouth, two words of it. And she'd stopped it, with her own mouth. In front of everybody. In front of Thea.

Nobody said anything for a long time. Drew was sitting a bit apart, watching everyone's feet. Salty was cleaning his greatsword. Tiger was, for once, not bored.

Xola was sitting next to Thea with her trident across her knees. She was looking at Gigi. She'd been looking at Gigi since they sat down, with no expression on her face at all.

Thea spoke first.

"You were watching me," said Thea.

Gigi looked at the floor.

"Yes," she said.

"What were you looking for?"

Gigi didn't answer. Everybody in the tunnel was very carefully not listening. You could feel them not listening. Niko was looking at the ceiling. PenguChi was looking at her own feet. Even Tiger had gone quiet.

"Habit," said Gigi, eventually.

"That's not what I asked."

Gigi looked up. Thea was looking straight at her across the tunnel, with earth in her hair and the long scratch down her cheek. Her face was perfectly calm. She'd asked it the way she'd asked on the roof. *What were you?* Straight. Not hard. Just straight.

Gigi made herself say it.

"The wrong," she said. "I was looking for the wrong. In how you were standing. Out of—" She stopped. "Out of habit. I didn't mean to. It just started. Like it always did."

The tunnel was very quiet.

"Was there any?" said Thea.

Gigi looked at her for a long time.

"No," she said. "You were standing exactly right. You were perfect. I was looking for something that wasn't there. Same as always." She swallowed. "And I laid it late, because I was looking at you instead of the holes. And you went down. That's mine. That's all mine."

Thea didn't say anything for a moment.

"Then watch the holes," she said.

Gigi blinked.

"Next time," said Thea. "Watch the holes. Don't watch me. I'll be fine. I'm a wheel. I'll take whatever comes out of that hole and send it somewhere." She shifted the shield on her knees. "But I can only do that if I'm not watching behind me. If I'm watching behind me for things coming through the thorn, I'm not watching the hole. And she'll come out of it into my back. Like she did."

Gigi understood. She understood all at once, and it went through her like cold water.

"You have to trust the thorn," she said.

"Yes," said Thea. "And you have to trust the Turn. You have to not watch me. You have to lay the thorn and *leave me there* and trust that I'll be fine." She looked at Gigi steadily. "Can you do that?"

Gigi looked at her hands on her staff.

"It'll fall down," she said. Very quietly. It was out before she could stop it. The other old thing, the one that wasn't blame but was its cousin.

And Thea, across the tunnel, almost smiled.

"Then build it like it's going to," she said. "That's what you always used to say. *Build it like it's going to fall down.* You used to say it about everything."

Gigi stared at her.

She had used to say it. She'd forgotten. She'd said it about everything. Every stream, every plan, every project. She'd said it so often it had turned into a sort of joke. And somewhere in the bad year it had stopped being a joke and turned into something else. *It'll fall down.* About everything. About them. Until she'd made sure it did.

But before that, a long time before, it had been *build it like it's going to fall down*. Which meant: build it better. Build it so it holds when the bit you're sure of gives way. Build it twice.

"A hedge," said Gigi slowly.

"What?"

"You build a hedge in rows," said Gigi. "Two. Not one. Staggered. So if the first row goes, there's another behind it." Her hands had tightened on the staff. "I've only been laying one. Across each hole. One row." She looked at the chamber mouth. "If I lay two. Behind each other. Then when one goes, it doesn't matter. And I don't have to watch."

Drew, at the side of the tunnel, made a very small sound. It might have been a laugh.

"That's more thorn," said Niko. "Can you do that much?"

"No," said Gigi. "Not on my own. Not forty holes, twice."

"Then I'll hold the first row," said Niko. "While you lay the second. Spoken Edge. I can say *hold* to a hedge. I did it to a wingtip in March. A hedge is easier. It wants to hold."

Gigi looked at Niko. Then at Drew. Then at Thea.

Thea nodded.

"Again," said Thea.

***

They went in again.

This time Gigi didn't look at Thea once.

It was the hardest thing she'd ever done in the game. Harder than the counter. Thea went down the bowl at a run with the shield up and stood at the big hole, alone, with the digging coming up toward her. And Gigi stood at the top and didn't look. She looked at the holes. Only the holes. *Lie, lie, lie.* The first row, round the wall, forty holes, black thorn coming up across every one of them. And Niko beside her, saying *hold, hold, hold* to each one as it rose, in his quiet even voice, so that the thorn held.

[ Spoken Edge · Hold ]

Then the second row. *Lie, lie.* Behind the first, staggered, the way you'd plant a hedge. So that if a brood came through the first, it came into the second, bent double, slow, and Xola or Tiger was there.

She could hear the fight going on below her and she didn't look at it.

She heard Thea say *left*, and something go left. She heard Slep's glaive. She heard Thea say *right* and PenguChi's spear go in, with forty seconds behind it. She heard the Burrow Mother come up out of the big hole with a sound like a hillside falling. She heard Thea take it. The whole weight of it, on the shield. She heard the sound Thea made, a grunt, low, the sound of somebody being pushed by something very big. And she didn't look. She laid the next hole.

She heard Thea *turn*.

She'd never heard a Turn before. She'd seen them, on stream. You couldn't hear them on stream. But in the chamber she heard it. The shield's rim grinding round on stone, and Thea's feet, and a great weight going sideways that had meant to go straight. And the Burrow Mother went into the thorn.

Into Gigi's thorn. The first row, across the low holes on the left.

The first row broke.

Gigi heard it go. A sound like a gate coming off its hinges, the hawthorn tearing. She felt it in her staff, a jolt that went up both arms into her shoulders. And the old thing came up in her, for one second, *it'll fall down, it's falling, I said it would*.

The second row held.

She'd laid it behind the first, staggered, half a pace back. The Mother came through the first row and into the second and stopped. Stuck. Bent over, the way things came through thorn, with her great blind head down and her ploughshare hands caught in the spines.

"*HOLD*," said Niko.

[ Lay · holding · second row ]

And everyone was on her.

Salty's greatsword came down on her neck. Drew slipped in and answered under her arm. Tiger skimmed across her back and was gone again before she could turn. PenguChi had been holding since they came back in, two minutes or more. Her spear went in under the shoulder with all of it behind it. Gigi had never seen anything hit that hard in her life.

[ Sentinel · Patience · held 134 s · First strike ]

And Thea, up close, at the Mother's head, brought her hammer down.

***

There was one more thing. Gigi only found out afterwards, from Tif, that it had happened.

While she was laying the second row on the right-hand wall, a brood came through a hole behind her. A low one, by the tunnel mouth, where the first row had gone weak and she hadn't got to the second yet. It came out of the wall at her back while she was facing the other way, laying, not looking. It came at her neck.

And Xola was there.

Gigi didn't see her coming. She felt a hand in the collar of her coat, pulling her backwards hard. Then the trident went past her ear into the brood, close enough that she felt the wind of it. She sat down very suddenly on the floor of the tunnel mouth with Xola standing over her.

Xola looked down at her.

"Don't," said Xola.

"Thank you," said Gigi.

"I said *don't*."

"Thank you anyway," said Gigi.

Xola looked at her for a long moment, with the trident in her hand and the gills at her neck wide open. Then she looked at the chamber. At the second row of thorn, holding, with the Burrow Mother caught in it. And everybody on her, and Thea at her head with the hammer.

"You laid a second row," said Xola.

"It'll fall down," said Gigi, from the floor.

"It didn't," said Xola.

And she held out her hand.

Gigi looked at it. Then she took it, and Xola pulled her up, and let go at once. Then Xola went back down into the bowl, without another word, to stand at Thea's right shoulder where she belonged.

***

The Burrow Mother went down slowly, the way big things did. All of her, sinking into the bottom of the bowl like a hill going to sleep.

[ The Burrow Mother · defeated ]

And the line of light on the tunnel floor, the one that ran down the south-west road and stopped at the big hole, didn't stop any more. It ran on into the hole and down, out of sight. A second later, from somewhere far behind them, back up the tunnel toward the round town, there was a sound like a bell.

[ The South-West Road · walked to its end ]

Nobody said anything for a while. The thorn withered, both rows, and went to dust. The roots creaked overhead. Somebody was breathing very hard; it was Tiger, who'd skimmed eleven times.

Gigi stood at the top of the bowl with her staff, and her arms shaking. She looked down at last.

Thea was at the bottom, by the big hole, with her shield on her arm and her hammer hanging from her hand. Her face was scratched and filthy and her hair was full of earth. She was looking up at Gigi.

Neither of them moved.

"Good thorn," said Thea.

Gigi didn't trust her voice. She tried it anyway.

"Good turn," she said.

That was all. Neither of them said anything else. Neither of them needed to.
