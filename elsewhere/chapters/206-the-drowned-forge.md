Jun Liang came down the last of the switchbacks into Masmark singing *WE NEED A LEADER*, because it had worked the first time.

He'd sung it on the riverbank at Brackenford in February a year ago. The last of the seven to arrive, coming over the bridge, at the top of his voice. RX had counted them twice and got eight. Xav had laughed so hard he'd had to sit down. And then Penhallow had brought the charter, and Val had named it, and it had been Riverbank ever since.

So he sang it again. Coming down the north-west road into the biggest city in the world at the second dusk, the night before the island opened. With the other eight behind him and Xyro at the back, at the top of his voice, so the whole north-west harbour heard.

"*WE NEED A LEADER*—"

"We've got one," said Xav, behind him.

"*WE NEED A LEADER WHO*—"

"Jun Liang."

"*—WHO DOESN'T WALK ROUND GEESE*—"

Val kicked him. She did it without looking up from her book, walking, the way she'd kicked him under every table for nine years. He went on singing anyway, because he was Jun Liang and that was what he did. He liked singing. He liked people. He liked walking into places loudly so everybody knew he was there. He'd been like that since poly.

At the bottom of the road there was a fish cart. And standing beside it, with a fish on a stick, was the man he'd modded for.

***

He hadn't modded for a long time.

He'd been one of the first. Back when Dec streamed every week, years ago, to ten people and a swamp of chat, Jun Liang had been the one with the green sword. He'd banned the idiots and kept the chat tidy. Then Dec had streamed less, and Jun Liang had modded less. The green sword had sat beside his name in Dec's chat for years like a coat on a hook. Tif had done the work. He'd lurked. *satayboy*, with a green sword, saying nothing.

He'd typed once in a year. On the day of the duel. *!eve.* He didn't know why. It had just come out.

He looked at Dec standing by the fish cart now. At the shield and the plain lamp and the arrow in the strap of his pack with one white feather. At the woman with gold horns beside him, close.

Jun Liang stopped singing.

"*Oh*," he said.

"Hi, Jun Liang," said Dec.

"Hi," said Jun Liang. He looked at the woman with the gold horns. Then at Dec. Then at the woman again. He'd seen the clip of the gate. Everybody had. He'd watched it on the road eleven times, with Val saying *stop watching it* every time and then watching it over his shoulder. It was different, in person. "Hi. Hi. Hello."

"This is Hyv," said Dec.

"I *know* who Hyv is," said Jun Liang. "Everybody knows who Hyv is. Hyv's *famous*. Hyv's the most famous person in the *game*. She's more famous than *you* now."

Hyvred went red to the horns.

Dec looked at Jun Liang for a long moment. Then something happened at the corner of his mouth.

"Yes," he said. "She is."

Behind Jun Liang, Val made a small sound. He turned round. She'd stopped walking. She'd closed her book. She was looking at Dec with an expression Jun Liang hadn't seen on her in nine years. She'd been saying *declan's a bit weird* for years.

"*Oh*," said Val. Very quietly. "Oh, he's not weird any more."

"He's still a bit weird," said Ryan, beside her.

"He's still a bit weird," agreed Val. "But not *like that*."

***

The netloft was the smallest Hall Jun Liang had ever seen, and the best.

It smelled of fish. The roof sagged. There was one bench and a window and two iron hooks with a blue banner on them. The banner had sixty gold dots and a gold star in the middle that nobody had painted. And in the door, with her hands in her pockets, Wendy.

"I'm the gate," said Wendy.

"*WENDY*," said Jun Liang, and tried to hug her, and she held up one finger. Not that one. The first one.

"Everybody gets asked," said Wendy. "Every time. Who'd stand next to you?"

Jun Liang thought about it. He looked at the eight behind him, the poly lot, nine years. Xav with his sketchbook. RX counting the lamps on the lane. Malcolm with his stick. John with his mirror. Azac with his shield and his bread. Ryan. Val. And Xyro at the back, who wasn't poly but had walked fourteen days with them and said about four words, all of them good.

"All of them," he said.

"That's not a name," said Wendy.

"It's *nine* names," said Jun Liang. "I can say them all if you want. Sketch, theidiot, Serenity, Mango, Pingu, Resent, Sakami, xyrolizea, and—" He stopped. He looked past Wendy, into the netloft, at the man at the far end of the bench by the window with the gold-horned woman beside him. "And him. He's always been in the list. He's just never on it."

Wendy looked at him for a long moment.

"Fair," she said.

***

Kei read the twenty on the morning of the fourteenth, in the netloft, standing on the bench. There wasn't anywhere else to stand where everybody could see him.

"The Crown Below," said Kei. "A hall for twenty. Opens at the tenth dusk. Tonight." He held up the map. He'd drawn the island on it, very carefully, from the fish man's boat, and the steps going down. "We go in at eight. Not a minute before."

"Not a minute before," said Xav, from the floor, and Kei nodded at him.

"Ice tanks," said Kei. "Hyv calls. Gigi and Thea hold the gaps, Xola beside them. Salty and Azac on the line with Ice. Xav, RX, Ryan, Drew on the flanks. Higu and John see. Malcolm and Val, magic. Wendy, Xyro, bows. Bee, shields and walls." He looked at Jun Liang. "Jun Liang."

"Yes!"

"Hammer," said Kei. "And whatever else comes up."

"*Whatever else comes up*," said Jun Liang, delighted. "That's my *favourite* job."

"I'll call," said Kei. "From the middle. Like the eye." He rolled up the map. "Twenty. Nobody alone. Threes inside threes." He looked down at all of them, on the bench and the floor and the window seat, in the smallest Hall in Masmark. "That's all."

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Then Jun Liang said, because somebody had to, "*WE NEED A*—"

Val kicked him.

***

They went out to the island at the tenth dusk, and every boat on Great Lake Masmark went with them.

Jun Liang had never seen anything like it. He'd never seen anything like Masmark, but this was something else. The whole lake moving. Hundreds of boats coming off the harbours all the way round the city as the light went. Meridian's three blue barges with ten rowers each. Sable Court's narrow black boats, fast, leaving white lines. The Open Hand's great red ship, anchored out, sending boats. VANTA's two plain grey ones. A hundred others, every colour there was, all going the same way. Toward the island in the middle, with its tower, and the steps going down under the water.

And the fish man's fishing boat. Very low in the water. With twenty people in it who should have been in two boats. Salty rowing.

"*We need a bigger boat*," sang Jun Liang, quietly, so Val wouldn't hear.

"I heard that," said Val.

On the island, at the foot of the tower, where the old wide steps went down into the lake, the crowd was enormous. Thousands of people on the rocks and the shingle. Everyone who wasn't going in, come to watch the ones who were. And at the water's edge, where the steps went down, twenty or thirty parties of twenty. Each with their banner. Each standing apart, waiting.

The Open Hand, all in dark red, under their black open hand. Meridian, under the gold line. A woman in plain blue at their front, very small. Sable Court in black with silver at the cuffs, and a man at their front who never stopped moving. VANTA, plain, under their black banner with one white line, and a woman with a bow at their front, perfectly still.

And Constellation, at the end, under a blue banner with sixty gold dots and a star in the middle.

Nobody looked at them. Jun Liang noticed that. He'd been noticed his whole life; he made sure of it. Here, nobody looked. Twenty people in plain gear from a round town nobody had heard of.

Then the woman in plain blue at the front of Meridian turned her head and looked straight at them. At Dec, at the front, with his shield. And nodded. Once.

And the man at the front of Sable Court turned too. He looked at Dec across thirty paces of shingle and said, just loud enough to carry over the crowd:

"*You're slow.*"

"*You're early*," said Dec.

And the crowd, which hadn't been looking at Constellation at all, turned round, all of it, and looked.

***

The window came at twenty past eight. To everybody on the island at once.

[ The door is open. ]

And the lake drew back from the steps.

Jun Liang would never forget it. The water at the bottom of the island, where the old wide steps went down, just *moved*. It drew back on both sides of the steps like curtains being opened, slowly, with a sound like a long breath out. Two walls of green water standing up on either side, and the steps going down between them. Dry. Glistening. Step after step, down into the deep, toward a door at the bottom that you could see now. Old black stone, with a crown carved on it.

The crowd on the shingle went completely silent.

Then the first party went down. Sable Court, fast, in black, without looking back. The man at the front went down the steps between the two green walls of water like a river going down a hill. Then Meridian. Then VANTA. Then the Open Hand, twenty in dark red, in step. Then the others, one after another. And as each party went through the black door at the bottom, it closed behind them and opened again for the next. Every one into its own way down.

"*Constellation*," said Kei, very quietly, at the top of the steps.

They went down.

***

He counted the steps. Everybody did; you couldn't not. Between the two walls of green water, with fish swimming in them, looking out at you.

He counted a hundred and six to a landing. And on the landing, the black door with the crown on it. Through the door, a stair cut in the rock, going down. Dark, and dry, and cold.

"A hundred and six," said RX, behind him. "A hundred and six. Did everyone get a hundred and six?"

"A hundred and six," said John.

"That's the round town," said Hyvred, from the front, beside Dec. Her voice was odd. "The two hundred and twelve. The first flight's a hundred and six."

Nobody said anything.

They went on down the stair through the rock. And at the bottom of it, a long way down, a room.

***

It was a forge.

Jun Liang knew it the moment he came out of the stair. He'd been at Joss's forge behind the Hart for a year, working the bellows and singing while Salty and Ina and Drew and Gabe hit things. He knew what a forge smelled like, even a dead one. This one was very dead.

It was huge. A great round chamber under the lake, with a ceiling of black rock so high he couldn't see it. And water dripping from it, always, everywhere, in a slow cold rain. In the middle, a forge the size of a house. A great stone hearth, cold, black, with bellows on either side as big as boats. Their leather gone green with age. An anvil in front of it you could have slept on. And on the far side of the chamber, the way on. A great iron gate, a portcullis, down. Sealed. With a crown on it.

And chains. Running from the portcullis across the floor of the chamber to the forge, to the hearth, to a great iron ring set in the stone at the back of it.

"It's shut," said Kei. "Till the forge is lit."

"It's *wet*," said Salty.

He was right. Everything was wet. The cold rain from the ceiling came down on the hearth and the coals in it, black and sodden. On the bellows. On the anvil, which had water standing in it.

And at the edges of the chamber, where the rain ran down the walls, it pooled. Still water. Black and flat and still.

Things were standing up out of it.

***

"*Ten*," called Hyvred. "*No. Twenty. They're coming up out of the pools. All round. They're going to the—*" She stopped. "*There's nothing to go to. There's no light. Everything's dark. They're going to the lamps. Ours.*"

Everybody's lamps. Twenty people, twenty lamps. The only lights in the drowned forge.

"*Line*," said Kei. "*Ice, Salty, Azac. Line. Round the forge. Threes.*"

They made the line. Jun Liang watched it happen, the way he'd watched it on Dec's stream for a year from the outside. Dec at the front, shield up, going first, the way he always went first. Salty on his left with Right Size, the black sword the length of a man. Azac on his right with the biggest shield in the guild, saying *I'm a wall*. And round them, in threes, everyone else.

"*Smiths*," called Kei. "*Salty, no, Salty's on the line. Drew. Jun Liang. The forge. Light it.*"

"*Light it*," said Jun Liang. "With *what*? It's soaking!"

"*Whatever else comes up*," said Kei.

Jun Liang looked at him. Then at the forge. Then at Drew, who'd come out of the line and was standing beside him, looking at the sodden coals with his face doing the thing it did when he was watching somebody's feet.

"It's not the coal," said Drew. "Look."

Jun Liang looked.

The coal was wet. But under it, in the hearth, very deep, there was something. A glow. Very faint. Red. Like a coal under ash, a long way down, that had never quite gone out. In three hundred years, or three thousand, under the lake, in the cold rain.

"It's still alight," said Drew. "Underneath. It's just smothered." He looked at the bellows, as big as boats, green with age. "It needs air."

"Then we pump," said Jun Liang.

***

The bellows were as big as boats and as heavy as houses, and you couldn't work them on your own. It took both of them, one on each side of the hearth, pulling the great handles down with their whole weight. Leather that hadn't moved in three hundred years groaned like a ship.

*Whoosh.*

Air went into the hearth. The wet coal hissed. Deep under it, the red glow brightened. Just a little.

Behind them, on the line, something hit Dec's shield. Jun Liang heard it. He heard Hyvred call *left* and Thea say *Turn* and Gigi say *Lie*. He heard Salty's black sword go round in the dark and Azac say *I'm a wall*.

*Whoosh.*

The glow brightened. The coal on top steamed.

*Whoosh.*

It wasn't enough. He could feel it wasn't enough. They were pumping as hard as they could, him and Drew, and the fire under the coal was coming up so slowly. And on the line behind them, the things from the pools were coming in faster. Dec's window flickered in the corner of Jun Liang's eye. *91%. 84%.*

"*Faster*," said Drew, through his teeth.

"I'm *going* faster!"

"*It's not about faster*," said Drew. He was watching Jun Liang's feet. "You're out of time. We're both pulling at different times. Half the air's going nowhere." He looked up. "We need to pull together. On a beat."

Jun Liang looked at him.

On a beat.

At Joss's forge, behind the Hart, for a year, he'd worked the bellows and sung. And the fire had always done better when he sang. Nobody had ever been able to explain it. Ina had said the fire liked the company. Salty had said it was because Jun Liang worked the bellows in time with the song without noticing. Gabe had said it was magic and told the bellows about SimpleMMO.

"Drew," said Jun Liang. "Do you know *We Need a Leader*?"

Drew stared at him.

"Everybody knows *We Need a Leader*," said Drew. "You've been singing it for a *year*."

"Then pull on the *need*," said Jun Liang, and sang.

***

"*WE NEED A LEADER*—"

*Whoosh.* Both together, on *need*.

"*WE NEED A LEADER*—"

*Whoosh.*

The fire came up.

He felt it. Through the great green handles of the bellows, through his arms, through the stone floor. The air going in together, both sides at once, on the beat. The red glow under the coal brightening, and brightening, and the wet coal on top beginning to steam and then to hiss and then, at the edges, to catch.

"*WE NEED A LEADER WHO*—"

*Whoosh.*

He didn't know the rest of the words. He'd never known the rest of the words. He'd made them up every time. He made them up now, at the top of his voice, in a drowned forge under a lake, with a fire coming up through three hundred years of wet coal.

"*WE NEED A LEADER WHO WON'T LEAD US*—"

*Whoosh.*

"*WHO SITS AT THE END OF THE BENCH*—"

*Whoosh.*

"*WHO WALKS ROUND GEESE AND SWEEPS UNDER CHAIRS*—"

*Whoosh.*

Behind him, on the line, somebody laughed. He thought it was Hyvred. Then somebody else. Then he heard, very clearly, over the sound of the fire, Dec's voice, the one he'd modded for nine years, saying, quite calmly, to nobody:

"*That's not a leader.*"

"*IT IS NOW*," sang Jun Liang, and pulled.

The forge caught.

All of it at once. The coal went up in a great roar of gold and white. It filled the hearth and went up the black flue into the dark. Heat came off it like a wall, and light, so much light, all at once. Every corner of the drowned chamber lit gold. The cold rain from the ceiling hissed into steam as it fell.

At the edges of the chamber, the things from the pools stopped.

They'd been coming at the twenty lamps. Now there was a fire the size of a house in the middle of the room, roaring. They leaned. All of them, at once. Away from the lamps, toward the forge. And then, as the heat hit them, back. Back into the pools. Down into the still black water.

And on the far side of the chamber, on the chains that ran from the forge to the great iron ring, something moved. A clank. A grinding, deep in the stone. And the portcullis with the crown on it began, very slowly, to rise.

***

There was a ring of stones beyond the portcullis. A kept place. Twelve iron posts round a flat stone with a crown on it, and the cages on the posts already lit, as if somebody had known they were coming.

[ The Crown Below · the first flight · kept ]

[ *Nothing comes inside.* ]

They sat down in it. All twenty. On the stone, inside the posts, with the forge still roaring behind them through the open gate.

Jun Liang lay on his back and looked at the dark above, and his arms hurt at fifty per cent, which he'd turned up to that morning without telling anybody. Because Dec was on fifty and Salty was on fifty. And somebody had once said *I'm not standing somewhere softer*, and he'd thought that was the best thing he'd ever heard.

"Jun Liang," said Dec, beside him.

He turned his head.

Dec was sitting on the stone with his back against an iron post. His shield across his knees, at seventy-one per cent, with Hyvred asleep on his shoulder. She'd fallen asleep about four seconds after sitting down. Calling did that to you, apparently.

"That wasn't a leader," said Dec.

"I know," said Jun Liang. "I made it up."

"You always make it up."

"I always make it up," agreed Jun Liang.

Dec looked at him for a long time. The way he'd looked at the stream on the first day of the game, Jun Liang thought, a long time ago, when it was ten people and a swamp. And a man called satayboy with a green sword beside his name, lurking, saying nothing.

"Thank you for modding," said Dec. "All those years."

Jun Liang didn't say anything. For the first time in about nine years, he couldn't think of a single thing to say.

So he did the other thing. He sang. Quietly, for once. Not *We Need a Leader*. Something else, with no words, just a tune. The kind you'd hum in a kitchen. And Dec sat against the iron post with his eyes shut and Hyvred asleep on his shoulder, and listened. And at the far side of the ring, Val, who was going to be a voice actor one day and had a voice that made you trust her, started humming it too.

At eight in the morning the egg threw them all out, together, in the kept place, with the first flight behind them.
