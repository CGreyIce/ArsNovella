She saw it come because she could see again, and she would have given anything, for about an hour, not to.

They went in at eight. Thursday, the twenty-first of December, the longest night. Out of the dark onto the hill, inside the ring, the way they'd come out of it every night for three weeks. Twelve stones, standing. The flat stone in the middle with the worn crown. And over it, standing straight up out of the mouth of a plain clay jar, the middle. Gold.

She came out a minute before him. She always did. So she could see him come out of the dark.

Left side first. Like he was feeling for a wall.

"Hi," said Hyvred.

"Hi."

He looked at her. Then past her, north, at the dark outside the ring. She looked too.

It was already moving.

***

Isa had said it gathered. *On the longest night it gathers at the middle before it goes.* She'd said it at a long table, and on the north steps, and in a letter in Ama Corrie's small hand. Hyv had read the letter three times. *Gathers.* She'd thought it would be like weather. A wind getting up. A darkness coming over the sky like cloud.

It wasn't like weather.

It was like the still water standing up.

All of it. All round. Out past the foot of the hill, past the edge of the light, as far as she could see in every direction. The flat black still water that had lain round the hill for three hundred years without a ripple. Rising. Not in waves. The whole surface of it, everywhere, at once, going up. Slowly. Smoothly. The way the lake under the round town had stood up on the longest night two years ago, under the Seven Below, before he put it in a stream.

Except this wasn't a lake. This was the dark's own water. The place it came from. A great deal of it, Isa said.

It went up past the foot of the hill. Past the bottom of the slope. Up, and up, black and smooth and silent, a wall of still water all the way round the hill on every side. Higher than the stones. Higher than the ring. Until it stood round them like the sides of a well, and the hill was at the bottom, and the ring was at the bottom of the hill, and the middle was at the bottom of everything.

And then it leaned in.

***

"*Ice*," said Hyvred.

She didn't mean to say his name. She meant to call. She'd told him *I'll call it, for everybody*, at the north stone, on the eve. She'd been going to say *north, all round, rising, leaning*. It came out as his name.

"I see it," he said.

He was already moving. To the north side of the ring. The twelfth stone, the one they'd raised. He went to it the way he went to the front of everything. Not fast. Before it had decided where to come.

"*Places*," he said. Not loud.

They went. They'd practised it, on the eve, round the fire. Kotori had said *what if it comes from everywhere*, and he'd said *then we stand everywhere*. Nine people. Twelve stones. Seven of them at the stones, facing out, with their backs to the middle, and gaps between them that nobody could fill. Suzu at the flat stone, with the flame. Kotori beside him.

Hyv's stone was the seventh. East side. She stood at it with her bow and looked out.

She could see all of it.

That was the thing. She could see all of it. Edge to edge, in the gold light from behind her. A wall of black still water standing up round the whole hill, as high as a house, as high as the Hall of Roads. Smooth as glass, and leaning in. Toward the ring. Toward the twelve stones and the nine people at them. Toward the flame standing over the jar on the flat stone.

And it couldn't come in.

She saw it reach the stones. The top of the wall, leaning over, coming down toward them. And stop. At the ring. Exactly at the ring. As if there were glass there. *Nothing comes inside.* The black water leaned on the ring from every side at once, all the way round, and the ring held.

*Twelve of twelve.*

She let out her breath.

***

It was the breath that came through.

She saw it first. She'd thought she would. Edge to edge. She saw the flame move.

It had stood straight up over the jar for twenty nights. Not leaning. Not toward anything. Everything leaned toward it.

At a quarter to nine, it leaned.

Not much. A hair. South-east. Away from something.

She turned her head. North-west. The second stone, where Xola was. And between the second stone and the third, where Thea and Gigi were, in the gap between them, where the ring held but didn't touch: the air was moving.

Not the water. The water was held, out past the stones, leaning. The air. Coming through the gap between the stones. Cold. Pulling. Like somebody on the other side of a door breathing in very slowly to blow out a candle.

The dark couldn't come in. But it could breathe.

"*North-west*," said Hyv. Loud. Clear. The way she'd called the fells and the bottom of a lake and the top of a bell tower. "*Between two and three. It's breathing on it. The flame's leaning.*"

***

He got there before she'd finished.

She saw him go. Off the twelfth stone, the north one, round the inside of the ring. Past the first. To the gap between the second and the third. And stand in it. With his shield up, facing out, into the breath.

The flame stood up straight.

She saw it happen. Over the jar. The lean going out of it, the gold standing up again. Because the breath that had been coming through the gap was coming into his shield now. And stopping there. And behind him, in his lee, the air was still.

The way nineteen people had stood in his lee at the bottom of a lake, with a crown pulling at everything, and been still. *It doesn't pull things that stand still.*

He stood still.

She saw the number in the corner of his window. She could see it from the seventh stone; she could see everything. The breath going into his shield, and him holding it. Under the surface. The way he held everything.

"*It's moving*," said Hyv. "*North. Between twelve and one.*"

He went.

***

It went on for three hours.

She'd tell Bee about it afterward, on a call, at nine in the morning, and not be able to. She'd try. *It was like a dance*, she'd say, and that was wrong. *It was like the cellar*, and that was wrong too. It was both, and neither. It was a man going round the inside of a ring of twelve stones, all night, in the gold light, to wherever the dark was going to breathe next. Before it breathed.

She called it. Every time. She could see it. Edge to edge. The black water leaning on the ring, and where it was leaning hardest. Where the air between two stones was starting to move, before it had moved. The way you can see a wave start to come in before it's broken.

"*East. Seven and eight.*" Her own stone; she stepped aside, and he was there.

"*South-west. Four.*" He was there.

"*North again. Twelve.*" There.

He wasn't fast. He didn't need to be. He went the way the river went on the bridge. Lucan's tempo, borrowed, a breath ahead of where the breath would be. Round and round the inside of the ring. The flame standing straight up over the jar behind him, because wherever the dark breathed, he was already standing in it.

Kotori healed him on the beat. Every beat, whether he needed it or not. *Ting.* From the flat stone, beside Suzu, with her short white staff and the last tin of tea in her lap. She couldn't see where he'd be. She didn't need to. Hyv called it, and Kotori heard the call. And the beat went to where Hyv had said, and he was there when it landed.

*Ting.*

"*West. Five and six.*"

*Ting.*

Hako narrated. All of it. Out loud, the way he'd done it in the nothing. From the eighth stone, in the voice, smooth and British and unhurried. So everybody could hear where everybody was. *The tank now proceeds to the western gap, where the dark, a creature of considerable persistence, is attempting once more to extinguish*— And Thea at the third stone laughing, and Gigi saying *Hako*. And Hyv heard where every one of them was, and saw it too.

She'd never had both. Seeing and hearing. Edge to edge and everybody's voice. In the light, and in the dark, at once.

She called it.

***

At about eleven, the breath came from everywhere.

She saw it start. All round. Not one gap; all of them. Every gap between every stone, all twelve, at once. The black water leaning on the ring from every side, harder than it had leaned all night. And the air in every gap starting to move. Toward the middle. All at once. Like somebody with twelve mouths breathing in.

"*All of them*," said Hyv. Her voice cracked. "*Ice. All twelve. At once.*"

The flame leaned. Not south-east, not away from one thing. *Down*. Sinking toward the mouth of the jar, getting smaller. As if the air round it were being pulled out from every side at once.

He couldn't be in twelve places.

She saw him stop. At the north stone. The twelfth. With the breath coming through all twelve gaps at once and the flame going down behind him. And she saw his face, in the gold light, going small. She saw it do something it had never done in any fight, on any stream, at the bottom of any lake.

It didn't know what to do.

***

Suzu stood up.

At the flat stone. On the south side, where the mothers sat. He'd been sitting there all night with his hand on the edge of the stone, on the notches. Saying nothing. Keeping it. He stood up now. With the flame going down over the jar in front of him.

He put his hands round it.

Not on the jar. Round the flame. Both hands, cupped, the way you'd shelter a match in a wind. Round a flame with nothing under it, standing over a jar on a stone. Close enough that it lit his hands from inside, gold.

"*Here's the middle*," said Suzu.

Loud. She'd never heard Suzu loud. Not once, in two years. He said it out loud, to all twelve gaps at once, to the black water standing up round the hill as high as the Hall of Roads.

"*Here's the middle. Not yet.*"

And at the twelfth stone, the swamp guy turned round.

***

She saw him do it.

He'd been at the front all night. Facing out. Into the breath, wherever it was coming from. Going round the ring to the next gap before it opened. Always facing out. The way he'd faced out at everything for two years. Like the heron, on its stone, never once looking back at what was behind it.

He turned round. All the way. And looked at the middle.

At Suzu, with his hands round the flame. At Kotori beside him, on her knees, with her staff up. At the eight of them, at their stones, with the breath coming through every gap. At her, at the seventh, with her bow, calling.

She saw his face.

It knew what to do.

"*Everybody*," he said. "*In. Now. Round it.*"

***

They left the stones.

All of them. At once. On his word. The way nine people had pulled a fallen stone upright on the *boom*. They left the twelve gaps open and came in. Off the ring, across the moss, to the flat stone in the middle. Round it. Round Suzu, with his hands round the flame. Close.

Shoulder to shoulder. Back to the middle. Facing out.

Nine people in a ring round a flame, inside a ring of twelve stones, inside a wall of black water standing up as high as a house.

And the breath came through the gaps in the stones, all twelve, all at once. Across the moss, toward the middle.

And met them.

Not his shield. All of them. Nine backs, shoulder to shoulder, close. A ring inside a ring. And the breath came into it from every side at once. And stopped.

*It doesn't pull things that stand still.*

They stood still.

And behind them, in the middle of nine people, in the lee of all of them at once, the flame stood up. Straight. Gold. Between Suzu's hands.

***

"*Hold*," said the swamp guy.

He was beside her. On her left. Close. She could feel his shoulder against hers, and on her other side, Thea's. And past Thea, Gigi. All the way round. Nine.

She could see all of it.

Edge to edge. The black water leaning on the ring of stones from every side. The breath coming through the gaps and across the moss and into their backs. The nine of them, shoulder to shoulder, facing out, round the middle. And the middle, standing up, in their lee.

*I've never been at the front of anything with that much behind me*, he'd said, at the north stone, to Gigi.

He wasn't at the front now. None of them were. There wasn't a front. There was a ring, facing out, all the way round. Everybody next to everybody.

"*Hold*," he said again.

She heard Kotori's beat behind her, *ting*, landing on all of them, one after another, round the ring. Not on him. On everybody. Round and round. She heard Hako, on her right past Thea, still narrating, very quietly, under his breath, in the voice. *Here, on the longest night, in the middle of everything, we observe nine travellers standing still.* She heard Suzu, behind her, with his hands round the flame. Saying it over and over, not loud now, very quiet, the way you'd say a thing to somebody who was falling asleep.

*Here's the middle. Not yet. Here's the middle. Not yet.*

She held.

***

Midnight came. She knew, because the game knew. The third dawn, at midnight, when it would have been light in the game on any other night.

It wasn't light. It was the longest night.

But at midnight, the breath stopped.

All at once. All twelve gaps. The air going still across the moss, the cold going out of her back. The way a room goes still when somebody who's been searching for something sits down, finally, because they've found it.

She looked up.

Over the ring of stones, over the black water standing up round the hill. At the top of it, high above them, the edge of the wall of still water. Leaning in.

It stopped leaning.

She saw it. Edge to edge. All the way round. The great black wall of the dark's own water, standing round the hill as high as the Hall of Roads. And it stopped leaning in toward the middle. And stood. For a long moment. Straight up. The way the flame stood.

Looking at it.

Not *for*. *At*.

And then it went south.

***

She'd never seen anything like it. Nobody had. She'd tell Bee and not be able to, the way she couldn't tell her anything about this night.

The whole wall of black water, all round the hill, turned. Not fell. Turned. Like a crowd at the end of something, all turning at once to go home. And moved. South. All of it. Away from the hill, back down over the still water, over the three hundred stones and the hawthorn that had flowered in December. Toward the edge. A tide going out. A tide going south, all at once, the way Isa said it did. Every longest night for three hundred years.

Toward the road. Toward the round town.

But not looking.

She could see that too. In the way it moved. Not reaching. Not leaning. It went the way it always went, the way water goes downhill, because that was what it did on the longest night. Not because it had lost something. It knew where its middle was. It had looked at it all night, and it knew.

It went south to the round town because it always went to the round town.

It didn't go *for* anything.

"*It's going*," said Hyv. "*South. All of it. It's not looking.*" Her voice went. "*It knows where it is.*"

***

The hill was quiet.

Nine people standing in a ring round a flat stone, shoulder to shoulder, facing out. And the twelve stones round them, standing. And past the stones, the still water lying flat again, round the foot of the hill, black, like a mirror, with the ring upside down in it. And the dark going away south over it. A great long shadow moving across the water toward the edge, and the road, and home.

Nobody moved.

Then Suzu took his hands away from the flame.

She heard him do it. Behind her. Slowly. And the flame stood up over the jar on its own, gold, straight, in the middle of the crown on the flat stone. Not leaning. Not toward anything.

Everything leaned toward it.

She read it. They all did. She heard eight people read it at once, the small sounds people make.

Then the swamp guy, beside her, on her left, close. His shoulder against hers. He let go.

He didn't spend it on anything. There was nothing left to spend it on. It just went. Out of him, the way she'd seen it go out of him into a crown at the bottom of a lake, and a river on a bridge. Into nothing. Into the still water round the hill, flat and black. It went out across it in a ring, all the way round, very faint, the only ripple the still water had made in three hundred years.

Then nothing.

He sat down. On the moss, where he was. With his back against the flat stone, and the flame over his head.

She sat down beside him.

***

"You turned round," she said.

"Suzu said *here's the middle*," said the swamp guy. He was breathing very hard. "Loud. I'd never heard him loud." He looked up at the flame. "And I couldn't be in twelve places."

"So you turned round."

"So I turned round," said the swamp guy. "And everybody was there."

She looked at him for a long time. Edge to edge. All of him, at once, in the gold light, sitting against a flat stone on a hill in the middle of the dark's own water. At twenty-something per cent, with nothing left in his window but his own name and a number.

"Nobody was at the front," she said.

"No."

"Everybody was next to everybody."

"Yes," said the swamp guy. He looked at her. "You called it. All night. Every gap. Before it opened." He was quiet. "I'd have been at the front of nothing. In the dark. Guessing." He shook his head. "You saw it. And you called it. And I got there first because you saw it first."

"I go first," said Hyvred.

"You go first," said the swamp guy.

She put her forehead against his. In the light. Where everybody could see.

"*Mrp*," she said.

"I know," said the swamp guy. "It's allowed."

***

They kept it till dawn. All nine. Sitting round the flat stone, with their backs to it, facing out at the flat black water with the ring upside down in it. Kotori opened the last tin. The eleventh. And made tea on Slep's fire, the horrible kind, from the leaf that grew on the backs of the waystones. And it was warm, and it remembered being near a light. And Hako narrated it, very softly, and nobody told him to stop.

At about four, Thea sang. Very quietly. Gigi's head on her shoulder.

At a quarter to eight, Suzu stood up at the flat stone.

"*Set one to watch*," he said.

"*So the rest may sleep*," said eight people.

And very far away, south, past the edge and the road and ninety-four lamps, in a round town that had held the dark all night, sixty-one people said it too. And twenty of Meridian on a road. And a woman at a crossroads who wasn't on the Roll yet.

And an old woman on a bench on a roof, by the eye, who'd been looking north all night.

Hyv didn't know that. Not yet.

But just before the egg took her, she thought she saw something. Very far away, south, at the edge of everything, past the edge of the world. A very small light. Gold.

Looking back.
