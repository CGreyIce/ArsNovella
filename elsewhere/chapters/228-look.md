He came out of the egg at eight on Saturday morning with his hips aching, which wasn't possible, and lay there.

The back room at Melaka Raya. White tiles, the fan going round, two pods side by side. His and Suzu's, and Gabe's upstairs in the front room by the window. The lid humming open over him. Out in the street, a motorbike, and somebody calling to somebody else across the road in Hokkien.

His hips didn't ache. They couldn't. The pod didn't do that; pain stayed in the game. But he'd stood in a cellar ankle-deep for four nights going down into the floor until the water stopped moving round his feet. And his body had decided, on its own, out here, that it should have ached. So it did, a bit, in the way a word you've said too many times stops meaning anything and starts just being a shape.

*It is slow because it is heavy.*

He lay there for a long time.

***

Gabe came down at a quarter past with two cups of kopi, too sweet, the way he always did. He put one on the shelf by Ice's pod and sat on the edge of Suzu's with the other. Suzu was still in. Suzu would be in until noon; he'd gone in late, to sit with Isa.

"Ninety-six," said Gabe.

Ice looked at him.

"Salty told me," said Gabe. "On a letter. At two in the morning. In capitals. He said *NINETY-SIX* and then *HE BORROWED MY TURN* and then *I'M NOT CRYING* and then *WENDY SAYS I'M CRYING*." Gabe's own eyes were wet. They usually were now. "You borrowed his turn."

"Yes."

"Dylan's turn."

"Yes."

Gabe looked at him for a long time over the kopi. The big enormous face that had been his friend since a school choir, and had never once in all those years successfully not said something.

"Why'd you say yes?" said Gabe.

***

Ice didn't answer straight away.

He picked up the kopi and drank some. It was too sweet. It was always too sweet. He'd never once told Gabe, in a year and a half of mornings, and he never would.

"He asked properly," he said.

"Lots of people ask properly," said Gabe. "You said no to all of them. Since the seven. Since October. Four hundred people on a forum, Dec. Kestrel's people. Haneul's people. The Open Hand asked you on a harbour in *February*." He put his cup down. "You said yes to Xav because it's Xav. You said yes to the seven because you'd decided seven. Why him?"

Ice thought about it.

He thought about a bridge over a gorge in the wind game, a long time ago, and a man who never stopped moving. About a board every morning with Ice in the top twenty and the next tank in the thousands, and Lucan going round him. *I couldn't move you.* About the bottom of a lake, and Kestrel at the top of the steps saying *you've never once wanted to beat anybody*. And him saying *I wanted to beat Lucan. Once. On a bridge. A long time ago. I think I stopped wanting to.*

"I don't want to beat him," he said.

Gabe looked at him.

"I used to," said Ice. "In the wind game. Every morning. I wanted to beat him so much I couldn't think about anything else. And I don't remember how most of them ended." He looked at the kopi. "Because I wasn't fighting him. I was fighting the wanting."

"And now?"

"Now I just want to fight him," said Ice. "Properly." He thought about it some more. "All of it. Everything I've got. I've never done that. Not with anybody. Not even Xav. I used one, with Xav. And then the other one, at the end." He looked at the fan going round. "Nobody's ever seen all of it. At once."

Gabe didn't say anything for a long time.

"Why does he get to?" he said at last. Quietly, for Gabe. "Lucan. Of all people. Why does *he* get to see all of it?"

"Because he's been training for a month," said Ice, "to see it."

***

He went home at nine and slept until two. He didn't dream about anything. He woke up in his room with the curtains shut and the afternoon coming round the edges of them, and lay there.

Through the wall, on their side, somebody was moving about. A presence. He liked it there.

He got up and made tea.

When he went out into the corridor at three with the cup, the grille opposite was open, and the door. Deston was inside, on the folding chair, in the middle of the living room, with a box open in front of him. Unpacking. Slowly. The way he did everything now, as if there were time.

He looked up.

"Hi," said Deston.

"Hi."

Ice stood in the doorway. He didn't go in. He didn't go into people's homes without being asked. Deston knew that about him, the way he knew most things about him, from a long time of knowing him.

"Come in," said Deston.

He went in.

***

The sticker was on the wall opposite the door, in its frame. *NINETEEN MONTHS. TWENTY-TWO.* The bed was finished, with the one part left over that nobody could find a place for, which Deston had put on the windowsill like an ornament. There were two more chairs now. A rug. A kettle that wasn't the cheap one.

And on the counter in the kitchen, nothing. An empty space where a box from Singapore had sat, taped, for three months. With other things round it so nobody would know.

It had gone to Singapore three weeks ago, on the six o'clock bus. And come back with Deston on the next one, empty. And Deston had been unable to talk about anything else since. He'd called Ice from the bus. He'd called Gabe. He'd called the auntie, who'd said *aiyo* nine times and sent up a whole cake.

Ice looked at the empty space on the counter.

"You're going to win tonight," said Deston.

Ice didn't say anything.

Deston looked at him from the folding chair.

"Aren't you?"

"I don't know," said Ice.

Deston went very still.

He'd known Ice a long time. He'd been in his chat for years, lurking. He'd come to the fen on day two and found him already Lv 3. He'd sat on the ring in the market with a book and watched him take seven fights in a week, one from every class, and win every one before the other person had decided anything. He'd watched him fight Xav three times. He'd sung *Accord* on a hundred roads with Ice at the front, and felt where he was in the beat, and he'd always, always known.

"That's new," said Deston.

"Yes."

"You've never said that before. Before a fight."

"I've never not known before," said Ice.

Deston looked at him for a long time. Then he did something he didn't usually do. He got up off the folding chair and came across the living room and put his hand on Ice's shoulder. Just for a moment. The way he had on the south steps of the Hall of Roads in April, saying *you're slow*.

"Good," said Deston.

Ice looked at him.

"It's good," said Deston. "That you don't know." He took his hand away. "It means he's really good. And it means you're really going to try." He went back to the folding chair. "You never have to try. That's always been the thing about you. You just stand there and it happens." He picked up something from the box. "I'd like to see you try. Just once."

***

The auntie caught him at the lift at half past seven.

She came out of the kopitiam downstairs in her apron as he came out of the lift with Gabe, and stood in front of them both with a small plastic bag.

"For the bridge," she said.

Ice looked at the bag. Then at her.

"My grandson," said the auntie. "Watch you. Every night. On the phone. He tell me." She pushed the bag at him. "Tonight, the bridge. The one who always move." She sniffed. "You eat first. Cannot fight with empty stomach."

Inside the bag: two pieces of kueh, the green kind, Malaccan. Not too sweet.

"Thank you, auntie," said Ice.

"Win," said the auntie. And went back into the kopitiam without another word.

Gabe, beside him, made the kettle noise.

***

"I'm staying out," said Gabe, at the shoplot, at a quarter to eight. "Me and Suzu. He came out at noon. We're going to watch on my phone. On the big— on the screen, in the front room." He was standing at the door of the back room, not coming in. "Tif's staying out too. In Singapore. To mod. She said somebody has to type *chat be normal* and it might as well be her." He paused. "Hyv's going in."

"I know."

"She said she's not watching."

"She said she'd watch."

"She said both," said Gabe. "On the call last night. To Bee. Bee told me." He looked at Ice. "She's frightened."

"I know," said Ice.

He got into the pod. The lid came down over him, humming. Through it, very faint, he heard Gabe say something. *Good luck*, maybe. Or *Dec*. Or both.

The dark came up.

***

She was on the windowsill.

Of course she was. The room over Abel's, the bed and the chair and the window onto the harbour. And Hyvred, a minute before him, with her knees up and her tail hanging down the wall. Looking at him come out of the dark, the way she'd said she liked to.

She didn't say hi.

She looked at him for a long time. Her face was very pale under the gold horns. Her hands were holding her knees very tightly.

"You came out left side first," she said at last.

"Yes."

"Like you're feeling for a wall."

"Yes."

"I wrote something on the note," said Hyvred. "Out there. On my desk. Last Saturday. After the bridge."

He waited.

"I'm not telling you what," said Hyvred.

"Okay."

She hit him. Not hard. And then didn't let go of his arm.

"I'm going to show him everything," said Ice.

Hyvred went still.

"All of it," said Ice. "The one from the fen. The one from before. And the one I borrowed from Salty." He looked at her. "Nobody's seen all three. Not at once. Not Xav. Not anybody."

"Why *him*?"

"Because he asked properly," said Ice. "And because he's been training for a month to see it." He was quiet for a moment. "And because I want to."

She looked at him for a very long time. With her eyes that saw everything, edge to edge, in here. Him, and the room, and the window, and the harbour beyond it, all at once.

"Okay," said Hyvred.

She got down off the windowsill. She stood in front of him, very close.

"Then I'll look," she said.

***

Everybody was at the netloft.

All twenty-two. Riverbank at the long table, all eight of them, RX counting the cups. Val by the window with her book, a new one, open to a clean page. Kei with his maps. Salty in the door with Right Size planted in the cobbles, and Wendy beside him, and the red cloth on the second hook behind them. Thea and Xola and Slep and Xyro. Gigi. Higu. Bee, who'd come down from the netloft's gallery with her wings folded and a pencil behind each ear, and said nothing. Hako, not narrating. Rafi, with the big black sword. John, with his mirror.

And Xav, at the end of the long table, with nothing in front of him.

No sketchbook. Ice noticed. Xav had had his sketchbook on the table in front of him every morning for a year and a half.

"You're not drawing it," said Ice.

"I can't draw it," said Xav. "You said. It's not where. It's when." He looked at Val, by the window, with her book. "She's writing it. I'm watching it." He paused. "All of it. You said all of it."

"Hyv told you."

"Hyv told *everybody*," said Xav. "On the stair. Just now. She said *he's going to show him everything* and RX dropped a cup." His face was doing the thing it did, between being hit and being given a present. "I've known you nine years, Dec."

"I know."

"Nine years," said Xav, "and I'm going to see it on a bridge. In front of half of Masmark. Because somebody else asked."

"You can ask," said Ice. "In September."

Xav looked at him for a long time.

"I will," he said.

***

They walked to the north terrace at the second dusk. All twenty-two, up the lane, past the doors without red cloths on them. Past the bottom of the north-west road, where the fish man's cart stood under its striped awning.

Empty. Covered. The awning down.

Ice stopped.

He'd never seen it down. Not once. Not on Sundays, not at the third dawn, not on the morning of the longest night. The fish man had been at the bottom of that road for three hundred years.

He wasn't there.

"He's on the terrace," said John. Very quietly, at Ice's shoulder. "I saw him go up the road an hour ago. With a stool." He paused. "Abel's with him. And the netmender sisters. And the old man from the rope-walk."

Ice looked up the north-west road at the rim, where it went up and round toward the north terrace. Lit all the way up with lamps, and people.

He didn't say anything. He couldn't.

***

The north terrace was fuller than it had been on Saturday. He hadn't known that was possible.

Masmark had come. All of it, it seemed. The terrace above the bridge, the banks on both sides, the streets off the rim, the roofs. People on the parapets, on each other's shoulders, in the windows of every house that looked down on the third stream. People in boats, out on the lake, with lamps, looking up. Thousands. More than thousands.

He saw them without looking for them. The way he always did.

Kestrel at the parapet, leaning on her arms, VANTA in grey behind her. Fenn beside her, looking up. Meridian on the steps off the rim, in deep blue. Aurelie at their front, very small, not looking at anybody. Vane beside her, tall and still. Harlan behind them both, with both hands over his mouth already.

Further back, on his own, in dark red, a big man with a big face. Not with forty. Not with anybody. Standing at the back of the crowd with his arms folded, looking at the bridge.

And on the bank, near the front, on a stool: the fish man, with his boot face. Abel beside him with sawdust in his eyebrows. Two old women with net-needles in their hair. An old man and his grandson.

***

The bridge was empty.

Old stone, with a parapet you could lean on. The water going under it so fast it was white, loud, the whole length of it. Sable Court in a line at the east end, twenty in black. At the west end, nobody.

And in the middle, on his own, moving: Lucan.

Not standing still, the way he had on Saturday when he asked. Moving. All the time, the way he always did. His weight going from one foot to the other, his hands, his head. Like water over stones. Like the stream under the bridge, white and loud, never in the same place twice.

Ice stopped at the west end.

He turned round. Not to the crowd. To her. Hyvred, beside him, with her bow on her back and her hand in his, holding it so tight it hurt. Which it didn't, which wasn't possible, the same way his hips hadn't ached.

"Where do you want me?" she said. Very small.

"Here," said Ice. "At the end. Where I can see you."

"You can see everything."

"I know," said Ice. "But I'd like to see you."

She let go of his hand.

He walked onto the bridge.

***

Twenty paces. The water roaring under the stone. Thousands of people on the terrace and the banks and the roofs, so quiet he could hear every one of them breathing. Or thought he could.

He stopped. Ten paces from Lucan. In the middle of the bridge.

Lucan looked at him. Moving, always, a little, all over. His face very still in the middle of it.

"You're slow," said Lucan.

"You're early," said Ice.

He thought about a fen. About a stone in the water and a year and a half of waiting, and things coming up out of the black. He thought about a phone game with a stupid name, and twenty people at the front, and nobody beside. He thought about a cellar, and a ladder, and *Dylan, show me how you swing it*. About a doorway at the end of a corridor, and a cup put down without looking, and somebody saying *I wanted to see you the way I see you in there*.

*Okay*, he'd said. *Look.*

He didn't look back at her. He didn't need to. He could see her at the end of the bridge, at the edge of his eye, the way he could see everything.

She was looking.

"Whenever," said Lucan.

"Now," said Ice.
