The last time the nine of them had been in one place, it had been for one night, on the steps of a Hall in Brackenford, signing a piece of paper. Then five of them had walked north to a door and four of them had walked south to the chalk. Calvin had thought about that night a lot since. Nine names on a charter, and one night.

This time, Gabe said, coming over the last rise above the old fen on Saturday afternoon, nobody was leaving.

"Nobody?" said Calvin.

"Nobody," said Gabe. "Not for a while. Dest says. Dec says." He looked down at the fen spread out below them in the sun. Black water, reeds, the long causeway with its lamps, and the town on stilts at the end of it. "We're home."

Calvin had never been to Fernwick in his life. He'd seen it on Dennis's phone, at the café, once or twice, because everybody at the café watched the swamp guy except Dennis. He stood on the rise and looked at it, and thought that *home* was a strange word for a place you'd never been. Then he thought that maybe that was what made it a good one.

Then he ran down the hill. Obviously. Ahead, and then back, because he did that now. Then ahead again, all the way down to the causeway, while Rachael said *Cal* behind him in the voice that meant she'd given up.

***

They were on the Landing.

All of them. Calvin came along the causeway at a run and saw them before they saw him: five people on a wooden platform on stilts over black water, waiting. Deston first, tall, on his feet already. Then Kotori, in pale blue, with her bell. Then Sheng Yang, standing, because Sheng Yang always stood. Then Suzu, sitting on the edge with his feet over the water and an empty jar beside him. And Declan, at the end, on a bench, with a shield leaning against his knee.

Deston was already walking. Then Deston was running, which Calvin had seen him do perhaps twice in three years. Calvin was running too. They met in the middle of the causeway, and Deston caught him round the shoulders and held on, and Calvin held on back.

"*Cal*," said Deston.

"I made thirty."

"I *know*. I was on the call. I heard you shout. Half the White Hills heard you shout."

Behind Calvin, at a walk, came Dennis and Rachael and Gabe. Deston let go of Calvin and looked at the three of them. Calvin watched his face. Deston had introduced every one of them to everyone else, years ago, at different times, in different places. Deston was the hub; Deston was always the hub. And now his oldest friends were standing on a causeway in a fen with his newest ones, all in one place.

Calvin had never seen him look like that.

"Hi," said Deston, to the three of them. His voice didn't quite work. "Hi. You're here."

"Darling," said Dennis, "we're *thirty*."

***

The Landing was very full that night.

Not with strangers, though there were always a few. With them. Nine people, a company of nine, on a wooden platform over black water. The old woman from the steps had come down from her house and sat on a barrel with her pipe and looked at all of them, one at a time, the way you'd inspect a delivery.

She looked at Rachael first.

"You're the one who brews angry," said Sedge.

Rachael stared at her.

"*Gabe*," she said.

"I said you brewed like you were angry at the pot," said Gabe, from the bench. "It was a compliment."

"Is it angry?" said Sedge.

Rachael thought about it.

"Sometimes," she said. "Pots are very slow."

Sedge took the pipe out of her mouth and looked at her for a long moment. Then she laughed, the dry stick-breaking laugh. "Come and brew at my hearth tomorrow," she said. "I'll show you what slow's for."

She looked at Dennis next.

"You're the money one."

"I'm the *purse*," said Dennis, with dignity. "There's a difference."

"Is there?"

"The money one *has* the money. The purse *keeps* it." He considered. "And makes it look bigger than it is. Mostly, it becomes so."

Sedge looked at him a while longer.

"Hm," she said, which from Sedge, Calvin would learn, was very high praise.

Then she looked at Calvin.

"And you're the one that runs," said Sedge.

"I run *back* now," said Calvin. "That's the new thing."

"Hm," said Sedge again, and went back to her pipe, and Calvin couldn't tell whether he'd passed.

***

Calvin watched Declan all evening.

He couldn't help it. He'd known Declan for a year, through Deston. A man who'd come to things sometimes and sat at the end of tables and said very little, and been very funny when he did. A man Deston would have walked into a fire for. Calvin had liked him from the first night, the way you liked people who didn't need you to like them.

But he'd never watched him. Not properly. He didn't watch streams; he'd never had the patience. He'd seen the clips everybody saw. The church, the plank, the window. He'd seen Dennis's phone at the café. He knew the jokes, the way everybody at the café knew the jokes. *If he says trust, it's over.* *If he goes dark, type !dark.* *He will not tell you what he found. Don't ask.*

He'd heard that one a hundred times. On the wall of the café, written on a napkin by somebody, as a joke. *Don't ask.* The whole internet knew it. Thirty thousand people a night didn't ask. They'd been not asking for six months. It was the most famous thing about him, apart from the shield.

At ten o'clock, on the bench at the end of the Landing, Declan said to the air in front of him, "Going dark," and turned his stream off.

Nobody on the Landing reacted. Deston didn't look up. Kotori didn't look up. Suzu didn't look up. Sheng Yang, standing, didn't look up. The five of them just went on doing what they were doing, as if he'd said *I'm going to put the kettle on*.

Calvin looked at them. Then at Declan.

Declan was standing up. He picked up his shield. He looked out along the north walk, the long wooden walk on its posts going away into the reeds in the dark. He was going somewhere. The five of them knew where. They'd always known where.

Calvin had known Declan for a year. He'd been in his company for a month and a half. He'd never once been told *don't*. Not by Declan. Only by a napkin on a café wall.

"Where do you go?" said Calvin.

The Landing went quiet.

Not completely. The water still went under the stilts. But everybody stopped. Rachael, halfway through saying something to Kotori, stopped. Dennis looked up from his notebook. Gabe, on the bench, put down his cup very slowly and looked at Calvin with an expression Calvin couldn't read. It might have been *oh no*. It might have been *finally*.

Declan looked at him.

"When you go dark," said Calvin. He'd started, so he finished. He always finished. "Every night. You go somewhere. They know where." He nodded at the five. "Thirty thousand people don't ask. The whole internet doesn't ask. There's a napkin on the wall at the café that says don't ask." He took a breath. "I'm asking. Where do you go?"

Nobody said anything.

Declan looked at him for a long time across the Landing, with his shield in his hand, in the dark.

Calvin had asked a lot of questions in his life. The one nobody else would. He'd asked teachers why. He'd asked Deston, once, whether he was scared of anything, and Deston had thought about it for a day and said *yes*. He'd asked Gabe on a hot ridge who stood two paces from him. He'd watched people's faces when he asked. Most of them got a look, a small closing-up, a door going shut.

Declan's face didn't do that. It did something else. Something Calvin had only seen once before, on Deston, the day he'd said *yes*.

It looked like somebody being glad they'd been asked.

"Come and see," said Declan.

He didn't say it to Calvin. He said it to the four of them: Calvin and Rachael and Dennis and Gabe. The four who weren't the five.

"All of you," he said. "You're in it now. It's yours too."

Behind him, Suzu got up off the edge of the Landing and picked up his empty jar. Kotori stood up with her bell. Deston closed the book in his lap and stood. Sheng Yang was already standing. The five of them looked at the four of them, and nobody said anything, and nobody needed to.

Calvin looked at Gabe.

Gabe was smiling. Not much. Just at the corners.

"I've been," said Gabe. "In January. He showed me." He picked up his hammer. "You'll like it, Cal. There's no running."

"There's no *running*?"

"Nothing comes inside," said Declan. "So there's nothing to run from." He turned toward the north walk. "Come on. It's a quarter of a mile. Stay on the planks till I say."

They went.

Nine people, one behind the other, along the north walk in the dark. Declan first, with his shield. Calvin second, because he'd asked. He went ahead without being told, and then remembered, and came back, and walked at two paces behind Declan. He found, to his surprise, that he didn't want to be anywhere else.
