Kei drew the walk the way he drew everything: in pencil first, so it could change.

He'd been drawing it on and off since April. At the eye, mostly, on the north parapet, with his lamp on the *Somewhere* bench behind him. Isa was beside him under the grey blanket at the second dusk, looking north at something she couldn't see. A sheet on his knee. The round town in the middle, small. The west road going out of it, past the goose and the ferry and the White Hills, fourteen days. At the far end of it, a big circle with a smaller one inside. A lake the size of a country, and an island.

*MASMARK · JULY · the bridge · properly.*

That part was easy. Everybody had signed. Sixty-two names in pencil and a lion and a word that might have been a sheep, and *SCREAMING*. He'd known they would. He'd known it since the first winter, when ten people in a swamp became sixty in a round town. Constellation went where its people were. It always had.

The hard part was who stayed.

***

"Somebody has to keep the gate," said Kite.

He was standing at the end of the long table with his hands at his sides, the way he stood in the gate. He'd come to Kei at the second dusk, on his own, which he never did.

"Tobi," said Kei. "He's asked."

"He's never kept it on his own."

"He's been asking people *who'd stand next to you* every morning since February," said Kei. "When you were at Tallow Green, he kept the gate ten days and nobody got past him without an answer." He looked at Kite. "He's ready."

Kite thought about that for a long time.

"I was going to stay," he said. "Keep it. While you all went."

"I know," said Kei. "Drew's walking. Ash is walking." He paused. "Drew's going to stand on a terrace in Masmark and watch his friend get hit on a bridge by somebody he's been waiting a year to get hit by. Ash has never been east of the White Hills." He put down his pencil. "Who'd stand next to them?"

Kite looked at him.

"That's not fair," said Kite.

"It's very fair," said Kei. "It's the fairest thing I've ever said."

Kite stood at the end of the long table for a while longer with his hands at his sides. Then he went to the board on the wall by the door, under the banner. He took the pencil off its string and wrote, under the sixty-two names, very small: *Kite (with Drew and Ash)*.

And then, under that, smaller: *Tobi has the gate.*

***

In the end, nine stayed.

Kei wrote them on the back of the sheet, where nobody would read them unless they turned it over.

*Isa.* She'd said so before anybody asked. *I'm not walking fourteen days. I'm old. I'll be on the roof.* And then, to Kei, at the eye, very quietly: *Somebody should be here when you come home.*

*Ama Corrie.* She hadn't said anything at all. She just hadn't signed. When Kei looked at her over the board she'd said *hm* and picked up her broom, which was the same thing.

*Ina*, for the forge. Old Joss wasn't well enough to be alone with the fire, and Ina wouldn't leave him.

*Tiger and Maigo*, for the first mile. They'd held it on three longest nights. They said somebody should hold it on an ordinary summer too, so it would know what that felt like.

*Newish and Winter.* Newish had her drum and the Hart. Winter had the stream with the blind white fish in the cold place at the end of the north tunnel. She said it would be lonely if she went.

*Niko*, because Niko had enchanted an arrow once that leaned home, and somebody ought to be home for it to lean at.

And *Tobi*, in the gate.

Nine. In a round town of hundreds, with a guild of sixty-two. Kei looked at the list on the back of the sheet for a long time at the eye.

"It's not enough," he said.

"It's enough," said Isa, beside him, not looking at him. "There were never more than one of us on the hill. For three hundred years." She folded her hands in the blanket. "Nine's a crowd."

***

"Who'll light your lamp?" said Isa, on the same night. "While you're gone."

Kei looked at it. His lamp, on the end of the *Somewhere* bench behind them. He'd set it there the night after the longest night, so the hill could see the round town. He'd lit it every dusk since. Seven months. He'd never once thought about who'd do it if he wasn't here.

"It's a kept wick," he said. "One of Malcolm's. It doesn't go out by itself."

"*Hm*," said Isa. "Something has to put it out."

"Nobody will."

"Then it'll burn till you're home." She was quiet. "I'll sit next to it. At dusk. Suzu won't be here to carry me up. Ina will." She almost smiled. "He's strong. He bends stars."

"You can't see it," said Kei. Gently. "From the bench. It's too close and too small."

"I couldn't see the hill's light for three hundred years," said Isa. "I knew it was there." She put out her old hand from the blanket and found the bench beside her, and the lamp's base on it, and left her hand there. "I'll know."

Kei sat on the north parapet beside her for a long time after that, with the sheet on his knee, not drawing anything. Then he turned the sheet over and wrote one more line at the bottom of the nine, on the back, where nobody would read it:

*Kei's lamp · Isa keeps it.*

***

Suzu fought six people in June.

Kei didn't arrange it. Nobody arranged anything to do with Suzu. But after the Seven Below, after *Even*, people came. Not hundreds, the way they'd come for Ice. A few. From other towns, from the Millrace road, from Brackenford. One from as far as Merrowgate, where Leaka and Curse had started. People who'd seen Tif's clip, *HE SAT DOWN*, eleven million, and wanted to know what it was like to be sat down in front of.

They came to the gate and asked Tobi where the one with the scythe was.

Tobi asked them who'd stand next to them. Then he told them: the north steps, in the morning, asleep.

They'd find Suzu asleep on the north steps beside Isa's empty chair. Isa had moved to the roof at dusk and the eye in the mornings, now, because she said the north steps were for him. And they'd stand at the bottom of the steps not knowing what to do. And after a while, without opening his eyes, Suzu would say *Sunday*. And they'd come back on Sunday.

On Sundays he fought at the settling ring. Ama Corrie's. Nine darker flagstones east of the fountain, thirty paces across. *Until one yields or falls. Nothing that leaves the ring comes back into it.* Kei said *begin*, the way he'd said it for Ice's seven. Hobb ran a book. Kotori stood at the edge of the nine stones with two cups.

Suzu won all six.

Not quickly. That was the thing Kei noticed, from the south steps, with his map on his knee. He could have won them all quickly. Kei had seen the third bout in the Seven Below; he knew. But he didn't. He let every one of them go on until they'd done everything they'd brought. Until he'd seen all of it. And then he sat down, or lay down, or went somewhere silly. *Shhhhk.* And it was over.

After every one, he'd sit on the edge of the ring with Kotori's cup and talk to whoever he'd beaten. For a long time. Kei couldn't hear what about. But they'd come out of the ring looking at the floor of the round market the way people looked at Kei's maps, the first time. As if somebody had shown them where they were.

"He's *teaching* them," said Hyv, beside Kei on the steps, on the third Sunday. She'd stopped calling the fights. She just watched. "He's beating them and then he's *teaching* them. He's never taught anybody anything in his *life*."

"He taught Xav," said Kei.

"He *beat* Xav. Once."

"And Xav learned to be on time," said Kei. "That's teaching."

Hyv looked at him sideways.

"He looks *so happy*," she said. "It's disgusting. I love it."

***

On the last Sunday in June, after the sixth, Suzu came and sat on the south steps beside Kei. He'd never done that either.

"Can you put something on the board," said Suzu.

Kei looked at him.

"*Suzu · the ring · Sundays*," said Suzu. "So they stop coming to the north steps." He drank his tea. "It's effort. Being woken up."

Kei wrote it on the corner of his map. In pencil.

"Not for July," he said. "You'll be in Masmark."

"In Masmark too," said Suzu. "If anybody asks." He looked at the cup. "I think somebody will ask."

"Who?"

Suzu thought about it. Like a cat.

"Somebody who's never been sat down in front of," he said. And went back to the north steps to sleep.

***

He saw Ice at the forge on the Tuesday.

He wasn't looking. He was crossing the round market to the Hart at the second dusk with his map under his arm, and the forge door was open. Old Joss on his stool by the fire with his pipe. Ina at the anvil, with the mask pushed up on his head. And Ice, standing beside the anvil, talking to Ina. Quietly, with his hands.

Kei didn't stop. He went on to the Hart. But he saw Ina's face. Ina had stopped hammering. He was looking at Ice the way he'd looked at the first star he ever bent that held. And then he nodded. Once. And put a short bar of black iron in the fire. The kind that didn't shine, out of the fourth tunnel, quenched in the dark.

Kei went into the Hart and sat down and didn't think about it.

He saw Ice at the long table with Hana on the Thursday.

Not at the long table. At the end of it, by Hana's five, by the window, late, when most people had gone up. The two of them. Ice talking, very quietly. Hana listening with her hands flat on the table. Kei was at the far end with his map. He didn't hear any of it.

He saw Hana's face go white.

Then red.

Then she put both hands over her mouth. And then she got up and came round the end of the table and put her arms round Ice's neck, hard, the way you'd hold on to somebody who'd just told you something you'd been waiting years to hear. And Ice sat very still in the chair and let her. With his ears going pink.

Milli, coming down the stair from the eye, stopped dead on the bottom step. He looked at the two of them for a long time. Then he turned round and went back up the stair, very quietly, the way you'd leave a room you'd walked into at the wrong moment.

Kei looked at his map.

He didn't draw anything. He didn't write anything down. He just sat there at the far end of the long table with his pencil in his hand, not using it, for a long time.

***

Ice came to him on the Friday, at the eye.

Isa had gone down. It was just Kei on the north parapet with his lamp on the *Somewhere* bench behind him and the walk sheet on his knee, nearly finished. Ice came up the two hundred and twelve on his own, without Hyv, which he hadn't done in six months. He sat down on the parapet beside Kei and looked north.

"Can you put something on the board," said Ice.

Kei had heard that sentence twice that week now.

"For the walk home," said Ice. "Not the walk there. After the bridge. Coming back."

"What?"

Ice was quiet for a while.

"The notch," he said. "At the top of the crater road. The first one, going west. Where you come up out of Masmark and see it all behind you." He looked at the gold point at the bottom of the north. "The second dawn out. Early."

Kei didn't ask why.

He'd known Ice two and a half years. He'd drawn a line from him to everybody on the ceiling of the Hall of Roads, at the eye. *Next to.* He'd seen him at the forge with Ina, and at the long table with Hana, and he'd seen Hana's face go white and then red. He'd seen Milli turn round on the stair. He could have drawn it on the map in front of him without asking anything at all.

He didn't draw it. He wrote, at the bottom of the walk sheet, in the space after *MASMARK* and the bridge and the date, very small:

*the notch · the second dawn out · early*

In pencil.

Ice looked at it.

"Pencil," he said.

"Pencil isn't asking," said Kei. "I told Aurelie that. In January." He put the pencil behind his ear. "Pencil isn't knowing either."

Ice looked at him for a long time.

"You know," said Ice.

"I don't know anything," said Kei. "I've got a map with a word on it in pencil." He looked north. "You can rub it out."

"I'm not going to rub it out."

"I know," said Kei. "That's why it's pencil."

***

Hyv found it on the Saturday, the day before they left. Of course she did. She saw everything. She was standing in front of the walk sheet on the wall of the banner room, under the banner, reading it top to bottom the way she read everything, edge to edge. She stopped at the bottom.

*the notch · the second dawn out · early*

She turned round and looked straight down the banner room at Kei, at the head of the long table.

"What's *the notch*?" said Hyv.

"A place on the crater road," said Kei. "At the top. Going west."

"I know what the notch *is*. Why's it on the board?"

"I don't know," said Kei.

Hyv looked at him for a long time. Edge to edge. Kei looked back at her and didn't do anything with his face. He'd had two and a half years of practice at not doing anything with his face at the head of a table.

"You *know*," said Hyv.

"Pencil isn't knowing," said Kei.

She looked at the board again. At the word. Then, slowly, at the first of the nine chairs, where Ice was sitting with his soup, very carefully not looking at anything.

Kei watched her see it. Or not see it. He couldn't tell which. She stood in front of the board for a long time. Then she turned round and walked down the banner room to the first of the nine. She sat down on Ice's knee, the way she did, with her arm round his neck and her tail round his ankle. She didn't say anything at all.

Ice went pink.

Hyv put her chin on his shoulder and looked at the board across the room, at the word in pencil at the bottom. And Kei thought, from the head of the table, that she'd decided not to look at it any more. The way you'd decide not to open a door you knew somebody was going to open for you.

***

They went out through the south gate on Saturday the seventh of July, at the third dawn. Fifty-five of them, and Riverbank's four going home, and a sheep.

Tobi was in the gate. Nineteen, very serious, with his crank crossbow on his back and his hands at his sides where people could see them.

"Who'd stand next to you?" said Tobi.

He said it to Kei first, because Kei was at the front with the map. Kei had thought about it all week, the way you thought about a thing you'd be asked by somebody who meant it.

"Everybody behind me," said Kei. "And nine who stayed."

Tobi thought about it.

"That's a lot of answers," he said.

"I've got a lot," said Kei.

Then the rest of them came through the gate. Fifty-eight, counting Riverbank. Tobi asked every one of them, one by one. It took nearly an hour. Nobody minded. Gabe said *Rae* and Rae said *Gabe* and Tobi wrote them down on a slate as a pair. Leaka said *everybody I've ever met* and Tobi said *that's not an answer* and Leaka said *it IS* and Tobi said *fair*. Suzu said *Kotori, with tea* and Kotori said *him, asleep*. Hyv said *the swamp guy* without even stopping. Ice, last, at the back with PenguChi and the sheep, said:

"Everybody. And her. And the ones who stayed."

"That's three answers," said Tobi.

"I've got three," said Ice.

At the top of the south steps, Isa sat in her chair under the grey blanket with Ama Corrie behind her, hand on the back of it. She couldn't see them. Not from there. She raised her hand anyway.

Ama Corrie didn't raise anything.

"You're slow," she said, to all of them. Loudly. Across the round market.

"We're walking," said Kei.

"*Hm*," said Ama Corrie. And then, as they turned down the south road toward the milestone and the west: "Mind the goose."
