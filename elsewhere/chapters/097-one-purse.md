Dennis read his inventory every night before he logged out, the way other people read their bank balance. It was the most relaxing part of his day.

[ tomatosoup30 · Inventory
4 gold 61 silver 8 copper
Steamstone ×7 (uncut) · Steamstone ×3 (cut) · Scaldback hide ×2 (Cal's; to sell)
A wand · a spade · a very good hat ]

Four gold. Sixty-one silver. It had been nine silver on the first night. He'd done that in two weeks from a standing start, in a hospital, which Rachael still insisted it was. He'd done it with a spade, mostly. Excavation was digging, and the Warm Hills were full of steamstone, little green-blue stones that grew in the cracks round the vents and glowed faintly when you cut them. Jewelcrafting was cutting. And cutting, it turned out, was the difference between three silver and forty.

He'd played the same game for years with Deston and Gabe, a different one, on a screen. He'd been the one with the money in that too. Somebody had to be. Deston was too nice to charge anyone anything and Gabe forgot money existed.

***

He hadn't shown anyone his class.

It was the only thing in his inventory he couldn't put a price on. He'd read it on the ridge on Wednesday morning, under the gold sky. He'd shut it before anyone saw, and he'd been carrying it round for three days like a coin he didn't recognise, turning it over.

[ Class: Gilder
One who makes a thing look like more than it is.
Gild: what you point at looks greater, and is looked at. ]

Most of the time, the second line said in small letters underneath, *it becomes so*.

He'd always been good at that. Making things look like more than they were. A stone, a deal, a bad week, himself. He'd never been sure whether it was a nice thing to be good at.

He found out what it was worth on the road to Brackenford.

They were on the second day of the walk, Saturday, coming down out of the Warm Hills into wetter country. Calvin came back over a rise at a run, the way he did now, and stopped two paces off and said, "Five of them. Wolves. Big ones. Coming."

[ Fen Wolf ×5 · Lv 13 ]

Gabe put his shield up. Rachael went to two paces behind him. Calvin went wide. And Dennis took his wand out of his belt and looked at Gabe's back for a second. Big, solid, square. Gabe, who stood two paces from everybody and had nobody two paces from him.

He pointed the wand at Gabe.

*Gild.*

Nothing happened to Gabe. He didn't get bigger, not really. He didn't change at all, to Dennis. But the wolves came over the rise and saw him, and every single one of them stopped dead. Then they went for him. Not for Calvin out wide. Not for Rachael two paces back. Not for Dennis, who was the softest thing on the road and knew it. All five of them went for Gabe as if he were the only thing in the world worth biting. As if he were three times the size he was and made of gold.

Gabe took all of it on the shield, and grunted, and his hammer got hotter and hotter. And when it came round it sent the biggest wolf sideways into the ditch.

Afterwards, with the wolves dead and Rachael Mending Gabe's arm, Gabe looked at Dennis.

"What did you do?"

"I made you look expensive," said Dennis.

Gabe thought about that. "They didn't go near Rae."

"No."

"Or you."

"No."

"Or Cal."

"Cal was in a *tree*."

Gabe looked at him for a long moment. Then he said, "Do that every time," and went back to having his arm mended.

So that was what it was worth. Dennis opened the window, finally, on the road, and turned it round so they could all see. He watched their faces read it. Rachael said "*Ha*," the way she'd said it about her own. Calvin read the small line out loud, *it becomes so*, and looked at Dennis as if he'd known all along.

"It's worth Gabe," said Dennis. "That's what it's worth."

***

Deston rang on the Monday night, from the north, to tell them about the founder.

"The charter's at Brackenford," he said. "Same Hall as Xav's. Same clerk. A company holds twelve. One name, one banner, one purse. But there's a rule." A pause. "The founder can't belong to any named party."

"So not Declan," said Dennis.

"Not Declan. Not me. Not anyone in MH. Not Gabe, he's still in it." Another pause. "Xav had to leave us to found his."

Dennis was walking at the back, with the light going. He looked at his own name in the little party window in the corner of his eye. *tomatosoup30*. And the empty space beside it where a party name would go, if he'd had one.

"I'm not in anything," he said.

"No," said Deston. "That's the point."

Dennis stopped walking.

"Dest," he said. "Are you asking me to *found* something?"

"I'm asking if you'd like to."

Nobody had ever asked Dennis to found anything. He'd joined things, and helped run things, and been the one who knew where the money was in things. And he'd made other people's things look like more than they were, often, for free. But nobody had ever asked him to put his name at the top.

"Who holds the purse?" he said.

"The founder."

Dennis started walking again. Faster.

"Tell Declan," he said, "I'll see him on Thursday."

***

Brackenford was a river town at a ford, with a bridge at the east end and a big grey stone in the square that everyone stopped to look at. Dennis had never seen so many travellers in one place. Hundreds of them, in every kind of armour, in every kind of face. And all of them, every few minutes, glancing at the stone.

The Adventurers' Hall was at the top of the square. The clerk was an old man with ink on his cuffs and a pen behind each ear. His name was Penhallow, and he looked at Dennis across his desk with the expression of a man who had seen a great many founders and expected very little from any of them.

"The fee," said Penhallow, "is one hundred and fifty gold."

"I'd like to talk about that," said Dennis.

"The fee is the fee."

"Everyone says that," said Dennis, warmly, settling into the chair, "until somebody asks them properly."

He asked properly. He asked three different ways. He tried the one about the size of the company, nine travellers, not twelve, surely a smaller charter. He tried the one about loyalty, a company that had already brought the Hall one charter (Xav's), surely worth something to the house. He tried the one that wasn't really a question at all, the one where you just sat very comfortably and smiled and let the silence do the asking.

Penhallow listened to all three without moving. When Dennis had finished, he took the pen from behind his left ear and turned it over once in his fingers.

"Young man," he said, "you asked very properly."

"Thank you."

"The fee is the fee."

Dennis sat back in his chair and looked at him with enormous respect.

"You'd do well in the market," he said.

"So I've been told," said Penhallow. "Usually by people who are about to pay it."

***

Moonlight Harvest came down out of the north on Thursday afternoon.

Five of them, over the east bridge, out of the fen road. They'd walked three days to get there, back the way they'd come, away from whatever it was at the top of the herons that they'd all been going towards. Dennis watched them come over the bridge from the Hall steps. He'd heard about them for months. From Deston. From Gabe. From the café, where everybody watched the swamp guy except Dennis, who'd been busy.

The swamp guy was in front. Declan. Ice. He looked like himself and he didn't. The same way of walking, the same way of looking at a place as if he'd already worked out where everything was. But dressed in charcoal and dark leather, with a shield on his back, and a face like the drawings of his face. Then Deston, tall, already waving. Then a boy with long silver hair and a scythe, and a pack on his shoulder that glowed faintly on one side through the canvas. Then a girl in pale blue with a small white staff and a brass bell. Then a quiet young man with a long sword across his back, walking behind Declan the way you'd walk behind somebody you'd decided to follow.

Calvin went first. Obviously. Calvin went down the Hall steps and across the square at a run and nearly knocked Deston over, and Deston laughed and caught him. Then Gabe went, slower, and Deston and Gabe did the thing they did, a hand on the back of the neck and a shove, which was how two men who'd been in a choir together since they were thirteen said *I missed you*.

Then Rachael went. She didn't run. She walked across the square and stopped in front of Declan. Exactly as far away as she liked, and no closer. He stopped too, at that distance, without being told. He'd known her nine years.

"Hi, Rae," said Declan.

Rachael looked him up and down.

"You look like a drawing of you," she said.

"I get that a lot."

"It's not a compliment."

"I know," said Declan, and his face did something that Dennis, from the steps, would have priced quite high.

Then Rachael said, "Your moonbell draught."

"Yeah?"

"What's in it? Exactly. All of it."

And Declan told her. Right there in the square, in front of everybody, with half of Brackenford walking past them glancing at the stone. He told her every herb, and how much, and how long, and what went wrong if you rushed it. The way he'd always answered every question anybody had ever asked him. Properly, all the way through, as if it were a good question. As if you hadn't been silly to ask. Dennis watched Rachael listen. She didn't interrupt once. She'd once told Dennis that Declan was the only person she'd ever been able to ask anything at all.

The girl in pale blue had come up beside them. Kotori. Dennis had heard about Kotori. Rachael had told her about the class on the walk in, and Kotori had asked to see it, and she was reading it now over Rachael's shoulder.

*One who decides who is helped first, and is not sorry.*

Kotori read the last three words. Then she laughed. A small surprised laugh, as if somebody had told her a joke she'd been waiting a long time to hear.

"I used to be the other kind," she said.

"What other kind?" said Rachael.

"The sorry kind."

Rachael looked at her for a moment.

"Don't be," she said.

***

Calvin asked Declan the question on the Hall steps, because of course he did.

"You were nearly at the door," said Calvin. "Dest said. You were three days from it. Why'd you come *down*?"

Declan looked at him. He didn't seem to mind the question. Dennis was learning that Declan never minded questions.

"You were nearly at Brackenford," said Declan.

Calvin waited for more. There wasn't any more.

"The door's been there a long time," said Declan, eventually. "It can wait a week."

***

Declan had put the fee on the desk an hour earlier. One hundred and fifty gold, in one go, without saying anything, the way you'd put down a cup. Dennis had picked it up before Penhallow could, and counted it, and put it in the Hall's box himself. Then he'd opened a fresh page in the little notebook he'd bought in Mistbath. In very neat letters at the top he wrote *THE PURSE*. Underneath, *Owed to Declan: 150 gold*.

"It isn't owed," Declan had said.

"Everything's owed," said Dennis. "That's what a book is *for*."

"Suzu argued me up to two per cent once."

"Suzu's a sweetheart," said Dennis. "I'm not. Nought per cent. And I'll have it back to you before he's paid you a cent of his."

Somewhere behind them, Suzu said, "Hey."

Now Penhallow held out a pen.

"The name," he said, "if you please."

"Moonlight Harvest," said Dennis.

Penhallow looked at him over the charter. "There's a party of that name."

"There is," said Dennis. "They're standing behind me."

Penhallow looked past him, at the five on the steps, and Gabe, and Calvin, and Rachael. Nine people on the steps of his Hall in the dusk. His face didn't do anything. Dennis was beginning to like him very much.

"A party may come under a company of its own name," said Penhallow, "if every hand of it signs."

Dennis wrote it in. *MOONLIGHT HARVEST.* In his neatest letters, the ones he saved for things that mattered. Then he held the pen out.

Declan took it first, because they all looked at him. Then Deston. Then Suzu, who signed with a little flourish. Then Kotori, very small and very neat. Then Sheng Yang, who signed the way he did everything, once and without fuss. Then Gabe. Then Calvin, who pressed so hard he nearly went through the paper. Then Rachael, last, in a quick plain hand, as if she didn't have time to waste on it, though Dennis saw her look at it for a long time afterwards.

***

They had one night. Then the five went back north, up the fen road, toward the herons and whatever was waiting at the top of them. And Gabe and the three of them turned south and west, to keep going.

"Thirty," said Gabe, to Declan, at the east bridge at first light. "I'll bring them up to you."

"Don't rush."

"I'm not going to rush. I'm going to do it properly."

"Same thing, with you."

They did the thing with the back of the neck and the shove. Then Declan turned to go, and stopped, and looked at Dennis.

"You can lead," said Dennis, before he could say anything. "Obviously. I don't want to *lead*. Leading is unpaid. I'll hold the purse."

Declan looked at him for a moment.

"Deal," he said.

He went off over the bridge with the other four. Dennis stood at the end of it and watched them go. Five people walking north into the fen, one behind the other, the way people walked when they knew where they were going.

Then he got out the little notebook and opened it, and looked at the first page.

*THE PURSE.*

*Owed to Declan: 150 gold.*

He looked at it for a long time, and then he added a line underneath, very small, in the same neat letters.

*He says it isn't.*
