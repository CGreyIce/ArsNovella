Ice could read everybody he'd ever fought, and he'd never once been able to read Suzu.

He'd never told anybody that. Nobody had asked. People asked him how he read things. On forums, at gates, on a bridge in Masmark with a sword gone over the parapet into a stream. *How did you know she'd draw there? How did you know the back foot? Where did you learn the river?* And he told them, mostly. He looked at people until he knew what they'd do, and then he went first. It wasn't magic. It was just paying attention for long enough.

He'd paid attention to Suzu for longer than anybody alive. Since before the wind game. Out there, at a desk, in other people's games, on ladders where Suzu sat near the top. In the guild Ice dragged him into one winter, and in a back room at Melaka Raya with two pods side by side.

He still couldn't tell where Suzu would be. Ever. In anything. He could tell where Suzu wouldn't be. That was all.

It was the best thing about him.

***

"Why Suzu?" said Hyv.

The north tunnel, on the Tuesday, at the second dusk. The deep workings, where the Delvers came up out of the rock in fours. She'd stopped calling for a moment between packs. She was sitting on a broken pillar of grey stone with her bow across her knees, looking at him.

"Because Xav's good enough now," said Ice.

"For what?"

"For Suzu."

Hyv looked at him for a long time in the lamplight.

"I can't call him," she said. "I told Xav. On the fountain. I've never once been able to call Suzu." She was frowning, the way she did when she saw something she didn't understand. "I can call *you*. Every time. Even on the hill, round the inside of the ring, a breath ahead of every gap. I can see where you're going to be." She looked at him. "Can you call him?"

Ice thought about it. Properly. She deserved it properly.

"No," he said.

Hyv went very still on the pillar.

"You can call *everybody*," she said.

"Not him."

"You read *Kestrel* from the moon. You read *Lucan's back foot* on a bridge. You read Xav for a year and a half until he could make you sit down against a wall." She was staring at him. "And you can't read *Suzu*?"

"I can read where he won't be," said Ice. "That's all. I've never once known where he will be." He looked at the tunnel wall, where the next four Delvers were going to come out in about twenty seconds. "Nobody has. That's why nobody's ever asked him."

"Has he ever beaten you?"

"We've never fought," said Ice.

"Why not?"

He thought about Suzu on the edge of the egg in the back room, on Monday morning. *That's effort*.

"He's lazy," said Ice.

Hyv looked at him for a long time.

"If you fought him," she said slowly. "Properly. All of it. Who'd win?"

The Delvers came out of the wall. Four of them, grinding, the way they always did. Ice went first, the way he always did. The river, the slope, the cut on the beat. Hyv's arrows through the joints of their shoulders before she'd finished deciding to loose. The four came apart in the lamplight round his feet.

[ Lv 67 ]

He stood in the deep tunnel with his sword down and his shield up and looked at the window.

"I don't know," he said.

Hyv got up off the pillar. She came and stood in front of him in the dark, among four piles of grey stone. She looked up at his face, edge to edge.

"You've never once said that," she said. "About a fight. In two years."

"I know."

"You always know."

"Not with him," said Ice.

***

Gabe found out on the Wednesday, out there, in the kitchen. He'd been in the round town on Saturday night when Ice said it at the long table. He'd heard it. He'd screamed, along with everybody, without really thinking about what it meant. And then on Wednesday morning, at the counter with his kopi, he thought about it.

"*Suzu*," said Gabe.

Ice put the kettle on.

"*Our* Suzu. The one who lives across the corridor. Who sleeps at the long table. Who carried a jar for nine months and said *good* about it." Gabe put the kopi down. "Dec. *Dec*. I've lived with him for two years. I've never seen him fight a *person*."

"You've seen him fight."

"Unwatched. Things in the dark. *Moonbells*." Gabe stared at him. "Is he *good*?"

Ice made the tea. Two cups. He gave one to Gabe.

"You'll see," he said.

"Everybody keeps *saying* that—"

The door of the guest room opened at the end of the corridor. Suzu came out, a third awake. He came into the kitchen, took the second cup of tea off the counter out of Ice's hand without asking, and drank half of it. He looked at Gabe.

"Morning," said Suzu.

That had been his first line in Ice's chat, years ago, in a stream with eleven people in it. *morning*. Ice had never forgotten it.

Gabe looked at him with his mouth open.

"Are you *good*?" said Gabe.

Suzu thought about it. Like a cat deciding whether to get up.

"Yes," he said. And went back to the guest room with Ice's tea, and shut the door.

Gabe stood at the counter for a long time.

"He took your *tea*," he said, at last, faintly.

"He does that," said Ice. And made another.

***

He sat on the north parapet at the eye on the Wednesday night with Hyv on his knee, and looked at the round town.

It was a thing he did now. Every night, near the end. Hyv on his knee with her arm round his neck and her tail round his ankle. Looking north at the gold point at the bottom of the sky. And then round, at everything else, all of it, lit.

He could see the yard behind the Hart from up here. Milli's door, standing on its own in its frame on two hinges with stars on them. As he watched, Alice came out of the Hart's back door and walked across the yard and through the door. From one side of nothing to the other. Milli came after her. They stood on the far side of the doorway for a moment, the two of them, and then walked back through it the other way, laughing. Ice couldn't hear them from the roof. He could see them laugh.

He could see the Hart's kitchen window. Chernn dipping. Deston on the stool beside her with the wick-rod, three weeks married, out there. Still on the stool.

He could see the north door. Gabe and Rae standing in it, side by side, not holding it. Just standing in it, the way Gabe did now on quiet nights, looking up the road at the ninety-four flames.

*It's catching*, Alice had said, a year ago.

He looked at Hyv, on his knee. She was looking north at the gold point, the way she did. She saw everything. She was very loud now, about him, about everything. He'd said *she's very loud and I love it* at the long table three weeks ago, and *mine*. Out loud, once. And the room hadn't fallen down.

He thought about asking her something.

Not now. Not here. He didn't know where. He didn't even know what he'd say. He thought about it the way he'd thought about the weight, a long time ago, before Salty showed him how to start it. A thing that wasn't ready yet, that would be very heavy, that you'd have to know exactly where to set your feet for.

Somewhere with a view, Alice had said once. She'd been talking about something else.

"You're thinking," said Hyv. Without looking at him.

"Yes."

"About Saturday?"

He didn't say anything.

She turned her head and looked at him. Close. Edge to edge. For a moment he thought she'd see it. She saw everything. But she just looked at him for a long time, and then put her head back on his shoulder, and looked north.

"You'll tell me," she said. "When it's ready."

"Yes," said Ice.

It was the first time she'd ever looked straight at something he was thinking and not seen it. He didn't know why. He thought maybe it was because he didn't know what it was yet himself.

Or maybe she'd seen it, and decided to let him go first.

***

Kei pinned the walk board up in the banner room on the Thursday, on the wall by the door, under the banner on its hooks.

*MASMARK · JULY*
*the bridge · properly*
*Everybody who wants to watch*
*On foot · fourteen days · from Sat 7 Jul*
*Nobody alone*

Under it, sixty-two names in pencil, and a lion, and a word that could have been a sheep, and *SCREAMING* in letters bigger than Gabe's. Kei had added one line since the stream, at the very bottom, in his small neat hand:

*Before that: Sat 19 May · the Seven Below · Sketch v. Suzuyunn · best of three*

People stopped in front of it all evening. Ice watched them from the first of the nine. They'd read the top, about the bridge, and nod. Then they'd read the bottom line and stop, and read it again, and look round the banner room for Suzu.

Suzu was asleep. Beside Kotori, with his chin going down toward his soup.

"He's *asleep*," said Leaka, very loudly, in front of the board. "Two days before. He's *asleep*."

"He's lazy," said about six people at once.

"Darling," said Dennis, from his ledger, "Hobb's taking money on him drawing. Somebody put a silver on it at forty to one." He didn't look up. "I can't tell you who. It wasn't me."

Kotori, beside Suzu, very carefully did not look at anybody.

***

On the Thursday, at the third dawn, he went and sat on the north steps.

Suzu was there, asleep, on the step below Isa's chair, with the scythe across his knees. Isa was awake. She turned her face toward Ice as he sat down on the other side of her chair.

"The one under the floor," said Isa.

"Hi, Isa."

"You've known this one longest," said Isa. She didn't look at Suzu. She didn't need to; she could hear him breathing. "Of all of them."

"Yes."

"What's he like?" said Isa. "In a fight."

Ice thought about it. He'd been thinking about it all week.

"I don't know," he said. "I've known him longer than anybody. I've watched him in more fights than anybody. And I've never once known where he'd be." He looked at Suzu, asleep. "He's the only one."

Isa was quiet for a long time.

"My mother said there was always one," she said. "On the hill. In every life. One person you couldn't see coming, however long you looked." Her old hands moved in the blanket. "She said it was a gift. That there was somebody in the world who could still surprise you." She turned her face back to the north road. "She said most people never get one."

Ice didn't say anything.

On the step below, without opening his eyes, Suzu said: "I'm not asleep."

"I know," said Isa.

"You knew?" said Ice.

"He breathes slower when he's asleep," said Isa. "I've sat next to him every morning since March. He's been awake since you sat down." She almost smiled. "That's the only time I've ever known where he'd be. And only because I'm blind."

***

He went down to the Seven Below on the Friday, late, after the long table. He'd thought it would be empty.

Suzu was lying on the floor of the ring on his back with his hands folded on his chest, looking at the ceiling. In the middle of the seven silver lines, where they met. Like a man lying in a field.

Ice sat down against the north-east wall, under the mark.

Neither of them said anything for a while.

"If I lose," said Suzu, to the ceiling.

"You won't lose."

"If I win all three."

"You won't," said Ice.

Suzu turned his head on the stone and looked at him.

"He'll stop looking," said Ice. "After the first one. He'll stop looking for you where you're going to be. He's been practising on RX all week."

"I know," said Suzu. "I watched him get RX. From the roof. On the forty-first time."

"Then you know."

Suzu looked back at the ceiling.

"Even," he said.

Ice looked at him for a long time across the round pale room.

"You bet on it," he said. "At Hobb's. A silver. At forty to one."

"Hobb told you?"

"Dennis told me. Dennis can't not."

Suzu lay on the floor of the Seven Below and smiled at the ceiling. About a millimetre.

"It's not a bet if you know," he said.

"You don't know," said Ice. "Nobody knows. That's the point of you."

"I know where I won't be," said Suzu. "And I know where he won't be, now. Since the forty-first." He shut his eyes. "That's the same as even."

Ice sat against the wall and thought about that for a long time. He was still thinking about it when Suzu's breathing went slow and he was asleep for real. On the floor of the ring. The night before. In the middle of the room where everybody would be looking tomorrow.

Ice didn't wake him. He went up the stair and left the lamp lit.

***

On the Saturday night, at a quarter to eight, Suzu was awake for the words.

He was always awake for the words. But usually he woke up for them and went back to sleep. On Saturday he was awake before Kei stood up. He was sitting up straight in his chair beside Kotori with his scythe against the table beside him, its edge catching the lamp, sharp for the first time. And he was looking at nothing in particular, the way he looked at things.

"Set one to watch," said Kei.

"*So the rest may sleep*," said sixty-two people and a sheep.

Suzu said it too. Then he stood up, picked up his scythe, and walked out of the banner room toward the stair down to the Seven Below. Without saying anything to anybody. Kotori got up and went after him with a cup.

The long table watched him go. Nobody said anything for about three seconds.

"He's *awake*," said Leaka, very quietly, for Leaka.

***

The step was full. Fuller than it had ever been, for Xav and Ice.

RX and Malcolm, with the notebook open on his knee to a clean page. *SKETCH v. SUZUYUNN*. Val with her satchel and a blank sheet ready. Gabe, too big for the step, with Rae beside him. Leaka and Curse. Xola and Slep; Xola had written to Thea and Gigi every day since they went north to see the hawthorn flower, and would write tonight, in capitals, about all of it. Hako, muttering under his breath in the voice, *the elusive harvester, observed for the first time in its natural*— until Xola hit him. Kite and Ash and Drew. Dennis with his ledger. Hobb the turnip carter, of all people, at the top of the stair, with his slate under his arm and a look of great anxiety.

Hyv on the bottom step, with her knees up and her tail round her feet. Not calling. Nobody had asked her not to. She just knew there'd be nothing to call.

And Kotori, beside the ring, at the edge of the silver lines, with a cup in each hand.

Ice didn't sit on the step.

He went to the corner by the hatch. At the top of the stair, behind Ama Corrie's counter, where the lamp didn't quite reach. The corner where Suzu had stood for every one of Ice's fights with Xav, every time, watching. Where nobody looked. Ice stood there in the dark with his shield on his back. He looked down the stair into the round pale room with the seven silver lines, and waited.

Hyv, on the bottom step, turned her head and looked up the stair. Straight at the corner. Straight at him.

She didn't say anything. She just looked. Then she turned back to the ring.

Below, in the middle of the Seven Below, Xav stood with his blades out and down. And across from him, three paces off, with his long silver hair over one shoulder and an old scythe in his hands that had never once been this sharp, Suzu.

Not in the corner.

In the middle of the room. Where everybody was looking.
