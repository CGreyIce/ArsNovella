Hana had written the word *Partner* in her notebook seven times in three years, and rubbed it out seven times.

She always wrote it in pencil. That was the point of pencil. You could write a thing you wanted so much it was embarrassing, look at it for a minute, and then make it go away before anybody saw. It had been on the first page of the notebook she had before this one, small, in the corner. And on a list of goals she'd made one New Year, third from the bottom. And once, very late at night, after a stream where forty people had watched her play a puzzle game for four hours, it had been on its own, in the middle of a blank page, written very hard.

She'd rubbed that one out too. It had left a dent in the paper. You could still feel it if you ran your finger over the page.

Then Curse had given her a fountain pen at a hawker centre in Singapore, because pencil was a crime. And the egg had come. And she'd stopped writing things she could rub out.

***

She streamed every day.

She hadn't meant to. Three times a week had been her rhythm for three years, Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday. Three times a week was what she'd had energy for, before the hiatus. But the egg didn't care about rhythms. Every evening at eight she lay down in it. Every evening Milli and Jared were there on the third ledge of Quarrymouth when she woke up, the same ledge, two feet apart. And every evening the number in the corner of her eye was higher than she could quite believe, and she turned the stream on, because how could you not.

She dug.

That was what she did, mostly, every evening. She went down into the tunnel under the third ledge with her pick and a lamp, past the beam Jared had cut and the others he'd cut since. She worked on the wall of little walking figures, very slowly, a piece at a time. Not digging into it. Digging round it. Taking away three hundred years of quarry rubble that had been piled against it, so that more of the line of little people could be seen. More and more of them, carrying their lights along the wall into the dark.

Milli asked her on the eighth night.

They were sitting on the edge of the third ledge after the stream, with their legs over the quarry. The sea was black below them, and the lamps of the harbour were going up one by one. Jared had gone to sleep in America; it was very early there, or very late. It was just the two of them. Same ledge. Same town. Same room, nearly.

"Is it closer?" said Milli.

She knew what he meant. She'd known him long enough.

*One day.* The thing they'd said at the bottom of her list in December, *talk about one day*, and then talked about, on the balcony and the car-park roof and a lot of calls at the hour in the middle. It had stopped being a word a long time ago. It had turned into a place they were both going, without a map, without knowing how far it was.

"I don't know," said Hana honestly. "I think so."

"In here," said Milli, in his careful English. "Every night. Same town." He looked at the harbour lamps. "It feels closer in here."

"It is closer in here," said Hana. "You're two feet away."

"Two feet," agreed Milli. He was quiet for a moment. "In December I was eleven thousand kilometres away, and then I was two feet away, for two months. Then eleven thousand again." He turned his head and looked at her. "Now it's two feet every night. And eleven thousand every morning." He smiled. "I like the nights better."

She put her head on his shoulder and didn't say anything. She didn't need to.

***

She applied on the twelfth day.

She'd done the sums in the notebook, in blue ink, the way Curse would have wanted. Twelve days streamed. Twenty-five hours. An average she'd never seen in her life, so far over the line that it looked like a typing error. She'd checked the numbers four times. Then she'd filled in the form, very carefully, on her laptop at her desk in front of the egg. She'd read every question twice, and answered every one honestly, and pressed send. Then she'd sat looking at the screen for a long time.

She didn't tell anyone. Not Milli. Not Jared. Not chat. Not Ice, who'd raided her on the first night with twenty-five thousand people and called her Alice. Not Leaka, who'd have shouted it to the whole internet before Hana had finished the sentence.

She just wrote it in the notebook.

*Applied.*

In pen. Where she couldn't rub it out.

***

The email came on Tuesday, the thirteenth of April, while she was in the egg.

She didn't see it. She was three ledges down a quarry, in a tunnel, by lamplight, taking rubble away from a little stone man with a hat and a light. Jared was telling them about his week at work, which had been long. Milli was telling Jared he should become a carpenter in a quarry town instead. Jared was saying he'd consider it.

She came out of the egg at midnight and sat up in the gel, in her room in Singapore, and reached for her phone the way she always did. She looked at the screen, close to her face.

*Welcome to the Twitch Partner Program.*

She read it.

She read it again. She read it the way Hyvred read things, a piece at a time, as if each word might turn into a different word if she looked at it too fast. *Welcome. To the. Twitch. Partner. Program.* Her name. Her channel. *We're excited to have you.*

She sat in the egg in the dark in her room, with the lid up and the phone in her hands, for eleven minutes. She didn't move. She didn't cry. She'd thought she would. She'd thought about this moment for three years, and in all of them she'd thought she'd cry.

She didn't. She just sat there, and looked at it, and felt something in her chest go very still and very quiet. As if something that had been holding on for a long time had finally been allowed to let go.

Then she got out of the egg, and went to her desk, and opened the notebook to a clean page.

She took out Curse's pen.

She wrote it in the middle of the page, on its own, in blue ink. Not hard. Not small. The ordinary size, the size you'd write any word. As if it were just a fact.

*Partner.*

She looked at it.

She couldn't rub it out.

*Then* she cried.

***

She rang Milli first. It was six in the evening in the Netherlands. He picked up on the first ring.

"Hana?"

She didn't say anything. She held the phone up to the camera, close, so he could see the screen.

There was a long silence.

"*Hana*," said Milli.

Then she rang Jared, who was at work. He answered anyway, in a whisper, from what sounded like a cupboard. She held the phone up, and there was a silence. Then a sound like a man in a cupboard trying very hard not to shout, and failing.

Then, because she'd promised herself she would, she rang Curse. He was in Australia, where it was two in the morning. He picked up on the fourth ring, sounding like he'd been asleep, and said "*What*." She held up the phone.

He looked at it for a long time.

"Did you write it in pen?" said Curse.

"I wrote it in pen."

"Good," said Curse, and hung up, and went back to sleep. Hana laughed so hard she had to sit on the floor.
