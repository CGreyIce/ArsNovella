title: Least Effort

Suzu had been lazy his whole life, and he'd never once thought it was the same thing as not caring.

People got that wrong. They saw him asleep at the long table with his chin going down toward his soup. On the north steps in the sun. On a stone in the still water, sitting up, in the nothing, because there was nothing to do until dawn and it was a waste not to. They thought lazy meant he didn't mind about things.

It meant the opposite. It meant he minded so much about the few things he minded about that he didn't have anything left over for the rest.

He minded about Ice. He'd minded about a light in a jar, for nine months. About Isa, about Kotori, about the long table at a quarter to eight. About a stone the right size in the still water and a notch cut with a thorn. He'd minded about those enough to go first across three hundred stones in the dark. To say *no* to Ice on the two hundred and sixteenth, which nobody had ever done.

And he minded about fighting.

He'd never told anybody in here. Nobody had asked.

***

Out there, a long time ago, he'd been near the top of every ranked ladder he'd ever played.

He didn't think about it much. It wasn't a secret. It just wasn't the kind of thing he said. Nobody in here had ever asked what he'd been good at before.

And he'd never once gone where a fight expected him to.

That wasn't a skill either, the way he saw it. It was just being lazy properly. Everybody in a fight was looking somewhere. At the tank, at the healer, at the gap they'd decided you'd use. The place they were looking was where all the effort was. Where you'd have to push through them to get anything done. So he went where nobody was looking. It was always less effort. You'd step off a boardwalk into black water on the right. Two people on the planks would be waiting their turn, looking at the man with the shield in the middle. And you'd come up beside them, and cut once, low and level. And that was two.

He'd done that in the first winter, in the fen, by a channel, against Nightjar. It was the only time in two and a half years he'd fought a person in here.

Xav had been there. In the reeds, behind Kite. He hadn't seen it.

Nobody had, really. Ice had felt the boardwalk go lighter. That was all.

***

"You volunteered me," said Suzu.

Out there, on the Monday morning, in the back room at Melaka Raya. The two of them had come out of the eggs at the same time, side by side, the way they had since the eleventh day of the game. The back room was grey with the morning. Gabe's egg was already open in the corner; he'd gone to make kopi.

Ice was sitting on the edge of his egg with his feet on the floor, looking at the wall.

"Yes," said Ice.

"At the long table. In front of everybody."

"Yes."

"That's effort," said Suzu.

"I know," said Ice.

Suzu sat on the edge of his own egg and looked at him. He'd known Ice a long time. Longer than the wind game, and the guild Ice had dragged him into one winter. Longer than the shoplot, and the guest room, and the pass, and the bus home to his mum's at weekends. He'd known him long enough to know that Ice never did anything at a long table without thinking about it properly first.

"Why," said Suzu.

Ice thought about it. Properly.

"In October," he said. "A year and a half ago. The first one, in the Seven Below. Xav at three. You'd have beaten him."

"Yes," said Suzu. He didn't have to think about that one.

"In January you'd have beaten him. In May, in the cellar, you'd have beaten him." Ice looked at the wall. "September. I don't know. Maybe."

"Maybe," agreed Suzu.

"January this year," said Ice. "He drew with me. Seventy minutes. He learned to hold still off Malcolm in a ditch." He looked at Suzu at last. "He's good enough now."

"For what?"

"For you," said Ice. "He's worth the effort now."

***

Suzu didn't say anything for a long time.

He thought about the hatch at the top of the stair down to the Seven Below. Behind Ama Corrie's counter, in the corner, where the lamp didn't quite reach. He'd stood there for every one of Ice's fights with Xav. October, January, September, January. Not on the step with the others. Above them, in the corner by the hatch, where nobody looked. Watching. The way Ice had been in everybody's chat for years. Nobody had noticed Suzu was there until afterwards, if they noticed at all.

He'd watched Xav learn the river off Ice and practise it on RX until RX dreamed about it. He'd watched him read the rain, the one after, off Val's page. He'd watched him walk into the weight on purpose in September, just to see it come round. And in January he'd watched him hold still for nine minutes at one per cent, the way Malcolm held still by a ditch full of moonbells.

He'd watched every bit of it. And every time, standing by the hatch in the dark, he'd thought the same thing. He hadn't said it, because nobody had asked.

*Nearly.*

"You knew," said Suzu.

"Since I've known you," said Ice.

"I never said."

"You never had to." Ice looked at him. "You stand by the hatch. Every time. In the corner. I can see you from the ring." He almost smiled. "You think nobody sees the corner."

Suzu looked at him for a long time.

"Hyv sees the corner," he said.

"Hyv sees everything," said Ice. "She's never said either."

***

"I don't want to be good at it in here," said Suzu, eventually.

He hadn't known he was going to say it. It came out the way *no* had, on the two hundred and sixteenth. It was true, and it was first.

Ice waited.

"People ask you," said Suzu. "Every day. On forums. At the gate. *FIGHT ME (ICE)*. Fourteen hundred of them." He looked at his hands. "You fought seven. And said *that's all*. And they still ask. You can't go to Masmark without somebody on a bridge wanting you properly." He was quiet. "If I'm good at it in here, people will ask me too."

"Yes."

"That's effort."

"Yes," said Ice. "It is."

Suzu sat on the edge of the egg in the grey room.

"You do it anyway," he said. "You get asked, and you say *okay*. And you go."

"I like it," said Ice. Simply. The way he'd said it to Xav against a wall in September. *I like it. It's the best part.* "Not the asking. The fight. When somebody's good enough that you have to use all of it." He looked at Suzu. "You like it too."

Suzu didn't answer.

"In the fen," said Ice. "By the channel. When you came up out of the water beside the spear and the staff. I saw your face in the green light when the spell went off." He was quiet. "You were smiling. About a millimetre."

Suzu looked at the floor of the back room for a long time.

Then, very slightly, at the corner of his mouth, about a millimetre.

"Yes," said Suzu.

***

He told Kotori on the Tuesday, in the Hart's kitchen, with the kettles.

He didn't have to. She knew. She'd known him in here longer than anybody but Ice. She'd stood behind him on a post in the water at Nightjar, and on the hill, and on every one of three hundred stones going north, with her small level voice saying *keep counting*. She'd seen the millimetre in the green light too. She'd told Xav *he'll be very good* and then not looked at him, because she was very bad at not looking at people when she was keeping something.

"You're going to like it," said Kotori.

"Yes."

"You're going to like it a *lot*."

"Yes," said Suzu.

Kotori poured two cups. She gave him one. She leaned against the range with the other and looked at him over it.

"Will you let him win one?"

Suzu thought about it.

"No," he said.

"Good," said Kotori. Which was his. She'd borrowed it a long time ago. "He'd know."

***

He didn't practise.

Xav came looking for him. Suzu knew because Xav was very bad at looking for people quietly. He looked in the settling ring and the north tunnel and the timber yard and the Hart's yard, where Milli's door stood on its own. And every time he looked in a place Suzu wasn't, Suzu was somewhere else. Not hiding. He was asleep on the north steps, mostly, with Isa. It was just that nobody ever looked at the north steps.

Xav found him there on the Thursday. Suzu heard him come. He heard him stop at the bottom of the steps and stand there for a long time. He heard Isa tell him to go away.

He didn't open his eyes. That wasn't laziness. He was listening.

To how Xav stood. How long he stopped for. The sound of his boots on the bottom step when he turned round to go: left foot first, turning on the ball of it, the way he did in the ring before he moved. He'd watched it from the corner by the hatch four times. He didn't need to watch it again. He just needed to hear it once more, so he'd know it was still the same.

It was the same.

***

On the Thursday afternoon, he went and watched Xav practise.

Not from the market. From the roof. Up the two hundred and twelve, round to the north parapet at the eye, where Hyv sat on Ice's knee in the evenings. He lay down on his front on the warm leads of the dome with his chin on his arms and looked down at the settling ring by the fountain. Xav was in it with RX, a long way below, small.

Xav was practising not looking where RX was going to be.

Suzu watched him do it for an hour. RX hit him in the ear with a training stick about every fifteen seconds. Xav kept getting hit, and kept doing it anyway. Looking at where RX *was*. Not where he'd be. Trying to unlearn a year and a half of being early.

*Don't look for him where he's going to be*, Ice had told him. In the Seven Below, against the wall. Suzu had heard; he'd been in the corner by the hatch.

He watched Xav get hit in the ear forty times in an hour, and not stop.

And on the forty-first time, Xav didn't get hit. RX went left, very fast, the way RX did when he was getting cocky. Xav didn't go left after him. He didn't go anywhere. He just stood where he was, looking at where RX *was*, and put his training stick in the place RX's ear was about to arrive at.

RX walked into it. *Tock*.

"*Ow*," said RX, faintly, from a long way below.

On the roof of the dome, on the warm leads, with his chin on his arms, Suzu smiled. About a millimetre.

"*Good*," he said, to nobody.

He'd have to go to the Seven Below on Saturday and do some work after all. He found he didn't mind.

***

Malcolm found him on the Wednesday, with his notebook.

"Rules," said Malcolm. He sat down on the step beside Suzu without being asked. He'd started doing that since the draw, sitting down beside people. "Best of three. Until one yields or falls. I call it if it goes long."

"Okay."

"How long will it go?" Malcolm had his pencil ready. "I like to know. For the notebook. I'll say a number, and then I'll be wrong, and I'll write that down too."

Suzu thought about it.

"Not March," he said.

Malcolm looked at him for a long time. Then he wrote it down. *Not March*. And underlined it, and went away looking pleased. As though somebody had finally given him a prediction worse than his own.

***

Hobb opened a book on the Wednesday night, at the Hart.

He was the turnip carter from the Millrace road who'd run a book on *when* the six came, two summers ago. And then on *how long* at the class fights. He did it at a table by the fire with a slate and a stub of chalk and a lot of people round him. Suzu went past on his way to the kitchen and stopped to read the slate, because it was there.

*SKETCH v. SUZUYUNN · BEST OF THREE*
*Sketch 2–0 · evens*
*Sketch 2–1 · 3 to 1*
*Suzuyunn 2–1 · 8 to 1*
*Suzuyunn 2–0 · 20 to 1*

Most of the money was on *2–0*. A lot of the round town had seen Xav draw with Ice. Nobody had seen Suzu do anything but sleep.

"There's no line for even," said Suzu.

Hobb looked up at him. He went slightly pale. He'd never had one of the fighters come and read his slate before.

"Even?" said Hobb.

"One each," said Suzu. "And the last one level."

"That's not a result. That's a—" Hobb stopped. "There's no line for that. Nobody'd bet that."

Suzu took a silver out of his coat and put it on the table by the chalk.

"Write one," he said.

Hobb looked at the silver for a long time. Then, slowly, at the bottom of the slate, under *20 to 1*, he wrote: *EVEN · one each, the third level · 40 to 1*. And took the silver, and wrote *Suzuyunn* beside it in a very small hand, as if he didn't want anybody to notice.

Dennis noticed. Dennis noticed everything that was written down anywhere.

"Darling," said Dennis, at the next table, very quietly, to his ledger. "He's just bet on a *draw*. Against *himself*."

"It's not a bet if you know," said Kotori, passing with a tray. Which was Dennis's, from the class fights. And went on into the kitchen without looking at anybody.

***

On the Thursday evening, after the roof, he took the scythe to Joss's smithy.

Ina was at the anvil. Old Joss was on his stool by the fire with his pipe. They both looked up when Suzu came in. Then they looked at the scythe in his hands, because nobody had ever seen him bring it in before.

"You've never sharpened that," said Ina.

"It never needed it," said Suzu. "For moonbells and Unwatched. They don't mind."

"And for people?"

"People mind," said Suzu.

Ina took it. He turned it over in his hands in the firelight. A long curved blade on a long ash snath, old, worn pale where hands had held it. A war scythe once, a widow's husband's, straightened for a war and bent back to mow for forty years. *It always cuts a bit high*, the widow had said, when she gave it to Suzu in the first week. It hadn't, with him. For him it had cut exactly the right height from the first stroke. Nobody knew why.

Ina sharpened it. Slowly, on the big stone, with Joss watching. Neither of them said anything. When it was done, Ina ran his thumb along the edge, very lightly, and looked at it for a long time.

"It's sharp now," said Ina.

"Thank you."

"It's never been this sharp." Ina held it out. "Somebody's going to find out what it's for."

Suzu took it.

On his stool by the fire, Old Joss took his pipe out of his mouth.

"My grandfather mended a scythe like that, once," he said. "For a man from the hill country. Before the war. The man said it was for grass." He put the pipe back. "It wasn't for grass."

***

He sat with Isa on the north steps at the third dawn on the Friday. The way he did every morning. He'd carried her out, and set the chair down at the top of the steps facing up the north road, and sat on the step below with the scythe across his knees.

He didn't sleep. For once.

"You're awake," said Isa.

"Yes."

"You're never awake." She turned her face toward him, close. "Is it tomorrow? The fight?"

"Tomorrow."

"Are you frightened?"

Suzu thought about it. Properly. He'd learned that off Ice, the way everybody learned everything off everybody now.

"No," he said.

"Why not?"

"Because I know where everything will be," said Suzu. "And I won't be there."

Isa was quiet for a long time.

"You carried a lamp for nine months," she said. "Into the dark. Across my mothers' stones. And set it on the crown. And you came back in the spring with another stone, for the way." Her old hand came out of the blanket and lay on his head, the way it did. "And now your hands are empty."

"I've got a scythe," said Suzu.

"*Hm*," said Isa. "A scythe's for cutting things that are ready." She took her hand back. "Is he ready?"

Suzu thought about Xav in the settling ring on the forty-first time. Not going left. Standing where he was, and putting the stick where RX's ear was about to be.

"Nearly," he said.

***

At a quarter to eight on the Saturday morning, out there, before the night of the fight, he lay in the guest room at Melaka Raya. He'd come out of the egg and gone straight to bed. He looked at the ceiling with the fan going.

He thought about the corner by the hatch.

He'd stood there four times. Five, counting January. Watching a man with two blades and a sketchbook chase his oldest friend across a round pale room, and get closer every time. Nobody had seen him there except Ice and Hyv, and neither of them had said.

Tomorrow night he wasn't going to be in the corner.

He was going to be in the ring. With three hundred thousand people watching through Ice's eyes from the step, and Malcolm calling it, and Kotori on the beat. In the middle of the room, where everybody would be looking.

It would be the most effort he'd ever gone to in his life.

He found he was smiling at the ceiling. About a millimetre. Then a bit more than that.

Then he went to sleep, because it was a waste not to.
