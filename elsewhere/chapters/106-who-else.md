Hana had been a Partner for four days, and she'd cried on every one of them, and she was going to cry today too. She'd known that before she came through the door. She'd told Milli so on the ledge. *I'm going to cry in front of Kestrel*, she'd said, and Milli had said *yes*, and kissed her forehead, and said *tell me everything after*.

She'd cried at the city. Obviously. Anybody would have cried at the city.

She'd cried at Tiger in the fountain, laughing, which counted.

And she'd cried, a lot, at Hyvred walking across the square in a straight line with her fists at her sides. Hyvred hitting Ice twice in the arm. Then Ice putting his hand on Hyvred's head between her horns, and Hyvred making a noise like a kettle and going to hide behind Bee's wings, which you could see straight through. Hana had laughed so hard at that she'd had to hold onto the balustrade. Tiger had held onto her. Drew had held onto both of them and said *I told you* about four times.

And she'd cried at Leaka. Leaka had waited, for once in her life, until Hyvred was safely behind Bee's wings. Then she'd marched across the square to Ice holding something out in both hands: a hot apple from the woman at the fair in Merrowgate, still steaming, carried through a door from the other side of the world.

"CGI," said Leaka.

"Leaka."

"EAT SOMETHING GREEN."

"It's red."

"IT'S A *GESTURE*, CGI."

And he'd eaten it, all of it, standing in the square, while she watched him with her arms folded. Then she'd said, quite quietly for her, "I can't type in your chat any more." And he'd said, "I know. Tif made a command." Leaka had gone bright red and come straight back to Hana and put her face in Hana's shoulder and said into it, "*HE KNOWS ABOUT THE COMMAND.*"

So she was quite wrung out already, at twenty past eight, when the man in the grey coat finally got everyone to stand still.

***

"Hi," he said. "I'm Teo. Community. I'm the one who writes the letters."

He was standing at the top of the terrace steps, under the great dome, with the white city going away gold behind him. He looked young and nervous and very pleased, like somebody who'd been waiting for months to show people a thing and was suddenly afraid they wouldn't like it.

"Thank you for coming," said Teo. "Some of you came a long way to get to a door. Some of you are in the middle of something." His eyes went to Ice, and to Kestrel, and away again quickly. "We know. We'll be quick."

Nobody said anything. Twenty people on a terrace in a city with no name. Even Tavinho was quiet, and even Maigo, and even Leaka, which Hana hadn't known was possible.

"This is one of the capitals," said Teo. "There are five. None of you can get here yet. None of you will for a long time." He looked out at the towers. "Nobody gets in without earning it. Not even you. Especially not you. We just wanted you to see it first. Before anyone. So you'd know it was real. That it's here, and it's waiting, and it's worth the walk."

"What's it called?" said Tiger.

"We're not telling you," said Teo, and grinned for the first time. "Not yet. That's yours to find out. Same as everyone."

"*Rude*," said Tiger, delighted.

***

There was a page to sign.

It came up in front of each of them, the way windows did. A long plain page of agreement: what they could say about tonight, and what they couldn't, and for how long. *Nothing about the city. Nothing about the doors. Nothing about what you've been told until we say.* Hana read all of it. Vivian, apparently, had read Ice's version and covered it in pencil, and Hana had found that so comforting she'd read hers twice.

At the bottom there was a line for her name.

She didn't sign with her finger, the way the window wanted. She took the pen out of her coat, Curse's fountain pen, the one she'd brought in with her from the white room. She unscrewed the cap and signed in blue ink, in the air, on a window in a city with no name. *Hanarisu.* The ink stayed. The window didn't seem to mind.

[ The ELSEWHERE Creator Program
Welcome, Hanarisu. ]

Then, beside her name, floating where everyone could see it, a very small grey egg with a line through it like a horizon.

[ Creator ]

She looked at it for a long time.

"It's in pen," said a voice beside her.

She turned. Ice was standing there, rubbing his left arm absently, looking at her badge.

"It's in *pen*," said Hana, and burst into tears again.

***

Teo answered questions after that. He was better at it than Hana had expected. He said *I don't know* when he didn't know, and *I'm not allowed to* when he wasn't, and he never once said *we're so excited*. Drew, beside her, looked faintly impressed.

Tavinho asked whether there'd be dancing in the game. Teo said there already was; Tavinho had just been doing it with a sword.

Mirae asked, very precisely, how long it would take a fully levelled party to reach the capitals, assuming optimal play. Teo said *longer than you'd like*, and Mirae wrote it down as if it were a number.

Tiger asked whether he was allowed to fall in the fountain again. Teo said he could fall in anything he liked.

Maigo asked whether they could name the city. Teo said no. Maigo said he had eleven. Teo said he was sure they were very good, and Kei said, gently, *they're not*.

Higu asked whether the dome had a ceiling. Teo looked at her for slightly too long and said *every dome has a ceiling*, and Higu wrote that down.

Drew asked what they actually wanted from the nineteen of them. He asked it calmly, the way he asked everything. He'd been to these before, he'd told Hana, and there was always a real answer under the polite one.

Teo looked at him for a moment.

"Nothing you aren't already doing," he said. "Honestly. We watched you all since launch. You walked places nobody'd walked. You held things all night. You built a guild out of people who'd never met. You fell in canals." Tiger cheered. "You showed people what it's *like* in here. Not what it's for. What it's like." He shrugged. "That's all. Keep doing that. We'll pay you a bit and show you things early. That's the deal."

Drew nodded slowly. "Huh," he said, which from Drew was a standing ovation.

And then Kestrel asked a question. From the far end of the terrace, without unfolding her arms.

"Who's first through the next door?"

Everyone looked at her. Then, without meaning to, everyone looked at Ice.

Ice didn't look at anybody. He was looking out over the balustrade, north, past the last towers of the city.

"Whoever gets there," said Teo.

"Huh," said Kestrel. "Good."

***

Afterwards, when people had broken up into little groups along the terrace, Hana went and found Ice at the balustrade.

He was standing on his own, which was how he usually stood. A little apart from Suzu, and Drew, and Hyvred. Hyvred was on the far side of the terrace with her bow up, loosing arrow after arrow at a gold ball on top of a tower, so lost in it she hadn't noticed he'd gone. He'd spent half an hour beside her. Hana had watched him step away from her in the middle of a shot, very quietly, the way you'd leave a room where somebody had fallen asleep. He was still looking north.

Hana looked where he was looking.

From up here you could see the whole city falling away gold in every direction to its walls. And past the walls, a long way off, further than anything, at the very edge of the world, there was a darkness. Not cloud. Not night. The hour before sunset lay over everything else, and it didn't lie over that. It was just dark. Flat and still and very far away.

Nobody had said anything about it. Teo hadn't mentioned it. Hana didn't think anybody had noticed but the two of them, and Kestrel, who was at the other end of the balustrade looking at exactly the same place. And Kei, further along, very still, looking at it the way you'd look at somebody you'd met before.

"Ice," said Hana.

"Alice."

"You *knew*."

He looked at her.

"About the email," said Hana. "Fifty-two minutes. You said *check your email in about an hour*. You said *don't ask how I know*." She folded her arms. "I'm asking."

Ice looked back out at the dark for a moment.

"Vivian," he said.

"*Vivian?*"

"They wrote to the company in March. The letter." He rubbed his arm again. "Vivian wrote back. She struck some things out. And she asked who else was in it. They wouldn't say. So she told them who else *should* be."

Hana stared at him.

"Vivian did that?"

"I asked her to."

"*Ice*—"

"They said yes," said Ice. "They said *when she's a Partner*. That was the rule. Partners and people they'd watched. So I waited." He shrugged slightly. "You did the rest. You wrote it in pen. I just knew it was coming before you did."

Hana stood at the balustrade of a city with no name, in front of a darkness at the edge of the world. She cried so hard she had to put both hands over her face.

"You're always crying," said Ice.

"I'm *always* crying," said Hana, through her hands. "I've *always* been crying. You've *known* me for *years*, you *know* I'm always crying—"

"I know."

"*Don't* be nice to me—"

"Hyvred said that," said Ice. "On Monday. Same words."

Hana took her hands away from her face and looked at him. Then across the terrace at Hyvred, who had just turned round with her bow, found him gone, and was stamping her feet on the white stone while Bee laughed at her. Then back at Ice, who was rubbing his arm.

She started laughing. She couldn't stop. She laughed with her face still wet, at the edge of the world, until Tiger came over to find out what was funny. Then he fell over on the wet patch he'd left on the terrace himself, and that made it worse.

***

She came back through her door at nine o'clock onto the third ledge at Quarrymouth.

Milli was sitting on the step outside her door, in the dark, waiting, the way he'd said he would. The quarry went down below them in ledges and stairs and lamps, and the sea went out beyond it, black and quiet. On the wall at the back of the ledge, the old buried city's half-smiling face looked out over all of it.

"Well?" said Milli.

Hana sat down beside him on the step.

"I can't tell you," she said. "I signed a thing."

"In pen?"

"In *pen*."

Milli looked at her. At her wet face, and her red eyes, and the little grey egg floating beside her name.

"Was it big?" he said.

"It was *so* big," said Hana, and leaned on him, and he put his arm round her. They sat there on the step at the top of the quarry for a long time, while she didn't tell him anything at all.

Then she turned her stream back on.
