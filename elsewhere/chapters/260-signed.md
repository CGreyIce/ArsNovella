Kei stood at the top of the south steps at the first dawn with his map case under his arm, and watched the round town come home from the longest night.

They came in from everywhere. That was the thing about the dawn after. Nobody had told them to. Everybody who'd held something came to the south steps of the Hall of Roads at the first dawn and sat down on them. The way they had last year, and the year before, when it was ten people and a swamp and a gate.

The windows first, because the windows were nearest. Deston and Chernn and Dennis came out of the houses round the market where they'd been all night, keeping four hundred candles. Chernn had wax on her hands. Deston was red to the ears for no reason anybody could see. Dennis had his pencil ledger and was saying *four hundred and thirty-one, darling, I counted twice*.

Then the well. Hana and Milli and Jared, from the triangle by Jared's oak lid. Hana with the star in her hand, warm, not lit. Milli's arm round her. Jared saying *y'all* to everybody who went past, softly, as if it were good morning.

Then market north. Xav and RX and Malcolm. Xav with a new page in his sketchbook he wouldn't show anybody. RX saying *eleven hundred and four* under his breath, which nobody asked about.

The banner room. Cal and Sheng Yang and Newish, from the long table, where they'd held the room with the chair by the north door empty and the jar gone from the table. Cal said *I went ahead in order to come back* to Kei on the way up the steps. Kei said *I heard*.

The north door. Last. Because the north door had held the most. Salty and Wendy, not quite holding hands. Bee, white, with her pencils, walking very slowly. Tiger and Maigo and Niko, from the first mile. And Gabe, with his shield on his back and his face wet. And Rae beside him. Not a pace behind. Beside.

Kei looked at that for a long time.

Then the roads.

***

Meridian came in from the north-east road at a quarter past eight. Twenty in deep blue under one gold line. Harlan at the front with his great two-bladed axe, talking. And at the back, very small, in plain blue, with a tall still man beside her: Aurelie.

She came up the south steps past the round town, the way she'd come up them in September with her feet hurting. And stopped at the top, beside Kei.

"North-east road," said Aurelie.

"I know," said Kei. "I heard you held."

"We held," said Aurelie. She looked down at the round market, at all of them on the steps. At the fountain, and the ring of nine dark stones, and the Hart. "It wasn't looking. Harlan says it was like holding a road against the tide. You don't fight the tide. You just stand there till it goes out."

"Tiger said *like weather*."

"Tiger's right," said Aurelie. She looked at him. "First longest night. Next to somebody." She was quiet. "I've done eleven Long Nights in two games. Ten thousand people. Sixty-one Halls. I've never once held a road for somebody else's town." Her face did something. "Next year, ask me again."

"I will," said Kei.

Behind her, Vane, tall and still, looked down at the round town.

"We're fine," he said.

"I know," said Kei.

***

He was watching the north-west road.

He'd been watching it since the first dawn. Since the lamps came back on from the far end and Higu called it from the eye, and the longest night ended, and everybody started coming to the steps. He'd watched the windows and the well and the north door and Meridian. He'd been watching the north-west road the whole time, where it came into the market between two houses. Waiting.

At twenty past eight, three people came out of it.

Side by side. Not quite in step. Drew on the right, with his shield on his back, walking the way Drew walked, every step put down as if it mattered. Kite on the left, with an empty bow and an empty quiver. And in the middle, with two short curved blades crossed on her back and a dark coat with loose threads at the collar: Ash.

They stopped at the end of the road. Where it came into the market.

Kei watched Ash stop.

He'd sat with her at the milestone in August. With two cups. And she'd asked him, properly, if she could sign. And he'd said *not today*. *Nobody signs on the day they come in. Ask me again on the longest night. Here. If you still want to, after that, you'll know what it is.*

She'd said *I'll be here*.

She'd been there. Four months. And on a stool at the end of the long table for two weeks. And on the sheet. *Kite. Drew. Ash.* And at a crossroads all night, where the drove road crossed the north-west road. Holding the corner. He'd had Kite's call at five. *North-west. We're fine.* Vane's line. Borrowed.

She was standing at the end of the north-west road, in the morning, looking at the steps. At him.

Kite said something to her. Kei couldn't hear it.

She came across the market.

***

Kite went past her. To the gate.

Kei watched him do it. Kite went across the market a few paces ahead of Ash, round the fountain and the ring of nine stones. And up the south steps, past the round town, which watched him and said nothing. Into the south gate of the Hall of Roads beside Kei. And turned round. And stood in it, with his hands at his sides, where people could see them.

Ash stopped at the bottom of the steps.

The round town was very quiet. Sixty people on the steps, and twenty of Meridian, and an old woman asleep in a chair inside. They'd all heard about the milestone. They'd all walked past it for four months, at the second dusk, and seen somebody sitting there with two blades across her knees. They'd all heard about the chipped cup, and the new one, and *you*, and *fair*. Nobody said anything.

"Who'd stand next to you?" said Kite.

He asked it the way he asked everybody. Kei had watched him do it a thousand times. Not to win anything. Because it was what you asked, at a door.

Ash looked up at him.

"Kite," she said. "Drew." She was quiet for a moment. "Everybody whose road I held." She looked past Kite, at Kei, beside him. "And him. If he says yes."

Kite looked at her for a long time.

"That's four answers," he said.

"I've been practising," said Ash.

Something happened to Kite's narrow face that Kei had seen once before. In August, at this gate, when Ice came up the road and gave him three.

"Fair," said Kite.

***

She came up the steps.

Not fast. Slowly. Past the round town on either side. Past Moonlight Harvest, and Gabe, who was crying, and Rae beside him, who wasn't. Past Hana's five, and Riverbank's three, and Bee with her pencils. Past Tiger, who'd stood at the first mile last year on his own saying *not yet*. He looked at her as she went past him and didn't say anything, and nodded once.

She stopped in front of Kei. Two steps below. Where you stopped, at the gate, to ask.

"Can I sign?" said Ash.

***

Kei didn't answer straight away.

He never did. He looked at her for a long time. At her calm narrow face, older round the eyes. At the two blades on her back, and the loose threads at her collar where a little dark bird had been. Cut off, badly, the winter before last, the way Kite had cut his.

He thought about the long table round the fire on a Sunday, the first winter, arguing about Kite. *He took fourteen people's days.* Chris counting them from the public death log. Wendy saying *that's three*.

He thought about Chris at the milestone in May. *Three hundred and eleven.*

He thought about *start with three*.

"Drew," said Kei.

Drew, at the bottom of the steps, looked up.

"How many?" said Kei.

"Seventy-eight," said Drew. "Gaps. I counted. Off her feet." He was quiet. "And one in front of Kite. When he ran out of arrows. She didn't decide it." He looked at Ash's back. "Her weight was even. All night. She wasn't waiting for anything."

Kei looked at Ash.

"You know what it is now," he said. "You said you wanted to know. In August. At the milestone."

"I know what it is," said Ash.

"What is it?"

She thought about it. Kei watched her think. He'd watched a lot of people think on these steps.

"It's choosing a road," said Ash. "At a crossroads. At dusk." She swallowed. "And then somebody's shield on your right. All night."

Kei looked at her for a very long time.

"Yes," said Kei.

***

The round town let out its breath.

Not loudly. It didn't go off, the way it had for Chernn under *Kai*, or for Milli standing up at the long table. It just breathed out. Sixty people and twenty of Meridian, on the steps, in the morning, all at once.

Ash stood two steps below him and didn't move.

"*Yes*?" she said.

"Yes," said Kei. "Ama Corrie's got the Roll. On the counter." He stood aside. Out of the gate, the way Kite did when he'd asked. "Mind the third step."

"There isn't a third step," said Ash. "Not on these."

"There's always a third step," said Kei. "Ama Corrie says."

***

Ama Corrie had the Roll open on the counter before Ash got there.

Of course she did. She'd been standing behind the counter since the first dawn with her ledger and her broom, and the Roll beside them. And Isa asleep in her chair through the banner room door, under the grey blanket. She looked at Ash over her spectacles as Ash came across the Hall.

She didn't say anything for a long time.

"You sat at the milestone," said Ama Corrie.

"Seven months."

"In the rain."

"Some of it."

"I saw," said Ama Corrie. "From the step. With my broom." She sniffed. "Every evening. You sat in the same place. You never once came up." She looked at Ash. "Most people come up too soon."

She turned the Roll round on the counter. So it faced Ash. And held out the pen.

Not a new page. Kei saw that, from the door. Ama Corrie had turned back. To a page from the first winter. The founding, and the ones who came after. Kei's at the top, and down. And a third of the way down, in a hand that had shaken a little, *Kite*.

And under *Kite*, a line. Empty.

***

"I didn't keep it," said Ama Corrie.

Ash looked at her.

"I keep the pairs," said Ama Corrie. "When two came who were going to be one, one day. I kept the space under *Kai*." She looked at the page. "I didn't keep this one. It kept itself." She was quiet. "Nobody ever wrote there. Every time somebody came after him, the pen went to the next line. I never told it to. It just did."

She tapped the empty line under *Kite* with one finger.

"Some things are pairs," said Ama Corrie. "Some things are just next to each other." She held out the pen again. "This is next to."

Ash took the pen.

She looked at the empty line for a long time. Then at Kite, in the door of the Hall, beside Kei, with his hands at his sides.

Kite didn't say anything. He didn't need to. He looked at the line under his own name, and then at her. And nodded once.

She wrote.

Small. Plain. Not shaking. In the space under *Kite*, on a page from the first winter.

*Ash.*

***

Ama Corrie looked at it over her spectacles.

"Three letters," she said.

"It's the only name I've got," said Ash.

"Hm," said Ama Corrie. And then, very quietly, the thing she said when something was kept, so quietly that only Ash and Kei and Kite heard it: "Good reason."

Out on the steps, sixty people heard nothing. But they saw Ash put the pen down. And Kei, in the door, nod once. And they went off. Softly. The way the round market had gone off under Jared's lanterns when the wind dropped. Gabe loudest, of course. *ASH, ASH, ASH*. Hana crying. Leaka screaming into Curse's sleeve. Rachael saying *finally*. Chris saying *three hundred and eleven, and then three, and then seventy-eight, I have a sheet*. Nobody told him to put it away.

Bee was already at the banner on its hooks under Ama Corrie's stair, with a brush and a pot of gold. Kei watched her paint a dot. Small. On the deep blue, near Kite's. And then two lines from it, very short. One to Kite's dot. One to Drew's.

Sixty-two.

***

Ash stood at the counter for a long time after.

Kite came and stood beside her. Kei watched from the door. He didn't go in. It wasn't his.

"The bird," said Ash.

Kite looked at her collar. At the patch of darker cloth and the loose threads, where she'd cut it off, badly, the winter before last. Some of the threads were still hanging. A few. You could see where.

"I cut mine off in a ditch," said Kite. "With this." He took the skinning knife out of his belt. Small, worn, the one he'd held at the crossroads all night in one hand with the other open. "I did it badly. I pulled the last two threads off the day I signed."

"I know," said Ash. "I watched it. On a stream. From a hedge."

"Do you want me to?" said Kite.

Ash looked at him.

"Ask," said Kite. "It's allowed. The swamp guy says."

Ash didn't say anything for a long time.

"Will you?" she said.

Kite put the knife to her collar. Very carefully. The way Ina bent a star, the third line under. He cut the last of the loose threads, one at a time. Four of them. They fell on the floor of the Hall of Roads by Ama Corrie's counter.

Ama Corrie came round the counter with her broom and swept them up. Under, not round.

"*Hm*," she said.

***

Kei read it out. On the top step. He didn't usually read letters out. He read this one.

Ash, at the bottom of the steps, sitting between Kite and Drew with a cup of Kotori's horrible tea, which she'd made herself, put her face in her hands.

"*What does it mean now*?" said Gabe, very loudly, to everybody.

Nobody answered him. They didn't need to. Ash took her hands away from her face. Her face was doing the thing it had done in August at the milestone, when Ice came up the road. Nobody in Nightjar had ever seen it.

"It means *not today*," said Ash. "And not tomorrow either." She looked at the cup. "It means *stay*."

***

He went up to the eye at the second dawn, on his own. Not alone; Higu came. She always came.

The bench was there, on the south side, pale ash, *Somewhere*. He didn't sit on it. He went to the north side, to the parapet, to his brass arm, and unrolled the north sheet on his knee.

The round town. The road. Ninety-four dots, every one with a little circle beside it for a lit lamp. The old hall. The twenty-two. The edge.

And past the edge, in pencil, where he'd drawn it from what Isa said, a long time ago, very small: a hill in still water with a ring round it.

He looked at it for a long time.

Then he looked north, past the parapet. Past the moor, past where the road went over the top. To the bottom of the sky. He couldn't see it. He hadn't been able to see it last night, except when Higu put her light out and he looked through it. And then only just, if he knew where.

He knew where.

He took his pen. Not the pencil. Ink. And went over the hill on the sheet. The ring, and the twelve stones, and the flat one in the middle. In ink, so it would stay. And then a line. From the round town, at the bottom of the sheet, up the road, past the ninety-four and the twenty-two and the edge, across the still water, to the hill.

One line. In ink. From here to there.

Higu leaned over and looked.

"That's not between two people," she said.

"No," said Kei.

"All your lines are between two people."

"I know," said Kei. He looked at it. "This one's between everybody and the middle." He was quiet. "Every line on the sheet goes through it now. If you follow them far enough."

Higu looked at it for a long time.

Then she put her hand on his, on the pen, and didn't say anything. She didn't need to. She'd said *Kei* at a gate once, when somebody asked who'd stand next to her, and he'd said *Higu*.

He wrote beside the hill, very small, in the place where Curse would have put a label. One word.

*Middle.*
