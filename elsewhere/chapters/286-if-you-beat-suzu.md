title: If You Beat Suzu

Xav had been chasing the same man for a year and a half, and he'd finally caught him, and it had been a draw.

He thought about it the whole way from Masmark. Fourteen days on the west road, with RX complaining about his feet and Malcolm saying *it'll be May* every time somebody asked how long. Round the goose, which sat down. Over the ferry, where he cut his fourth *o7*. Up the long road through the White Hills to the round town, in the rain, in the spring.

A draw. Seventy minutes. Twenty-seven each. Both at one per cent, three paces apart, nine minutes of neither of them moving. Then Malcolm, from the step, shutting his notebook. *It'll be March.*

Closer, he'd said, against the wall afterwards. *Yes*. *Again*. *Yes*.

He'd drawn it about forty times since, in a sketchbook, on the road. Two people against a wall. He couldn't get Dec's face right. He'd never been able to get Dec's face right. It did something in fights that didn't stay still long enough to draw.

"You're doing it again," said RX, on the last day, at the top of the rise where you first saw the dome.

"Doing what?"

"The face. The *I'm going to win this time* face." RX sat down on a milestone. "You did it all the way to January. And then you drew."

"I didn't lose."

"You didn't *win*," said RX.

Xav looked at the dome. Low and pale at the bottom of the valley, with the round town round it, and the seven roads going out. And by the fountain, new since he'd left in February, a tall green pole. With something at the top of it that caught the light.

"May," he said.

"*It'll be May*," said Malcolm, behind him.

"It *is* May, Malcolm."

"I know," said Malcolm. "I've been saying it for a fortnight. It's nice to be right."

***

They came in through the south gate on the Saturday. Kite was in it, hands at his sides.

"Who'd stand next to you?" said Kite.

"RX," said Xav. "And Malcolm. And Val, when she gets here on Tuesday, with her pages." He thought about it. "And Dec, across from me. In the Seven Below. In a fortnight."

Kite looked at him for a moment.

"That's four answers," he said. "And one of them's across from you."

"That's where he stands," said Xav. "Across. That's the best place."

Kite thought about that. Then he moved over in the gate, the small way he did, to let them past. "Fair," he said.

***

The long table was full. It always was now. Sixty-one chairs and a stool, and Riverbank's three on the bench along the wall where visitors sat. Xav had sat there in January for a fortnight before the draw. He'd liked it. You could see everybody.

Dec was in the first of the nine. Hyvred beside him, not on his knee for once, with her chin on her hand. Gabe across from them with a girl beside him at no paces at all. Somebody had told Xav about that on the road, by letter, three times, in capitals. Leaka, probably. Deston and Chernn at the end, smelling of wax. Suzu beside Kotori, half asleep.

Suzu was usually half asleep.

Xav waited till the soup. It was Kotori's. You didn't talk over the start of Kotori's soup. Then he got up off the bench and walked down the long table to the first of the nine and stood there with his sketchbook under his arm.

Dec looked up.

"May," said Xav. "The one after the draw. The Seven Below." He'd practised this on the road. It came out exactly the way he'd practised it, which he hadn't expected. "I want to go again."

The long table went quiet. It did that now, when Xav stood up in front of Dec. Everybody had seen the clip. *IT'LL BE MARCH*. Two million. Malcolm had asked Tif three times to stop sending it to him.

Dec put down his spoon.

He thought about it. He did that. You asked him something simple, and he thought about it properly before he answered, and you had to stand there and wait. Xav had been standing there and waiting since poly. He didn't mind. It was part of it.

"You can," said Dec.

Xav breathed out.

"If you beat Suzu," said Dec.

***

Nobody said anything for about three seconds.

Then Leaka said "*WHAT*", very loudly, and dropped her bread. Then the whole long table looked down it at Suzu.

Suzu was asleep.

Not quite. Nearly. Sitting beside Kotori with his chin going down toward his soup, his long silver hair over one shoulder, his eyes about a third open. He'd been like that through most of the long table for two and a half years. He woke up at a quarter to eight for the words and went back to sleep after.

Kotori nudged him.

Suzu opened his eyes the rest of the way. He looked down the long table at Dec, and at Xav standing beside him with his sketchbook. He didn't look surprised. Xav noticed that, afterwards. He didn't look anything.

"*Suzu*?" said Xav.

"Suzu," said Dec.

"You want me to fight *Suzu*." Xav looked down the table. "Suzu doesn't fight. People. He's never—" He stopped. He tried to remember a single time. In two and a half years. In the first winter, in the fen, by a channel, there'd been something. A man going off a boardwalk into black water. He couldn't remember what happened after. It had been the night he took Kite, from behind, in the reeds. He'd been busy. "Suzu doesn't *fight* anybody."

"Nobody asked him," said Dec.

Xav stared at him.

"You beat Suzu," said Dec. "Then we go again. The Seven Below. Whenever you like." He picked up his spoon. "That's the deal."

"Why?"

Dec thought about it. Properly.

"Because you should," he said.

That was all. He went back to his soup. Hyvred, beside him, had turned her head and was looking at him with an expression Xav couldn't read. She looked like somebody who'd just seen a thing she'd been watching for two years finally move.

***

Xav walked back up the long table to the bench.

He didn't sit down. He stopped at Suzu's chair, on the way, because it was there. And because he didn't know what else to do.

Suzu looked up at him. Very calmly. A third awake.

"Will you?" said Xav.

He hadn't asked anybody to fight him in a year and a half. He'd only ever asked Dec. Everybody else asked *him*. He'd forgotten how you said it to somebody who wasn't Dec.

Suzu thought about it.

Not the way Dec did. Dec thought about things like a man turning a stone over to see the other side. Suzu thought about it like a cat deciding whether to get up. Xav watched him do it. It took a while.

"Okay," said Suzu.

"Saturday fortnight. The Seven Below. Malcolm calls it."

"Okay."

"Best of three?" said Malcolm, from the bench. He had his notebook out already. "If it's going to be a thing. It should be a proper thing."

Suzu looked at Malcolm. Then at Xav. Then, for a moment, down the long table at Dec, in the first of the nine. Dec wasn't looking at him. He was eating his soup.

"Best of three," said Suzu. "Okay."

And put his chin back on his hand. And as far as Xav could tell, he went back to sleep.

***

He asked RX about it that night, on the south steps, at the third dawn.

"He's *lazy*," said RX. "That's the whole thing about Suzu. Everybody knows. He sleeps at the long table. He sleeps on the north steps. He slept on the *hill*, Hako said, in the still water, on a stone, sitting up, in the *nothing*." He was eating chestnuts. "He carried a jar for nine months and said *good* about four hundred times. That's Suzu."

"He carried a jar three hundred stones," said Xav. "Into the dark's own water. And set it on a crown. And took the lid off."

"Okay, he's lazy and *brave*."

"Has he ever fought anybody? A person?"

RX thought about it.

"The class fights," he said. "In the market. The seven. He was at the ring for all of them." He ate a chestnut. "Holding the chestnuts. Not fighting."

"Before that?"

"Nightjar," said RX. "The first winter. In the fen, by the channel. That's all I know about." He looked at Xav. "You were *there*. You took Kite out of the reeds."

"I know," said Xav. "I was busy." He tried again to remember. A boardwalk, a black channel, Dec in the middle with his shield. Two of Nightjar's on the planks waiting their turn, a spear and a staff. Then a splash on the right, and a sound like grass being cut, and two people going down at once across the shins. "I didn't see it. I heard it."

"Heard what?"

"A scythe," said Xav.

***

He asked Malcolm on the Monday. Malcolm said *it'll be March* and then, when Xav looked at him, *sorry. Habit. I don't know. I've never seen him fight.*

He asked Val on the Tuesday, when she came in from Masmark with her pages. Val had pages on everybody. Dashes and dots and three kinds of mark, the way people moved, the way they read a fight. She had a page on Dec, and a page on Lucan, and a page on Kestrel's held draw, and a page on Xav himself that he wasn't allowed to see.

She went through her whole satchel at the long table.

"I haven't got one," said Val.

"You've got one on *everybody*."

"Everybody who *fights*," said Val. She looked down the long table at Suzu, asleep beside Kotori. "I've never seen him do it. You can't write a page about somebody you've never seen."

He asked Kotori on the Wednesday.

She was in the Hart's kitchen with the kettles. She'd stood beside Suzu longer than anybody in here: Moonlight Harvest, the first winter, a jar and three hundred stones. She didn't look up from the kettles when he asked.

"He'll be very good," said Kotori.

Xav waited.

That was all she said. *He'll be very good*. Then she poured four cups of tea, and gave him one, and went back to the kettles. Very carefully, she didn't look at him.

***

He asked Hyvred on the Wednesday night, last, because he'd been putting it off.

She saw everything. That was what she did. She'd stood on the bottom step of the Seven Below for every one of his fights with Dec and called nothing, because he'd asked her not to. But she'd seen all of it, edge to edge. If anybody in the round town knew what Suzu looked like in a fight, it was her.

She was on the fountain rim with Dec's arm. She let go of it when Xav sat down, which he'd never seen her do for anybody.

"You want to know about Suzu," said Hyvred.

"Yes."

She was quiet for a long time. Longer than he'd expected.

"I can't call him," she said.

Xav looked at her.

"Everybody," said Hyvred. "I can call everybody. In a fight, in a hall, on a hill. I see where they are and I see where they're going to be, and I say it, and they go." She was looking across the market at nothing. "*Left two.* *Thea.* *Turn.* I've called this whole guild for two years. I called the swamp guy round the inside of a ring on the longest night a breath ahead of every gap."

"I know."

"I can't call Suzu," said Hyvred. "I've tried. On the hill. On the stones. In the Seven Below in the dark, practising, before we went north. I look at him and I see where he's going to be. And he's never there." She turned and looked at Xav. Edge to edge. "He isn't *anywhere*. Until he is. And then he's already done it."

Xav didn't say anything.

"I asked him once," said Hyvred. "On the hill. How he does it. He said *I'm lazy. I go where it's least effort.*" She almost laughed. "And then he went first across three hundred stones in the dark. And said *no* to the swamp guy on the two hundred and sixteenth. And I couldn't have called either of those if you'd given me a year."

Beside her, Dec said nothing at all. He was looking at the fountain. Xav could see his face. It was doing nothing, very carefully.

"Good luck," said Hyvred. And took Dec's arm again.

***

He practised on RX on the Thursday morning, before he went looking for Suzu.

Not the river. He'd practised the river on RX all the way down a road last year until RX dreamed about it. Something else. He stood in the settling ring by the fountain and told RX to move. Anywhere, anyhow, and he wouldn't look where RX was going to be. He'd only look where RX *was*.

It was terrible. He'd spent a year and a half learning to look a breath ahead. To read the rain, the one after, the river. Everything he had was about being early. He stood in the ring and tried to be on time instead, and RX hit him in the ear with a training stick four times in a minute.

"You're *slow*," said RX, delighted. "You're actually *slow*. I've waited a year and a half for this."

"I'm practising."

"You're practising being *hit*." RX hit him again. "Do it more. I like it."

***

He went to watch Suzu practise.

He'd thought that was obvious. You had a fortnight before a best of three in the Seven Below against somebody you'd never seen fight. You went and watched them practise. He'd done it to Dec, for a year, from terraces and tunnels and the bench at the edge of rings. He'd watched Dec practise the weight on Salty's shield until he could hear it in his sleep.

Suzu didn't practise.

Xav looked everywhere. The settling ring by the fountain, nine dark stones. Empty. The north tunnel, where Dec went down with Hyvred to fight Delvers. Not there. The long road out to the timber yard, where Jared had found the green ash. The south steps. The Hart's yard, where a door stood on its own in a frame on two hinges. Milli walked through it every evening and came out the other side, smiling, for reasons nobody explained to Xav.

He found Suzu on Thursday on the north steps, in the morning of the third dawn, asleep.

Sitting on the top step with his back against the north door, which stuck. His scythe across his knees and his eyes shut, in the early sun. Isa beside him in her chair, under the grey blanket, also asleep. The two of them side by side on the north steps, looking up the north road at nothing, breathing slowly.

Xav stood at the bottom of the steps for a long time.

"He does this every day," said a voice behind him.

Isa. She wasn't asleep. Her eyes were still shut, but she wasn't asleep. "Since the spring stone," she said. "He carries me out, and we sit. And he sleeps." A small dry laugh, like a stick. "He's earned it."

"I'm fighting him," said Xav. "On Saturday fortnight."

"I know," said Isa. "Ama Corrie told me." She opened her eyes and looked at him, close, the way she had to. "You're the one with the two blades. Who fights the one under the floor."

"Yes."

"*Hm*," said Isa. "And now you're fighting this one."

"I've never seen him fight."

Isa looked at Suzu beside her for a long time. At his long silver hair, and the scythe across his knees, and his eyes shut in the sun.

"My mothers used to say," said Isa, "that the ones who sleep in the day are the ones who aren't frightened of the night." She closed her eyes again. "Go away. You'll wake him."

Xav went away.

***

On the Friday night, he found Dec in the Seven Below.

Not fighting. Sitting on the floor against the north-east wall, under the mark Xav's right blade had made a year and a half ago, under a Falling Cut. Where they'd sat in September, and January. He was on his own. Hyvred had gone up the stair with Kotori. Dec was just sitting there, in the round pale room with the seven silver lines, looking at the ring in the middle.

Xav came down the stair and sat beside him. Against the wall. Shoulder to shoulder.

"Is he good?" said Xav.

Dec didn't say anything for a while.

"Yes," he said.

"How good?"

Dec turned his head and looked at him. And his face did the thing. The small sudden one, the one Xav had never once got right in a sketchbook. The one that meant somebody was about to see something they hadn't seen before.

"Ask me after," said Dec.

Xav stared at him.

"You know," he said. "You *know* something."

"I've known him a long time," said Dec.

"*How* long?"

"Long enough," said Dec. "Since I've known him, I've known."

Xav sat against the wall of the Seven Below and thought about that. Out there, a long time ago, before the wind game. Dec and a man with long silver hair who slept at the long table and said *good*.

"Has he ever beaten you?" said Xav.

Dec looked at the ring in the middle of the floor.

"We've never fought," he said. "Not in here."

"Why not?"

Dec thought about it for a long time.

"He's lazy," he said at last. He almost smiled.

"Everybody keeps *saying* that."

"It's true," said Dec. "It's the truest thing about him." He got up off the floor. He stood looking down at Xav against the wall. "Saturday fortnight. Best of three. Malcolm calls it." He paused. "Don't look for him where he's going to be."

"What does that mean?"

"You'll see," said Dec. And went up the stair.

Xav sat on the floor of the Seven Below on his own for a long time, under the mark from his own blade. He took out his sketchbook. He tried to draw Suzu.

He couldn't. He'd never once watched him long enough to know what he looked like when he moved.
