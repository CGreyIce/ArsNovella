Gabe was at the east gate before the sun was off the bottom of its rail, and Xav had known he would be.

He'd known since the class hall. Since Quill had turned the ledger round and pointed at the empty line, and said *went out the back toward the east fields, nothing out that way but sheep and the old chapel*. Since Gabe had put his finger on the blank space where Declan's name should have been and not said anything. Xav had lain awake on the first night in a borrowed bed, listening to Gabe not sleeping in the next one, and known exactly where they'd be at dawn.

So he was there too. So were the others. Rui Xiang leaning on the gatepost, spinning a knife across his knuckles without looking at it. Malcolm with his staff and his face of quiet doom. John with his chin up, already looking east, as if he'd decided this was a good idea before anyone had asked.

Gabe looked at them all, standing in the grey light at the gate, and something in his face went complicated.

"You don't have to come," he said.

"We know," said Xav.

"I'm just going to look."

"We know."

"It's probably nothing. It's probably just an old chapel."

"Then we'll look at an old chapel," said Xav, "and be back for lunch."

Gabe looked at him a moment longer. Then he turned round and went out through the gate, and they went after him.

***

The east fields were all rises and walls and gorse, and Woolbacks.

They didn't know about the jaw yet. Calvin wouldn't post it in World until the middle of that day. So they did it the hard way, the first hour: swords sticking in wool, Gabe going over backwards with a sheep on his shield, Malcolm saying *I told you* to nobody in particular every time anything went wrong. It was ugly. It was also, Xav thought, exactly what Declan would have wanted for them. Learn the floor. Get hit. Get up.

Rui Xiang learned fastest. He always had. In every game they'd ever played, Rui Xiang's skill list filled up the way other people's inventories did: he'd pick a thing up, try it twice, and have it. His hands had spent years doing fast, precise things on a keyboard at three in the morning, and they'd come through the white room with all of that still in them. By the third Woolback he'd stopped going for the wool at all and started going *under*, dropping low and coming up with both knives. By the fifth he was doing it without looking.

"Under," he said, sliding back out from under a dead sheep. "Under the chin. There's no wool. It's like a gap."

"How did you know that?"

Rui Xiang shrugged, which was how Rui Xiang explained most things. "Tried it."

Xav filed it. He filed everything. He'd been filing since he woke up in the white room, sitting very still with his hands on his knees while everybody else panicked. He'd been reading the room the way he read every room, waiting to see where the first move would come from before he made his own. Wait, wait, and move once. Declan used to say it drove him mad.

The chapel was on the third rise.

***

It was smaller than Xav had expected. Four walls of grey stone, no roof, a young birch growing up through one corner. A doorway with no door in it.

And light.

That was the first thing they all saw, before they'd even got close. Light coming out of the empty doorway. Not sunlight. It was the middle of the morning, and the sun was over to the east on its rail, and the light coming out of the chapel door was gold, warm, steady, like a lamp left on in a room. It lay across the threshold and down the first few metres of the slope in a soft wash, and the long grass there was greener than the grass anywhere else on the rise.

Gabe stopped walking.

They all stopped. They stood at the bottom of the last rise and looked at it, the five of them, the gold coming out of the dark doorway into the grey morning.

"Okay," said Malcolm, after a while, very quietly. "That's not nothing."

Gabe went up the rise. He didn't run. Xav had thought he would, and he didn't. He walked up, slowly, steadily, like someone walking up to a door they weren't sure they wanted to knock on. He stopped on the threshold, ducked under the low lintel, and went in.

Xav went in after him.

***

There was a statue at the far end where the altar should have been.

A knight, life-size, grey stone, feet planted, a round shield on his left arm. No, Xav saw, looking again. There was no shield. The left arm was raised as if to hold one, and the arm was empty. In the knight's right hand, held up and out over the aisle toward the door, was a stone lantern.

The lantern was lit.

Not with fire. There was nothing in it to burn. It was carved stone, solid, its little door open, and inside it, where a flame would have been, there was light. Gold, soft, steady, filling the carved cage the way water fills a cup. It came down the whole length of the chapel, through the long grass in the aisle, and lay across the doorway they'd just come through.

Nobody said anything.

Over their heads, on the inside of the lintel, carved deep, there were words. Xav read them out loud, because somebody had to.

"*The light is kept by those who stand.*"

Gabe was walking down the aisle toward the statue. Slowly. Through the long grass. He stopped in front of it and looked up at the stone knight's smooth, worn face. It had no expression; a hundred years of rain had taken it off. Then Gabe looked at the lantern.

He reached up, slowly, and put his hand on it.

A window opened in front of his face. Xav could see it from where he stood; Gabe had tipped it, without meaning to, the way you tip your phone toward somebody when you can't believe what's on it.

[ The Unkept Vigil
This vigil has already been kept.
]

Gabe stood there with his hand on the lantern and read it. Xav watched him read it twice.

"Already been kept," said Gabe. His voice was strange. "*Already.*"

"By who?" said John.

Gabe asked it. He asked the window, out loud, the way Calvin asked his Support things. "By who? Who kept it?"

The window didn't answer. It just sat there, small and plain and polite, saying *already*. The lantern glowed under Gabe's hand, and the stone knight looked down the aisle at the door with his blank, patient face.

***

Xav went back to the door.

He didn't know why at first. It was just where his feet went. He stood in the empty doorway with the gold light falling down the aisle behind him onto his back, and he looked out at the morning, and he looked down.

The threshold was a single block of grey stone, worn smooth in the middle by a century of feet. And on it, on the very edge, there were two marks. Faint. Scuffs, really, the kind of scuff a boot makes when it's set hard and dragged an inch. About a shoulder's width apart. On the outer edge of the step.

Exactly where you'd put your feet if you were standing in this doorway and weren't going to move.

He crouched down and looked at them. Then he looked up, out of the door, at the slope below.

The long grass on the rise outside the chapel had been flattened. Not in a path, the way a person walking flattens grass. In *arcs*. Wide curving swathes of it, pressed down and broken, coming up the slope from three directions toward the door, all meeting at the threshold. As if something had come up the rise from the fields, over and over, in a crowd. And it had all stopped here, at this step, and gone no further.

*The light is kept by those who stand.*

Xav stayed crouched in the doorway for a long time.

"Xav?" said Rui Xiang, behind him.

"He stood here," said Xav.

Nobody said anything.

"Right here. On this step. He put his feet here and he didn't move." Xav touched the scuff marks with two fingers. "And something came up that hill at him. A lot of something. From three sides. All at once, for a long time." He looked at the flattened grass. "And he held the door."

"Against what?" said Malcolm.

"I don't know. Something that wanted to get to that." Xav nodded back down the aisle at the lantern. "And couldn't."

"How do you know it was him?"

Xav didn't answer straight away. He looked at the scuff marks on the threshold. A shoulder's width apart. Set hard. The way you'd stand if you were the one at the front, and everything behind you depended on your not moving.

He'd watched Declan stand like that a thousand times. In every game. In every doorway of every dungeon. Shield up, mouth running, *if I die it's a skill issue, trust*. Taking everything the room had so that the four people behind him didn't have to.

"Because he always tanks," said Xav. "It's what he does."

***

They didn't post it.

Gabe wanted to. They were sitting on the slope below the chapel in the late morning, eating the bread they'd brought, with the gold light lying across the threshold behind them, and Gabe had his window open. He'd got as far as *World*. Xav watched him type *found something*, and stop.

"Don't," said Xav.

Gabe looked at him.

"If you post it," said Xav, "thirty people come out here this afternoon to look at a lamp. And they stand in his doorway. And they put their hands on it. And they read *already*." He looked at the chapel. "It's his, Gabe. Whatever he did here, he did it on his own, on the first day, and he didn't tell anyone. He didn't put it in World. He didn't write it on the wall. He just did it, and left." He paused. "We don't get to tell everybody for him."

Gabe looked at the half-typed line for a long time.

Then he deleted it. Two words, letter by letter. *found something*. Gone.

"He's going to get himself killed," said Gabe, to the window.

"Maybe."

"He stood in that door all night, Xav. Against God knows what. On his own. On the *first day*." His voice cracked. "What if he does that on every floor? What if he does it on a floor where it doesn't work?"

Xav didn't have an answer. He'd been asking himself the same thing since he crouched in the doorway.

"Then we'd better catch up," he said.

On the walk back across the east fields, Malcolm fell into step beside him. Malcolm didn't usually fall into step beside anybody. He walked at the back, as a rule, with his staff, expecting the worst and quietly annoyed when it didn't arrive.

"If he's doing that on every floor," said Malcolm, low, so Gabe in front couldn't hear, "standing in doors on his own all night, he's going to need somebody who can patch him up afterwards."

"Probably."

"I'm not a healer. I'm the other kind of cleric. The buffs. The stuff that makes the shield harder before it gets hit." Malcolm looked at his staff as if it had personally let him down. "That's no use to somebody who's already been hit all night."

Xav looked at him.

"I'm just saying," said Malcolm. "If we ever catch up. Somebody should be able to fix him. And Rachael's in Deston's party, and Kei's in Hyvred's, and it's going to be me standing next to him, isn't it, because it's always us." He sighed. "So I'm going to learn the other kind as well. In case. As a backup."

"You always have a backup."

"Because the first thing always fails," said Malcolm, with total conviction. Then, more quietly: "He used to make our whole class laugh, you know. In the first year. Before anybody knew anybody. He'd just talk to everyone until they did." He was quiet for a few steps. "I'd like him to be in one piece when Gabe punches him."

Xav didn't say anything. But he looked at Malcolm for a moment longer than he usually would. That night, in the borrowed bed in the inn, with Gabe not sleeping in the next one, he opened the rankings and looked at the name at the top.

*Voyager.*

He'd told someone about that probe once. In his first month at poly, in a classroom that smelled of whiteboard markers, in a whisper. He remembered exactly who.

He didn't let himself think about it any further than that. He closed the window, turned over, and looked at the wall until morning.

***

They didn't catch up. Not for a long time.

That was the thing nobody had told Xav about catching up: you had to be ready to. And they weren't. They found that out over the next ten days. Gabe at Ulla Dray's forge making nails. Rui Xiang filling his skill list from the trainers in Hearthstead, three new skills in a week, faster than anyone. Malcolm working out, slowly and pessimistically, that his little cleric's blessing could go into a sword or a shield as well as into a person, and making them harder to break. He always put on two, never one, because he assumed the first would fail. John lighting up the dark under the floor-1 bridges with little floating points of light, finding things nobody else had found.

And Deston, every few days, at the long table at the Spoon, saying *careful* without saying it. Looking at Gabe the way you look at a pan that's about to boil over.

*We're being careful*, Gabe had typed into World on the first night. Xav had seen it. Everybody had.

So they were careful. They watched Deston's party go up, and Yan's, and Thea's, and Hana's, and then Hyvred's. They read every guide and every warning. They trained until they were sure.

On the thirteenth morning, they went up the valley to the Fold.

***

Xav found out what his thing was called in the Fold, which he thought, afterwards, was probably right.

It was the curve. Everybody had told them about the curve. *When the bell comes off, it stops going in straight lines. It follows you. Don't dodge sideways.* The guide in World said have something it can hit. Thea's notes said three point two seconds. Chi's line said talk slowly when it screams.

So when the bell came off, and the Bellwether screamed, and the cold went through all of them, Xav did what he always did. He didn't move. He waited.

He watched it come out of the scream. Watched its head swing round, looking for somebody. Watched it pick Rui Xiang, who'd cut the strap. Watched it lower its horns. And Rui Xiang was still half frozen, and Xav didn't shout, didn't run, didn't do anything at all. He waited until the ram had committed, until all its weight was going one way and couldn't go any other.

And then he moved. Once.

Not at the ram. Across its line. Two steps and a shove that put Rui Xiang flat on the grass and Xav's sword in the ram's path, edge out, angled. The Bellwether went over him on its curve and the sword opened its shoulder from end to end. It stumbled, wrong-footed, into the split stone it hadn't meant to hit.

[ Unique skill: Once
Wait. Then move once. It's enough.
]

He read it lying on his back in the cropped grass, with Rui Xiang on top of him swearing.

*Wait, wait, and move once.* He'd been doing it his whole life. Declan used to say it drove him mad. And then, about a year after they met, Declan had started doing it too, in every game. Waiting longer than he wanted to. Moving once. He'd never said where he'd got it from. Xav had never asked. He'd known.

He lay there and looked at the gold edge of the window, and thought about a scuff on a threshold, a shoulder's width apart. About somebody who'd stood in a doorway all night and not moved once, not one step, because moving would have been the wrong move.

*Wait, then move once.*

He wondered if Declan had been waiting, all night, in that door. And what for. And whether the moment to move had ever come.

***

They climbed the stair that afternoon, the sixth party up. A hundred and twelve steps. Nobody counted. At the top, Gabe opened the door, and the cold came through it like a fist. And beyond it the whole world was white, pines and snow and mountains, and far down the valley a little wooden town in a stockade.

Gabe stood in the doorway and didn't move.

Xav came up beside him and looked at what he was looking at. There was nothing there. Snow, a slope, the edge of the trees going away east. That was all.

"He came through here," said Gabe. "Tiger said. East. To a big pine."

"I know."

"Four hundred metres along the trees."

"I know, Gabe."

Gabe stood there a moment longer. Then he pulled the inadequate Hearthstead coat tighter round his shoulders, and turned the other way, down toward the town, and started walking.

Xav watched him go. He'd known Gabe for years, through Declan first, and then on his own. He knew what that had cost.

*We're being careful.*

He followed him down.
