Yan didn't sleep the night after the Prism. He lay on his bedroll in the round room on the third ring with Suzu round his neck and watched thirty-five candles burn in the dark.

He did that sometimes. On bad nights. He'd done it on floor 1, in the inn in Hearthstead, the first week, when he couldn't stop seeing them and didn't know what they were. He'd done it on floor 4, in the hall on the first square, with the silence pressing on the roof. It helped. He couldn't have said why. Lying in the dark, looking at the lights of everybody he loved, all of them burning, all of them still there.

Salty's, across the room, enormous, roaring softly in his sleep like a banked forge. Tiger's, flickering so fast even asleep that it looked like it was humming. Wendy's, small and still and very bright, right next to his own bedroll. She'd put her bedroll there without asking. Cursiffe's, dark blue-black, like light through a bottle of ink. It had changed lately, he'd noticed, since the statue on floor 5. There was a little more light in it, at the edges. As if somebody had turned the ink up to the window.

He looked at them all, one by one. Thirty-five. And he thought about the one that had gone out in the water at dusk.

He'd watched it go. He'd told Wendy *somebody should*, and he'd meant it. Deep in the glass, where four cracks met, a candle. Not a coal. A real one, cold and white and very steady, like a light in a lighthouse. And when Wendy's arrow went in, he'd watched it flare. Once. Bright. The way Tiger's flared when he was frightened.

And then go out. Slowly. White to grey. And the thread.

*Yun.*

Suzu, in his head. Soft. She'd been quiet all evening.

"I'm all right."

*You're not.*

"No." He looked at the ceiling. "I'm not."

She shifted round his neck, cool and dry, her head moving up along his jaw until it rested by his ear. She didn't say anything else for a while. She didn't have to. She was there. She'd been there since the first morning, since before the first morning, round his neck in the white room when he opened his eyes.

"Where do they go?" said Yan.

He'd never asked her. Not in a hundred and forty-six days. He'd watched a hundred lights go out, sheep and wolves and eels and wisps and shardbacks. He'd watched the threads go up off them, too faint to call light, and gone. And he'd never once asked.

Suzu was quiet for a long time.

*Somewhere*, she said at last.

"That's not an answer."

*It's the only one there is, Yun.* Her voice was very gentle. *They go somewhere. Not nowhere. Somewhere.* A pause. *That's all I know. That's all anybody knows. It's enough.*

He lay there with that.

*Somewhere.*

It didn't fix anything. But he found that he could breathe a bit better, after.

***

He thought about the white room a lot, that night. He didn't know why. Maybe because of the Prism, and the light in the glass. Maybe because of the slate in the round room, which still said what it said. *A man in a white helm. One stroke. A fox. Not this time.* He'd read it every day since Drew wrote it, and every day it had made the same cold place open up somewhere under his ribs.

He'd seen him. On the first morning.

Everybody knew that. Gabe and Hana and Yan, the three who'd seen someone in the white room in the first minute and then been told by thirty-five calm voices that there were only thirty-five players. He'd said so, on floor 1. He'd said so on floor 2. *I saw someone. By the arch.* Black and white and a flash of teal, gone in a second behind a snake.

Behind a snake.

What he'd never said, to anyone, was what else he'd seen.

He'd started seeing the candles on the first morning. That was the thing. He'd always known that. He'd opened his eyes on the white floor and the first thing he'd seen, before faces, before the circles, before anything, was lights. Thirty-four of them, round the room, under thirty-four breastbones, burning. He hadn't known what they were. He'd thought for a second he was dying. And then he'd looked toward the arch.

And there'd been one more.

Standing. Three metres from the arch, between two circles, very still, looking down at the sleeping people. A figure in black and white with a flash of teal.

And a candle.

Yan had looked at it for one second. Maybe less. Before Suzu uncoiled across his face and covered his eyes, cool and white and sudden. And when she'd moved away, the figure was gone, and the arch was a wall, and the lights were rising out of the floor saying *Welcome, Player*.

One second. He'd never told anyone what he saw in it. He'd never been sure.

It hadn't looked like the others.

Every candle in that room had been new. That was the only way he could put it, even now, after a hundred and forty-six days of looking at them. They'd been tall and straight and fresh, like candles somebody had just lit for the first time, with all their wax in front of them.

His hadn't.

His had been low. Burned down. The way a candle looks at the end of a very long night, when you've sat up with it until dawn. Short, with the wax run down its sides in long cold drips. As if it had been lit a very long time ago, and had been burning ever since, and had very little left.

And it had been the brightest light in the room.

That was the part Yan had never been able to make sense of. A candle that low should be guttering. Should be small and blue and nearly gone. And it had been *blazing*. Steady and gold and so bright it hurt, brighter than Salty's, brighter than any of them. Like somebody had taken a candle that was nearly burned out and decided it wasn't allowed to go out, not yet. Not for anything. And held it up.

One second. Then a snake across his eyes.

He'd never told anyone because he'd never been sure. And because, if he was sure, he didn't know what it meant. And then, lately, since the cistern and the slate, he'd started to think he might know what it meant. And didn't want to.

"Suzu," he said.

*Mm.*

"In the white room. The first morning. When you covered my eyes."

She went very still round his neck.

"Did you mean to?"

She didn't answer for a long time. So long that he thought she wasn't going to. And then she said, very quietly:

*Yes.*

Yan lay in the dark and didn't breathe.

"Why?"

*Because he didn't want to be seen*, said Suzu. *I could tell. The moment you looked at him. He was standing there looking at all of you, and he didn't want any of you to look back. Not yet.* A pause. *So I helped him.*

"You *helped* him?"

*He was carrying something very heavy*, said Suzu. Her voice was gentler than he'd ever heard it. *I could see it. The way you see the candles, Yun, I see what people carry. And his was so heavy. And he was holding it all by himself, in a white room, with everybody asleep.* She was quiet. *It seemed rude to watch.*

Yan didn't say anything at all.

*You saw his light*, said Suzu. *Didn't you. Before I covered your eyes.*

"Yes."

*What did it look like?*

He told her. In a whisper, in the dark, so the others wouldn't wake. Low. Burned down. Wax run down the sides. And brighter than anything.

Suzu was quiet for a long, long time.

*Yes*, she said at last. *That's what it looked like to me, too.*

"What does it mean?"

*I don't know, Yun.*

"You do."

*I don't know*, said Suzu again. Very softly. *But I think it means he's been burning a lot longer than the rest of you. And I think he knows exactly how much he's got left.* Her head pressed against his jaw. *And I think he's decided to spend all of it on you.*

***

He got up before dawn and went to the lip of the pit. He couldn't lie there any more.

Somebody was already there. He'd known somebody would be. There always was, lately. He saw her candle before he saw her.

Thea was sitting on the lip at the top of the bucket chain with the lantern beside her, looking east. Her shoulder was still bandaged loosely, under her jersey. She didn't turn round when he came up, but she moved over a little on the stone, and he sat down beside her.

They didn't talk. Thea had always been easy to not talk to. He looked out at the Glass going grey in the dawn.

And then, because he couldn't help it, because he never could, he looked at her candle.

It had changed.

He'd noticed it a few days ago and not let himself think about it. Thea's candle had always been one of the clearer ones. Steady, bright, a clean blue-white at the heart like the screen of something left on at night. It was still that, at the centre. But it had changed at the edges. There was a colour there that hadn't been there on floor 5. Warm. Amber. Like a lamp seen through a window, on a winter night, from a road outside.

And it *leaned*.

That was what he hadn't been able to stop seeing for days. Candles leaned. They leaned toward the people they loved when they were close. Suzu's toward him, always, like a plant on a windowsill. Tiger's toward Wendy's, though Tiger would never say it. Salty's toward everybody. It was the one thing he'd seen in a hundred and forty-six days that he'd never told anybody, because it wasn't his to tell.

Thea's candle leaned east.

Toward the Glass. Toward wherever on the far side of the Glass there was a stair, and a door, and a floor above. All the time. Even now, sitting beside him in the dawn, with nobody out there she could see. It leaned the way a plant leans on a windowsill toward a window it's never been let out of.

He looked away. Quickly. It wasn't his.

"You're doing the thing," said Thea, without turning her head.

He froze.

"You look at people," said Thea. "Under the ribs. You've done it since the first day. I notice things." She was still looking east. "I'm not asking. I just noticed you did it to me just now. And then looked away really fast." Her mouth did something at the corner. "Which is usually what people do when they've seen something they think is none of their business."

Yan didn't say anything for a long time.

"It's not my business," he said at last.

"Okay."

"It's yours," said Yan. "Whenever you want it to be."

Thea looked at him then. For a long moment. Her bright blue eyes with the star-shaped glints in them went over his face. He couldn't tell what she saw. He'd never been able to see what other people saw.

Then she looked back east.

"Thank you," she said quietly.

They sat on the lip of the pit together until the sun came up.

***

On the last night, Gabe was the one on the lip.

Yan hadn't meant to go up. He'd meant to sleep. They were going up the stair tomorrow, at dusk, after the Prism. Everybody was supposed to sleep. But he'd lain on his bedroll in the round room watching thirty-five candles, and one of them hadn't been in the room. He'd known whose. So he'd got up.

Gabe was sitting at the top of the bucket chain, in the wolf coat, with the lamp shield across his knees. Drew had stopped sending him to bed after the first week. There didn't seem to be much point. He didn't look round when Yan sat down. He moved over a bit, the way Thea had.

They sat there. The moon was up. The Glass was silver all the way to the edge of the world.

"You saw him," said Gabe, eventually.

Yan didn't need to ask who.

"In the white room," said Gabe. "First minute. Me, you, Hana. We all saw somebody. And the lights said thirty-five." He was looking east. "We've never talked about it. You and me. Isn't that weird? A hundred and fifty days."

"It's weird," said Yan.

"I saw his face," said Gabe. "For about a second. He was looking at me. And I started to say his name, and then the lights came up out of the floor between us and he was gone." He turned the shield a little on his knees. "What did you see?"

Yan was quiet for a long time.

He thought about a candle. Burned down to almost nothing, and brighter than anything in the room. He thought about Suzu across his eyes, cool and white and sudden, on purpose. He thought about *I think he's decided to spend all of it on you.*

It wasn't his to tell. None of it. Not to Gabe. Not like this.

"I saw him looking at everybody," said Yan. "One at a time. Like he was counting." He swallowed. "And he looked like he hadn't slept. In a really long time."

Gabe didn't say anything for a while.

"He never slept," he said at last. Quietly. "On calls. Ever. You'd log off at three and he'd still be on. You'd log back on at nine and he'd *still be on*." His mouth did something. "Like he was scared that if he closed his eyes, everybody would be gone when he opened them."

Yan didn't say anything.

"He's been awake for a hundred and fifty days," said Gabe. "Hasn't he. Up there. On his own. Nobody to watch the door." He ran his thumb along the dark red rim of the shield, over the little iron rose on the inside, where a hand would go. "Hyv says his light goes small when he's scared. She says it's been small for most of six."

"Yeah," said Yan.

"I just want him to sleep," said Gabe. His voice cracked, a little, and he let it. "That's all. I don't even care about the rest. I'm not even that angry any more. Ok, I'm a bit angry. But mostly I just want to find him, and sit outside whatever door he's behind, and tell him to go to sleep. And then not go anywhere. All night. So he knows somebody's watching it."

Yan looked at him.

Gabe's candle was enormous. It always had been. Big and steady and warm, the kind of light you'd want at your back on a dark road. And it leaned. East, up, toward the stair. It had leaned that way since floor 1. Yan had just never let himself see it.

"He'll let you," said Yan. "When you find him."

"You think?"

"I think," said Yan slowly, "he's been waiting a long time for somebody to tell him he's allowed."

Gabe didn't answer. But after a while he put his big hand on Yan's shoulder, briefly, the way he did with everybody. Heavy and warm. Then he took it away again and looked east.

Round Yan's neck, Suzu lifted her head and looked at Gabe for a long moment. Then she put it down again, and said nothing at all.

***

They went up on the hundred-and-fiftieth day.

They killed the Prism again first, at dusk, the way they had before. It was quicker now. Ina and Winter knew the trick. Cursiffe called the gaps from his rock without looking at his pages. Wendy took the shot. Yan pointed. He didn't look away. Somebody should.

Then the stair. It was in a little stone arch on the far side of the Still Water, half in the shallows, where the dead Prisms lay. They went up it in the dark, in a long line, the way they always did. Calvin first. Rachael on his collar. Drew last.

Yan was near the back, with his party. He counted the steps with everybody else, out loud. Thirty. Forty. Fifty. Their voices going up the stair ahead of them.

And he looked at the candles.

He couldn't help it on stairs. Thirty-five of them, going up in a line in the dark ahead of him, like a procession of lanterns. Gabe's, near the front. Huge and steady, and it leaned too. Up the stair. Toward the top. Toward the door. Toward whatever was on the other side. It had leaned that way since floor 1, if Yan was honest. He'd just never let himself see it.

*Two hundred. Two hundred and fifty.*

He thought about a candle in a white room. Burned down to almost nothing. Blazing.

*I think he's decided to spend all of it on you.*

*Three hundred.*

He found that he was holding Suzu, round his neck, with one hand. He hadn't decided to. She let him.

*Three hundred and twelve.*

There was a door.

Calvin stopped in front of it. Everybody stopped behind him. Thirty-five people standing on a stair in the dark, with Thea's lantern and John's little lights and thirty-five candles that only one of them could see.

"Okay," said Calvin. Quietly. "Look at my back. Nobody runs."

He opened the door.

And from somewhere far below, outside, in the night, a bell rang.

One note. Deep and slow and very old, rolling up out of the dark toward them, through the open door, down the stair. The kind of bell that's been ringing the hours in the same place for a very long time.

Yan stood on the three-hundred-and-eleventh step and listened to it.

It rang once. Then it stopped. And in the silence after it, standing in the doorway with the night air coming in, he heard something else. Very faint. Under the echo of the bell. So faint he wasn't sure it was there at all.

Other bells. Many of them. Hanging somewhere in the dark below, not ringing. Just moving a little, in the wind, against each other. The way bells do when they're waiting to be rung.
