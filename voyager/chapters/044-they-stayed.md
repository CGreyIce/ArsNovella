He started reading World every night after the Quiet.

He hadn't, before. For twenty-five days he'd let Alea keep it for him in a log he didn't open, the way you keep letters in a drawer. He'd read Gabe's whisper every morning, pinned at the top, because she'd made sure he would. He'd read Thea's post about the padlock once, on a tussock in the Fen, and then closed it fast. The rest he'd left. Every line of it was a door he couldn't walk through, and it was easier not to look.

But after the Quiet, sitting on the edge of the dry fountain with his face wiped and his ribs mending, he'd thought: *it's safe now. The square's safe. When they come down that stair, nothing's going to come off the roof.* And something in him that had been clenched for twenty-five days, for ten years, let go a little.

So that night, in the empty room on the high terrace where he slept, he said: "World. From the start. Read it to me."

And Alea did.

***

It took three nights. There was a lot of it.

Gabe, on the first night, typing his name over and over. *you punched a goat you absolute idiot.* He'd read that before, on floor 2. He laughed at it again anyway.

Calvin: *PSA. STAB THE SHEEP UNDER THE JAW.* Salty: *we found that an hour ago.* Calvin: *ok but did you TELL anyone.*

Deston, four careful lines about the ram. *4. Don't go alone. Please.* And under it Calvin: *(he means me)*.

Wendy: *bring a coat. bring two coats. bring the person you love most and lie on them.*

Tiger: *hey voyager. or whoever. if you see this. you peel onions really well.*

Chris: *KEEP KILLING THINGS. it's not wasted. it's banking.* Thea: *chris you're a genius.* Thea: *hard. obviously.* Gabe: *is he ok.* Chris: *i think he's ok. i'll keep logging.*

He lay on the floor of the empty room on the high terrace in the Red City, with his hands behind his head, and listened to Alea read it all in her dry, careful voice. Every line. All of them. Thirty-five people he'd watched die once, talking to each other across two floors and a white valley, all through the first three weeks. Arguing. Helping. Being rude to each other. Being kind.

They didn't know he was listening. That was the strange part. Most of it wasn't for him. Most of it was just them, being them, the way they'd always been. Calvin asking things too loudly. Newish being rude. Hyvred in capitals. Chris writing everything down.

He'd forgotten. He realised that on the second night, lying on the floor listening. Ten years in the Spire, and somewhere along the way, after the first deaths and the second and the tenth, he'd forgotten what they'd sounded like before. When they were all still there. When it was just thirty-six people in a tower, finding things out, telling each other.

He listened to all of it. He didn't skip anything.

***

On the third night, she got to the Fen.

"There's a gap," said Alea. "After they all went up to floor 3. On the twenty-second day. Nobody posts anything for about a day." A pause. "And then there's this. From Chris. On the twenty-third."

[ World · Chris: all 35 have voted to stay on floor 3 and train. everyone through tier 2, everyone to lv 20, before anyone goes up. the note's pinned by the door if you want to read it. ]

Ice sat up.

"*The note's pinned by the door*," he said.

"In the smoking hall. In Reedmoor. I think." Alea's voice was very careful. "Deston showed it to them, Ice. All of them. On the twenty-third day. And they voted. And they *stayed*."

He sat on the floor of the empty room in the dark with his arms round his knees.

*Be careful. Train longer on the 3rd floor.* He'd written it with a fingertip in a white room, on the first morning, in the first minute, before anybody's eyes were open. He hadn't known whether Deston would believe it, or whether anybody else would, if Deston showed them. He'd trusted it to Deston because Deston was the only one he'd ever trusted with anything that heavy. And Deston had carried it in his shirt pocket for three weeks, and then pinned it on a wall where everyone would see it, every time they came in or went out.

*Everyone to Lv 20, before anyone goes up.*

"Read the rest," he said. His voice wasn't working.

[ World · Calvin: did my hard trial. it said bring all of them back. i brought all of them back. i'm the scout who brings everybody home now ]

He put his hand over his face.

[ World · Drew: group formed. The Thirty-Five. I'm general apparently. somebody thank me ]

[ World · Chris: thank you drew ]

[ World · Hyvred: THANK YOU DREW ]

[ World · Newish: thank you drew (rude) ]

He laughed. It came out wrong. *Drew.* Of course it was Drew. Drew, who'd run a whole tournament from his bedroom once and held it together with a spreadsheet. Ice had stayed behind in the call afterwards, because somebody had to say thank you. And now Drew was general of thirty-five people in a swamp, and asking somebody to thank him, and getting thanked by everybody at once.

*The Thirty-Five.*

He looked at it. *Thirty-five.* Not thirty-six.

"They're counting right," said Alea, very gently. "That's all. There are thirty-five of them down there."

"I know."

"It doesn't mean—"

"I know, Alea." He wiped his face. "It's fine. It's right. There are thirty-five of them." He looked at the word on the window for a long moment. "There's thirty-five of them, and they're together, and they're careful, and Drew's in charge. That's—" His voice went. "That's everything I wanted."

***

The last line was from Thea.

Alea didn't say anything before she showed it to him. She just opened it, quietly, at the bottom of the log, and let it sit there.

[ World · Althea: found the lantern at the top of the green hill. on the bench by the door. i'm carrying it. the wisps tell the truth now, we can see what they are. whoever left it. thank you. ]

He read it for a long time.

*I'm carrying it.*

He'd left it on the bench in the ascension room on the eighteenth day, lit, for whoever came next. He'd thought about it on the stair, a little. Who'd pick it up. He'd thought maybe Hyvred, who could already see through lies. Or Deston, or Drew. Somebody careful.

*I'm carrying it.*

Thea was carrying it through the Fen. Past the wisps. The same wisps that had taken her in the first week, the first time, and walked her into the black water with her face very calm. The same lights that had shown her home. She was carrying a lantern he'd taken out of the Lanternjaw's mouth, and the wisps went pale round her and looked like what they were. Nothing could walk her off the path.

He'd pulled her out of the water on the fourth night, the first time, by the back of her jersey, while she fought him.

This time, he hadn't had to. This time she was carrying the light.

"Ice," said Alea.

He didn't answer.

"Ice. Lodestar."

He looked at her. She was sitting on the floor beside him, small and teal and white in the dark of the empty room, with her tail round her feet. She was looking at him with her two white pinprick eyes, very bright.

"*You are the light they steer by,*" said Alea. "*Anyone who can see you can find the way.* You said it doesn't do anything. You said it's a skill for other people and there's nobody there." She tipped her head. "Ice. The tower in the middle of the Fen. That's your light. You put it there. It's been burning since the thirteenth night, and you can see it from every walkway in Reedmoor. And Thea's carrying your lantern. Everywhere she goes. Your light, in her hands."

He didn't say anything.

"They've been looking at you," said Alea. "Every night. For a month. They just don't know it." Her voice did something. "They've been steering by you since the Fen. That's why they stayed."

He sat in the dark for a very long time.

***

The Kiln was on the forty-fourth day.

He did it because it was there, and because he'd decided to clear the floor before they came. He didn't want to leave anything on floor 4 that hadn't already met somebody who knew how to stand in front of it. The Kiln was the last thing.

It lived in the furnace hall at the very bottom of the Red City, by the dry river. A great black barn of a building, cut into the canyon floor, with a chimney going up through four hundred metres of rock. The first time, it had taken the thirty-four of them who were left three tries, six weeks of fear, and every healer they had. It was a giant made of iron and fired clay, three times his height, with a furnace for a heart. And when it was angry, the furnace burned so hot the clay of it glowed white and nothing could touch it.

He stood in front of it in the furnace hall with his shield up and the ember warm behind his breastbone, and waited for it to vent.

It always vented. That was the trick. It burned hotter and hotter until it couldn't hold the heat any more, and then it opened the iron plates on its chest to let it out, a great roaring breath of fire. For four seconds, the furnace heart was open to the air.

He stood in the fire. That was the hard part. He stood in front of it with his shield up while it breathed fire on him, and let the ember mend him from the inside as fast as the fire burned him from the outside. The Hartshorn Cloak smoked. The Vigil Shield went red at the rim. His bar went down and down and the ember pulled it back up, down and up, down and up, like a man bailing out a boat. He held on until the fire stopped.

Then he put his sword into the open heart.

[ Floor 4 has been cleared. ]

[ First Clear · Floor 4 · The Kiln

He sat down on the floor of the furnace hall in front of the dead Kiln with smoke coming off him, and looked at the reward.

*Kiln-heart Iron.* An ingot, the size of his hand, dark red and still faintly warm, with something moving deep inside it like a coal. A smith's metal. The kind a Blacksmith spent years on far floors looking for, and found one piece of, if they were lucky.

He knew exactly who should have it.

"The first time," said Alea quietly, "Gabe made your shield on floor sixty-one. From a dragon's shoulder plate. It took him four days."

"I know."

"It broke. At the end."

"I know." He turned the warm red ingot over in his hand. "It didn't break till the end, though. It held the whole way up."

He put the Kiln-heart Iron in his inventory, at the very back, where he wouldn't lose it. He didn't leave it on a bench anywhere. This one, he thought, he'd give by hand.

***

He didn't go up.

That was the part Alea didn't understand at first, though she didn't say so. The stair to floor 5 was open, at the back of the furnace hall. Floor 5 was there. First clears were there, and new floors, and everything he'd come back for. In the first run he'd never once cleared a floor and not gone straight up. Maigo had said it at a table on floor 2: *he doesn't wait*.

He waited.

"If I go to five," he said, sitting on the high terrace by the door at the top of the stair, on the evening of the forty-fifth day, "and they come up to four, there's a whole floor between us."

Alea looked at him.

"I don't want there to be a floor between us," said Ice. "Not on four. Not on the first floor where it's not kind." He looked down the canyon at the first square, and the dry fountain, where nothing would come off the roof any more. "When they come up those stairs, I want to be one floor away. Not two. Close enough that if something goes wrong—"

He stopped.

"Close enough," he said.

The fox didn't argue.

So he waited on floor 4. He cleared the rest of the Red City, district by district, slowly, carefully, until there was nothing in it that he hadn't met. He levelled. At twenty-five a little padlock came up beside his level, and he looked at it and left it there for now. He cooked dust-hound with the last of the floor 3 herbs, which was terrible, and ate it anyway. Every evening he sat on the red terrace by the door at the top of the two hundred and four steps, where anyone coming up from floor 3 would come out. He watched it. And every evening, he asked Alea for the rankings.

***

*Day 45.* All thirty-five: floor 3.

*Day 47.* Floor 3. Average level sixteen.

*Day 50.* Floor 3. Seventeen.

A month, on the fiftieth day, since he'd come up the stair to floor 4. Thirty-two days. He sat on the terrace in the red dusk with the dry wind coming up the canyon. He looked at the door at the top of the stair, which had not opened. Not once. Not in a month.

The first time, they'd come through that door on the thirtieth day. Thirty-six of them, at level eight or nine, tired of the swamp, and Calvin in front.

It was the fiftieth day. Nobody had come.

"Ice," said Alea.

"Yeah."

"They're on three. All of them. The whole group. Seventeen, average. Some of them eighteen. Rui Xiang's nineteen, and smug about it." She paused. "They're not coming up. They're not coming up until they're ready."

"I know."

"They stayed," said Alea.

He looked at the door for a long time.

*Be careful. Train longer on the 3rd floor.* A fingertip. A white card. The first minute. And Deston, who he'd known since they were thirteen in the second row of a school choir, had read it, and believed it, and made thirty-four other people believe it too.

Nobody had come through the door.

Rachael and Dennis were on floor 3, in a swamp, alive, being rude to each other about eels. Calvin was on floor 3 with both arms, the scout who brought everybody home. Somewhere in the middle of the Fen, a light he'd lit was burning on a drowned tower. And somewhere on a walkway in Reedmoor, someone with a black-and-pink-and-blue ponytail was carrying his lantern.

They'd stayed.

He sat down on the red stone of the terrace. Not because his legs had decided for him, this time. Because he'd decided. Because for the first time since the white room, he didn't have to be standing up.

"Good," said Alea, very softly.

She came and sat beside him, the small fox shape, teal and white in the red dusk, close enough to touch. And for a while neither of them said anything at all.

***

At the top of his window, the way it had been every morning for fifty days, there was a whisper.

*dec. i know its you. im not mad. ok im a bit mad.* And further down: *just tell me youre ok. one word. please.* And under it: *sent*.

He looked at it.

He thought about answering it. He'd thought about it every morning for fifty days. He thought about it now, on the terrace, in the dusk, with nothing left on floor 4 that could hurt anyone. With thirty-five people safe on the floor below, all together, and careful, and steering by a light they didn't know was his.

*Not yet.*

Not because it wasn't safe. Because it wasn't *time*. Because if he answered it now, Gabe would be up the stairs before he'd finished reading, and the whole careful month would come undone in an afternoon. And because there were ninety-six floors left above this one. Somewhere up there, things were waiting that he hadn't met yet in this life. He'd have to go first, and stand in front of them, and keep standing. Until they were all upstairs, and safe, and he could stop.

*When they're all upstairs and safe*, he'd told Alea under a pine on floor 2. *I want to answer it. With "ok". And then about a thousand other words. And then I'll let him hit me.*

"Soon," he said to the whisper, quietly. "Not yet. But soon."

He closed the window.

Down the canyon, the red dusk was going darker, the way it did on floor 4, never quite reaching night. The dry wind came up from the bottom of the Red City, from the furnace hall and the dead Kiln, past the first square and the dry fountain and the place by the fountain where nothing would ever come off the roof again. It went up the walls to the high terrace where a man sat with a fox made of light, beside a door that hadn't opened.

Below him, one floor down, thirty-five people were getting ready.

He'd go first. He'd keep going first. He'd stand in every doorway between here and the top. And when they came up behind him, each floor would already have met somebody who knew how to stand in front of it.

But not tonight.

Tonight he just sat on the terrace, and let himself be glad, and didn't go anywhere at all.
