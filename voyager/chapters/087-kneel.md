The last jetty in Wendmere went out a long way onto the lake, past the last of the stilts, past the last of the houses, to where the spray thinned out and the water went flat and green and quiet. It was old wood, grey and soft with wet, and it creaked when thirty-six people walked down it at once.

"*Everybody*," said Drew, at the land end, counting them onto it like a ferryman. "*Everybody*. Hako, that means you too. Put the fish down."

"It's not *my* fish."

"Then put somebody else's fish down."

He walked out to the very end and sat down on the boards with his legs over the water. It was the morning of the fifth day on floor 8. The mist was still lying on the lake in long white strips, and the falls were roaring behind them, and up on the balconies of the city the river-folk had come out to watch. Slowly. In twos and threes. With cups of something hot. Oarin had brought a stool. Behind the white curtain of the middle fall, high up in the cliff, there was a lamp lit in a cave that Ice didn't let himself look at.

The thirty-five sat down on the jetty behind him. Not in a line. In a heap, the way they sat round any fire. Gabe on his left. Deston on his right. Thea a little way back with the lantern on her knees. Calvin as close to the edge as Rachael would let him.

"You don't have to do it with us watching," said Alea, on his shoulder.

*I know.*

"I'm just saying it. Somebody has to say it every time."

*I know. Thank you.*

He looked at the little grey padlock beside his name. It had been there five days. Forty-five, and a lock, and *Tier trial required to advance*, sitting there every morning under Gabe's two whispers like a patient dog.

"Open it," he said.

***

The windows came up over the water, in the mist.

He'd never once read a trial out loud. Not the first time. Not this time. Not at ten on floor 2, not in the Fen, not on the terrace on four with the canyon going down and down below him. It had always been him and the fox and three small windows, and whatever he chose, he chose on his own.

He read them out loud.

[ Tier 3 trial · Paladin
Easy: Guard someone.
Normal: Lead someone.
]

"*Guard someone*," said Ice. "That's the easy one. *Lead someone*. That's normal."

Nobody said anything. He could feel them all behind him, listening. Thirty-five threads, pulled tight, all pointing at the back of his head.

And on the right:

[ Hard: Templar
Kneel. Let them hold.
Those whose names you have set down may enter.
]

He read that one out loud too. His voice went a bit funny on *kneel*. He read it twice.

There was a long silence on the jetty.

"What's a Templar?" said Calvin.

"An order," said Chris, behind him. Quietly. "Knights. In an order. It wasn't one person, it was—" He stopped. "It was a lot of them. Together. That's what the word means."

"So it means more than one," said Higu.

"Yeah."

"*Those whose names you have set down*," said Drew slowly. "Dec. That's what you did on four. Isn't it. In that hall. Hyv said. You set down names."

"Thirty-five," said Ice.

"So that's us," said Drew. "That's us, isn't it. *Those whose names*. It's saying we can come in."

"*Kneel*," said Gabe. On his left. He wasn't looking at the windows. He couldn't see them, nobody could but Ice, but he wasn't looking at the air where they were. He was looking at Ice. "*Let them hold*." He was quiet for a moment. "Dec. You've never knelt for anything in your life."

"I knelt on four."

"On your own," said Gabe. "In a cell. With nobody there." He looked out at the water. "This says *let them*. That's a different thing."

He knew it was. That was why his voice had gone funny.

He was thinking about the book. The big cracked book in the dead library, the first time, on the thirty-fourth floor. The stone knight on the cover. He'd read it two thousand times in the dark. He was trying to remember the page after the ash. There'd been very little left of it. Water had got in. There'd been a line at the top, he thought, and nothing under it but a brown stain the shape of a hand.

"*A Templar is not one knight*," said Ice slowly. Remembering. "*A Templar is what the knights are for.*" He swallowed. "That's all I've got. There was more. It was gone."

"*What the knights are for*," said Deston. On his right. Very softly.

He sat at the end of the jetty with his legs over the green water and the windows hanging in the mist in front of him.

He'd taken the hard one every time. He'd decided on floor 3. He'd said it on four, *every tier, every time*. And he'd never once had to ask anybody, because there'd never been anybody to ask.

"You don't have to take the hard one," said Alea.

*I know.*

He turned round.

Thirty-five faces on a wet jetty. Gabe and Deston on either side of him. Thea behind, with the lantern. Rachael with her hand already on Calvin's collar. Drew at the back with his arms folded. Higu beside him. Thirty-five people he'd set down, name by name, on a stone floor in the dark, for a thing that had been listening.

"It means you," he said. "If I take it. It means you all come in. And I don't know what's in there. Alea doesn't know. Nobody's ever done it." He stopped. "It says *kneel*. I think it means I can't fight. I think it means whatever's in there comes, and I stay down, and you—"

"Hold," said Rachael.

"Yeah."

"*Ice*," said Higu. Calmly. "Is this you telling us where it is? Or you asking us how?"

He looked at Higu for a moment.

"Asking," he said. "I'm asking."

Higu nodded slowly, as if a small thing had been put in its proper place.

"Then yes," said Higu.

"Yes," said Drew.

"*Yes*," said Calvin, already scrambling up. "Yes, obviously, yes, I've been waiting since floor *one* to—"

"Sit *down*," said Rachael, and pulled him down by the collar. "Yes."

It went round the jetty. Not all at once. One at a time, each in their own voice. Azac. Xola. Bee. Hako, with his hand still on somebody else's fish. Winter. Malcolm, and then Malcolm saying *yes* a second time, as a backup. Thea, last of all, quietly, with her hand on her left shoulder.

Gabe hadn't said anything.

"Gabe," said Ice.

"I said *accepted* on seven," said Gabe. "At the door. That covers it." He leaned over and put his hand on the back of Ice's neck. "Go on, Dec. Touch the stupid window."

He touched the window on the right.

[ Trial accepted: Templar
Kneel where you stand. Do not rise.
Thirty-five may enter.
]

***

The lake went white.

Not the mist. The *world*. The jetty, the falls, the green water, the city hung across the fall like a net. It all went white at once, from the edges in, like paper burning backwards, and then there was no lake and no sound at all.

And then there was a floor.

A white floor. Round. And not that big. And thirty-six circles of faint light on it, in a ring around the middle, each one a little bigger than a person. And faint lines running from every circle in toward the centre, where nothing at all stood.

"*Oh no*," said Calvin. In the silence. Very small. "Oh no, oh no, I *hate* this—"

They were standing in their circles. All of them. He could see them round the ring, blinking, turning, putting their hands out. Thirty-five people standing exactly where they'd woken up, a hundred and fifty-eight days ago, on the first morning, in bodies they'd never had on Earth.

He was standing in his.

He looked down at it. The thin ring of white light round his boots. He'd never stood in it. The first time, he must have. He couldn't remember. This time he'd woken up a minute early, and got up, and stepped out of it, and run. And Gabe had pushed himself up on one elbow in the circle next to it, and looked across, and seen it empty.

Gabe was in the circle next to it now.

He was looking at Ice's circle. At Ice standing in it. His face had gone very strange.

"*There you are*," said Gabe. Very quietly. As if he'd been looking at an empty circle for a hundred and fifty-nine days and had only just now been allowed to stop.

Ice knelt down.

His knees found the white floor inside the ring. He put his sword down in front of him, flat. Then the lamp shield, on top of it, face up, with Jared's iron rose on the rim catching the light. He put his hands on his knees.

*Do not rise.*

And on the far side of the ring, there was the arch. The one way out of the white room. He'd run through it once, a hundred and fifty-eight days ago, without looking back.

Something was coming through it.

***

He knew it before he saw it. He knew it by the *quiet*.

Every sound in the white room went out, from the arch inward. Calvin's small voice. Somebody's boots. The faint hum of thirty-five systems. It all went, the way sound goes out of a room when somebody shuts the door on it. Until the only thing left in the world was a slow, heavy thud. Very faint. Very deep. Like a heart, beating somewhere a long way under the floor.

And it came through the arch.

Tall. Thin. Too many joints in its long arms and legs, so that it moved wrong, folding and unfolding. A smooth head with no face on it at all. The first time it had been ash-grey, the colour of the dust in the Red City. This one was white. White like the room, so that it was hard to see, so that your eyes kept sliding off it. It came through the arch on all fours, and then it unfolded, slowly, until it stood up taller than anyone in the room. And it turned its blank head toward the ring.

Toward Calvin.

"*Ice*," said Alea. In his head. The only voice left in the world. "*Ice, that's*—"

*I know.*

"*That's the Quiet. That's the first square. That's—*" The fox's voice cracked. "*You told it. On four. You said every name and where. You told it where they*—"

*I know, Alea.*

He knelt in his circle with his hands on his knees and watched the thing that had killed Rachael and Dennis come across the white room toward Calvin, and didn't move.

He felt the threads go.

All thirty-five, at once. Not hurt, yet. Just *pulled*, hard, the way a rope pulls when the thing on the end of it sees something it's afraid of. They knew it. Of course they knew it. They'd found its body on four. Rui Xiang had found it on the rooftops, dried by six weeks of wind, with its heart stabbed through. Calvin had stopped talking at it, on the stair. Bee hadn't been able to look.

*Whoever did this went in silent.*

They'd never once asked him why he'd killed that one first. Of everything on four. Why that one.

Calvin was standing very still in his circle.

He wasn't talking. That was the first thing Ice saw. Calvin, who never stopped talking, who'd talked his way down the stair into the first square the first time and into a thing on a roof, had gone completely silent. He'd put both hands over his own mouth.

*Both* hands.

And Rachael was in the circle next to him, and she hadn't run. She had one hand on his collar. The other was flat out to her side, palm down, the way you hold a dog back. Toward Dennis.

*Stay.*

And Dennis stayed.

***

Drew had his hand up.

Ice saw it across the ring. In the silence. Drew, at the back, with his buckler on his arm and his bladed knuckles on. One hand up, flat. Then two fingers, pointed. Then a fist.

They'd made hand signs on floor 3. For the Fen, where the wisps listened. For when you couldn't shout. Ice hadn't known that. Nobody had told him. He watched thirty-five people read them anyway.

The tanks went first. Without a sound. Gabe out of his circle on Ice's left. Azac, big and calm, from across the ring. Jared. Salty with the greatsword. Hako. Chris with his longsword and his shield. Drew last. Seven of them, walking, not running, so their boots wouldn't sound on the white floor. They came in round Ice's circle in a ring of their own. Shields out. Facing away from him.

They'd put him in the middle.

Behind the shields, the healers. Rachael, letting go of Calvin's collar at last. Xola. Maigo. Kei with Dose round his wrist. Newish, not counting, for once in her life, with her lips pressed shut. Malcolm, already blessing, silently, with both hands. Yan, with Suzu quiet in his ear. Deston.

And the rest went out. Hyvred to the edge of the ring with her bow. Higu beside her. Winter. Wendy. Aleaka. Rui Xiang just *gone*, the way he went. Tiger. Milli, who did better on his own and better still outnumbered, and had decided this morning to do neither.

Thea came and stood behind Ice's circle. Right behind him. Close enough that he could feel the lantern's warmth on the back of his neck. She didn't touch him. She raised her hand.

The ward came up round all of them. Faint and blue. He'd seen it in the Fen, on a wisp, on the reed path. He'd seen it the first time, on the hundredth floor. The last thing she'd ever—

He looked at his hands on his knees.

*Do not rise.*

***

The Quiet went for Calvin first.

Of course it did. It was built out of what he'd told it, and that was what he'd told it. *Calvin ran ahead talking into the first square.* It went across the white floor in long silent sweeps, folding and unfolding, straight past the ring of shields, straight at the circle where Calvin stood with both hands over his mouth.

Calvin wasn't there.

There was a Calvin there. Standing in the circle. Talking. *Loudly*. Waving both arms over his head. The only sound in the white room except the heart, a voice that was absolutely, unmistakably Calvin's, going *OI, OVER HERE, YEAH YOU, NO FACE, LOOK AT ME, I'M THE ONE YOU*—

The Quiet hit it, and it went to coloured smoke.

And Dennis, on the far side of the ring, with his hands up and a look on his face Ice had never once seen on it in two lives, made another one. And another. Five Calvins, round the edge of the white room, all shouting. Then Rachaels. Shouting. Telling the Calvins to shut up.

*Dennis was killed running to her, shouting.*

Dennis wasn't running anywhere. He was standing in his circle, shouting through forty other people's mouths, and the Quiet was going round the ring, killing smoke.

Ice knelt in his circle and felt something go in his chest that he didn't have a name for.

Then the Quiet turned round, and came for him.

***

It hit the ring of shields at the speed of a falling tree.

He felt it. Through Bearing. All seven tanks at once, the shock of it going up their arms. *Hurt*. Not badly. Hurt. Gabe's shoulder. Azac's wrist. And where it touched them, the sound went. He saw Salty's mouth open in a shout and nothing come out. He saw Chris's shield take a blow that should have rung like a bell, and make no noise at all.

They held.

It came again. Long arms, too many joints, reaching over the shields. Hyvred's arrows went into it from the edge, three, four, silent, white. Winter's frost spread up one leg. Ina's fire, orange, the ordinary one, the one he'd lit the old hearth with, washed over its back and slid off. Rui Xiang came out of nowhere at its knee and was gone again.

It didn't care. It wasn't fighting them. It was going *through* them. To him.

He felt every hit.

That was what he hadn't thought about, on the jetty. Bearing. *You will always know which way they are, and when they are hurt.* He'd had it since four. He'd felt Thea go red on six, a long way off, across the glass. He'd felt Gabe's punch. He'd never once had all thirty-five of them in one room, being hurt, one after another after another, while he sat with his hands on his knees.

Azac's ribs. Jared's arm. Hako, knocked flat and up again. Drew's knuckles, split. Rachael's cold blue, going out to all of them, steadying. Xola's water, under it. Malcolm's blessings, and then his backups. Newish with her lips pressed shut, healing in a rhythm Ice could feel even though nobody could hear it, on the beat, on the beat, on the beat.

*Let them hold.*

He'd never in his life been the one behind the wall. Not once. Not in ten years. Not in two lives.

It was so much worse than being the wall.

***

It got Gabe on the ninth time.

He saw it happen. One long arm came over the top of the shields, slow, almost lazy, and swept Gabe's shield aside the way you'd move a curtain. And for a second Gabe was open, in front of it, with his arm out, with nothing in front of him at all.

And the other arm came down. At his elbow.

*At the elbow.*

Ice was up.

He didn't decide to be. He just was. On one knee, then on his feet, with his hand on his sword, in his circle, with the lamp shield sliding off it onto the white floor. And the window came up in front of his face before he'd even straightened.

[ Trial · failing · You stood up. ]

And the circles began to go out.

Not his. Theirs. Round the ring, one at a time. Calvin's. Then Dennis's. The faint white rings on the floor, going dark, and the people standing near them flickering. Going thin. The way a picture goes when you look at it too long. Hyvred, at the edge, put out her hand and looked at it and he could see the white floor *through* it.

*Thirty-five may enter.*

He understood it all at once, the way you understand a fall when you're already falling.

If he stood up, it was just him. That was the trial. If he stood up, they went out. Back to the jetty, back to the lake, out of the white room. And he'd be in here on his own with the thing from the first square, the way he'd always been on his own. With his sword in his hand and his shield up and nobody behind him.

He could win that. He knew he could. He'd won it before. In silence, on a roof, with a bell. It was the easiest thing in the world.

Gabe was looking at him.

The Quiet's arm had stopped. Hung there, a hair from Gabe's elbow, while the room flickered. Waiting. And Gabe was looking straight past it at Ice, standing in his circle with his sword half up.

Gabe opened his mouth and shouted.

Nothing came out. The thing had touched him. There was no sound left in him at all.

So he said it the other way. Slowly. Silently. With his whole face. Gabe, who'd once sent a whisper at four in the morning asking for one word, and waited a hundred and fifty-one days for two letters.

*DOWN. DEC.*

And then he pointed at the floor.

Ice knelt.

He put the sword back down. He put the shield back on top of it, face up, the rose on the rim. He put his hands back on his knees. It was the hardest thing he'd ever done. Harder than four. Harder than the Fen. Harder than the hundredth floor.

[ Trial · resumed ]

The circles came back up round the ring. One by one. White.

And Azac, big and calm, who'd been standing in Ice's old place in a party of five for a hundred and fifty-nine days, stepped in front of Gabe and took the arm on his own shield, and the sound went out of it.

They held.

***

After that, he didn't move again.

He didn't know how long it went on. There was no time in the white room. There was the heart, slow and heavy under the floor, and the silence, and thirty-five threads in his chest pulling and pulling and pulling. He felt everything. Every hit, every heal, every time somebody went down and got up. He knelt in it with his hands open on his knees, and he let it go through him. He didn't hold it. He didn't hold any of it. He'd learned that on four.

*Set down what you carry.*

He let them carry it.

And somewhere in it, Deston knelt down beside him.

Outside the circle. On the white floor, right at its edge, with his knee touching the ring of light. Deston, who linked the party's mana. Who'd sent everybody's strength down thirty-five threads in a clock room on seven. He didn't say anything. He couldn't. Nobody could. He just held out his hand.

Ice looked at it.

*Do not rise.* It didn't say *do not take a hand*.

He took it.

And the light went.

Not out of Deston. Out of *him*. The amber that had lived in his chest since the Fen, the light he'd been since he pressed a candle into himself on a drowned tower. It went down his arm into Deston's hand, and out of Deston down the threads. Gold and amber. Down every faint line on the white floor, the lines that ran from every circle in toward the centre of the room, where nothing had ever stood.

Up from his circle. Along the line. To the centre. And out again from the centre, along thirty-five lines, to thirty-five circles. And up through every one of them, through the boots of everybody standing in them, in a column of soft amber light.

The way it had gone on the first morning. Thirty-five pillars of light. Only amber, this time. And this time, thirty-six.

He felt it land.

He felt Gabe's shoulder stop hurting. He felt Azac's ribs knit. He felt Hako's breath come back, and Drew's knuckles close, and Rachael, steadying everyone, suddenly have something to steady her. He felt every one of them, all thirty-five, standing in his light, and he understood what it was.

*Where you stand, they are safe.*

And then the other half came. Back down the lines. From the circles to the centre, from the centre to him. Thirty-five threads of other people's light, coming the other way for once. Rachael's cold blue. Xola's water. Malcolm's blessings. Calvin's wind. Gabe's weight. Thea's ward, closing round him from behind, warm. He'd felt it in a clock room on seven, and he'd thought it was the first time.

It was the first time he'd stood still long enough to let it come all the way in.

*Where they stand, so are you.*

He knelt in the middle of thirty-six columns of light and cried. He didn't make a sound. Nobody could.

***

The Quiet came through the shields one last time.

It came straight over the top, folding, all its long arms out. And for a moment it was right there in front of him. Over him. Its smooth blank face a foot from his, white, and he could hear its heart, the only sound in the world, very loud now, thud, thud, *thud*. And he knelt with his hands on his knees and looked up at the thing he'd been twenty metres from, the first time, on a stair, with Rachael going past him in the dark.

He didn't lift his hands.

Gabe's shield came over his head. Then Azac's. Then Jared's, with the iron roses, and Chris's, and Salty's greatsword flat across them all. A roof. Thea's ward over that, blue. And thirty-five threads of light in it, holding.

The arms came down on them. Silent.

They held.

And Calvin came over the top of the shields.

He'd come from the edge of the ring at the speed he came at everything. Wind behind him. Silent. Up Salty's back and off Gabe's shield and into the air over all of them. A dagger in each hand.

In *each* hand.

He came down on the Quiet's back with both arms and put both blades into the place where the heart was.

The heart stopped.

The sound came back into the world all at once. Every sound. Boots, breath, thirty-five people gasping, somebody's shield finally ringing like a bell, Calvin landing on his backside on the white floor and going *OW*. The Quiet stood there for one long second, very still. Then it came apart. Not like a monster. Not like light. Like ash, the grey ash of the Red City, falling, until there was nothing left of it on the white floor at all.

"*Did I get it*," said Calvin, from the floor. "*Rachael. Rachael, did I get it, I couldn't see, I had my eyes shut*—"

"You got it," said Rachael. Her voice was shaking. "You got it, you idiot. Get up."

Ice knelt in his circle and looked at Calvin's arms.

Both of them. Holding both daggers. Waving them about. Getting told off for it.

He couldn't stop looking at them.

***

[ Unique skill: Sanctuary

He read them through his tears. He read them twice. Then a last one came up, a long time after the others, small, at the bottom.

[ Order: thirty-six ]

"*Thirty-six*," said Alea. Very quietly. "Not thirty-five. It counted you."

*It's an order*, he thought. *It's what the word means. It wasn't one person.*

"I know what it means, Ice." The fox sat down on his knee, between his hands. She was looking up at him with her two white pinprick eyes. "I'm saying it because I like it."

***

The white room went out like a lamp, and the lake came back.

The jetty. The mist. The roar of the falls, so loud after the silence that half of them flinched. He was kneeling at the end of the old wood boards with his sword and his shield in front of him, and thirty-five people were sitting and lying and sprawled behind him in a heap, gasping, exactly where they'd been.

Up on a balcony, Oarin lowered his cup slowly.

"You sat very still," the river-folk called down, in his deep slow voice, across the water. "All of you. For the time it takes to breathe twice." He considered them. "And then you all fell over."

"*Twice*," said Hako, flat on his back on the jetty. "*Twice*. That was about a *year*."

"It was about an hour," said Higu.

"It was about a year, Higu."

Calvin was sitting up. Flexing his hands. First one. Then the other. He looked down at them, a bit puzzled, the way you look at your hands after you've been holding something very heavy. Then he looked up, and saw Ice looking.

"What?" said Calvin. "*What*. What's on my arm."

"Nothing," said Ice.

"You're *staring* at it."

"It's a good arm," said Ice. "You've got two good arms, Calvin."

Calvin looked at him for a long moment, completely baffled.

"*Thank* you?" said Calvin.

And Ice laughed.

He didn't mean to. It just came. Sitting on the end of an old jetty in the mist with tears all down his face, and Calvin looking at him as if he'd gone mad, and thirty-four people starting to laugh too, because he was. It went out across the flat green water, and up the city, and over the falls.

High up in the cliff, behind the white curtain of the middle fall, the lamp in a cave went out. Then came on again.

***

Gabe sat down beside him on the end of the jetty, slowly, with his shield on his knees. It had a new dent in it. He looked at it as if he were proud of it.

"You stood up," said Gabe.

"I know."

"Took you about four seconds."

"Yeah."

"Took you a hundred and fifty-three days to sit down." Gabe looked out at the water. "Four seconds to stand back up. That's progress, Dec."

"You told me to get down," said Ice. "You couldn't even talk. You told me anyway."

"I always tell you," said Gabe. "You just don't usually listen."

Deston came and sat on his other side. He didn't say anything. He held his hand out again, palm up, on the wet boards between them. Ice looked at it. Then he put his own hand in it, and they sat like that, the three of them, at the end of the jetty, the way they'd sat at the end of the second row a long time ago.

Behind him, a little way back, he could feel Thea. She hadn't come closer. She was sitting with the lantern on her knees, the way she'd been sitting before. But something had changed in her thread. He didn't know what. Ever since the clock room it had pointed back at him, steady, the way a compass points.

It was pointing at him now. It was also, very slightly, *warm*.

"Your foot's not tapping," said Alea.

He looked down at his feet, hanging over the green water.

It wasn't.
