Hana had never shown anybody Glamour. Not Milli. Not anybody. She'd had it since floor 3, for sixty days, and she'd used it exactly once.

The once had been enough.

***

The padlock came up on the hundred-and-fourth day, on the rocks below Tether, in the middle of a fight with three gale-kites. She didn't notice it until the third one was down and Newish was yelling *ONE, two, three, GO* at nobody, because she'd got stuck in the rhythm. Then Hana looked at her window and there it was. A little grey lock, next to the number twenty-five.

She looked at it for a long time.

"You've got the face," said Milli. He was sitting on a rock nearby, cleaning his daggers. His arms were still pink with new skin under the bandages. "The trial face."

"I haven't got a face."

"You've got a face for everything, honey. You just think you hide them."

She closed the window. She didn't open the trial. Not that day.

She opened it two days later, alone, on the roof of the inn in Tether at dusk, with the kites humming round her and the gulls settling on the ridges for the night. She'd told Milli she was going up to watch the sunset. That was true. She watched it. And when it had gone down into the cloud, red and slow and real, she opened her window and asked for the trial.

[ Tier 2 trial · Mage
Easy: Make a light that isn't there.
Normal: Make a light that fools a frightened eye.
]

And on the right:

[ Hard: Make them see what you saw.
Hold it until it's true.
]

She sat on the roof of the inn and read it four times.

*What you saw.*

She knew what she'd seen. She'd been trying not to know it for a hundred and six days.

***

It had been the white room. The first minute. She'd woken up on a white floor in a white circle with thirty-five other circles round her. And three metres away, between her circle and the next, there had been someone standing.

Black hair, messy, falling over his eyes. A streak of teal through the fringe on one side. A white shirt with the sleeves rolled up. A black vest with teal tabs on it, like the tabs on a jacket in a game. Standing very still, looking down at the sleeping people round him. Looking at each one, with an expression on his face she'd never seen on anybody's face in her life and didn't know the name of.

She'd known him. Instantly. Not the clothes. The *face*. She'd watched that face make stupid expressions on stream for years. She'd seen it laugh at its own jokes before it finished them. She'd opened her mouth.

And Milli had grabbed her arm. *Honey?*

When she looked back, there'd been nobody there. And then the lights had come, all thirty-five of them, rising out of the floor. And every single one of them had said, in its calm, kind, certain voice: *There are thirty-five players.*

She'd said, on the second day, out loud, *it's Ice*. She'd said it on floor 1 when the name was blank, and on floor 2 when it was *Voyager*. Everybody believed it now. Of course they did. Nan Gully had looked at Gabe and said *brother*. But that wasn't the same as what she'd seen. They believed *Voyager* was Ice. She'd *seen him*. Three metres away, in the white room, with that look on his face. And then thirty-five calm, kind, certain voices had told her she hadn't.

She'd never quite stopped wondering whether they were right.

That was the thing she'd never told anyone. Not Milli. Not Jared. Not Gabe, God, especially not Gabe. Every time she said *it's Ice* out loud, there was a small cold voice under it that said *are you sure you saw anything at all?*

***

The first time had been floor 3. Her trial at ten.

She'd taken the hard one then too. She hadn't told anybody what it was. *Hard: Make something out of nothing that a frightened person would believe.* It had put her somewhere dark, a room she didn't know. There'd been a child in the corner, crying, frightened of the dark, and she'd had to make a light the child would believe in. She'd tried. She'd reached for something, anything, a candle, a lamp, the moon.

And what had come out of her hands, standing in the dark room in front of the crying child, had been *him*.

Black hair. Teal streak. White shirt, sleeves rolled. Standing very still, three metres away. Looking at the child with that look on his face.

The child had stopped crying. The trial had passed. *Glamour*, the window had said. *Show them something that isn't there. It looks like whatever you hold in mind.*

And Hana had come back from her trial and refused to show anybody what it did.

Because it took whatever you held hardest. That was the thing. You didn't choose. You reached, and it took the thing at the very bottom, the thing you held onto when you weren't holding anything else. And what she'd been holding, at the bottom, since the white room, was a man three metres away whom thirty-five voices said wasn't there.

If she used it in front of Gabe, and *that* came out—

She couldn't. She'd never risk it. She'd rather never use it at all.

***

She touched *Hard*.

[ Trial accepted
Make them see what you saw.
]

And she was in the white room.

It wasn't like remembering. It was like being there. The white floor under her hands, cold and smooth. The white circle round her. Thirty-five other circles, and thirty-four people waking in them, slowly, groaning, sitting up. Gabe, enormous and confused. Thea, very still. Calvin already asking where they were. Every face exactly right.

And three metres away, between her circle and the next, there was nobody standing.

She looked at the empty place for a long time.

Then a hand closed on her arm.

"*Honey?*" said Milli.

She looked round. He was there, sitting up in his circle, his eyes wide, his ears flat. Exactly the way he'd looked. Exactly the way he'd sounded.

"Milli," she said. "There's someone there."

He looked where she was looking. At the empty place.

"There's nobody there, honey."

And out of the white floor, all round the room, the lights began to rise. Thirty-five of them. Soft white balls of light, one in front of each person, rising up out of the floor like bubbles out of water. They spoke all at once, calm and kind and certain.

"*Welcome, Player. There are thirty-five players.*"

*Make them see what you saw.*

She got up. She stood in her white circle with Milli's hand still on her arm and faced the empty place. Thirty-four of her friends were sitting up round her, looking at her, puzzled. She reached.

Glamour came up out of her hands the way it had on floor 3. Easily. Too easily. And there he was.

Three metres away. Black hair falling over his eyes. Teal streak. White shirt, sleeves rolled to the elbow. The black vest. Standing very still, looking down at the people round him with that look on his face.

All round the white room, thirty-four people turned their heads and saw him.

She heard them. She heard Gabe make a sound. She heard somebody say *Declan*—

"*There are thirty-five players*," said the lights.

And he flickered.

Just at the edges. Like a picture on a screen when the signal goes. She felt it in her hands. The Glamour, wavering. Because the lights said so, calm and kind and certain, and the small cold voice at the bottom of her said *are you sure?* And she wasn't. She wasn't sure. She'd never been sure. She'd been telling herself for a hundred and six days that she was, and she wasn't.

He went out.

The room was white and empty. Thirty-four people looked at the place where he'd been, and then at her, puzzled, sorry for her. And Milli's hand tightened on her arm.

"*Honey?*"

And the lights rose again out of the floor, all thirty-five, calm and kind.

"*Welcome, Player. There are thirty-five players.*"

The minute had started again.

***

She tried it seven times.

The second time, she held him longer. She gritted her teeth and held on, and when the lights spoke he flickered and she held on harder, and he lasted another ten seconds before he went. The third time she tried to make him brighter, more solid, more *there*, and the harder she tried the faster he went, as if trying were the opposite of it.

The fourth time, she used Beguile.

She'd had Beguile since floor 2. Her first skill, the one that came the way her charm always came, easily, like breathing. *Make them love the wrong thing. Just for a moment.* She'd used it on wolves and on clerks and on Nan Gully's price, once, very slightly, and felt guilty about it after. She'd thought: *if they won't believe it, I'll make them want to.* So she held up the Glamour of him, three metres away. Then she reached out with Beguile to all thirty-four of them, the way she reached for a stream chat when it was flagging. *Look at him. Love him. Believe in him.*

They looked at him. Every one of them. Their faces went soft. They loved him. Of course they did. They already did.

And the lights said, *There are thirty-five players*. And he went out like a candle.

[ That is the wrong thing to love. ]

The window sat in the air in front of her, small and grey, in the empty white room.

She sat down on the white floor and put her face in her hands.

***

She didn't know how long she sat there. In the trial there was no time. Just the white, and the circles, and the minute starting again and again round her. Milli's hand on her arm. *Honey?* The lights rising. *There are thirty-five players.*

*That is the wrong thing to love.*

She thought about it. She made herself. She was a methodical person. She'd built a whole channel, years of it, by working out why things didn't work and fixing them.

Beguile made them love the wrong thing. That was what it was *for*. She'd tried to make them love him, believe him, see him. And that was the wrong thing, because—

Because she didn't need them to.

She sat on the white floor very still.

She'd been trying to make thirty-five people see him. To make them believe her. To make the lights stop saying he wasn't there. Every time, she'd held him up in front of them like a picture and waited to see whether they'd believe it. And every time the lights spoke, she'd looked at their faces, at Milli's face, to see if it was working. And the moment she looked, the moment she *needed* them to see it, the small cold voice said *are you sure*. And she wasn't. And he went out.

*Hold it until it's true.*

It wasn't *their* truth. It never had been. It was hers.

She'd seen him. She had. Three metres away. Black hair, teal streak, that look on his face. Nobody else had to believe it. The lights could say whatever they wanted. She was the one who'd been there. She was the one who'd opened her mouth to say his name.

She stood up.

Milli's hand closed on her arm. "*Honey?*"

"I know," said Hana. "It's okay. I saw someone."

She reached. Glamour came up out of her hands, easily. And there he was.

Three metres away. Standing very still, looking down at the sleeping people round him with that look on his face.

She didn't look at the others. She didn't look at Milli. She didn't look at the lights, rising round her out of the floor. She looked at *him*. Only at him. The way she'd looked at him in the real white room, for that one second, before Milli grabbed her arm. And she let herself see it. All of it. Every detail. The way he stood. The teal in his hair. The expression on his face that she'd never seen on anybody's face and hadn't known the name of.

She knew the name of it now. She'd seen it on Gabe's face in the street in Tether, sitting on the stones. She'd seen it on her own face in Milli's eyes, on the edge of a broken span, all night.

It was the face of somebody looking at people they'd lost. And somehow found again.

"*There are thirty-five players*," said the lights.

"I know," said Hana, quietly, to them, without looking away from him. "I counted. I'm not counting players. I'm saying what I saw."

He didn't flicker.

He stood there, three metres away, solid and still. The lights said it again, *there are thirty-five players*. And he didn't go anywhere, because she wasn't holding him up for anybody any more. She was just looking at him. And he was there, because she'd seen him. Because she knew she had.

The lights said it a third time.

Then they didn't say it again.

She was on the roof of the inn in Tether in the dark, with the kites humming and the real stars out, sitting with her knees up and her face wet.

She sat there for a long time.

***

She showed Milli the next morning.

Not *him*. She wasn't ready for that. But she sat on the end of their bed in the inn with Milli sitting up against the wall, sleepy, his ears still flat on one side from the pillow. She held out her hand, palm up, and reached. Just a little. And in her palm, small and perfect, there was an onion.

A wild onion. Round, papery, the outer skin peeling. Exactly like the frozen onion skin Tiger had given her on floor 2, the one she kept inside her crown.

Milli looked at it. He reached out a finger and touched it. His finger went straight through.

"That's *it*?" he said. "That's what you've been hiding for sixty days? An *onion*?"

And Hana laughed so hard she fell off the end of the bed.

When she'd stopped, lying on the floor with her crown sideways, she told him. All of it. The white room, and the floor 3 trial, and the crying child, and what had come out of her hands. The small cold voice. The seven tries, and Beguile, and *that is the wrong thing to love*. Milli listened to all of it sitting on the bed with his tail still. He didn't interrupt. He never did. He just listened, the way he did, as if everything she said was worth getting exactly right.

When she'd finished, he was quiet for a while.

"You saw him," he said.

"I saw him."

"I didn't." He looked at his hands. "I grabbed your arm. I was looking at you." He was quiet. "I've always been sorry about that. You know. That I grabbed you. That you looked away because of me."

She sat up on the floor and looked at him.

"You grabbed me," said Hana, "because you woke up in a white room in a body with a tail and the first thing you did was look for me." She got up and sat beside him on the bed. "Don't you dare be sorry about that."

He didn't say anything. But his tail came round and curled over her wrist, which it hadn't done since floor 4.

***

They went up the Spindle on the hundred-and-tenth day, all thirty-five of them.

Gabe ate the eggs first. Nan Gully made them, behind her hut, at dawn. Salt fish and gull eggs and the little twist of grey-green herb out of the paper, the way the unsigned note had said. *For the big one in the coat. Put it in his eggs the morning he goes up.* Gabe ate them standing up, by the winch, with Nan Gully watching him from her basket. When his window came up, he read it and didn't say anything. He just stood there for a while in the dawn holding the empty pan. Hana saw the window over his shoulder.

[ Kitecliff eggs · AGI +3 · Sure-footed · 4 hours ]

"Pell's herb," said Nan Gully. "Off the Kitecliff. Only grows there." She looked at Gabe. "He went up there. Five days. Learned to stand. Then he came down and cooked those eggs for himself the morning he killed it." She put her pipe in. "He wanted you to have the same breakfast."

Gabe put the pan down very carefully.

The crown of the Spindle was thirty metres across and bare, and there were two dead birds on it.

That was the first thing they all saw, coming up over the top of the spiral path in a long line. Two great grey shapes lying on the rock, one a little way from the other. Huge. Wings half spread. Feathers the colour of rain, gone dull. They didn't go anywhere. They'd been lying there in the sun and wind for weeks, and the smell came off them on the wind.

"Two," said Hyvred, very quietly.

"He killed it twice," said Malcolm. He said it the way he'd said it in the furnace hall, in the same flat, certain voice. "Once to clear it. Once to leave."

Gabe looked at the two dead birds for a long time.

And then the third one came round the needle.

***

It saw them. Hana watched its head turn: a yellow eye the size of a plate, looking at thirty-five people on the crown.

"Nobody stands still," said Drew. His voice was very calm. "Nobody. Lean. Like Gabe showed you. Keep moving. Don't give it anything to choose."

They'd practised. For four days, on the edges of the islands round Tether, in the wind, thirty-five people learning to sway like kites. Gabe teaching them what Pell had taught him, badly, with lots of swearing and falling over. Hana had learned. Leaning on the wind turned out to be a bit like leaning into a song you knew well. Most of them had learned. Some of them hadn't, quite. Calvin still braced when it gusted. Dennis still grabbed things.

The bird came round.

It didn't stoop. It went round them, high, and again, lower, looking. Thirty-five people on the crown of the Spindle, all moving. All swaying. All leaning on the wind, small and constant, never still. It came in low on the fourth pass and Rui Xiang went under its leg with both knives, and Tiger went under the other. It screamed and went up, bleeding.

It came round again, and Winter put a frost arrow into the wound and the leg went white to the knee.

It went up. High, directly over the crown. Hana felt the wind change.

It came down from straight above. A column of it, a hand pressing on a table. Everybody went down. Thirty-five people on one knee or flat on the rock, pressed into the stone by the wind of its wings, unable to lean on something that came from straight overhead. Unable to move. Still. All of them. Every single one, pinned to the crown like insects on a card.

And above them the Squall folded its wings.

It could choose now. Hana saw it understand that. Thirty-five still things on a crown, and it could choose any one. She saw its yellow eye go over them, over the bodies flat on the rock, searching. She saw it go past Calvin, past Drew, past her. She saw it stop on Gabe. Gabe in the wolf coat, the biggest, on one knee in the middle of the crown with his round shield over his head, pressed down. Still.

It had seen Gabe on the island on the first morning. The kite-folk had looked at him. Everything on this floor seemed to know which one was Gabe.

*The bird comes down on whoever stands still.*

Hana reached.

***

She didn't decide what to make. There wasn't time. There was only time to reach, the way she'd reached in the white room, and let Glamour take whatever was at the bottom.

And at the bottom, she found it was ready. As if it had been waiting a hundred and ten days.

On the far side of the crown, past Gabe, past all of them, by the edge, a man stood up.

Not him. Not his face. She held it away from his face with everything she had. She felt it want to be him, felt it pulling, black hair and teal and white shirt and that look, wanting to come up into the light. She didn't let it. She'd learned, in the white room, how to look at a thing and hold it exactly as it was. She held this exactly as it was.

It was the shape.

The shape from the furnace hall on floor 4. The one on the floor in front of the dead Kilns, unscorched, in the middle of the black glass. The one Gabe had stood in, and fitted, nearly. A man with a shield. Standing. Feet apart, shoulders forward, shield up, braced. A wall. The dull red of the Red City's stone, with no face at all. Just the shape. Just the way he'd stood, alone, in front of the fire. Twice.

She made it stand on the edge of the crown of the Spindle, still as stone, braced against the sky.

And then she used the new one.

*Centre Stage. Put something in the middle of every eye. It stays there as long as you can bear to look at it yourself.*

She put it in the middle of the bird's eye.

She felt it go. She felt the Squall's great yellow eye leave Gabe and swing round, across the crown, to the edge. To the man with the shield, standing there. Braced. Still. A wall. The stillest thing on the whole floor. It stood exactly the way the bird had been made to kill.

*It stays there as long as you can bear to look at it.*

She looked at it.

It was the hardest thing she'd ever done. Harder than the white room. She looked at the shape on the edge of the crown, the shape of a man standing alone in front of something that was going to kill him. And she didn't look away. She didn't let it become his face. She didn't let herself think about him standing exactly like that in the Kiln's fire, alone, with nobody behind him. Twice. She just looked. She held it still in the middle of the bird's eye and in the middle of her own, and bore it.

The Squall came down.

It came down out of the sky like a thrown spear, faster than anything, straight at the shape. Hana didn't blink. The shape didn't move. It stood braced on the edge of the crown with its shield up, waiting, the way a wall waits. The bird hit it with everything it had. All the speed of the stoop, all the weight of a barn-sized bird, talons first.

And went straight through.

It hit the rock. The whole crown jumped. Stone came up in a fountain of chips. The Squall lay on the edge of the crown with its legs folded under it and its wings flung out, stunned, its head jarred sideways by the shock of its own landing. Its white frozen leg had cracked under it.

The wind was gone.

"*NOW*," said Drew.

They were up. All thirty-five. Gabe first, leaning, moving, with his shield. He went in under the beak as the great head came round and took the strike on the shield, and went *with* it, the way Pell had taught him, and didn't fall. Salty and Jared on the wings, pinning them, leaning on them. Hako's spear in its side. Ina's fire. Newish, counting, *ONE, two, three*, and everybody hitting on the *one*. And Rui Xiang, who'd read a perfect kill off the roofs of the Red City and had been trying to make one ever since. He went under the wing, low, into the place where the wing met the body, and up. Into the heart.

[ Floor Guardian defeated · thirty-five Players ]

The Squall lay on the edge of the crown, beside its two dead selves, and didn't move again.

Hana let the shape go.

It went out like a candle. One moment a man with a shield standing at the edge of the crown. The next, nothing. Just the edge, and the sky beyond it, and the cloud a long way down.

She sat down on the rock.

***

Milli found her there a minute later. He sat down beside her without saying anything, and his tail came round and curled over her wrist.

"It wanted to be him," she said. Very quietly, so nobody else would hear. "The whole time. It kept pulling. It wanted to have his face."

"I know."

"I didn't let it." She was shaking. "Gabe was right there. If it had been his face, standing there, and the bird going through it, and Gabe watching—" She stopped. "I didn't let it."

"I know, honey," said Milli. "I saw."

She leaned on him. After a while she stopped shaking.

Across the crown, Gabe was standing by the edge where the shape had stood. He wasn't looking at the dead bird. He was looking at the rock at his feet, where there was nothing, where there'd never been anything, where the Squall had gone straight through and hit stone. He stood there a long time. Then he looked across the crown at Hana.

He didn't say anything. He didn't need to. He'd stood in that shape on the floor of the furnace hall. He knew whose it was.

He nodded to her, once.

She nodded back.

***

The kites came at noon.

Seven of them, off the Kitecliff, out across the Reaches in a long line, high, higher than the Kitecliff, toward the Spindle. They went round and round the crown above the thirty-five of them in a ring, slow, laughing. The boy turned loops, and Calvin yelled up at him to do another one, and he did, and Calvin cheered.

The old one came down last. Pell. Tall and thin, feathers lifting along their arms. They landed on the crown light as a gull and unbuckled their wings. They looked at the three dead birds. Then at the thirty-five people standing among them. Then at Gabe, swaying a little in the wind, not holding anything.

"Better," said Pell.

Gabe laughed. It came out cracked.

Pell looked round at all of them. The old face did something that, in a person, might have been a smile.

"Kill it often," they said.

"He told you to say that?" said Gabe.

"He told Nan. Nan told me. I told him." Pell looked out at the needle's edge, where the shape had stood. They looked at it for a long moment, with their large dark bird's eyes. As if they could still see it.

"Whose shape was that?" they said.

Everyone went quiet.

Hana stood up. She was still shaking a little. Milli stood up with her.

"A friend's," she said.

Pell looked at her. Then back at the empty edge of the crown, where a man with a shield had stood braced against the sky. Feet planted. Shoulders forward. A wall.

"He doesn't stand like that any more," said Pell.

And they buckled on their wings, and stepped off the edge of the crown into the wind, and went up to join the others in the sky.

Nobody said anything for a long time. Thirty-five people stood on the crown of the Spindle among three dead birds, in the real sun, and looked at the edge where Pell had gone. Overhead, seven kites went round and round, high, in a sky that was theirs for one more day.

Hana put her hand up and touched the crown on her head. The iron rose on the front of it. And inside it, tucked in where nobody could see, a frozen onion skin from a camp under a pine on floor 2.

*He doesn't stand like that any more.*

She didn't know what that meant. Nobody did. But she held on to it, the way she'd held the shape, and didn't look away.
