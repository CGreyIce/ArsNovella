The eye didn't move.

He hadn't known what he expected. That it would shut, maybe. Or flinch. Or that the heart would go *once* so hard the jam would come apart under his knees. Something. It didn't do anything. It went on looking up at him through the clear black ice, round and dark and old, with that light moving in it very far down, and the heart under it went on beating. *Once. Once. Once.* Fast and shallow, the way his own had gone on the Grey before Borne, when he'd stood out there alone in the wind with the bell so far off it might as well have been on Earth.

*It's listening*, said Alea. Very quietly. *I think. I can't read it. There's nothing to read. It's all grey.* A pause. *But it's listening, Ice.*

He knelt properly.

He'd been kneeling already. It wasn't that. It was the other thing. The thing the trial had asked of him on the worst night of the first year, in a circle, when he'd knelt for the first time in his life with the thirty-five round him and been told *do not rise*: you didn't kneel with your knees. You knelt with the rest of you. You put down the part of you that was always halfway to its feet, ready to go first, ready to be the one. You put it down on the ice in front of you like a pack you'd carried too long.

It was very hard. It was always very hard. It was getting less hard.

He put it down.

[ Sanctuary ]

The light went out of him.

Amber. The colour of the lantern. It went out from his knees across the clear ice in a ring, slow, the way water goes out from a stone, and it was warm. It reached Gabe on his right and went round him and Hearthwall, and the shield went *tick* on the ice and glowed a little at the rim. It reached Rachael on his left, kneeling with her bare hands flat. It reached Niko, standing a pace behind with his sword. It reached Sabe, three slabs off with her pole, and Tuli, three slabs the other way. And it kept going.

It had never gone this far. On the night of the trial it had been a circle as wide as a room. Now it went out across the top of the jam, over the heaved slabs, down the glittering face of it, and he felt it go, the way you feel your own arm reach. Down to the rock at the foot of the jam. To the thirty standing there in the afternoon light. To Deston, with his hand on his chest. To Azac, beside him. To Hako and the dog and Bee. To the very edge, where the ice met the shore, where somebody was holding up a lantern with an amber light in it that leaned.

The two lights touched. His and hers.

*Where you stand, they are safe.*

And then it went down.

He hadn't meant it to. He didn't know it could. It went down from his knees through the clear black ice, the way the warm had gone from Hearthwall, and down, and he felt it reach the curve of the pale curled shape under him, the size of a house. And go round it. The whole of it. The heart was right under where he knelt. It was where he stood.

The eye changed.

He couldn't have said how. It didn't blink. It didn't move. Something in it, the light moving far down at the bottom, came up a little. Toward him. The way a fish comes up toward a lamp held over dark water.

*Ice*, said Alea. *Ice. It's in it. It's in the circle.*

"I know."

*You've got it in Sanctuary.*

"I know." He didn't look away from the eye. "It's where I'm standing."

***

Niko knelt down beside him.

He didn't say anything. He went down on one knee on the clear ice, just past Ice's right hand, past the edge of Hearthwall, and drew his sword, and laid the edge of it flat on the ice. And drew it. Slowly. A single line, as long as a forearm, scored into the surface, right beside where Ice was kneeling.

The line didn't fade. It stayed there, thin and bright, very faintly humming, the way the arrow had hummed in the rock at dawn.

"*Wait for me*," said Niko. Under his breath. To the line.

He stood up. He sheathed the sword. He stepped back to where he'd been standing, a pace behind, where Ice could see him if he turned his head, and put his thumb on the hilt.

"So you can find this place," said Niko. "If you go anywhere."

Ice looked at the line. Then at Niko.

"I'm not going anywhere."

"I know," said Niko. In his flat voice. "It's for if you do."

***

"Right," said Rachael.

She hadn't moved her hands. She'd had them flat on the ice the whole time, through Sanctuary, through Niko's line, her bare fingers spread wide on the clear black over the heart's eye. Her face was very calm. He knew that calm. It was the one she wore in the house for the wounded, before she went in.

"I'm going to take some of it," said Rachael. "Into me. Then I'm going to give it to you." She didn't look at him. She was looking at the eye. "Don't be polite about it, Dec. When I give it, take it. Don't make me hold it while you decide."

"I won't."

"You will. You always do. You stand there with your hands half out going *are you sure*." She breathed in through her nose. "Don't."

*Burden.*

He didn't see a window. It wasn't his skill. He felt it. Through her thread, the gold one going out from under his breastbone to her, a yard away: something opened in Rachael. Like a door into a cold room. And the cold came in.

She made a sound. Not a loud one. A sort of short breath in through her teeth, the sound you'd make putting your hand into a bucket of meltwater to find something dropped at the bottom. Her fingers went white on the ice. Then her wrists.

"*Oh*," said Rachael. Very quietly.

He felt what she felt. Through the thread. Not all of it. A bit. It was enough.

It was the river.

That was the first thing. The whole of the river's winter. Months of it. Every snow that had fallen on every slope above the Mother since autumn, every frost, every grey morning, all of it gone down into the ice and the ice gone down the river and piled up here, at the door, against the cliffs. All of it leaning. And behind it the lake, four days of spring and melt, black and rising, pushing. And under all of it, holding it, one pale arm in the rock.

And under that, the thing he hadn't expected.

The fear wasn't new.

He'd thought it would be. A fright. A thing that had woken up four days ago in the dark with the whole river on top of it and panicked. It wasn't. It was old. It was as old as Keddle, older; it was the oldest thing he'd ever felt in either life. Every spring. Every spring for longer than there'd been people on the river to count the springs, it had woken up in the dark at the bottom of the Mother and found itself holding everything. On its own. And not known any way to put it down. Except all at once. Because that was the only way it had ever been put down. All at once, with a roar, the jam going and the lake going and the stilts going and the poles going, and then the long cold quiet of summer at the bottom of the river, when nothing was holding anything, and then. Then the next spring. And waking up. And all of it on top of it again.

And nobody had ever once come close.

Not to sit. People had come close to *cut*. To push, to burn, to break. Sabe's gran's gran had come close with poles and axes, and they'd done what they could, and every spring it had let go of everything at once because it didn't know anything else. And nobody had ever once come close and knelt on the ice over it and said hello.

He knew it.

That was what took his breath. Not the cold. He knew it. He knew it the way you know your own handwriting on an old page you'd forgotten writing. *All or nothing.* Holding everything, on your own, because you'd always held everything on your own, because nobody had ever been close enough to hold a bit. And then, at the end, when it was too heavy, letting go of all of it at once. On a pass. On the hundredth floor. On a white floor in a white room with a sound like a door shutting.

He'd been that.

He'd been that for a whole life and most of another.

Rachael's hand came off the ice.

The left one. White to the wrist. She didn't take his hand. She'd never in her life taken anybody's hand that wasn't a patient's. She took his wrist instead, hard, the way she held Calvin's collar, her fingers like ice round the bone.

"Here," said Rachael.

He didn't decide.

[ Borne ]

It came into him.

***

It was colder than the river had been.

He'd thought nothing could be. On day 720, under the ice, with Calvin's wrist somewhere in the dark and the hole lost, the cold had gone into him so hard and so fast that he'd stopped being able to tell which way was up. He'd thought that was the bottom of cold. The place below which there wasn't any more.

This was below it.

It came up his arm from Rachael's fingers. Up through the wrist, up through the elbow, into his shoulder and his chest, and where it went he stopped being able to feel anything but the weight. Not of the ice. Of the *holding*. The whole river's winter, a bit of it, a corner, the part Rachael had been able to carry in two bare hands. A corner of it was more than he'd ever carried. A corner of it was the Grey.

*Ninety-four*, said Alea.

"Ninety-four," said Ice. Out loud.

Beside him, Gabe lifted his head.

"*NINETY-FOUR*," said Gabe.

He said it down the jam. The way he'd called the line on every wall they'd ever held, that great flat voice of his that went through anything. It went down the face of the jam, over the slabs, through the roar of the flood on the right, down to the rock at the bottom, where thirty people were standing.

Where one of them was holding a lantern.

*Ninety-one.*

"Ninety-one."

"*NINETY-ONE.*"

Ice didn't look down. He looked at the eye. It was looking at him. It had felt Rachael take a bit; he could see that it had. The light in it had come up a little more.

*Eighty-seven.*

"Eighty-seven."

"*EIGHTY-SEVEN.*"

And then the gold came.

It came up the threads from below. From the foot of the jam. From Deston. Thirty-five thin gold threads, going out from under his breastbone to everybody, the ones he'd felt for two years and used once, on the Grey, and otherwise only for knowing where they were: something came along them the wrong way. Toward him. Warm. Gold. Deston's gold, the colour of the Grey. *What one of you has, all of you may share.* It came up all thirty-five threads at once and met the cold in his chest.

And took some.

Not much. A little. Off the top. The way you'd take one end of a heavy thing off somebody's shoulder without asking. It took a little of the cold out of his chest and went back down the threads with it. All thirty-five. A thirty-fifth each.

And then, below, he felt Azac.

*Overlap.* He'd never felt it from this side. It was like watching somebody take a bowl of water and pour it out across a long table, so that every cup got a little, so that no cup got enough to spill. The cold that Deston had taken went out down the thirty-five threads, and Azac spread it, and it went thin. Thinner. Thin enough that it was light.

*The whole line takes it. Lightly.*

He felt them take it.

All of them, at once, the way you feel weather. He felt Hako's thread take its bit and plant, like a man taking the weight of a shield on his shoulder and not moving his feet. He felt Maigo's take it into the cliff, through his bare feet, into the rock. He felt Chi's go small and stubborn round it, and Calvin's give a jump, and Bee's flicker and then hold. He felt Xav's go very still. He felt Higu's thread take its bit and do something he'd never felt a thread do before: *write it down*. He felt Kei's measure it. He felt Yan's, high up on the ledge, the farthest one, take its bit of the river's cold and turn it over, curious, the way he'd turn over a stone to see what was under it.

He felt Ina's. Which had been cold already. Which took a bit more, and didn't complain.

And he felt Thea's.

At the edge of the ice, at the foot of the jam, with the lantern. Her thread took its thirty-fifth of the cold and didn't change at all. Didn't flinch. Didn't tighten. Just held it, like she held the lantern. Steady. Leaning toward him.

*Eighty-eight*, said Alea.

He stopped.

He hadn't noticed it go up. It had gone up.

"Eighty-eight," he said.

"*EIGHTY-EIGHT*," said Gabe. And then, quieter, not down the jam, just to him: "That's up."

"That's up."

*Holding*, said Alea. Very quietly. As if she couldn't believe it. *Ice. It's holding. You're not going down. It's going out of you as fast as it's coming in. Thirty-five ways.*

Rachael's hand was still on his wrist. White to the elbow now. Her face was grey and calm and her other hand was flat on the ice, still taking, still carrying it from the eye to him, to Borne, to Deston, to Azac, to everybody. Like a chain of buckets. Like the line on the Long Acre passing up sheaves.

"Better," said Rachael. Through her teeth. "You took it."

"I took it."

"You didn't say *are you sure*."

"I nearly did."

"I know." Something happened at the corner of her mouth. "I felt you nearly do it."

***

"It moved," said Maigo.

From the cliff. From his place on the rock right beside arm 1, with his bare feet flat on the stone and his eyes shut. He didn't say it loudly. He never said anything loudly. It carried anyway, the way his voice did, the way a root goes through soil.

"Where?" said Sabe.

"At the end. The tip. The very tip of it." Maigo's face didn't move. "It's not gripping with the tip. Not as hard. Like a hand when it's falling asleep."

Sabe put her pole down. *Tock.* She tipped her head and listened. On the other side of Ice, three slabs off, Tuli did the same. *Tock.* The two of them, the old woman and the girl, listening down through the ice with their heads on one side like birds.

"Not yet," said Sabe.

They waited.

He knelt on the ice with his hands flat and the cold coming into him and going out of him thirty-five ways, and waited, and it was the strangest waiting of his life. He wasn't doing anything. He was doing the hardest thing he'd ever done. He was holding a bit of it, and letting go of the bit he was holding, and holding the next bit, over and over, and not getting up.

*Once*, went the heart. *Once.* A little slower.

"Not yet," said Tuli.

*Once.*

"*Now*," said Sabe.

He felt it.

Not through his hands. Through the whole jam. Something at the far end of arm 1, under the cliff, opened. A little. The way a fist opens one finger. And the part of the jam that finger had been holding, a slab the size of the Copper Spoon and everything piled on it, groaned, and shifted, and slid. Slow at first. Away from the cliff. Toward the open door on the right, toward the flood. It went over the edge of the jam with a sound like a whole street falling down, and the flood took it, and it went through the Narrows. Turning. Breaking. Gone.

The jam shook under them. Sabe and Tuli leaned on their poles. Niko took a half-step, then put his foot back.

And behind the jam, up the valley, the lake dropped. He felt that through the threads too: thirty people at the foot of the jam turning to look up the valley as one, at the water against the cliffs going down. A hand. Half a hand.

"One finger," said Maigo. Eyes shut.

And the heart gripped harder.

He'd known it would. Chris had written it down. *Each release makes the heart grip harder with what's left.* It came into him through Rachael's hand like a wave coming up a beach, the cold all at once doubled, the holding all at once tighter, and his chest went tight round it.

*Seventy-one.*

"Seventy-one."

"*SEVENTY-ONE.*"

And then, behind it, the gold. Thirty-five threads. Taking. Spreading. Thin.

*Seventy-four.*

"Seventy-four."

The eye was looking at him.

It had let go of one finger's worth. It had gripped harder with all the rest, the way it always had, the way it had with arms five and four and two and three. And the grip had come into a man kneeling on the ice over it, and gone out of him thirty-five ways, and he was still there.

The light came up a little more in the eye.

"Again," said Rachael. Very quietly.

***

They did it eleven times.

He didn't count. Alea did. He counted his numbers, and said them, and Gabe said them down the jam, and that was all the counting he could do. The sun went across the gap of sky between the black cliffs, slowly, and the shadows of the cliffs went across the jam, and they knelt there in it.

*Now*, and a finger. *Not yet. Not yet. Now*, and another. And every time, the jam groaned and a piece of it went over the edge into the flood and through the door, turning, breaking, gone. And every time, the lake dropped a hand. And every time, the heart gripped harder with what was left, and it came into him through Rachael's white hand, and the numbers went down, and he said them, and the gold came up the threads and took it and spread it and the numbers came back.

Not all the way. Never all the way. Each time a little less far.

*Sixty-two.*

"Sixty-two."

"*SIXTY-TWO.*"

*Sixty.*

He'd promised. On eleven, after the tow, with Thea and Xola both shouting at him. On fourteen, in the healing, every day, with her sitting by the bed being angry. He healed at sixty. Not at twenty. Not at nine. Not when he'd decided he'd had enough. At sixty. Every time.

"Sixty," said Ice. "It hurts."

He put a hand on his own chest. Mend Self. Plain. The first thing he'd ever learned, two lives ago. It went into him and warmed a little of the cold.

"*SIXTY*," said Gabe. Down the jam. And then, louder, a thing Gabe had obviously been waiting two years to shout down anything: "*HE'S HEALING.*"

And from the foot of the jam, faint, under the roar of the flood, he heard thirty people make a noise. Not a cheer. More like a laugh. A sort of shocked laugh, all at once, the way people laugh when the thing they've been told about for years turns out to be true.

Rachael's fingers tightened on his wrist.

"About time," said Rachael.

***

The jam was a third of what it had been by the time the shadows reached the middle.

The heaped slabs had gone from the outside in. Peeled off, finger by finger, the far end first, the way Sabe's gran had said. What was left was the core of it: a low island of broken ice against the foot of the left cliff, glittering, groaning a little, with the flood running past it on the right. And in the middle of the island, the clear black pan they were kneeling on. And under the pan, the heart. And one arm, from the heart to the cliff, still gripping.

Thinner. Maigo said it was thinner. He'd stopped saying fingers. He said it was holding with the palm now, and the heel of the hand.

"Two more," said Sabe. Leaning on her pole. Her voice was very tired. "Maybe three. Then the last."

*Fifty-eight*, said Alea. *You need to—*

"Fifty-eight. It hurts." Mend Self. "Sixty-one."

"*SIXTY-ONE.*"

He was so cold. He couldn't feel his knees. He couldn't feel the hand Rachael was holding, or the hand flat on the ice. He could feel the eye, looking at him. It hadn't stopped looking at him since he said hello. And the light at the bottom of it had come up all afternoon, a little with every finger, like somebody coming up a long stair toward a door with a lamp behind it.

"Not yet," said Tuli.

He felt it before anybody said anything.

Not through the jam. Through the eye.

The light in it came up. Not a little. All at once. Up from the bottom of the eye like something let off a rope, up, past where it had been, past the middle, toward the top. Toward the ice. Toward him.

And kept going.

"*Ice*," said a voice.

High up. From the ledge. A voice he'd never in either life heard raised: cool and level, always, about the dead and the living and everything in between, and now, from the top of the black cliff, *shouting*.

"*ICE. IT'S GOING UP.*"

***

He understood it all at once.

It had felt them. All afternoon. A man on the ice over it, kneeling. Something taking the weight. A bit. And a bit. And thirty-five threads going out of the man to thirty-five people, and the weight going thin, and the man still there. It had felt itself held. For the first time in all the springs there had ever been.

And it only knew all or nothing.

It had been holding everything on its own for so long. And now, for the first time, something was holding some of it, and it had looked at that, all afternoon, with its old dark eye. And understood the only way it knew how.

*Somebody's got it.*

*So I can let go.*

*All of it.*

Not the arm. Itself. The light was going up out of the bottom of it the way Yan said lights went, at the end, in everything that let go of everything at once. The heart was putting itself down. And when it did, the arm would let go of the cliff. All at once. With the palm and the heel of the hand and everything that was still left, the last third of the jam and the lake behind it, half a mile of water, and the Float moored in the reed-bay with three hundred people on it, and the summer moorings below, and Keddle's people on the cliff by the stair door.

*All at once*, said Alea. *Ice. It's going to let go of all of it.*

"*No*," said Sabe. On her pole. Hoarse. "*No — no, not all, not ALL—*"

"It's all going," said Maigo. From the cliff. Eyes open now, wide, and green. "The palm. The heel. It's all—"

The jam groaned along its whole length.

And Ice did what he had always done.

He didn't decide. He'd never decided. He'd never in his life decided to take all of something. It was just what his hands did. On the Grey, on the pass, on a white floor in a white room. Something was letting go of everything, and there was nobody there but him, and so.

So he took it.

All of it.

He reached down through Rachael's hand and through the ice and through the light of Sanctuary into the heart under him with Borne, and he took hold of everything it was letting go of. The whole river's winter. The last third of the jam. The lake. The weight of a hand on a cliff. He took it all into himself so the heart wouldn't have to put itself down, so it could stop, so it could hold on, so nothing would go.

*Fifty*, said Alea. *Forty-two. Ice — Ice, thirty-five—*

He didn't say them.

He couldn't. He couldn't find his mouth. It was too cold. It came into him all at once through Rachael's hand, so fast she cried out and nearly let go, and it filled his chest and went past his chest, and the gold came up the threads to take it, and couldn't. There was too much. It was coming in faster than thirty-five threads could take it out. He was holding it. On his own.

*Thirty-one.*

"*DEC*," said Rachael. "*DEC — let go of it — you're—*"

*Twenty-nine.*

He heard Gabe say something. Not down the jam. Right beside him. He couldn't hear what.

Then he couldn't hear anything.

***

It was dark.

He knew this dark. He'd been in it fourteen days ago. Under the ice. With Calvin's wrist somewhere and the hole lost. Black, and cold below the bottom of cold, and no up. No way to tell which way was up. Just the weight of the water on every side, pressing.

Only this time it wasn't water.

It was the river's winter. It was all of it. It was every spring the heart had ever woken into. He was inside the holding, and the holding was everything, and there was nothing else, and he was holding it, the way he'd held everything his whole life, and it was so heavy, and it was so dark, and he was so tired.

And in the dark, below him, a light.

Big. Old. Rising.

It was going up past him. The heart's light. Slow, enormous, like a moon coming up out of black water, going up past him in the dark toward wherever lights went when they let go of everything. And he saw it. Close. He saw it the way Yan must see them. Not a thing in the ice. A light. Very old, and very tired, and frightened, and going.

*Don't*, he thought at it.

It went on rising.

*Don't. I've got it. I've got all of it. You don't have to. I'll hold it. I'll hold all of it. You can stop.*

And the light went on rising, and he understood, in the dark, with the whole weight of the river on him, why it wasn't stopping.

Because he was showing it the wrong thing.

It had watched him all afternoon. A man who held a bit, and let a bit go, thirty-five ways. And then, at the end, at the very end, when it mattered, it had watched the man do exactly what it did. Take everything. On his own. All at once. Because that was what you did, when it mattered. When it was everything. You held all of it, alone, until you couldn't, and then.

He was telling it to stop with his whole body doing the thing it was doing.

*Oh*, he thought.

*Oh. That's what it looks like.*

He'd never seen it from outside. He'd never once in two lives watched somebody hold everything on their own and seen what it looked like from where Thea stood. From where Gabe stood, in the shape on the floor of the furnace hall, after. From the door of the Long Barn, with a lantern.

It looked like somebody going.

*Twenty-six*, said Alea. Very far away. Very small. *Ice. Please.*

He couldn't let go of it. He didn't know how. He'd never let go of anything a bit at a time in his life. He'd only ever held, or dropped.

But he knew how to ask.

He'd learned that. Late. On fourteen, on the Grey, alone, at fifteen percent, when he'd finally, finally asked. Two years of them carefully not looking at him, and he'd asked, and they'd come. All of them. Through Borne. *What you cannot carry, they will.*

He didn't have a mouth. He didn't need one. Borne wasn't a thing you said.

*I can't hold this*, he said. Down the threads. All thirty-five of them. In the dark. *It's too much. I took all of it. I'm sorry. I can't. Help.*

They came.

Not lightly. Not a thirty-fifth each, spread thin by Azac like water poured across a table. They came up the threads all at once, all thirty-five of them, the way the line on the Grey had come, and took hold. Not of the weight. Of *him*. He felt Gabe's hand in his collar on the ice, somewhere very far up, and Rachael's on his wrist, and then the gold, Deston's gold, coming up thirty-five threads so bright he could see it in the dark, a net of it under him, and every thread with a person on the end of it pulling. Hako, planted. Chi, small and stubborn. Calvin. Dennis, shouting something. Xola, furious, pulling; he could feel her teeth gritted. Gigi. Hana, saying a word he couldn't hear. Higu, writing. Every one. Every single one of them.

*Where they stand*, he thought, *so are you.*

The weight went out of him.

Not all of it. Most of it. Into them. Thirty-five ways. Not thin. Heavy. He felt them take it. He felt them stagger, at the foot of the jam, thirty people all at once going down on one knee on the rock with the cold of a whole river's winter in their chests, and get up again. And hold.

And the light that was rising past him, in the dark, stopped.

It hung there. Huge. Close. He could have touched it.

It had watched him do it.

It had watched him take everything on his own and start to go. And then it had watched him ask. And it had watched thirty-five people come for him all at once and take the weight off him, and hold him, and not let him go anywhere.

He looked at it in the dark. It looked at him.

*Not all of it*, he said to it. Quietly. He didn't have a mouth. It didn't matter. *Not yourself. You don't have to let go of you. Just a bit. That's all. A bit. And somebody holds that. And then the next bit.*

*I didn't know either*, he said. *I've only just learned. Today. Just now. You saw.*

*You saw what it looks like.*

The light hung there.

Then, very slowly, it began to go back down.

***

"*I'm here*," said Niko.

He heard it. In the dark, very far off, and very close. Niko's flat voice. Not shouted. Niko never shouted. Just said, plainly, the way you'd tell somebody where you'd put their keys. *I'm here.*

And the line answered.

A line of light in the dark, thin and bright, as long as a forearm. Humming. Right there. Beside him. The cut Niko had left on the ice beside his knee, that had kept, the way his cuts kept, waiting. *Wait for me.* Called home. Answering.

And past it, beyond it, leaning toward him through the black, the colour of a lantern.

He went to it.

He didn't swim. There was nothing to swim in. He just went, toward the line and the amber beyond it, the way you'd go toward a door in a dark house when somebody opened it from the other side and stood there with a lamp. The way he'd gone toward a *tock, tock, tock* under the ice fourteen days ago with Calvin's wrist in his hand.

He came up.

***

He was on his back on the ice.

The sky was in the gap between the black cliffs, high up, going gold at the edges. Gabe was over him. Gabe's hand was in his collar, both hands, and Gabe's face was white, and Gabe was saying *Dec, Dec, Dec* over and over without any space between them. Hearthwall was on his chest. Warm. The only warm thing in the world.

Rachael was on his other side. She had both hands round his wrist. Her arms were white to the shoulder. Her face was grey. She wasn't crying. Rachael didn't cry.

*Twenty-six*, said Alea. Shaking. *Twenty-six, Ice. Twenty-six. Say it.*

He found his mouth.

"It's stopped," said Yan.

Not shouting now. From the ledge, high up, cool and level again; it carried down the cliff like a stone dropped. But Ice heard what was under it. He'd never heard that under Yan's voice before. He didn't think anybody had.

"It's going back down. Into the middle. Where it was." A pause. "It's not going anywhere."

Ice turned his head on the ice.

The eye was right there. Beside his face. Through the clear black pan, an arm's length down, round and dark and old.

The light in it had gone back down to the bottom. Not all the way. Not where it had been in the morning, pulled in tight and small. Somewhere in the middle. Steady.

It was looking at him.

He looked back.

"Hello," he said again. Lying on his back on the ice with Gabe's hands in his collar.

And the heart went *once*.

Slow.

Slower than it had gone all day. Slower than it had gone since the first word. A long, slow, deep *once*, that came up through the ice into his back like somebody breathing out after holding their breath for a very long time.

"*Now*," said Sabe. Very quietly. From her pole. As if she couldn't believe it either.

And it let go.

***

A bit.

The palm. Maigo said so, from the cliff, with his eyes shut again and his face wet. "The palm. Not the heel. Just the palm." And the part of the jam the palm had been holding groaned, and shifted, and went over the edge into the flood, slow, and through the door. And the lake dropped a hand.

And the heart didn't grip harder.

That was the thing. That was the thing that made Sabe sit down on the ice with her pole across her knees. For the first time in all the springs there had ever been, the heart let go of a bit, and didn't grip harder with what was left. It just held what was left. The way you'd hold it. Steady. Not all or nothing.

It came into Ice through Rachael's hand, and it was a bit, and it was light. He barely needed the threads. He took it and passed it on, thirty-five ways, and Azac spread it, and it was gone.

*Thirty-eight*, said Alea.

"Thirty-eight." Mend Self. "Forty-one. It hurts."

"*FORTY-ONE*," said Gabe. Down the jam. Almost laughing now.

"Now," said Tuli. On her pole. Her voice cracked in the middle.

And the heel of the hand let go.

***

It took the rest of the afternoon. It took until the sun was off the jam and only on the tops of the cliffs. A bit at a time. Sabe and Tuli on their poles, *not yet, not yet, now*, and Maigo in the cliff saying what went, and Rachael carrying it, and Ice taking it and passing it on, and the numbers coming back up, slowly, and Gabe calling them down the jam, and every time a piece of the island going over the edge into the flood and through the door.

The lake ran out behind them.

Not all at once. Not a wall. Steady. A long steady pour through the Narrows, all afternoon, the flood running fast and high through the gap and the water against the cliffs up the valley going down and down, leaving a dark line on the rock where it had been, a line the height of three houses. He felt the thirty at the foot of the jam watch it go. He felt, very far off, through no thread at all, three hundred people on a raft in a reed-bay half a mile upstream feel the Float settle as the water went out from under it. Gently. Onto the reeds.

The last of the island went.

Until it was just the pan. The clear black pan, as wide as a room, in a slack eddy at the foot of the left cliff, with the flood running past it, and the four of them on it, and Sabe and Tuli, and Burr's rope going from the pan to a spur of rock on the shore where Tiger was holding it with both hands.

And under the pan, the heart. And one arm. Just the fingertips of it, now, in a crack in the cliff, holding nothing.

"Last," said Maigo. Very quietly.

Sabe didn't say *now*. She didn't need to. Nobody did.

The fingertips let go of the cliff.

The arm came out of the crack. Slowly. Pale and glistening, as thick as a tree, out of the rock, the way a hand comes off a bedpost at the end of a long bad night when the light comes in the window. It came back through the water toward the heart and folded against it, beside the four slack ones, and was still.

*Once*, went the heart. Slow.

It uncurled.

A little. Not all the way. The way a sleeper does, when they stop being frightened, and turn over, and their knees come down from their chest. The pale curled shape under the ice eased. Opened. And the eye, the round dark old eye, looked up at him through the clear black one more time.

The light in it was low and steady. Like a lamp somebody's turned down for the night.

Then it closed.

And the heart sank.

Slowly. Down. Away from the underside of the pan, down into the black water, the pale shape going smaller and darker and further, the five arms trailing, down and down into the deep of the Mother, until it was only a paleness at the bottom of the river, a long way down. And then not even that.

The ice under them went dark. Ordinary. Just ice, on a river, in spring.

Nobody said anything for a long time.

Then Sabe put her pole down on the pan. *Tock.* And tipped her head. And listened. A long time. Down through the ice and the water, to the bottom.

"*Once*," said Sabe. Very quietly.

They waited.

Ice lay on the ice and counted his own heart while he waited. He got to forty.

"*Once*," said Sabe.

She lifted the pole. She looked at Tuli. Tuli was crying, finally, without any noise, with her mother's pole held against her chest in both arms like something alive.

"Asleep," said Sabe. "Till next spring."
