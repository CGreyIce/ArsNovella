Azac had been the tank for two years, and he'd never once felt like it was his.

That was the secret. He'd never told anyone, because there was nobody to tell it to without sounding stupid. In every game he'd played with the Singapore lot since he'd first turned up in their voice calls, the front had belonged to Declan. Declan with his shield up and his mouth running, standing in the doorway of every dungeon. *Okay, I'll go first, if I die it's a skill issue, trust.* Declan walking into the first room to see what it did. Then Declan had moved to Malacca, and logged on less, and then hardly at all, and one night the group had been short a tank. Azac had said *I'll do it*, because he was the biggest, and somebody had to.

He'd done it every night since. He was good at it. He knew he was good at it. He just always felt like he was keeping the seat warm.

And now Declan was out there somewhere above them, doing it alone. Standing in the doorway of a whole world, with nobody behind him.

"*And so our hero,*" said Azac to the dawn, "*rose on the morning of the fifth day, and was afraid, and got up anyway.*"

"Is that you being afraid?" said Calvin, from the floor.

"It's literary, Calvin. It's a device."

"It sounded like being afraid."

"*Our hero declined to comment,*" said Azac, and went to find his boots.

***

They went up the valley in the early morning, the five of them, with the sun low on its rail behind them and the cropped grass of the Fold silver with dew. They'd spent the fourth night planning it round the long table at the Spoon, with Mother Crane pretending not to listen and putting extra bread in front of them every time somebody said *ram*.

Deston's plan was simple, because Deston said simple plans broke less.

"Azac holds it. Rae keeps Azac alive. I keep everyone going. Calvin hits it whenever it's stuck. And Dennis—"

"Dennis is the star," said Dennis.

"Dennis puts something in front of a stone whenever it charges, so it hits the stone instead of us."

"So Dennis is the star."

"Dennis is very important," said Deston diplomatically, and Dennis looked satisfied.

They stopped at the last wall above the Fold, the one Calvin had stood on. Below them, in the middle of the hollow, the Bellwether lay asleep on the cropped grass, as big as a horse and as grey as a thundercloud, with its triple-curled horns and its cracked bronze bell. The seven standing stones stood round it in a ring, one of them split from root to crown.

[ The Bellwether · Lv 6 · Floor Guardian ]

They were all level five. Every one of them. Rachael had looked at the numbers last night and said, flatly, *that's not enough*, and nobody had argued.

"Last chance to go home," said Azac.

"There isn't a home," said Rachael. "There's a tower."

"*Last chance to go back to the porridge,*" Azac amended.

Nobody went back to the porridge.

Azac climbed over the wall, walked down into the Fold with his sword drawn and his round wooden shield on his arm, and stopped ten metres from the sleeping ram. Then he did the thing he'd seen Declan do in a hundred dungeons and had always secretly thought was the coolest thing a person could do. He put the flat of his sword against the rim of his shield, and hit it.

*Clang.*

"*Wake up,*" he said. "*Our hero would like a word.*"

The Bellwether opened its yellow eyes.

***

The first thing Azac learned was that the ram was much, much bigger standing up.

It came up off the grass front end first, heaving, and kept coming up, until it was taller at the shoulder than he was, and its bell swung and rang. It was a deep, cracked, enormous sound that went round the Fold and off the stones and out over the valley. From somewhere up on the slopes, something bleated back.

"Woolbacks," said Calvin. "North-east. Two."

"Got it," said Deston. "Calvin, Dennis, take them. Azac—"

"*Our hero has this one,*" said Azac, and used Taunt.

He felt it go out of him like a shout he hadn't shouted. The Bellwether's head swung round and its yellow eyes locked onto him with an expression of total, deeply personal offence, and he thought: *oh, there it is*. That was the bit Declan always talked about. The bit where it looks at you and only you, and you know that everything behind you is safe for as long as you can stand there.

The Bellwether lowered its head and came.

He didn't dodge. He was a tank; tanks didn't dodge. He set his feet and put his shield up and took it.

It was like being hit by a car. Or what he'd always imagined that would be like, only worse. The ram's head hit the shield and the shield hit his chest and his chest hit nothing at all, because he was in the air. Then he was on his back on the cropped grass four metres away, staring up at the painted sky, with every bone in his body ringing like the bell.

[ HP 49% ]

"*Our hero,*" he wheezed, "*has made a—*"

"Stop narrating and get *up*," said Rachael, and her cold-water heal went into him like somebody pouring the ocean down his ribs.

He got up.

***

Dennis's first Double went wrong.

He put a second Azac on the grass two metres in front of a standing stone, gleaming slightly too much, exactly as planned. The Bellwether, which had turned round at the end of the Fold for its second charge, looked at it, and looked at the real Azac, and came for the real one.

"It's not working!"

"It's because I'm *taunting* it!" Azac shouted, running. "It wants me! It *only* wants me!"

"Then *stop taunting it!*"

"I can't! It's my *job*!"

He went sideways at the last second, not a dodge so much as a dive, and the ram's horn caught the very edge of his shield, spun him round and sat him down. It went past him and thundered off across the hollow, shaking its head.

"Okay," said Deston. He'd stopped at the edge of the Fold with his staff planted, and he had the face on: the thinking one, still, very quiet, while everything around him went mad. "Okay. Azac. Next time it comes, let your Taunt drop. Right at the last second. Let it go *off* you. Dennis, put the double behind you, in front of the stone. When it loses you, it'll look for the next thing. Give it the next thing."

"That's insane," said Dennis.

"That's the plan."

"No, I like it, I'm just saying it's insane."

Up on the slope, the bell rang again. Two more Woolbacks came over the north-east wall. Calvin was already on them, low and fast, dagger going under the jaw before they knew he was there. But there'd been two, and then two more, and now Azac could see two *more* on the far slope, already coming. And he thought about it, the way you think about something in the half-second you've got between one thing and the next.

The bell rang. Woolbacks came.

The bell rang. More Woolbacks came.

"*The bell!*" Calvin shouted, from the middle of the Woolbacks, at the same moment Azac thought it. "Dest! Every time it rings, more come! It's *calling* them!"

"Then shut it up," said Rachael.

"How?"

"I don't know, Calvin, you've got knives, *cut it off*."

Calvin looked at the Bellwether. At its neck. At the broad black leather strap the bell hung from.

Azac saw his face change.

"Oh," said Calvin. "*Oh.*"

***

The third charge, they got right.

Azac waited for it, shield up, feet set, Taunt hooked in the ram's head like a fishing line. It came across the Fold at him in a straight, thundering line, getting bigger and bigger and bigger. And at three metres, which was far too close and which he'd never forget for the rest of his life, he let go.

He just stopped holding it. The line went slack.

The Bellwether lost him.

He felt it happen. Its head came up a fraction, like a dog's when it hears its name from somewhere else. And there, two metres behind Azac, right in front of the split standing stone, was a second Azac. Gleaming slightly too much. Standing exactly where Dennis had put him, sword up, shield up, looking extremely heroic.

The ram corrected. It went for the one it could see.

Azac threw himself left and hit the grass, and the Bellwether went over the place where he'd been and straight through the fake Azac like a horse through a bedsheet. Head first, at full speed, it went into the split stone.

The *crack* was enormous. The stone shuddered from root to crown. The Bellwether stood there against it with its legs splayed and its eyes crossed.

[ The Bellwether · Stunned ]

"*Go!*" Deston shouted. "Calvin, *go!*"

Calvin went. Azac had known Calvin was fast. He hadn't known he was *that* fast. He came off the far side of the Fold like something fired out of a gun, across the grass in four strides, and up the ram's side, one foot on its shoulder. His dagger was already at the strap, sawing, sawing, the leather old and thick and stiff—

"*Two seconds!*" said Deston.

The strap gave.

Not all the way. Halfway. The bell lurched on its neck and hung crooked. The Bellwether came out of its daze, shook its head, and threw Calvin off its shoulder like a dog throwing off water. He landed rolling, came up on his feet, and grinned like a maniac.

"*Halfway!*" he shouted. "I got it halfway!"

The bell rang. It sounded different now: flat and wrong, like a bell with something stuck in it. Up on the slopes, the Woolbacks that were coming slowed down. Some of them stopped. One of them turned round and went the other way.

"It's working," said Rachael. "Calvin, it's working, do it again."

***

It took two more charges. Two more times Azac stood in front of the thing and held it on his line and let go at three metres. Two more times Dennis put a gleaming fake in front of the split stone, his face screwed up so tight his nose went white. The second time the fake was only half there, a flicker, and Azac thought *it's not going to work*, and the ram went for it anyway.

On the second of them, Calvin got the strap the rest of the way.

The bell fell off.

It hit the grass and rolled, and didn't ring. And the Bellwether screamed.

Azac had never heard anything like it. It wasn't an animal sound. It was too high and too big and too angry, and it went through his chest like the charge had. All seven stones seemed to hum. Every Woolback on every slope round the Fold turned and went away into the gorse as if somebody had called them home.

"Okay," said Deston, into the silence afterwards. "Okay. That's—"

The Bellwether turned its head and looked at Calvin.

Not at Azac. Not at the line Azac had hooked into it. At Calvin, who'd taken its bell. Calvin was standing on his own in the open grass at the edge of the Fold, three metres from the nearest stone, with a dagger in each hand and the grin still on his face. As Azac watched, the grin went.

The ram came.

Calvin went left, the way you went left. The way they'd been going left all morning.

The Bellwether went left after him.

It *curved*. Azac saw it happen and didn't believe it. The thing was the size of a horse and moving at the speed of a car, and it bent round in the middle of its charge like a dog going round a corner. It came round after Calvin with its head down and its horns level, and Calvin had nowhere left to go.

Azac didn't think about it.

He didn't use Taunt. There wasn't time. He just ran. He ran the way he'd never run in his life, faster than a big man should be able to run, across the corner of the Fold, and put himself between the ram and Calvin with his shield up. And in the half-second before it hit him, he thought, very clearly: *oh, this is what it's for*.

This was why you stood in front. Not to be brave. Not to keep the seat warm. Just so that the thing coming hit you instead of them.

It hit him.

***

He was on his back. He knew that. The painted sky was going round very slowly, like a ceiling fan. His shield was somewhere. His arm was somewhere. There was a sound in his ears like the bell, still ringing, though the bell was lying on the grass and would never ring again.

[ HP 3% ]

Somebody was shouting. A lot of people were shouting.

And then there was cold water. A great rush of it, pouring into him, down into his chest, finding the places that were broken and holding them very still. Behind it, under it, there was something else, something he'd never felt before: warm, gold, running in along his arm like a thread being drawn through cloth.

He turned his head. Rachael was kneeling beside him, grey-faced, both hands flat on his chest, her eyes shut. And from Rachael's back to Deston's hands, standing over her, ran a thin, bright line of gold. Then another, from Deston to Azac. And another, very faint, from Deston to Dennis, who was standing a few metres away with both hands out, his face white, holding something.

Deston was giving them his mana. Pouring what he had left down the gold threads, into Rachael, into all of them.

"*Dest,*" said Rachael, not opening her eyes. "*What* is that?"

"I don't know," said Deston. His voice was shaking. "It's mine. I think it's mine. Don't stop."

She didn't stop.

[ HP 31% ]

And past them, past the gold threads and Rachael's grey face and Deston's shaking hands, Azac saw the Bellwether. It was across the Fold, against the cracked stone, with its legs splayed and its eyes crossed. Dennis was holding it there. *Holding* it. He'd put a fake Calvin in front of that stone a heartbeat before the ram went round again, and it had gone straight through, and hit, and Dennis had kept the fake Calvin standing there. Still gleaming. Still waving its daggers. And every time the ram started to come out of its stun, it saw the fake Calvin right in front of it and tried to hit him again, and hit the stone again.

"*Somebody,*" said Dennis, through his teeth, "*please kill this thing, I'm going to pass out.*"

Calvin was already going.

Azac got up.

He didn't know how. He just did. He picked up his sword from the grass where it had fallen and walked across the Fold on legs that weren't sure about him, toward the Bellwether swaying against the stone. Calvin was already there, on its far side, stabbing at its flank. The fake Calvin flickered and held.

Azac put the point of his sword under the ram's jaw, where the wool was thin.

He'd learned that from Calvin, three days ago, lying under a dead sheep.

He pushed.

***

It lay down like it was going to sleep.

That was the thing he'd remember. Not the fight. Not the bell or the scream or the hit. The way the Bellwether folded its legs under it, front end first, and lay down on the cropped grass of the Fold as if it were very tired and had been waiting a long time to rest. It breathed out once, a long sigh, and the light went out of it, quietly, like a lamp going out in another room.

The five of them stood round it in a ring and didn't say anything.

Then the window came.

It wasn't the gold-edged window. It didn't go out across the valley to everybody. It was small and plain and just for them. And it said *first party*, not *first clear*, and Azac read it twice and understood. Somebody else had the *first clear*. Somebody else would always have it.

He found he didn't mind.

Dennis sat down on the grass, very suddenly, and put his head between his knees. Rachael sat down next to him, a polite ten centimetres away, grey and shaking and smiling at nothing. Calvin was lying flat on his back with his arms out, laughing, the same stupid bursting laugh he'd done on the first morning. Deston stood in the middle of the Fold with his staff planted and his eyes closed, and the last faint gold threads faded slowly from his hands.

Azac looked down at the Bellwether.

*It hit me instead of him*, he thought. *That's all it's for. That's all it ever was.*

He wondered if Declan had figured that out on his first day. Standing here in the dark on his own, with nobody behind him. Nobody for it to be *for*.

He thought he probably had. He thought that was probably why he'd gone.

***

The door in the hill was open.

They went in together, the five of them, into a round stone room with a cold fire-pit, a bench round the wall, and a stair at the back going up into the dark. A small window opened as they crossed the threshold: *Ascension Room · Safe*. Dennis said *oh thank God* and lay down on the bench.

There were ashes in the fire-pit. Fresh ones. Somebody had lit a fire here, a few nights ago, and let it burn down.

Nobody said anything about it. Everybody looked at it.

Then Deston went to the foot of the stair and looked up, and Azac came and stood beside him.

"We'll go up," said Deston. "Have a look. Come back down and tell the others. We're not staying."

"*Our heroes went up,*" said Azac, "*to have a look.*"

"Is there a lift?" said Calvin, behind them.

"Calvin."

"I'm just asking. It's a tower. Towers have lifts."

Azac put his foot on the first step.

"One," he said.

He didn't know why he did it. It just came out. And then he couldn't stop. "Two. Three. Four." And somewhere around twenty, Calvin started counting with him, and then Dennis, from the back, grumbling, and then Rachael, and then Deston. The five of them went up the dark stair inside the hill in single file, counting out loud, their voices bouncing off the stone, like kids on a school trip.

"*A hundred and ten.*"

"*A hundred and eleven.*"

There was a door at the top. Plain. Wooden. Light coming round the edges of it.

"A hundred and twelve," said Azac, with his hand on it. He frowned. "Huh. Weird number."

He opened the door.

Wind came through it. Real wind, cold and clean, smelling of pine and snow. And beyond the door, under a different painted sky, there was a different world.

Floor 2.

Somewhere out there, Azac thought, looking out at it, was the other tank.

"*And so,*" he said softly, "*our heroes stepped out onto the second floor of the Spire. And somewhere ahead of them, a long way off, someone had already been standing in the doorway for four days.*"

"That one was good," said Calvin.

"Thanks, mate."

They stepped out.
