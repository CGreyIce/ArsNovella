Calvin had read about a thousand transmigration novels, and in every single one the scout died early.

He'd known it since the top of the valley on floor 1, lying on his back under a sheep with his arm going the wrong way. He'd thought, very calmly: *oh, I'm the scout guy*. And then Rachael had come over the wall with her staff, and he hadn't died. But the thought had stayed. It sat in the back of his head like a stone in a shoe. Every time he ran ahead to look at something, he felt it. Every time he said *I'll go first*.

*The scout guy goes first, and something's waiting, and that's how the main character learns to be careful.*

And now there was a card pinned by the door of the smoking hall in Reedmoor. *Be careful. Train longer on the 3rd floor.* Every time Calvin walked past it, he thought: *that's for me*. He didn't know why. He just knew. Somebody who knew what was up there had written *be careful*. And the person in the whole of the Spire most likely to not be careful was Calvin.

So he trained.

***

The trials opened on the twenty-fourth day, for everyone, all at once. They'd all been at ten for days, banking, waiting. Chris's post in World had made sure nobody had stopped killing things in the meantime. Then one morning in Reedmoor, at the long tables in the smoking hall, thirty-five people opened their windows. And thirty-five sets of three windows came up, side by side, easy and normal and hard.

Calvin had never seen a room go so quiet so fast.

Everyone's were different. That was the first thing. They compared them across the tables, leaning over each other, reading. The Knights all had something about holding: a bridge, a gate, a door. The Clerics had something about keeping somebody alive. The Mages had something about understanding a spell well enough to unmake it. And the Rangers, Calvin's lot, had something about getting somewhere first.

*Easy: Cross the Fen to the eel-traps and back before the bell.*

*Normal: Cross the Fen at dusk and bring back a mireling's tooth.*

*Hard: Go ahead of a party through the wisps at night, and bring all of them back.*

Calvin read the hard one four times.

*Go ahead of a party. Bring all of them back.*

He looked up. Across the table, Rachael was reading her own windows with her mouth in a thin line. She looked up and caught him looking, and saw his face, and her eyes narrowed.

"No," she said.

"I didn't say anything."

"You've got the face. The top-of-the-valley face." She put her spoon down. "Which one is it?"

He tipped his window so she could see. She read it. She was quiet for a long moment.

"Bring all of them back," she said.

"Yeah."

"Not *go ahead*. Not *scout*. *Bring them back*." She looked at him. "That's different, Cal."

"Yeah," said Calvin. "I know. That's why I want it."

***

He didn't do it the first week. He wasn't ready. Nobody was. They'd voted to train, so they trained.

People did their trials one at a time, in the first week, and came back different.

Gabe did the hard one. Of course he did. He came back two days later soaking wet and limping, with the wolf coat torn at the hem and a look on his face Calvin had never seen. Grim, satisfied, a man who'd held something for a very long time and not let go. His window said *Tier 2: Sworn · Guardian*. He didn't talk about what he'd held. He just went to the little forge the eel-woman's brother kept at the end of the village and started hammering. He didn't stop until midnight.

Thea did the hard one. She came back with a nosebleed and a very quiet expression, and her window said *Tier 2: Adept*. Hyvred said afterwards that there were lines of light all over the walkway where she'd walked. Like writing. Like code. Thea didn't explain.

Rachael did the hard one. Calvin hadn't known she would. She hadn't told anybody. She just went out one evening with her staff, and was gone all night, and came back at dawn grey to the lips and so cold her hands shook. Her window said *Tier 2: Mender*. When Calvin asked what she'd had to do, she looked at him for a long time and said, "Keep somebody alive who'd decided not to be." She wouldn't say any more than that.

Deston did normal. Xav did normal: *I'll do the hard one when it's the right move*. Chi did normal and came back very calm. Hana did the hard one and came back with a new skill she called *Glamour* and refused to demonstrate. Maigo did the easy one, on purpose, because, he said, a tree should know when to rest. Yan did the hard one and came back with his hands white to the elbow. Maigo sat with him for an hour with his root-foot in the black mud under the walkway, giving it away, until they were warm again.

Every morning, at the long table, Chris read out the list. Who'd done it. Who hadn't. What they'd come back with. He drew it on Drew's slate in a column, and they ticked it off.

Calvin's name was the last one without a tick.

***

He did it on the twelfth night on floor 3.

He'd asked them himself. Deston, Rachael, Dennis, Azac. His party. He'd sat them down on the walkway at dusk and showed them the window and said: *I need you to come with me. All of you. Through the wisps. At night. And I need to bring you back.*

Nobody said anything for a while.

"That's the one where you go first," said Azac finally. "Our hero, alone, ahead of the party."

"It's the one where I bring you back."

"It's the one where you *go first*, mate."

"It's both," said Calvin. "That's the point. That's the whole point." He looked at them. "I'm the scout. I'm always going to be the one in front. I can't help it, it's how I'm built. But the scout guy always dies, in every book. Every single one. Because he goes first on his own, and he's fast, and nobody can keep up." He swallowed. "I don't want to be the scout guy. I want to be the scout who brings everybody home."

Rachael was looking at him very steadily.

"Okay," she said.

"Okay?"

"Okay. We'll come." She stood up. "But if you step off the path, Calvin, I'm hitting you with my staff again."

"That's fair."

"It's not fair. It's just what's going to happen."

***

The wisps came out at dusk, the way they always did. Quieter, since the night the lights went out. They kept further off over the black water and came closer more slowly. But they came.

Calvin went first.

He walked out along the reed path with his daggers in his belt and his party behind him in a line: Deston, Rachael, Dennis, Azac at the back. And the wisps drifted in from the water on both sides, pale and soft, and started to change.

His was a front door.

He knew it straight away. Home, with the light on over the door: a single bulb, with moths round it. The one that was always on when he came back late, no matter how late. It hovered over the black water to the left of the path and said *home*. And his feet wanted to go.

He didn't let them. He looked at it for exactly as long as he had to, and then away, and kept walking. And then he did the thing he'd worked out over twelve days of thinking about it.

He talked.

Not to the wisps. To the people behind him.

"Dest. Yours is on the right. Don't look at it. Look at my back." He didn't turn round. "Rae. There's one coming from the left, low. Eyes on me. Dennis, you're fine, you're doing great, keep going. Azac—"

"*And our hero,*" said Azac, from the back, in a voice that was slightly too high, "*looked at nothing but the back of the man in front.*"

"Good. Do that."

He kept talking. He talked the whole way. Along the reed path, across the planks over the channel, past the drowned trees, all the way to the far tussock and the eel-traps and back. Every few steps, he called out where the wisps were and who they were coming for. *Eyes on me. Look at my back. I'm right here. Follow me.* He didn't stop. Not once. His throat went raw. And behind him, in a line, his party didn't look at the lights. They looked at him.

And somewhere round the halfway point, he felt the wind come.

Not on him. *Behind* him. A faint push of moving air, coming off his back as he walked, like the wake behind a boat. It went back along the line to Deston, to Rachael, to Dennis, to Azac. He felt them feel it. Felt their steps quicken, just slightly, without them meaning to. It was easier to walk in his wake than out of it. Easier to follow than to stop.

He'd been fast since the first day. He'd felt the wind behind his legs on floor 1, the morning he ran up the valley on his own. He'd always thought it was for him.

It wasn't. It had never been. It was for whoever was behind him.

[ Unique skill: Slipstream
Go first. Everyone behind you goes faster. Nobody gets left.
]

He read it walking, without stopping, with the wisps drifting pale on both sides and his party in a line at his back.

"*Rae*," he said. His voice cracked. "Rae, I got a—"

"Don't stop walking," said Rachael.

"I'm not stopping."

"Then tell me at the end."

He told her at the end. On the walkway at Reedmoor, at dawn, all five of them, soaked to the knees and exhausted and *back*. He told her, and showed her the window, and she read it. She didn't say anything for a long time.

"*Nobody gets left*," she said finally.

"Yeah."

She looked at him. She didn't touch him; Rachael never did. But she looked at him for a long moment the way she'd looked at him at the top of the valley, kneeling over his arm. Then she nodded once, and turned round, and went inside to sleep.

[ Tier trial passed · Hard ]

[ Calvin · Lv 15 · Tier 2: Tracker · Rogue ]

Chris ticked the last name off the slate that morning, and everybody in the smoking hall cheered. Calvin hadn't known they were going to. He went red to the ears.

***

They went to the Black Mere four days later, all thirty-five, because Drew said they should see the stair before they argued any more about it.

Calvin would remember that fight for a long time. Not because it was hard, though it was. Because of what Hyvred did.

The Lanternjaw lived in a round black pool at the foot of a green hill. All it ever showed was its lure: a warm yellow light on a stalk, bobbing over the water, that looked like home. Half of them couldn't look at it. Calvin couldn't. It was the light over his own front door again, but realer, closer, like the door was right there and he only had to walk across the water to open it.

Hyvred looked at it and laughed out loud.

"It's a *bulb*," she said. "Guys. It's a wet grey stalk with a glowing bulb on the end of it, growing out of the head of a giant frog. Don't look at it. *Look at me.*" She put an arrow on the string, her left eye locking. "I'll tell you where the frog is."

They looked at Hyvred. Hyvred told them where the frog was. And when it put the lights out, the whole pool going black as the inside of a closed eye, Chi's voice came out of the dark, slow and calm: *it's okay. It's just dark.* John's little lights came on one by one under the water, like windows, showing them the shape of the thing coming up from below. And Drew called it, every second, from the hill. Thirty-five people in the pitch dark doing exactly what they were told.

When it was over, the door at the top of the green hill ground open. They went up to look at it. Just look, they agreed. Nobody was going up.

There was a lantern on the bench inside.

It was small, glass and dark iron, sitting on the stone bench by the door, lit. Its light was soft and warm and made everything round it look slightly more like itself. Nobody touched it for a long time. They just stood in the doorway of the ascension room in a crowd, looking at it.

"It's his," said Hyvred, very quietly. "It's all over the handle. The amber."

"He left it," said Gabe.

"For whoever came next," said Xav.

Thea went forward. She didn't pick it up. She crouched in front of the bench and read its window, and Calvin watched her read the words under the words, the way she did.

[ Lure-glass Lantern (Epic) · a lantern that never goes out
While you carry it, lights that lie look like what they are.
]

"*Lights that lie,*" said Thea. Her voice did something. "The wisps. And the lure. While you carry it, you can see what they are." She stayed crouched there. "He left it so we could get through the Fen."

"Then you carry it," said Hana.

Thea looked round.

"You read windows," said Hana. "You see what things really are. You should carry the thing that makes lights tell the truth." She tilted her head, her one visible eye very soft. "Besides. I think he'd want you to."

Calvin didn't know what she meant by that. He saw Thea's face, though, for just a second, before she turned back to the bench. Thea reached out and picked up the lantern by its handle. She held it in both hands, and the soft warm light came up into her face, and she didn't say anything at all.

She carried it back to Reedmoor. She carried it everywhere after that.

***

They chose the general on the fortieth day.

Calvin had thought it would be a big thing, a ceremony. It wasn't. It was the end of a long day at the smoking hall. Drew had been at the slate for an hour with Chris, drawing up who was going to hunt where tomorrow. Somebody, Newish, said, quite loudly: "Why don't we just make it official?"

Everybody looked at her.

"The window thing," said Newish. "The *group*. Thea found it. You can make a group, out of parties, and somebody's the general. Somebody's in charge of all of it." She waved her spoon at the slate. "And we've basically got one already. Drew's been doing it for a month. So let's just press the button."

There was a murmur. Calvin looked round. People were nodding.

"It doesn't have to be me," said Drew.

"Who else is it going to be?" said Newish.

"Deston."

Deston, at the end of the table, shook his head. "I had the note," he said. "I held onto it for three weeks. I don't want to hold everyone. I want to hold my party." He looked at Drew. "You've been holding everyone since Larchwick. You built the board."

"Xav, then."

Xav was quiet for a moment. "I wait," he said. "I wait and then I move once. A general can't wait. A general has to see everyone at once, all the time." He looked at Drew. "You do that already."

Drew looked round the long table. Thirty-four faces, looking back at him.

"I ran a tournament from my bedroom once," he said. "Dozens of teams. A long weekend. Nobody ever thanked me for it except one person." He touched the nameplate at his throat, the little *88EXP* on its chain. "He stayed behind in the call to say it. *Nobody ever thanks the guy running it.*"

The table had gone very quiet.

"I'll do it," said Drew. "If you want. But somebody has to thank me sometimes. Or I'll start writing rude things on the slate."

"I'll thank you," said Chris immediately. "Every day. I'll log it."

Drew laughed. He opened his window and found the little line Thea had pointed out weeks ago, under the party menus. *Form a group.* He pressed it, and a new window came up, and thirty-four people got a small window in the corner of their eye at once.

[ Drew has invited your party to a group. Accept? ]

Thirty-four people pressed *yes*. Calvin pressed it so hard he nearly knocked his bowl over.

[ Group formed: The Thirty-Five · General: Drew ]

"*The Thirty-Five*," said Hana, reading it. "Who named it that?"

"I did," said Drew. "Just now." He looked round the table. "It's what we are."

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"It should be thirty-six," said Gabe.

He said it quietly. He was sitting at the end of the table in the wolf coat, which he wore in the evenings now even though it was too hot, looking at the window.

Drew looked at him for a long time.

"Then when he comes back," said Drew, "we'll rename it."

Gabe looked up.

"That's a promise," said Drew. "I'm the general. I can do that." He turned the slate round, and wrote along the very top of it, in big careful capitals, above everything else:

*THE THIRTY-FIVE (FOR NOW)*

***

That night Calvin went out to the end of the walkway, where you could see the amber light on the drowned tower in the middle of the Fen.

He didn't know why. He just wanted to look at it. Somebody had walked out there through the wisps on his own, at night. He'd lit it. He'd put all of the Fen's false lights out at once. And Calvin had done a much smaller version of that walk with four people at his back, talking the whole way, and it had nearly taken everything he had.

*On his own.*

"Hey," said Calvin, to the light. Not loudly. "I'm the scout. In case you didn't know. I'm the one who goes first." He swallowed. "I'm not going to be the scout guy, though. I'm going to bring everybody back. You can stop worrying about me."

The amber light burned in the middle of the black water, small and steady, and didn't answer.

He didn't expect it to. He stood there for a while anyway. He felt the faint push of wind coming off his own back, out over the water, toward the tower, as if it wanted to go there. Then he went inside.
