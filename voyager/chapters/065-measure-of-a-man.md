They carried Thea back to the cistern on a litter of two spears and three cloaks, and laid her down in the hollow where he'd slept.

Nobody planned it. It was just the coolest place. Kei went down first, into the dry stone pit, and turned round at the bottom to take her from the hands coming down. And the only flat place at the bottom of the cistern that wasn't stones or ash was the hollow in the sand. Long, shallow, pressed in by twenty-five nights of somebody lying there. So that was where they put her.

She fitted it. Nearly. She was smaller than the shape in the sand.

Kei knelt beside her and looked at the shoulder.

Xola had closed it. That was the first thing, and it was a good thing. Xola's healing didn't push things shut, the way Rachael's did. It reminded them. *Axolotls regrow their legs*, Xola had said on floor 2, flapping a hand, and it had sounded like a joke until you watched her do it. A wound under Xola's hands remembered how to close, and closed. This one had remembered. The long cut across the top of Thea's left shoulder and down her upper arm was shut. Pink and raw and swollen, but shut.

But it was hot.

Kei put the back of his hand against it. Then against her forehead. Then her neck.

"She's hot," he said.

"It's floor 6," said Xola, from the other side. She was kneeling with her hands still on Thea's arm, water curling round her fingers. Her lavender-and-pink hair was stuck to her face with sweat. "*Everybody's* hot—"

"Not like that." Kei looked at the wound. At the redness round the edges of it, spreading, very slightly, under the skin. "That's not the floor. That's her."

Xola looked at him. Her violet eyes went wide.

"Infection," said Kei quietly. "Glass that wasn't glass. Whatever that thing was made of, some of it went in." He took his bag off his shoulder and opened it. "On floor 1 it'd close and that'd be it. On floor 1 you didn't *get* infections." He was already taking out jars. "This isn't floor 1."

"*Fix it*," said Xola.

"I'm going to."

***

He worked on her through the afternoon, at the bottom of the cistern, in the cool. Measure helped. It always did. It wasn't a skill that did anything you could see. No light, no water, no roots. It just told him *how much*. How much of the bitter root, how much of the salve from Ma Yarrow's recipe on floor 2, how much water, how much salt. *The difference between a poison and a cure is how much.* He'd saved Hako with a drop and a half of the same venom that was killing him. He knew how much mattered.

He measured, and mixed, and Xola held the shoulder, and Thea lay in the hollow in the sand with her eyes half open and didn't say anything.

She'd hardly said anything since the bowl. Not when they lifted her onto the litter. Not when they carried her across the Glass in the heat, with Higu and Drew making shade over her out of cloaks. Not when they lowered her into the cistern. She'd looked up at the slit of a helm, and at a small fox made of light, and then she'd gone somewhere inside herself, and Kei didn't think she'd come back out yet.

Gigi sat at Thea's head the whole time.

Kei noticed. Everybody noticed. Gigi, who was supposed to be lying down herself, who'd been carried half of floor 6 two days ago. She'd climbed down into the cistern before anybody could stop her and sat at the top end of the hollow with her back against the stone wall. And she'd put Thea's head in her lap, and not moved since.

Her silver hair was hanging down over both of them. The little blue flowers she kept in it had been dead since the heat, and she hadn't taken them out.

Kei didn't say anything to her about it. Nobody did. Everybody knew about Thea and Gigi. They'd been close, years ago, the three of them, Thea and Gigi and Xola. And then they hadn't been. Gigi had wanted more nights than Thea could give, when Thea's studies got hard. Thea had stopped staying up. And it had sat between them ever since, in the draft, on floor 1, every floor. Thea passing over Gigi in the white room. Gigi asking Drew on floor 2, carefully, *is she sleeping?*

Gigi had run into a hole in the air this morning saying *Thea, Thea, wait.* And the real Thea had knocked her flat and taken the edge across her own shoulder.

Gigi sat with Thea's head in her lap and didn't say anything at all for hours.

***

Dose spoke when the light in the mouth of the cistern began to go gold.

She'd been quiet all day. Round his neck, small and green and cool, with her head on his collarbone. She hadn't said a word since the hole broke. She'd only tasted the air, over and over, with her tongue. As if there was something left in it she didn't like.

Now she lifted her head.

*She's seen it now*, said Dose. In his head, the way she always spoke. Dry, and old, and faintly rude. *The one with the lantern.*

Kei went still with the salve jar in his hand.

"Seen what?" he said. Quietly. Under his breath.

*What I told you about. In the snow.* The small green head turned toward Thea. *Tell her she's not the only one who sees him. Do you remember?*

"I remember."

*Well,* said Dose. *Now she has seen it too.* And she put her head back down on his collarbone.

"Seen *what*, Dose?"

But the snake had shut her eyes. And Kei knew her well enough by now, a hundred and twenty-three days of her, to know she wasn't going to say anything else. Not today. Maybe not ever.

He looked at Thea. She was looking up at the round mouth of the cistern above her, at the gold sky. Her eyes were very far away.

*Tell her she's not the only one who sees him.*

He thought about a little fox of light, sitting on a white shoulder, looking down at her for one second.

***

When the sun was down and the stars were out over the mouth of the cistern, Thea opened her rankings.

Kei saw her do it. He was sitting beside her, checking the shoulder again. She lifted her good hand, slowly, and her window came up in the dark, small and blue, lighting her face from below.

The rankings. The list of all thirty-six names, in order, with their levels and their floors. Thirty-five of them bunched together in the middle, twenty-seven, twenty-eight, all *Floor 6*. And at the top, alone, the one name.

*Voyager · Lv 36 · Knight · Floor 6.*

She didn't look at the number. She looked under it.

Kei knew what she was doing. Everybody did, by now. *Source.* Every window had words under the words, and she could read them. On floor 3, at the long table in the smoking hall, she'd read the grey line under Voyager's class that nobody else could see, and said *Hidden class: Paladin*, and the whole room had gone still.

Kei watched her read it now. Her eyes going down the line, slowly, the way they did. Under *Voyager*. Under *Knight*. Past *Hidden class: Paladin*.

She stopped.

"There's another one," she said.

Her voice was very small and very hoarse. The first thing she'd said since the bowl.

Gigi looked down at her. Xola looked up. Kei leaned in.

"Another line," said Thea. "Under *Paladin*. It wasn't there on five. I check every night." She was staring at the window. "It's grey. Like the others. But I can't read it."

"What does it say?"

"It doesn't *say* anything." Her eyes moved over it, and moved over it again. "It's—there's a line. There's definitely a line. But where the words should be it's just—" She swallowed. "Blank. Like somebody's drawn a bar across it. A black bar. Like a document where somebody's—"

"Redacted," said Kei.

Thea looked at him.

"Like in the documents," he said. "When there's something they don't want you to read. They black it out."

Thea looked back at the window. At the black bar under *Paladin*, which only she could see.

Then she did something Kei had never seen her do.

She asked her light.

Everybody had one. The soft white balls of light that had risen out of the white floor on the first morning and said *Welcome, Player*. They sat by your shoulder when you called them and answered questions in their calm, kind, certain voice. *How do I learn a skill. What does this window mean. Where is the trainer.* They always knew. They always answered, at once, in the same calm voice. They never hesitated. Never once, in a hundred and twenty-three days, in front of any of them, had a light been asked a question and not known what to say.

Thea's light came up out of nothing beside her head, soft and white, lighting the dark of the cistern.

"The line," said Thea, very quietly. "Under *Paladin*. On Voyager's window. What does it say?"

And her light paused.

Kei saw it. He saw it happen. He was a foot away, and he saw it, and it went through him like cold water.

The light didn't answer. For one second. For two. It just hung there, beside Thea's head, soft and white. And it *flickered*. Very slightly. The way a candle flickers when a door opens somewhere in the house. Three seconds. Four.

Then it spoke. In the same calm, kind, certain voice it had always used. Except it wasn't, quite. It was a little slower than usual. A little careful.

"*That information is not available, Player.*"

And it went out.

Nobody in the cistern moved.

"They don't pause," said Kei. He heard his own voice from a long way off. "Ever. Thea. They've never paused. Not once. Not for anyone. Not for anything."

"I know," said Thea.

"It *paused*. It didn't know what to say."

"It knew," said Thea quietly. She was looking at the place in the dark where her light had been. "It knew exactly what to say. It just didn't want to say it."

She closed the window. The cistern went dark, except for the stars in the round mouth above them, and the lantern. Somebody had brought the lantern down and put it on a stone at the far end of the hollow, and it glowed amber, soft, on all their faces.

***

They talked about it that night. All ten. At the bottom of the cistern and on the lip of it, in the dark, with the stars too many above them and the Glass cooling all round.

Hako started it. Hako, who said everything he was thinking. He said it again, the word, the way he'd said it on the glass. Out loud, so all ten could hear.

"*Regressor*."

And then he looked at Kei.

So Kei explained it. Because somebody had to, and he'd read more of them than anybody except Hako. Sitting on the stone floor of the cistern with his back against the wall and Dose round his neck, and the lantern lighting the side of his face.

"In the stories," he said. "It's a guy who's already done it. All of it. Whatever the story is. The tower, the dungeon, the war. He went in, and it went wrong, and everybody died. And somehow he gets sent back. To the start. Before any of it happened. With everything he remembers."

The cistern was very quiet.

"So he knows," said Kei. "Everything. Where things are. What's going to happen. Which floor's going to be bad. Where the hidden stuff is, because he found it too late the first time. Who's going to die, and where." He was quiet. "And he tries to fix it. All of it. This time. And he never tells anybody, because he can't. Because who'd believe him. Because it'd change things. Because if he says it out loud, maybe it all goes wrong again."

"So he does it alone," said Hako. "In every single one. He goes off on his own and does it alone."

Nobody said anything.

Kei looked round at them. Drew, on the lip of the cistern above, with his arms on his knees, very still. Higu beside him, her long indigo-black hair hanging, her pale lilac eyes on Kei. Chi, sitting very close to Higu. Bee and Hyvred, together, Bee's sketchbook open on her knees. Xola, still with her hands on Thea's arm. Gigi, with Thea's head in her lap.

And Thea, lying in the hollow in the sand, with her eyes open, looking at the lantern.

"The note," said Kei. "*Train longer on the third floor.* Written before we'd seen floor 1. Because he knew floor 4 would be bad. Because it *was*, last time." He swallowed. "The thing on the roof. In the first square. He sat a week behind a wall watching that square before any of us were near it. Because he knew what was going to come down off that roof. Because it did. Last time."

"Mother Tallow," said Hyvred, very quietly. "*A day early.* Before the storm."

"Hessa," said Bee. "*Please stay alive till he comes.*" Her voice cracked. "He looked at her like he'd been to her funeral. Because he *had*."

"The chapel on the first day," said Hako. "Because he found it too late. The first time."

"Floor 5," said Drew.

Everybody looked up at him. He hadn't said anything all evening. He was sitting on the lip of the cistern with his arms on his knees and his face in shadow.

"Floor 5," said Drew slowly. "Two weeks. Two shields. The first floor he ever struggled on." He looked down at them. "If he'd done it before, why struggle?"

"Because it was different," said Kei. "That's the other thing that's always in them. He changes things. He saves people who died. He kills things before they can hurt anybody. And because he changes things, things *change*. They stop being how he remembers." He looked at the black glass of the cistern wall. "And that thing in the bowl. It wasn't how he remembered. I don't think it was there at all, last time. I think it was new. I think he came here thinking he knew what was waiting, and it was something else, and he couldn't get past it. Twenty-five days. In and out."

"Until the lantern," said Higu.

Kei looked at her.

"He couldn't get past it," said Higu, in her calm, slow voice. "Twenty-five days. And he got past it in about ten seconds, today." She looked across the cistern at Thea. "In her light."

The cistern was quiet.

"And *not this time*," said Hako. "That's the bit. That's the one that—" He stopped. "You don't say *not this time* unless there was a time."

"*Not this time*," said Chi, very softly, the way she said everything, slowly, so you'd hear it through anything.

And Kei looked at Thea.

He didn't mean to. His eyes just went there. He'd been watching her all evening, the way you watch a patient, without thinking about it. And so he saw it. Nobody else did, he thought. Maybe Gigi, who was looking down at her face. But Kei saw it.

Thea wasn't surprised.

Every face in that cistern had gone through it tonight. The cold, the disbelief, the sick slow *oh* as it fitted. Drew's face, Hako's, Bee's, Hyvred's. Kei had watched all of them. And Thea, lying in the hollow where he'd slept, with her shoulder burning and the lantern on her face, looked like someone hearing out loud a thing she'd known for a very long time. And had been hoping, all that time, that she was wrong.

Her lips moved. No sound. Just the shape of two words.

*I know.*

***

"It's a theory," said Drew, eventually.

He'd come down into the cistern. He was standing at the far end of the hollow with the lantern at his feet, looking down at all of them. His voice had gone back to the general's voice. Calm. Charismatic. The one that made you listen.

"It's a *story*," he said. "A good one. It fits. I can't see a hole in it." He looked at Kei. "But it's a story. Out of the books you two read. We don't know. We didn't see anything. We saw a man in a helm save Thea, and say three words, and run." He looked round at them. "*That's* what we saw."

Nobody argued with that.

"So," said Drew. "When we get back to Deepwell, everybody hears what we saw. Everybody. Gabe. All thirty-five. He saved Thea. He wore a white helm. He killed the thing with one stroke. He had a fox on his shoulder. He said *not this time*. He ran. That's the facts, and the facts go on the slate. Same day." He paused. "And the rest of it—"

"The club," said Thea.

Everybody looked at her.

Her voice was very hoarse. She was still lying in the hollow. But her eyes had come back. All the way back, from wherever they'd been since the bowl. She was looking at Drew.

"The rest stays with the club," she said. "Chris and Winter. And now all of you, I suppose. Because you were there." She swallowed. "Until we can ask him."

Drew looked at her for a long moment.

"Until you can ask him," he said.

"Until I can ask him," said Thea.

Drew nodded, slowly.

***

Kei sat up with her that night, because the fever went up before it came down.

It was the worst part, always. The middle of the night, when a fever turned. He'd seen it on floor 2, with the frost adder in Hako's blood. He'd seen it on floor 3, in Reedmoor, with an eel-fisher's boy. You sat there, and you measured, and you waited to see which way it went.

Xola fell asleep around two, sitting up against the wall with her hands still on Thea's arm. Hako slept on the lip of the cistern, sprawled, with his half-shield for a pillow. Drew sat on the lip awake, looking at the stars. Higu sat beside him, also awake.

Gigi didn't sleep at all.

She sat at the top of the hollow with Thea's head in her lap, and every so often she wiped Thea's face with a wet cloth Kei gave her, and didn't say anything. Kei measured out the bitter root and the salve and the water, a little at a time, and waited.

Around three, the fever turned.

He felt it go. Under his fingers on Thea's wrist. A loosening. And then the sweat came, all at once, cold and real, and Thea let out a long breath and opened her eyes. Clear.

She looked up at Gigi.

Gigi looked down at her. Gigi's red-brown eyes were wet. She'd been crying, silently, for some time, Kei thought. He looked away. He started packing his jars very slowly and carefully back into his bag, so that he'd be there, and not there.

"Gi," said Thea.

Gigi didn't say anything.

"You saw me," said Thea. Her voice was barely there. "In the bowl. That's what you were running at. You kept saying my name."

Gigi's mouth twisted.

"It showed me you," she said. Her voice was blunt, the way it always was. Bratty. Cracked down the middle. "It was so stupid. You were just *sitting* there. On the glass. With your knees up. Like you used to. On calls. At two in the morning. Waiting for me to stop being an idiot so we could keep talking." She wiped her face with the back of her wrist, hard. "It showed me you *staying up*. That's what I wanted. More than anything. That's what it found in me. You. Staying up with me. Like before."

Thea looked up at her for a long time.

"And then you knocked me over," said Gigi. "The real you. And it cut you open. Because I was running at a fake one." Her voice went. "*Thea*."

"I know," said Thea.

"You *idiot*."

"I know."

"I'm still angry at you," said Gigi. "About before. I'm still—"

"I know," said Thea. "Me too. At me."

Gigi stopped.

"I should have stayed up," said Thea. Very quietly. "Some nights. I should have. I didn't, because I was tired and I was scared of my exams and I thought there'd be time. I always thought there'd be time." She looked up at the stars in the mouth of the cistern. "There isn't always time, Gi. I think I've—" She stopped. "I think I've only just found out how much there isn't."

Gigi looked down at her for a long moment.

Then she bent her head, and put her forehead down against Thea's, and stayed there. Her silver hair fell over both their faces like a curtain, with the dead blue flowers in it.

Kei closed his bag. He climbed up the side of the cistern to the lip, quietly, and sat down beside Drew in the dark.

Drew didn't say anything. He just moved over a little.

*The difference between a poison and a cure is how much.*

Kei sat on the lip of a dry cistern in the middle of the Glass Waste and thought about a man who'd lain in that hollow for twenty-five nights. Alone. With his light gone small. With a cut in his hand and nobody to measure anything for him. Nobody to sit at his head. Nobody to wait with him and see which way it went.

How much could one person carry, before it stopped being a cure?

He didn't know. He didn't have the skill for that one. Nobody did.

*Not this time*, he thought. And looked east, toward the dark, where the man had gone.
