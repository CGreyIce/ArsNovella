They went into the gold together.

Not in a line. Higu had wanted a line; she'd stood at the top of the first arch with her rock and said *two abreast, Tiger in front, Drew at the back, nobody more than an arm from the next one*. And then she'd looked at the mist, going out over the gorge in front of them, so thick and gold and soft you couldn't see the next arch, and put the rock away.

"Hands," said Higu. "Everybody. Hold hands. Don't let go."

So they did.

All thirty-six. In a chain. Across the bridge, from one parapet to the other and back, a long loose knot of people holding hands, and the goat in the middle of it with Aleaka's hand on its neck, and the dog with Hako's hand in its scruff, and Sleet with her nose in the back of Aleaka's knee. And Ice in the middle. Thea's hand in his left. Gabe's in his right.

On every stair, Thea held his hand. She'd done it since the first stair they'd ever climbed together, without asking. A bridge wasn't a stair. She held it anyway.

Gabe had never held his hand in his life.

He held it now. Big and warm and rough, the smith's hand, the one that had made Hearthwall and punched him on the flour sacks in Evermorrow and sat on him on floor 1 so he'd sleep. He didn't say anything about it. He just held it, on Ice's right, where he always was, and looked straight ahead into the gold.

"Don't make it weird," said Gabe.

"I'm not."

"You're making it a bit weird."

"*Everybody's* saying that."

"Yeah, well," said Gabe. "You're making it a bit weird, mate."

They walked into the mist.

***

It was warm.

That was the first thing. He'd thought it would be cold, the way mist always was; the way fog on the river on fifteen had been, wet and grey and getting into your collar. It wasn't. It was warm like the air in a kitchen, gold and thick and soft, and it smelled of something he couldn't place for a long time and then could. Bread. Very faint. The smell of a kitchen in the morning when somebody's had the oven going.

He couldn't see anything.

Thea's hand. Gabe's hand. The pale stone under his feet, a little way, and then gold. The parapets either side, for a pace, and then gold. Nothing above. Nothing below. Nothing ahead. Just the bridge going on under their feet, arch after arch, the slope up and the slope down, and the warm gold all round them like being inside a lantern.

And the voices.

Not the Toll's. Theirs. Thirty-six people, holding hands in a chain in the gold, talking. Quietly. Because you couldn't see anybody past the person next to you, and the only way to know the chain was still there was to hear it.

Newish, somewhere ahead, counting. *One. Two. Three.* Under her breath. And then louder, for everybody, with Edda's old lantern swinging in her free hand and the stray in it humming on her beat. *One. Two. Three.*

Calvin, somewhere behind, talking. About nothing. About the mist, and the bread smell, and whether there'd be an inn at the other end, and what it would have for breakfast. Rachael not telling him to shut up. Letting him. Because if Calvin was talking, Calvin was there.

Salty. He couldn't see Salty. He heard him. Somewhere to the left, toward the parapet. *Tink.* Ressa's small hammer, on the stone of the parapet as they went. Not hard. Not *THUD*. *Tink.* Every four seconds. Keeping time. So that whatever they were walking through, nothing in it would be lost.

And Wendy, beside Salty. Singing. Very quietly. The last song of the night, at nine in the morning, on a bridge in the gold.

And Salty humming with her. He could hear it. He'd got the end right now.

*Watch each other*, he thought. *That's all. That's the whole trick.*

They weren't watching each other. They couldn't see each other. They were holding on, and talking, and counting, and keeping time, and singing. So nobody would be anywhere nobody knew.

Ruth's trick, he thought, with the eyes shut.

He didn't know how long they walked. Newish would know. Milli would know. He didn't count. For the first time on any bridge, in any life, he didn't count anything at all. He walked in the warm gold with Thea's hand and Gabe's and listened to thirty-four people make sure of each other, and let them.

***

Halfway across, Cursiffe stopped.

The chain stopped with him. All of it, all at once, the way a line of people stops when somebody in the middle does. Ice felt it come back through the hands. Somebody saying *what*. Somebody saying *Curse?*

"There's something on the parapet," said Cursiffe.

His voice came out of the gold to the right. Close. A few people along the chain. Very strange.

"Writing," said Cursiffe. "Cut. In the stone."

Nobody said anything.

The chain moved, without anybody saying to, the way water moves round a stone. Toward the right-hand parapet. Toward Cursiffe's voice. Until Ice was close enough, with Thea and Gabe still on either side of him, to see the pale stone of the parapet coming out of the gold, waist high, and Cursiffe's hand on it, flat.

And under his hand, cut into the top of the parapet, small, worn so soft by the warm mist that you could only just see it.

*30.*

And beside it, a letter. With a tail. Long. Going down.

*R.*

Cursiffe didn't say anything. He took his hand off it. He looked at it for a long time, with the gold mist all round him and Tiger's hand in his on one side and Wendy's on the other.

"She went over with all of them," said Ice. Quietly. "The Toll said. All thirty. Together."

Cursiffe looked at him.

"And she stopped," said Cursiffe. "Halfway. In the mist. And cut it." He touched the *30* with one finger. "So she'd know. Whoever came next." He was quiet. "That they'd all been here. Still."

He let go of Tiger's hand.

Not of the chain; Tiger took hold of his coat instead, without being asked. Cursiffe took out Mott's ink with his free hand and a pen, and bent over the parapet, and under Ruth's *30* and her *R*, small, square, not hurried, he wrote:

*36. — C.*

And watched it dry. In the warm gold. It didn't take long.

It held.

***

They came out of the mist at what Milli said, afterwards, was the six hundred and fourth arch.

He felt it before he saw it. The warm thinning. The gold going pale, then white, then thin enough to see through. And then the bridge going down a last long slope, and the mist rolling back off it like a sheet off a bed, and green.

The far side.

It wasn't a cliff. He'd thought it would be a cliff, on the other side of the gorge, the way the near side was. It was a hill. A long green hill, going up from the end of the bridge in soft folds, with sheep on it, and stone walls, and a path. Several paths. Going away from the end of the bridge up the hill in every direction, worn into the grass, pale, the way a path goes when a lot of people have walked it a long time ago and then fewer and fewer.

And the sky. Blue. Very wide. With the sun high in it.

They stood at the end of the bridge in the long grass and nobody let go of anybody for a while.

Then Calvin said, at a normal volume, "*Oh*," and Rachael laughed. Out loud. And let go of his hand.

And the chain came apart. Slowly. People letting go, one at a time, and standing there, in the grass, in the sun, on the far side of the last bridge. Looking at each other. At their hands. At the seven knots on every cord.

Thea didn't let go of his hand.

Neither did Gabe.

He stood between them on the green hill and looked up it.

Far up. On one of the paths. Going away over the top of the hill, small, so far off he could barely make them out against the blue: two figures. One small and bent, with a staff. One smaller. Walking side by side. Not fast. Not slow. The way people walk who've put something down and are finding out how to walk without it.

They didn't look back.

He didn't need them to.

"Maigo," said Ice.

Maigo was beside him somewhere. Barefoot in the long grass. He looked where Ice was looking. Up the hill. At the two figures going over the top.

He didn't say anything for a long time.

"Light enough," said Maigo.

The two figures went over the top of the hill and were gone.

Five.

He'd been ninety at the edge of the gorge last night. The Toll, he supposed. The heaviest thing it had ever been paid. Or the bridge. Or all of it, all at once, two lives' worth of something put down on a plain wooden table, and the tower counting it the way the tower counted everything.

*Ninety-five*, said Alea. On his shoulder. Very quietly. *Ice. Ninety-five.*

"I know."

*Five to go.*

"I know."

*You know what's at a hundred.*

He did.

Tier 6. The first hard trial he'd ever taken. In the first life he'd taken normal, every tier, up to the sixth, because normal was safer and he'd had nobody, and the sixth had been the first time he'd stood in front of the three choices and taken the hard one. Because by then there was nobody left to be safe for.

Every trial in this life he'd taken hard. Every one. Five of them. Every one a trial he'd never faced, because he'd never once faced a hard one at those tiers.

The sixth he had.

*It might not be the same*, said Alea. *Nothing's the same. Not since fourteen. It's not writing the first record any more. It might be a different trial.*

"It might."

*Or it might be exactly the same one.* She was quiet. *The first one you ever took hard. Alone. On a floor I can't see any more.*

He didn't say anything.

He looked at the window. At *Lv 95*. At the line that said *Lightbearer*. At the place under it, in the grey, where on every floor up to fourteen there'd been a line about the first record. *Matches.* Then *does not match.* Then, on fourteen, *does not match* in a voice that couldn't make it.

And on fifteen, nothing. And on sixteen, nothing. And on seventeen. *It's just watching*, Alea had said. *You. All of you. Her.*

He looked at the grey under his level line.

And there was something there.

***

He didn't see it at first. It was very faint. Fainter than any grey line he'd ever seen. Fainter than the *does not match* on fourteen. Like writing on a frosted window, after somebody's breathed on it, before it goes.

He looked closer. He could always read the record line; he and Alea, and nobody else, until Thea. He'd read *matches* and *does not match* on every floor up to fourteen. This was fainter than any of them. He had to look the way Thea looked. *Source. Every window has words under the words.*

He could see it.

*Record 2 of 2 · being written*

He stood very still on the green hill.

*Being written.*

Not *matches*. Not *does not match*. Not nothing.

*Being written.*

"Thea," said Ice.

She was still holding his hand. She'd been holding it the whole way across. She was looking at him. Not at the window. At him. At his face.

"You can see it," said Thea.

Not a question.

He looked at her.

"On sixteen," said Thea. Very quietly. "After the Moth. After you said *mine*. Your level came up, at the foot of the far stair, and I looked at the corner of it as it closed. I always look." She was quiet. "And it was there. Just for a second. Under your level. *Being written.* The first thing that had been in the grey since fourteen." Her hand tightened in his. "And then it went. Before you saw it."

"You didn't tell me."

"No."

"Why?"

Thea looked at him for a long time. In the sun. On the green hill on the far side of the last bridge.

"Because you'd spent two lives being told what you were," said Thea. "By a record. *Matches. Does not match.* By a book in Tell, in two hands, reading you out. By Alea's logs. By me, sometimes. On stairs." She was quiet. "I didn't want to be one more thing that told you. I wanted you to see it yourself. When you were ready to." She looked at the window in the corner of his eye, which she could see the edge of, the way she could always see the edges of things. "I thought you'd see it when you'd put something down. I didn't know what. I thought it'd be soon."

He didn't say anything.

"Was it?" said Thea. "Soon?"

"This morning," said Ice.

She looked at him. At the seventh knot on his cord. At the little lantern on his belt, humming its four notes, the middle right.

"Yes," said Thea. "I thought it might be."

***

*Being written*, said Alea.

She'd been very quiet. She said it now, on his shoulder, as if she were reading it off the window for the first time, the way she'd read everything off every window for two lives. Slowly. Carefully. In the voice she used for things she'd never seen before.

*Record 2 of 2. Being written.*

"I know."

*Ice.* She was quiet. *I've had the first record in my logs since the white room. The first time. Every day of it. Every floor. Every name. That's what I am. I'm the one that kept it.* Her voice did something. *And on fourteen it stopped matching, and on fifteen it went grey, and I thought: that's it. I'm a fox with a book nobody can read. I'm the keeper of a thing that isn't true any more.*

He didn't say anything.

*It's writing*, said Alea. *Not watching. Writing. The second one. This one.* She was quiet. *It hasn't got a record to match any more. So it's writing one. From scratch. From what happens.* A pause. *From what you do. All of you.*

"Who's writing it?"

Alea didn't answer for a long time.

*I don't know*, she said. *I don't know who wrote the first one either. I never knew.* She was quiet. *But I know what it's writing.*

"What?"

*Thirty-six people on a green hill*, said Alea. *Holding hands. With seven knots each.* Her voice went very soft. *That's the first line of it I've ever seen. And it's true.*

***

The stair was at the top of the hill.

Not a door. Not a trapdoor, not an arch. A stair. Cut into a great grey outcrop of rock that stood up out of the green at the very top of the hill like the knuckle of a fist, going up the side of it in a long straight flight and on, up, past the top of the rock, into the sky. Into cloud. Into nothing he could see.

It was very plain. Grey stone steps. Worn in the middle, a little, the way the steps up from the Narrows had been worn, and the steps up the far wall of the Hollow. As if a lot of people had gone up them a very long time ago.

Thirty, maybe. Three hundred and twelve years ago. In their second spring.

At the foot of the stair, there were strays.

He saw them as they came up the hill. A handful. Five or six. Pale and small and the size of apples, hanging in the warm air at the bottom of the first step, very still, the way Edda had said they hung at the foot of a stair. The way they hung over a cradle. Not drifting. Waiting.

They'd come up from sixteen, some of them. Through Turnstead. Down the Long Road behind the pilgrims, unnoticed, the way strays went everywhere. And here they were. At the foot of the next stair. Humming.

Hana's light, on her crown, hummed back at them.

So did Newish's, *one, two, three*. And Milli's. And Jared's, one deep note. And all of them. Thirty-five little iron cages, on thirty-five collars, humming at a handful of strays at the foot of a stair on a green hill on eighteen.

And the one on his belt.

He looked down at it. Gabe's lantern. The light in it. Humming. Not at the strays.

Up.

At the stair. At the top of it, wherever that was, up in the cloud. The way Hana's light had hummed up the far stair in the Hollow and Aleaka had said *I remember this*. *I went up this.*

It wasn't humming *I remember this*.

He didn't know what it was humming. He didn't have Heed. He looked at Aleaka. She was standing a few paces off with the goat, looking at the little lantern on his belt with her face very still. She'd been listening. She always listened.

She met his eyes.

She didn't say anything. She shook her head. Very slightly. *Not yet. I don't know yet.*

He nodded.

***

Higu stood at the foot of the stair with her rock.

The new-new one. From the Hollow. Full now, both sides. The left with the pool and the roots and the arrow going up, and the stair zigzagging up the far wall, and Turnstead's square, and a bridge with a *7* on it. The right with everything he'd ever answered. *A FOREST. A RIVER. HE DOESN'T KNOW. HE ASKED HOW. THERE. HELP.* And at the bottom, in her small hand, from the Long Road, *WHERE: here.*

She looked at the stair going up into the cloud.

Then she turned round and looked at him.

He knew what she was going to say. She'd said it at the foot of every stair since the white room. Since the Long Acre. *Where?* And he'd answered it, for a year and a half, from memory, and then on fifteen with *I don't know*, and on the jam with *there*, and at the foot of the Hollow stair he'd asked it first, and she'd laughed.

She didn't say it.

She held out the rock.

To him.

He looked at it. At the chalk in her other hand. Holding that out too.

"Your turn," said Higu.

He took the rock. It was heavy. Warm from her hands. Full on both sides; there wasn't any room. He turned it over and over in his hands, looking for a space. There wasn't one.

"It's full," said Ice.

"I know," said Higu. "Get another one." She almost smiled. "On the way up."

He looked at her for a long time.

Then he gave her back the rock, and the chalk, and bent down, there, at the foot of the stair, and picked up a stone. Out of the grass. Flat. Grey. Smooth. About the size of his hand. Blank.

He held it out to her.

Higu looked at it. Then at him. And her face did the thing it had done at the foot of the Hollow stair when he'd asked *where* before she could. And she took it. And the chalk. And she wrote, at the top of the blank stone, small, in the left-hand column, the first line.

*NINETEEN.*

And in the right-hand column, under nothing, she didn't write anything at all.

"We'll find out," said Higu.

***

He went up in the middle.

Hana first, because she'd said she'd keep going first until she got tired, and she hadn't. Milli with her. Tiger, who couldn't help it, at her elbow. Then the rest of them. Newish counting with Edda's lantern. Salty with Ressa's hammer in his pocket, and Wendy beside him, teaching him the end of a song. Rachael with her hands in her pockets, walking beside Calvin, not holding his collar, watching his back anyway. Dennis with a tab that said *Rachael. Owed: everything.* Chris with a grey notebook at the very bottom of his pack that he wasn't going to take out again. Ina with his mask on his head and his face bare. Yan with Working On It and Suzu and Wendy's hand on his coat when she wasn't holding Salty's. Gigi and Xola and Chi and Drew and Higu, with a new blank stone. The goat. Aleaka saying *with me* on every step.

He went up in the middle.

Thea's hand in his left. On every stair. The big lantern in her other hand, open, leaning at him.

Gabe on his right. Not holding his hand any more. Just there. Where he always was.

And on his belt, Gabe's terrible lantern. With a light in it that had asked *whose* in the dark for as long as it had been a light, and had *his* written in the grey where *whose* had been. Humming a song a girl had sung at two in the morning on the other end of a call a long time ago. Four notes.

The middle right.

The strays came up behind them. A handful. Slow. Step by step. The way the goat had climbed the hundred and twelve on floor 1.

Five levels to a hundred. Tier 6. The first hard one he'd ever taken, last time, alone, on a floor nobody could see any more. Four floors to twenty-two, where thirty people had been cried from the steps of the Assize in their second spring, and somebody had said *ask on twenty-two*.

He didn't know what was on nineteen.

He didn't know what was on any of them, now. Nobody did. Not him. Not Alea. Not the tower, which wasn't watching any more.

It was writing.

He went up the stair into the cloud, in the middle, with thirty-five people round him and a girl's hand in his and a light on his belt that knew whose it was, and for the first time in two lives, he didn't count the steps.
