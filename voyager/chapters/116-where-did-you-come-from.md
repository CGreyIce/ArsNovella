Nobody else had heard it.

He knew that straight away. He felt it through the threads. Thirty-five people sitting in the snow round him, looking at the Steward, waiting for it to do something. Not one of them had heard it speak. It had spoken at his shoulder. Only his. The way Alea spoke. The way nothing in this tower had ever spoken to him in ten years and two hundred and eighty-four days.

*Where did you come from?*

He sat there with it. In the snow, between Gabe and Deston, with the lamp shield on his knees and the gold light from the Gate lying across his boots and the window still in front of his face. *Irregular.* And under it, in grey, the two lines nobody else could see.

*One record does not match.*

*Record found: 1 of 2.*

It knew. That was what the second line meant. He'd always thought it might. He'd thought it on floor 4, in the House of Ash, when he'd said every name to a dark behind a grille and the dark had listened. He'd thought it on floor 6, when the Wanting had shown him a long table with thirty-five people at it that he'd only ever seen in his head. He'd thought it on floor 8 when the Great Fall stopped to look, and on floor 9 when every fern on the floor turned round.

He'd hoped he was wrong.

It had a record of him. Two. One that matched the white room, the first minute, twenty-six and level one. And one it had found. From somewhere else. From ten years of floors, and a hundred, and a fire on ninety-nine, and a throne, and a window with two buttons on it that Alea had said was older than the thing on the throne.

And it was asking him where that one came from.

"Ice," said Alea, on his shoulder. Very quietly. "You don't have to answer it."

"I know."

"It can't make you."

"I know, Alea."

He looked at the Steward. Tall and grey and very straight, at the foot of the Gate, forty metres off across the snow, with its book against its chest and its white face turned toward him. Waiting. The way it had waited every morning on the wall, between *thirty-five* and *thirty-six*.

He thought about what he'd say. He'd had a hundred and fifty-three days on his own to think about what he'd say to people, and he'd never once got it right. He'd thought about what he'd say to Gabe for a hundred and fifty-three days and in the end he'd said *ok*.

He looked round instead.

At Gabe, beside him, sitting cross-legged in the snow with his arms folded and Hearthwall on his knees, glaring at the Steward as if it owed him money. At Deston on his other side, with his hand on his shirt pocket. At Calvin, two rows back, who'd said *none* out loud to a mountain. At Rachael beside Calvin, flat and furious. At Dennis. At Drew at the end of the line with his empty hands. At Thea, behind him, with the lantern on her knees. At all of them. Thirty-five people sitting in the snow on the top of a pass in the dark of the moon, in front of a gate with spring coming through it, because they wouldn't go through without him.

He answered it.

Not at his own shoulder. He couldn't do that; he didn't know how. Out loud. Not loud. Just loud enough to carry across forty metres of snow to a thing with no face.

"Them," said Ice.

***

He felt thirty-five people turn their heads.

They hadn't heard the question. They'd only heard him. One word, out of nowhere, in the silence on the saddle. He felt Gabe go still beside him. He felt Thea, behind him, stop breathing.

"I came from them," said Ice.

He didn't look at any of them. He looked at the Steward.

"That's where," he said. "That's all of it. That's the whole answer. Wherever I was before, whatever your other record says. I came from them. I came back for them." He swallowed. "That's where I came from."

At the foot of the Gate, the Steward didn't move.

Then, at his shoulder, close, calm:

"*That is not a place.*"

"It's the only one I've got," said Ice.

He said it out loud. He didn't care if they heard. Let them hear that much. They'd heard worse; they'd read worse on a rock.

The Steward was quiet for a long time.

"*Records are not wrong*," it said. At his shoulder. Level. A clerk explaining a rule to someone who had misunderstood the form. "*Climbers are.*"

And it lifted its book.

Not open. Closed. It lifted it in both hands, in front of its chest, and held it there. And behind it, for the first time since they'd come over the lip of the saddle, the Gatekeeper lifted its head.

***

The snow came off its shoulders in a long white sheet.

It lifted its head slowly, the way a very old man lifts his head after a long time looking at the floor. The great grey helm came up. Plain. No crest, no face-plate, no slit. Just a smooth curve of grey iron, and under the brim of it, where a face should have been, dark. Hollow. Like every Rimed on the high side. Except bigger. Except it had been here first.

It let go of the rings.

The doors stayed where they were. Open a hand's width, with the gold coming through. The Gatekeeper took its hands off them and let them fall to its sides, and turned. Toward the saddle. Toward thirty-six people sitting in the snow.

And took a step.

The ground shook. Not much. A thud, through the snow, into his boots, the way the trip hammer under the second arch had come through the stones of Wendmere. Another step. Another thud. It was walking toward them. Slowly. Not hurrying. With its great gauntlets hanging at its sides, and its hollow helm bent toward the middle of the line.

Toward him.

"*Reconciling*," said the Steward. To everyone, this time. At every shoulder.

"*UP*," said Drew.

They were up before he'd finished saying it. Thirty-five people coming up off the snow at once, the way they'd come up off the moss at the log on floor 9, the way they'd come up off the stones of the fifth wall a hundred times. Shields. Staffs. Bows. And moving. Not into a line. Into a ring.

They'd drilled it. In the barracks, at the long table, on Higu's map. *If it comes for him. Not a line. A ring. Like the white room.* Tanks on the outside, shields out, overlapping, every shield covering the one on its left, all the way round. Healers inside. Ranged inside them, between, on the shoulders of the tanks if they had to. And in the middle—

"*Dec*," said Gabe. "*Kneel*."

He was already kneeling.

***

He'd thought about it for fourteen nights. He'd thought about it on the barracks roof and on the wall and on the step at two in the morning with Thea's shoulder against his. What he'd do, if it came for him. He'd thought about it the way he'd thought about every fight for ten years: where to stand, where it would come from, what he'd hit first.

And every time he'd thought it, he'd ended up in the same place. On his knees. In the middle of a ring. On the jetty on floor 8, with the river-folk watching, and a white thing built out of his own confession coming for Calvin first.

*Kneel. Let them hold.*

He knelt in the snow on the top of the pass with his sword flat in front of him and the lamp shield face-up on it and his hands on his knees. Exactly the way he'd knelt in the white room.

And let it go.

[ Sanctuary ]

The amber came out of him. Not up, the way it went up in the Down, a light for people to find. Out. Along the threads. All thirty-five of them, at once, the way it had gone out along the white lines of the trial to thirty-five circles. He felt it land. A warm hand, flat, between thirty-five pairs of shoulder blades. He felt them stand differently. All round him. The ring settling, like a building settling onto its foundations.

*Where you stand, they are safe.*

And he felt it come back. Down the threads. Into him. Rachael's blue, cool. Xola's water. Malcolm's, steady, with a backup behind it. Calvin's wind. Deston's gold, warm, all of it. Gabe's weight. Thea's ward. Every one of them, coming back down the threads into the middle of the ring, into him, kneeling.

*Where they stand, so are you.*

The Gatekeeper reached the ring.

***

It didn't hit them. That was the thing he'd remember. It wasn't a Rimed. It didn't come at the shields. It came to the edge of the ring and stopped, and stood there, huge, over them, with its hollow helm bent down, looking at the middle. At him.

And it reached.

Over the ring. Over the shields. One great grey gauntlet, the size of a cart, coming down out of the starlight over the tanks' heads toward the middle of the ring. Slowly. Carefully. The way you'd reach into a box for the one thing in it you wanted.

"*HOLD*," said Drew.

The gauntlet came down on the shields.

Not on him. On the ring. It couldn't reach him without going through the ring, so it went through the ring. It came down on Azac's shield and Gabe's and Chris's, three at once, where they overlapped, and pushed.

He felt it. All of it. Through the threads, the way he'd felt every hit on the Long Night, and it was worse than any of them. It was like having a mountain laid on your back. Azac's arm went, and Chris's legs, and Gabe made a sound through his teeth he'd never heard Gabe make. And then Azac said something, low, *what strikes the line, the whole line takes*, and the weight went out. Along the ring. Left and right. Into every shield, all the way round, all thirty-five, a little each. *And lightly.* Spread so thin that nobody broke.

The ring held.

The gauntlet pushed. The ring held. It pushed harder, and the snow under the tanks' boots went to ice and they slid, a hand's width, all of them, together, and held.

It took its hand away.

And reached again. Somewhere else. Round the side of the ring, low, between Jared and Hako. Its great iron fingers going in between the shields where they overlapped, prising. And they held. And it reached for the top, over Salty's head, and Salty put his chipped greatsword up into the palm of it and Ina put white fire across its wrist and it took its hand back.

And it reached for *Hako*.

Not the middle. Hako. It closed its fingers round Hako, round the whole of him, shield and spear and all, and lifted him out of the ring like a man lifting a cat out of a basket.

"*HAKO*—"

He felt the ring open. A gap. Where Hako had been. Right on his left. The way he'd felt a gap in a count on floor 2, on a stair, with thirty-five voices and no thirty-sixth.

And he felt somebody go through it.

Calvin.

Out of the ring. Through the gap. Talking. *Look at my back, look at my back*, at the top of his voice, and the wind going fast behind him, and Calvin went up the Gatekeeper's arm like a squirrel up a tree, both daggers out, and put them into the joint of its wrist where the gauntlet met the vambrace. And Winter's arrow went into the same joint, from the shoulders of the tanks, and the frost went out across the grey iron white. *What you hit stays where it is.* The fingers stopped closing. Froze. With Hako in them.

And Hako *twisted*, inside the frozen fingers, and got his spear arm out, and drove the spear down into the gauntlet's own palm, and the fingers opened, and Hako fell. Into the snow. Into Calvin's wind. And Calvin had him by the collar before he'd finished landing, and dragged him back through the gap into the ring, *nobody gets left*, and Chi stepped into the gap behind them and closed it with her shield.

"*It's fine*," shouted Hako, from the middle of the ring, on his back in the snow next to Ice. "*It's FINE*—"

"*Shut up, Hako*," said Rachael, with her hands already on him.

The ring held.

***

The Steward was watching.

He could see it. Past the Gatekeeper's legs, at the foot of the Gate, in the gold light from the doors. Standing very straight with its book against its chest and its white face turned toward the ring. Not doing anything. Watching. The way a clerk watches a process he's started, to make sure it runs.

And the Gatekeeper stopped reaching for the ring.

It straightened up. Over them. Its hollow helm turned, slowly, round the ring. At the shields. At the healers. At the thirty-five people standing in a circle in the snow round one man kneeling, every one of them covering the one on their left.

And it put both its hands together, over its head, and brought them down.

Not on the ring.

Into the middle of it.

Straight down. Both gauntlets. Locked together like a hammer, out of the stars, over the tanks, over the shields, over the heads of the healers, down into the middle of the ring where he was kneeling with his hands on his knees.

He saw it come. He'd seen things come before. He'd seen everything come, for ten years, and he knew exactly how long it would take and exactly where it would land and exactly how to get out of the way of it. Every part of him knew. Every part of him said *up*. *Now*. *Roll left, come up behind the shield, take it on the rim, you've done this a thousand times*—

*Kneel. Do not rise.*

He didn't rise.

And Thea stepped round him.

***

He saw her do it.

That was the thing. He saw it. The first time, he hadn't. The first time he'd been looking at the thing in front of him with his shield in two pieces on his arm and his whole attention on where it was going to hit, and he'd only known she was there when she was already there, in front of him, with her back to him, and the light coming up.

This time he saw her.

She came round from behind him. From where she'd been, with the lantern, where he'd asked her to be. She put the lantern down in the snow beside his knee, the way she'd put it down on the heap of green in the Down. Carefully. Next to his light. And she stepped round him. Past his left shoulder. Into the space in front of him. Between him and the two great gauntlets coming down out of the stars.

And she put her hands up.

And the light came.

He knew it. He knew it before it had finished coming. He'd seen it once. Once, in ten years, in the last ten seconds of everything, on the hundredth floor, in a room with black glass and a throne. A ward. Not the ward she'd had on floor 3 for the wisps, or the one she'd held over the ring in the white room on floor 8. A whole wall. Of light. Rewritten. Going up in front of her, in front of him, out of her two raised hands, bright and clear and full of lines like text, like code, like the words under the words, running across it so fast he couldn't read them. A wall of rewritten light.

He hadn't known she could make it. The first time, he hadn't known either.

The gauntlets came down on it.

***

Everything in him stood up.

Not his body. His body stayed. Everything else. Every year, every floor, every night on his own, every fight he'd ever had. It all stood up inside him at once and screamed. *She's in front of you. She's in front of you. You know what happens. You watched it happen. You sat on the floor afterwards and closed her eyes. GET UP.*

"*Don't*," said Alea. On his shoulder. Very fast. Very small. "*Ice. Don't. If you get up it's just you. Remember. If you get up it's just you again.*"

He remembered.

The white room. The trial. The ninth attack sweeping Gabe's shield aside, and him standing up, just for a second, and every circle in the ring going dark. Thirty-five people going thin. Because if he stood, it was just him. Just one man holding everything off everybody. The way it had always been. The way it had been on the hundredth floor.

*Kneel. Where you stand, they are safe.*

*Where they stand*—

She was standing. Right there. In front of him. One of them. Inside the ring. Inside the warm hand between the shoulder blades.

*So are you.*

He didn't get up.

He knelt in the snow on the top of the pass with his hands on his knees and his eyes open, watching her, watching the two great grey gauntlets come down on her wall of rewritten light, and he did the hardest thing he had ever done in two lives.

He let them hold.

***

The gauntlets hit the ward.

He felt it. Through her thread. All of it. The whole weight of the oldest thing on the high side of the pass coming down on a wall of light held up by two hands. The ward bent. Bowed. The lines of text running across it went slow, slower, the way the text on the throne's face had gone slow on the hundredth floor when his sword went in. He felt her knees start to go.

And then he felt the ring.

Thirty-five threads, all at once. All of them turning. Toward her. The way they'd turned in the Down toward a light. Not to him. To her. Rachael's blue going into her, down the threads, through the warm hand between her shoulder blades. Xola's water. Malcolm's two blessings, *in case the first one fails*. Deston's gold, every scrap of it, the way he'd given it all to Ice on the Long Night. Azac's voice, low, *and so the line held*. Calvin's wind. Gabe's weight. All thirty-four of them, pouring into one person standing in front of him with her hands up.

And his. His own. The amber, going out of him into her, the only way he'd ever been able to give it to anybody. Through Sanctuary. On his knees.

The ward stopped bending.

It held.

It held, with the gauntlets on it. The lines of text running across it sped up again, bright, fast, unreadable. Her knees stopped going. She stood there in front of him in the snow with her hands up and her ponytail coming loose and the pink and blue streaks lying across her cheek, holding a wall of light against the oldest armour in the world, and it didn't go through.

It didn't go through.

And Drew walked into the middle of the ring with nothing in his hands.

***

He didn't run. That was the thing. He walked. Out of the ring, from the end of the line where he'd been standing, in through the gap the healers made for him, into the middle, past Ice, to Thea's side. Barefoot. He'd taken his boots off on the saddle; Ice hadn't even noticed. In a navy track jacket, with his hands empty at his sides, breathing. In through the nose. Slowly. Hold it. Out.

He stopped beside Thea. Under the gauntlets. Under the ward.

He looked up at them. The two great grey iron fists, locked together, pressing down on the wall of light a hand's width over his head. The knuckles. The joints. The place, where the two gauntlets met, where the old iron was cracked.

Ice saw him find it.

*Strike once, with nothing.*

Drew put his feet down in the snow. He breathed out. All the way. Until there was nothing left in him at all.

And he hit it.

Once. Bare-handed. Up. Through the ward, which let him through, because it was Thea's and he was one of them. With his open hand. Not a fist. The heel of his palm, flat, into the crack in the old grey iron where the two gauntlets met.

The sound was like a bell breaking.

The crack ran. Out from where his hand had hit, across both gauntlets, fast, in a fine white web, the way the throne's face had cracked on the hundredth floor from the point of a sword. And the Gatekeeper's hands came apart. Off the ward. Off each other. Up, and away, the two great gauntlets going back up into the stars with the cracks running across them, slowly, and the Gatekeeper rocking back on its heels for the first time, Ice thought, in its whole existence.

*He'd made the thing stand, once, for half a second. Ice would have liked to tell somebody that.*

Ice saw Drew read the last line. Saw him read it twice. Saw him look down at his own open hand.

And Ice, kneeling in the snow, started to laugh, and couldn't stop, and didn't know if it was laughing.

***

The Gatekeeper didn't reach again.

It stood over the ring with its cracked hands hanging at its sides and its hollow helm bent down. Looking. At the middle. At Thea, with her ward still up, shaking, and Drew beside her with his hand still raised, and the man kneeling behind them in the snow.

Then it turned its head. Slowly. Toward the Gate. Toward the Steward.

The Steward was looking at its book.

It had opened it again. It stood at the foot of the Gate in the gold light and looked down at the page for a long time. Ice watched it. Its long pale hand moved over the page, slowly. Stopped. Moved again.

And then, at every shoulder, calm and level, it said:

"*The count stands.*"

Nobody moved.

"*Thirty-six*," said the Steward.

And closed the book.

At the edge of his vision, the window that had been in front of his face since the count went. Just went. *Irregular*, and the two grey lines under it, *one record does not match*, *record found: 1 of 2*. Gone. As if somebody had closed it.

He didn't know what that meant. He didn't think the Steward did either.

The Gatekeeper turned round.

It walked back to the Gate. Slowly. Thud. Thud. Through the snow. And it put its cracked gauntlets on the two great iron rings. And it turned them.

The doors opened.

Not a hand's width. All the way. The sound of a mountain clearing its throat, low and enormous, and the two grey doors swinging back into the black ice, and the gold coming out between them. Not a blade of it. A flood. Warm, and bright, and smelling of grass and water and flowers and summer, pouring out across the saddle, over the snow, over the ring, over all of them.

Thea's ward went out. She'd let it go. She stood there in the gold light with her hands still half up, looking at the open doors, and then her knees went for real, and Drew caught her by the elbow before she hit the snow.

She turned round.

She looked at him. Kneeling. Behind her. With his hands on his knees and his eyes open and his face wet and the snow all round him gold.

"You didn't get up," said Thea.

"No," he said.

"I thought you'd get up."

"I know," he said. "So did I."

***

They went through together.

He didn't remember who started it. Newish, probably. Somebody took somebody's hand. And somebody took theirs. And by the time anybody had thought about it, all thirty-six of them were standing in the snow in front of the open Gate in a line, hand in hand, the way you'd stand at the end of a long night on a call when you finally all agree to hang up at the same time.

He was in the middle. Gabe's hand in his right. Thea's in his left.

"Count," said Newish.

They counted. Names. All the way along the line. *Newish. Calvin. Hana.* All of them. Thirty-five. And at the end, when it came to him, he said *Ice*. And then, because it was the top of a pass on the tenth floor and the oldest armour in the world was standing beside them with its hands on the rings, and he could, he said the other one too.

"Declan," he said.

Gabe's hand tightened on his.

"Thirty-six," said Newish. "No gap."

They walked through.

The Gatekeeper stood beside the doors with its cracked hands on the rings and its hollow helm bowed, the way it had stood all winter, and didn't move. They walked past it, in a line, holding hands, into the gold. Under the arch of the Gate. Through the black ice, which went up on both sides of them like the walls of a tunnel, deep and clear, so that they could see down into it, a long way, and see their own reflections walking beside them, thirty-six, holding hands.

And as he passed the doors he looked back. Once. He couldn't help it.

The Steward was still standing at the foot of the Gate, in the snow, outside. In the starlight. Very straight. With its book against its chest. Not following. Looking at him.

And all down the throat of the pass behind it, all the way down toward the Bastion, the Rimed were falling.

He saw them go. Three hundred and twelve of them, in their ranks, on both sides of the throat, all at once. Coming apart. The packed snow and the ice going soft and slumping, and the old grey armour inside clattering down into the drifts, helm and breastplate and gauntlet, all the way down the pass in a long rolling sound like a mountain letting go of something it had been holding for a very long time. And far below, at the bottom of the pass, very small, five walls, one above the other, with fires on them.

He thought he heard a bell. The deep one, on the fifth wall. Ringing. Not the way it rang for the Winter. Something else.

And then the doors began to close.

The Steward didn't move. It stood in the snow and watched him through the narrowing gap. And just before the doors met, at his shoulder, only his, calm and level and very close, it said it again.

"*Where did you come from?*"

The doors closed.
