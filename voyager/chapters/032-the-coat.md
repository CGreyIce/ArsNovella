Gabe had been cold for four days, and he'd decided not to mention it, because everybody else was cold too.

That was the thing about floor 2. It wasn't that it was cold. Lots of places were cold. It was that the cold didn't stop. It was there when you woke up, under the furs in the bunk room at the back of the trapper's hall. It was there at breakfast. It was there all day out on the slopes, and it was there at night by the fire, getting into your back while your front cooked. Gabe was a big man and he'd always run warm, and he'd thought, coming up the stair, that he'd be fine. He hadn't been fine. The coat from Hearthstead was thin wool. It was meant for an autumn on the green floor, not for this.

He could have bought a fur, like everybody else. He'd meant to. But the trapper wanted most of a week's wolf money for one, and Gabe had been spending everything he had on iron at Ulla Dray's forge before they came up. He'd been making things. Practising. Getting ready for a shield he couldn't make yet, out of iron he didn't have yet, for someone he couldn't find.

So he'd been wearing Malcolm's spare *in case* fur over the thin coat for two days, and it was too small, and he hadn't said anything.

He didn't say anything on the morning they went to the Mere, either. He just pulled the too-small fur round his shoulders and walked north along the frozen river with the others, and thought about the stair.

***

It had been on the board since yesterday.

*STAIR TO FL 3: THE MERE. N OF PASS, TOP OF THE RIVER VALLEY. LAKE IN CLIFFS, DOOR IN FAR SIDE. (OLD STAG) — HYVRED.*

Hyvred had written it in big cheerful capitals, the way she did everything. Then she'd turned round from the board and seen Gabe standing behind her, reading it, and her face had done something strange. She'd looked at him the way you look at someone when you know something about them that they don't. Then she'd looked away very fast, and said *good luck!* far too brightly, and gone off to the other end of the hall.

She'd been doing that for four days. Every time he came into a room, Hyvred looked at him like that, and then away.

He'd asked her about it, the second time. Just asked her straight out, by the fire: *Is something wrong? Have I done something?*

And Hyvred, who was the most open and honest and talkative person he'd ever met, who cried at painted sunsets and told everybody everything, had gone red to the tips of her golden horns and said: "Ask Deston."

That was all. *Ask Deston.* And then she'd fled.

Gabe hadn't asked Deston. Deston was down on floor 1, with his party, still helping Chris's lot up through the Fold, and Gabe could have whispered him. He hadn't. He'd thought about it, and then he'd thought: if Deston knows something and isn't telling me, it's because he thinks I'll do something stupid with it.

And he'd been honest with himself, for once, and thought: *he's probably right*.

***

The Mere was exactly what Hyvred had written. A perfect round frozen lake at the top of the valley, in a bowl of grey cliffs, white and smooth as a dinner plate. On the far side, at the top of some steps cut into the rock, a door.

In the middle of the ice, standing perfectly still, was the Old Stag.

Gabe looked at it for a long time.

It was white. All of it. Bigger than the ram had been, horse-tall at the shoulder, its coat the white of old snow. Its antlers went up off its head like a winter tree, branching and branching, hung all over with long clear icicles that chimed against each other in the wind like glass. It was standing in the middle of the lake doing nothing at all, breathing slow clouds of steam, looking at nothing with its calm pale eyes.

"He did this on his own," said Gabe.

Nobody said anything.

"Eight days in. On his own. That thing." Gabe pulled the too-small fur tighter. "Wendy's lot tried it with five and Salty went through the ice."

"Wendy's lot didn't have John," said Xav.

John was already crouching on the shore with his staff.

***

It was the strangest thing Gabe had ever seen, and he'd seen a goat that had been punched unconscious.

John sent his lights down into the ice. Not across it, *into* it, one after another. Little points of white light, the size of marbles, going down through the white surface of the Mere the way coins sink into snow. Ten. Fifteen. Twenty, scattered all over the lake. Malcolm stood behind him with a hand on his shoulder, feeding him something warm and steady. Twice, because it was Malcolm.

And the lake lit up.

Not all of it. In patches. Most of the ice took the light and swallowed it, a faint dull glow, nothing. But here and there across the white surface of the Mere, the light went straight *through*. Those patches blazed up blue-white from underneath, like windows in a dark house at night. Big ones, small ones. One enormous one right in the middle, under the Stag's feet.

"Thin," said John, pointing. "There. There. All of those. Don't step on the windows."

"Don't step on the windows," Gabe repeated.

"That's it. That's the whole plan." John grinned up at him from the shore. "Don't step on the windows, and make it step on them instead."

Xav was looking at the lit-up lake with the expression he got when a plan was assembling itself in his head.

"Gabe holds it on the thick ice," he said. "Every charge. Next to a window. When it comes, Gabe goes sideways, once, and it goes in." He looked at Rui Xiang. "When it's in, you're on it. Hit it while it's floundering, then get off before it comes out." He looked at Malcolm. "Gabe's shield. Two of whatever you've got."

"I always put two," said Malcolm.

"I know. Put three."

Malcolm looked offended, and then thoughtful, and then put three.

***

It went the way Xav said, for a while.

Gabe walked out onto the ice with his round shield on his arm, now with an iron rim he'd beaten out at Ulla Dray's forge, his first real piece of work. He stood next to a glowing window, on the thick white, and banged his sword on the rim.

The Stag turned its head. All its icicles chimed. It lowered its great crown of antlers, wide as a cart.

It came.

He'd never stood in front of anything like it. The ram had been a ram. This was a wall of white and bone and ice coming across a frozen lake at him at the speed of a horse. The antlers were so wide there was nowhere to go left or right. He stood there, with Malcolm's three blessings humming in the shield on his arm, and waited. Xav had said wait. *Wait. Wait.*

*Now*, said Xav, quietly, from somewhere behind him.

Gabe went sideways. Once. Not far.

The Stag went onto the window.

It went through. A crack like a gun going off, water fountaining up blue-lit from below, and the Old Stag was in the lake up to its chest, thrashing, its hooves breaking the edge over and over. And Rui Xiang was on it before the water had stopped moving: a blur across the ice, two knives, four, six cuts, gone again before the antlers swung round.

[ The Old Stag · Floundering ]

They did it twice more. Each time Gabe stood next to a window, and each time Xav said *now*, and each time the Stag went through. The third time it stamped first: reared up and brought its hooves down. A line of ice-spikes came shooting up out of the lake at him, waist-high, one after another, straight at him. He got the shield down. The last spike hit it so hard he went backwards four metres across the ice on his back. But Malcolm's third blessing held, and the iron rim held, and *he* held.

Then it threw its head back and bellowed. And the snow came down.

***

It came down all at once, out of the ceiling, and it didn't fall. It *whirled*. Round and round the Mere, faster and faster, until there was nothing anywhere but white. Gabe couldn't see Xav. He couldn't see John on the shore or Malcolm or Rui Xiang. He couldn't see the cliffs, or the door. He couldn't see his own hand in front of his face.

He couldn't see the Stag.

Somewhere close, he heard the icicles chime.

He stood very still. He didn't know which way to go. There was no way to go. There was only white, and cold, and the chiming circling somewhere just out of sight.

And then, through the white, under his feet, he saw them.

The windows. John's lights. They were still down there under the ice, burning, and the whiteout couldn't touch them. They glowed blue-white up through the snow all round him, dim, blurred, but *there*. A great glowing patch just to his left. Another behind him. Another further off.

He couldn't see the Stag. But he could see where it would fall.

He moved. Carefully, step by step, until he was standing right at the edge of the biggest one, the great window in the middle of the lake. He could feel it under the toes of his boots, a faint give, a softness. He planted his feet on the thick ice right beside it and put his shield up.

The chiming stopped.

He heard it turn. Somewhere in the white, very close, he heard the great weight of it shift on the ice, and the long scrape of hooves.

"*Xav*," he said. Not loud. He didn't know where Xav was.

"*I'm here*," said Xav, right behind him. Close enough to touch. Xav had found him by the light too. "*Wait.*"

The Old Stag came out of the white three metres away.

"*Now.*"

Xav's hand hit him in the back, hard, a shove that sent him sideways off his feet. The Stag came through the place where he'd been and onto the great window in the middle of the Mere, and went through the ice like it wasn't there.

It was deep here. Much deeper. It went in up to its neck, its great crowned head above the black water, its hooves finding nothing. And its antlers, its huge, wide antlers, crashed down across the broken edge of the ice and caught there. It was holding itself up by them. Its whole weight hung on that crown of bone, braced across the hole, the icicles shattering. And its pale eyes rolled.

The ice under the antlers was cracking. It was going to slide off.

Gabe didn't think. He dropped his sword and got both hands on the nearest antler. Thick as his own arm, cold as iron, the icicles cutting his palms. He leaned back with everything he had and *held*.

He held its head up out of the water.

He didn't know why. Some part of him had looked at the thing hanging there by its own crown, trying not to drown, and grabbed on. Malcolm's blessing went into his arms. He felt it, hot and humming. And he held a creature the size of a horse up out of a frozen lake by its antlers.

"*Rui Xiang!*"

Rui Xiang came across the ice like something fired out of a gun. Two knives. Under the jaw, where the throat was stretched out white over the edge of the hole.

The Old Stag breathed out. One long, low sound, like wind leaving a house. Its pale eyes looked at Gabe, very calm, from a hand's width away.

He let go.

It went down into the black water slowly, crown last, and the icicles chimed one last time, and the snow stopped.

Gabe sat on the ice with his bleeding hands in his lap and looked at the hole in the middle of the Mere.

The whiteout was gone. The cliffs had come back, grey and close. On the far side of the lake, the door at the top of the steps was grinding open. John was coming across the ice from the shore with his lights still glowing under it like windows, and Malcolm behind him, already reaching for Gabe's hands.

"You held it up," said Xav.

He'd sat down next to Gabe on the ice. He didn't sound surprised. Xav never sounded surprised.

"I don't know why," said Gabe. "It was drowning."

"It was trying to kill you."

"I know. It was still drowning." Gabe looked at his palms, cut by the icicles. "Declan would've—"

He stopped.

"Declan would've done the same," said Xav. "That's what you were going to say."

"Yeah."

"Yeah," said Xav. "He would have."

***

The ascension room was in the cliff behind the door: round and small and dry, with a stone bench round the wall, and a fire-pit, and a stair going up into the dark at the back. The same as the one on floor 1. They went in, the five of them, wet and frozen and bleeding a bit and very, very tired, and a little window said *Ascension Room · Safe*. Malcolm said *finally* and sat down on the floor.

Gabe didn't sit down.

He was looking at the bench.

On the stone bench by the door, where you'd see it first as you came in, there was a coat. It was folded neatly, the way you'd fold a coat you were leaving for someone. Grey wolf fur, inside and out. Long. Stiff with age.

Nobody else had noticed yet. Gabe walked over to it. He didn't touch it at first. He stood over it and looked.

*A wolf-fur coat that stank to high heaven*, Newish had said, at the board, with her hands. *Grey. Down to his knees.*

He picked it up.

It was heavy. Heavier than it looked. It smelled exactly the way Newish said: smoke, and wolf, and old fur, and very faintly, underneath, of tallow. Of candles. Of a tiny hut on a mountain, where an old woman had been carried up a tower in it.

Gabe stood in the ascension room of floor 2, holding Declan's coat.

Behind him the room had gone quiet. They'd all seen. Rui Xiang had stopped in the middle of wiping his knives. Malcolm had looked up from the floor. John had his mouth slightly open, for once with nothing to say.

"He left it," said Gabe.

"He left it for whoever came next," said Xav, quietly.

Gabe looked down at it. Folded. Neat. On the bench by the door, where the next person through would see it first. Not dropped, not abandoned. *Left.*

And the next person through had been Gabe. Of course it had. Gabe always went first. Declan knew that. Declan had known that for eleven years.

"He left it for me," said Gabe.

Xav didn't argue.

Gabe took off Malcolm's too-small fur and handed it back without a word. He took off the thin Hearthstead coat. He put on the wolf coat. It came down to his knees. The shoulders were exactly right, as if it had been cut for him, and it was so warm that for a second his eyes stung and he had to look at the wall.

Four days. He'd been cold for four days and hadn't said anything. And somebody a floor ahead, who'd never once looked back, had folded a coat on a bench, as if he'd known.

"Of course," said Xav, behind him, in a voice of total resignation, "you got the coat."

Gabe turned round. Xav was looking at him with his arms folded and an expression Gabe knew very well: fond and exasperated, the look of a man who'd lost every coin toss to Gabe for years.

"What?" said Gabe.

"Nothing," said Xav. He looked at the coat for a moment longer. "It suits you. That's all. It looks like it was always yours."

Rui Xiang started laughing. Then John. Then Malcolm, from the floor, tired and helpless. Gabe stood in the middle of the ascension room in a dead hunter's coat that smelled of wolf and candles, with his bleeding hands, and found that he was laughing too.

***

Later, when the others were asleep round the fire-pit, Gabe sat on the stone bench by the door in the coat and opened his window.

The whisper box was there. The one he opened every morning. *dec. i know its you.* And further down, *just tell me youre ok. one word. please.* And under it, *sent*.

He opened a new line under it. He typed, with two fingers, slowly:

*found the coat. its warm. thank you*

He looked at it for a long time.

*You don't answer whispers when you're doing that*, John had said, under the great pine, in the snow. *You can't. You'd drop it.*

He thought about Declan reading this, wherever he was. On floor 3, at level ten, with his trial still waiting, holding whatever he was holding with both hands. He'd read *thank you*, and want to answer so badly it hurt, and not be able to. And it would be one more thing on top of everything else he was carrying.

Gabe looked at the line for a long time.

Then he deleted it. Letter by letter.

He closed the window, and sat on the bench in the dark in the wolf coat. It was warm. He looked at the stair going up into the dark at the back of the room, toward floor 3, and didn't go up it.

*Careful.*

He pulled the coat round himself and, for the first time in four days, slept warm.
