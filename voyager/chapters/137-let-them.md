The right leg came out at midnight.

He heard it. They all did. You couldn't hear anything on the Salt, not a voice, not a footstep, not your own breath, but you heard that. It came across three miles of white in the dark like a tree going over in a forest, a long splitting crack and then a groan that went on and on, and under it, through the salt, through the sleds, through the soles of his boots where he stood at the end of the last sled in the middle of the camp, the heart.

*Thump.*

"Forty breaths," said Maigo, by the still. Very quietly.

Nobody slept after that.

They didn't need telling. They'd stood round him and Thea at dusk when the left leg came out, all thirty-four of them, and stayed. The still went on, because the still went on. But nobody lay down. They sat on the salt in their places, the places Higu had written on the rock, with their weapons across their knees, in a ring round the middle, and watched the west.

He watched it with them.

He couldn't see much. Starlight. The white going grey and then black. Three miles off, against the stars, the line of the mast, and beside it two more lines that hadn't been there in the first life, and the hill on top of them. And the neck. Still turned. A white curve against the dark, and at the end of it, the head. He couldn't see the eye from here. He didn't need to. He could feel where it was looking, the way you feel somebody looking at the back of your neck in a room.

Not at him.

Thea sat on the salt at his right with the lantern in her lap and her other hand flat on the crust, and didn't look west.

"Alea," he said. Under his breath.

*It's walking*, said Alea.

***

It came at first light.

He saw it come the way you'd see weather come. A long way off, at the edge of the white, in the grey, something moving that shouldn't. Slowly. Too slowly for something that size, the way the Sower had moved on floor 9 when it stood up for the first time in a thousand years. One leg. A long time. The other.

It walked like a heron.

He'd seen herons on Earth. Everybody had. In parks, on rivers, standing in the shallows on one leg so still you'd think they were posts, and then the step, very slow, lifting the foot out of the water and folding it and putting it down again ahead, as if every step were a decision. This was that. A heron the size of a tower, walking across the Salt toward them in the grey dawn, lifting each white leg out of the crust and folding it at the backward knee and putting it down again ahead, and every time a foot came down, the salt round it cracked in a ring, and he felt it through the sled.

And the salt came off it as it walked.

Plates of it. A thousand years of it, crusted on its body and its wings like armour. Every step shook some loose. They came off in sheets and fell, turning, catching the first light, and broke on the white below, and under them, where they'd been, the thing was white too, but a different white. Feathers. Long and narrow and stiff, the colour of old paper, the colour of a shell. Still crusted, at the edges. Still kept.

The neck was up now. All the way. Higher than the mast it had left behind. And the head at the top of it, with the long spear of a beak, and the pale eye, pointed down. At the camp.

At her.

He'd stood in a lot of formations. Ten years of them. He'd been the front of most.

He wasn't the front of this one.

He was the middle. Higu had put him there with the chalk on the rock and nobody had argued, and he knelt there now, on one knee on the salt, with the lamp shield on his left arm and his sword point down in the crust beside him, and Thea standing at his right shoulder with the lantern in one hand and the other hand already moving, already writing, the way it did before a spell, small fast shapes in the air that left faint lines behind them like text on a window.

Round them, the healers. Rachael and Kei and Xola and Newish and Malcolm and Yan, in a close ring, close enough to touch. Maigo with his foot in what was left in the pot. Deston at the back, standing, with his hands out and his gold already going, thin threads out to everybody, *what one of you has*.

And round the healers, the wall.

Gabe in front. Directly in front of him, between him and the west, with Hearthwall up, the three flames on it glowing gold. Azac on Gabe's left with his shield. Jared on Gabe's right with the thorn-shield, the rose inside its rim. The leg team, Higu called them. Ina, with his mask down already. Winter, with an arrowhead in her teeth. Tiger, and Rui Xiang, and Milli, and Niko with his thumb on the line of tiny letters down his blade. Calvin. Spread out to the left, low, waiting, a long way from the ring.

Dennis, beside Higu, with his hands up and his face set.

The heart beat. Through everything. He felt it in his knee.

"*Thirty breaths*," said Maigo.

"*A mile*," said Hyvred.

He knelt there. He'd never knelt in a fight. Not once. Not in ten years. He'd knelt for the trial on floor 8 in the city by the water, and the window had said *kneel*, and he'd done it, and it had nearly killed him to stay down. He'd knelt on the deck of the *Patience* for an arm the width of a road. Both times he'd been the one holding. The dome. The light.

This time the dome was the last thing. Not the first.

*Not a wall*, said Alea. On his shoulder. *A place.*

"I know."

*You're shaking.*

"I know."

Thea's hand came down. Not the writing one. The other one, with the lantern. She let go of the lantern's handle with two fingers and put them on the back of his neck. Just for a second. Warm.

Then she took them away and went back to writing.

Dennis went first.

That was the plan. Higu's, and Dennis's own, at the aft sled in the grey with the rock between them. *It looks with its eye*, Dennis had said. *Fine. Let's give it something to look at.*

He put his hands out.

And out on the white to the left of the ring, fifty metres off, a woman stood up. With a lantern.

Thea. Exactly Thea. The hair, the face, the long coat, the way she stood. The lantern in her hand, amber, steady. Dennis's Double. He'd made one of Calvin on floor 1, in the Fold, with daggers, so real the Bellwether had hit it three times. He'd been doing it every floor since, better every time. This one walked. Slowly. Away from the ring. Out across the white, with the lantern held up.

And another. To the right. A third, further out.

Three Theas, walking away from the ring in three directions across the Salt in the grey, with three lanterns.

The long white head, a mile off and coming, stopped.

It hung there on the end of the neck. The eye moving. From one lantern to the next. To the next.

"*Yes*," hissed Calvin, somewhere on the left.

The head turned. Toward the double on the left. The neck bent. The great white body leaned. And it came. Faster. Out of the heron walk into something else, longer strides, the legs going out ahead and the feet coming down and the salt cracking in rings, toward the woman with the lantern walking away from the ring on the left.

The beak went down.

Ice had seen herons do that too. On Earth, on a river. The stillness, and then the strike, so fast you didn't see it, only the after, the head back up and something silver in the beak. This was that. The neck went back in a curve like a drawn bow and then the head came down out of the sky like a thrown spear, and the beak went into the double on the white, and through it, into the salt.

The double went white.

Not gone. Not light, the way Dennis's doubles went when they were hit. *White.* Salt. It came up out of the place where the beak had gone in like frost coming up a window, fast, in a rush, up the legs of the double and the coat and the arm and the lantern, and in a breath it was standing there on the Salt crusted from head to foot, white, kept, like the birds round the Mast with their wings out.

Then it went. Back into nothing. Because it was nothing. It had only ever been Dennis.

But the salt stayed. A white crust on the white, in the shape of a woman with a lantern, standing.

Ice heard Dennis make a sound beside Higu. Not a word.

"*That's what it does*," said Hyvred. Very quietly, from the edge. "*It keeps.*"

The head came up. Out of the salt. The beak, empty. It turned.

Not to the second double. Not to the third.

To the ring.

To the middle.

"*It's not looking with its eye*," said Hyvred. "*The dust. The dust never went to them. It's—*"

The Preserved came.

It came across the last half mile in about ten strides.

He watched it. He couldn't not. Kneeling on the salt in the middle with his shield on his arm and every part of him screaming to get up. A heron the size of a tower, with its wings half open now, shedding salt, white feathers and white plates and white dust coming off it in a long trail behind like smoke, and the legs going out and the feet coming down, and the heart, *thump*, through the salt into his knee, so fast now he could count it without Maigo. At the middle.

At her.

It went across the Salt like a bell.

Nobody stopped him. Nobody even tried. He stood up in the middle of thirty-five people in the grey dawn beside a dead heron the size of a tower and shouted *HELLO* at the sky with all the breath he had, and it went out across the white in every direction and came back off the side of the Mast, three miles away, faint, *hello*, and he laughed, out loud, and that came back too, and he put both hands over his face and sat down on the salt and cried.

Ina sat down beside him.

Ice watched them. Calvin crying into his hands, out loud, the first loud thing he'd done in thirty-seven days. Ina beside him, not touching him, because Ina didn't, with his mask pushed up, looking at the sky.

And then he heard the other thing.

Behind Calvin. Behind all of them. Coming in from the west, from past the Mast, from the edge of the world. Low. Soft. A sound he hadn't heard since the top of the four hundred and twelve steps, since the door, since the first morning on the Salt, when he'd stood on a grey rock and couldn't hear himself breathe.

He turned toward it.

They all did.

It came across the white toward them, and he felt it before he heard it properly. On his face. In his hair. Cool. Moving. It lifted the edge of Thea's coat. It lifted the white dust off the salt round the long white body and carried it, east, past them, in a long low drift. It went through the ring, between the tanks, round the middle, and Ina's fires under the steel tent, which had gone out at dawn, stirred in their ash.
