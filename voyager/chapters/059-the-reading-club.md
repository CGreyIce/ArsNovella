Chris had been keeping the page for ninety-seven days, and he'd never shown it to anyone.

It wasn't the graph. Everybody had seen the graph. He'd held it up at the long table on floor 3, and on floor 4, and on Thornback on the hundred-and-second morning, with the one line going up like a ladder. The graph was fine. The graph was *data*. You could show people data.

The page was something else.

He'd started it on the fourteenth day, on floor 1, after Winter said *he knew it was being saved*. He'd written one word at the top in small square capitals, *HOW?*, and under it, *knew XP banks at cap (W)*. And then he'd closed the notebook and not looked at the page again for a week. Because it had felt, writing it, like opening a bug report on a friend.

Then *knew which door (Quill)*. Then *knew about an hour (W, Mother Crane)*. Then *chapel — day 1*. It had gone on like that. A line here. A line there. Never more than one at a time. He didn't let himself think about what the lines added up to. He just wrote them down, the way he'd written down every weird thing a program did for nine years, in a text file called *notes_FINAL_v3_actualfinal.txt*, because the first rule of debugging was: *don't guess. Log it.*

On the hundred-and-eleventh evening, coming back across the bridges from Gull Island, he'd written three more.

*knew about Niko's nosebleed (d17, fl2, six people present).*

*"please stay alive till he comes" (Hessa).*

*"looked at me like he'd been to my funeral" (Hessa).*

And he'd stood in front of Drew's slate beside Niko, and looked at the page, and understood that he couldn't keep it in a notebook any more. Not on his own.

So he went to find Thea.

***

She was where she always was, on floor 5, in the evenings. On the eastern edge of the island outside Tether, sitting with her back against a rope post and her knees up. The little glass lantern was beside her on the grass, and she was looking at the Spindle. The sun was going down behind her, real and red. The needle in the middle of the sky had gone gold on one side and dark on the other.

She had a book open on her knees. A small, cloth-bound, much-handled book. He'd seen her buy it in Reedmoor, on floor 3, at a stall that sold eel-skin purses. She closed it as he came up. Not hurriedly. She just closed it, and put her hand flat on the cover, the way she had with Xav on the high terrace.

He sat down on the grass beside her. He didn't say anything for a while. Neither did she. That was one of the things he liked about Thea. She didn't need you to fill a silence.

"I've got a page," said Chris at last.

She looked at him.

"In my notebook. I've had it since floor 1." He took the notebook out of his belt pouch and held it on his knees, closed. "I've never shown anybody. Not even Winter." He looked at the Spindle. "I think you've got one too."

Thea didn't say anything for a long moment. Then she took her hand off the cover of the cloth-bound book.

"It's not a page," she said. "It's the whole book."

***

They traded.

He gave her his notebook, open at *HOW?*. She gave him her book. Then he sat there on the grass in the last of the light and looked at it and couldn't read a word.

It wasn't in English. It wasn't in anything. It was in an alphabet he'd never seen. Neat, small, angular, like a mix of runes and circuit diagrams, line after line, page after page, the whole book full.

"It's mine," said Thea, without looking up from his page. "I made it up when I was twelve. So nobody could read my diary." She turned a page of his notebook. "I've written in it ever since. Things I don't want anyone to read over my shoulder."

"Thea," said Chris, "I can't read this."

"I know." She held out her hand for it. He gave it back. She opened it on her knee, at the first page, and ran her finger down it, and read it to him out loud. Translating as she went. Slowly. In her quiet, level voice.

"*Day one. The note. 'Be careful, train longer on the third floor.' Written before any of us had seen floor 1. How did he know there was a third floor? How did he know it was the one to train on?*"

Chris went still.

"*Day one. Mother Crane. The onion boy. He knew where the Copper Spoon was before he could have seen it. He was peeling her onions at dawn.*"

"*Day one. Quill. Knew which door. Didn't look at the other two.*"

"*Day one. About an hour. Winter. He knew when we'd get to the class hall.*"

"*Day two. The chapel. Already kept. The first step of a hidden class, on the first night, in a place Quill said nobody goes.*"

She went on. He sat on the grass and listened, with his own notebook open on his knee, and followed her down his page with his finger. And every line she read, he found. Not all of them. She had things he didn't. *Floor 2: Mother Tallow. 'A day early.' He carried her up the tower before the Long Storm. He knew it was coming. Nobody knew it was coming.* She had the coat. She had the lantern on the bench in the Fen, *left lit, ready, for whoever came next, as if he knew we'd need it there.* She had the bright worn place on the high terrace. She had Rui Xiang's week behind the wall. *Why that square? He sat for a week watching one square, before anything happened there. As if he knew where it would happen.*

And he had things she didn't. The banked XP. The graph. Niko's nosebleed. Hessa's funeral face.

But mostly, line for line, they were the same.

Two people. Ninety-seven days. Two books, one in English and one in a made-up alphabet a twelve-year-old had invented so nobody could read her diary. Neither of them had ever shown the other. And they'd written down the same things.

Thea stopped reading. She closed the book.

The sun had gone. The Spindle was a dark needle against a sky going from red to violet. Out over the Reaches, the first real stars were coming out, one by one, too many.

"So it's not just me," said Thea quietly.

"It's not just you."

She let out a breath. A long one. As if she'd been holding it since floor 1.

***

"Okay," said Chris. "Okay. So. Bug report."

Thea looked at him.

"It's how I think," he said. "Sorry. It's how I've always thought. When something's wrong you don't guess. You write it down properly." He turned his notebook to a clean page and wrote, in his small square capitals:

*EXPECTED BEHAVIOUR:*

"Expected," he said, writing. "A guy wakes up in a white room the same minute we did. He knows what we know. Which is nothing. He works stuff out as he goes, like us. Maybe faster, because he's Ice, and he's always been good at games. But he's working it out."

*ACTUAL BEHAVIOUR:*

"Actual," said Chris. He stopped writing. He looked at the two words for a long time. "He knows what's going to happen. Before it happens. Not *stuff*. Specific things. A storm. A thing on a roof. Which floor to train on. Where a hidden class starts. Which old woman needs to be told to stay alive." He tapped the pencil on the page. "And on floor 5, for the first time, he got it wrong. Two weeks. Two shields. The first floor he ever struggled on."

"Because something on floor 5 wasn't what he expected," said Thea.

Chris looked up.

"*Expected*," he said.

Thea didn't say anything.

He looked at her face. She had the taking-it-apart face on, the one she used for windows, reading the words under the words. And under that face, he thought, there was something else. Something she'd taken apart already, all the way down, and found at the bottom, and wasn't saying.

"You've got a theory," he said.

"I've got a feeling," said Thea. She looked out at the Spindle. "It's not a theory until I can say it without it frightening me."

Chris looked at her for a long time.

Then he nodded, and wrote nothing at all under *ACTUAL BEHAVIOUR*, and closed the notebook.

"Okay," he said. "Then we need more data."

***

They needed more people. That was obvious. Thea said so first.

"Hyvred," she said, on the walk back into Tether in the dark. "She can see where he's been. Nobody else can."

"Kei," said Chris. "And Hako. They read the same stories. Those manhwa, the tower ones. If this looks like something, they'll know what it looks like."

"And Winter," said Thea.

Chris stopped walking.

"Winter," he said, "is going to kill us."

"Winter," said Thea, "is going to kill us *more* if she finds out last."

They looked at each other in the dark on the bridge.

"Tomorrow," said Chris. "After the others. When we've got something to show her."

"She'll still kill us."

"She'll kill us less."

***

They met on the hundred-and-twelfth evening, at the back table of the inn. The one in the corner by the kitchen door, where nobody sat because it smelled of fish. Chris, Thea, Hyvred, Kei with Dose round his neck, and Hako, who'd come because Kei came and because, he said, *anything with a secret table is automatically a good time*.

Thea read them her book. Chris read them his page. It took an hour.

When it was done, nobody said anything for a while.

Hyvred was the first. She had her elbows on the table and her chin in her hands, and her golden horns caught the lamplight. Her eyes were wet.

"It goes small," she said, quietly. "His light. When he's frightened. I told Rui Xiang that on floor 4. Behind the wall, it was small for a week." She looked at the table. "But on floor 1, in the ascension room, on the bench. The night he cleared it. It was *huge*. It went right up the wall. Like a man who'd done something he'd been frightened for a long time that he wouldn't manage." She looked up. "That's not how you feel when you clear a floor you've never seen. That's how you feel when you clear a floor you were scared you'd *fail* again."

The table was quiet.

"*Again*," said Hako.

"I don't know," said Hyvred. "It's just what it looked like."

Kei had been sitting very still, with one hand on Dose's head. The little green snake had her eyes shut. She'd been quiet all evening.

"There's something I never told anybody," said Kei. "From floor 2. Dose said it. I didn't know what it meant." He hesitated. "She said, *tell her she's not the only one who sees him.* That's all. She wouldn't say who *her* was. She wouldn't say anything else about it."

Everybody looked at Hyvred. Then, after a second, everybody looked at Thea.

Thea looked at the snake.

Dose opened one eye, looked back at her, and shut it again.

"Well, *that's* not ominous," said Hako.

***

It was Hako who said what everyone was thinking, in the end. He leaned back in his chair with his arms folded and his long legs stretched out under the fish-smelling table, and said it like the end of a joke.

"Okay. So in a story. If this was one of ours." He looked at Kei. "A guy turns up in a tower with everybody else. And he knows things before they happen. He knows where the hidden stuff is. He knows which old lady needs saving." He counted on his fingers. "It's one of about three things."

"Hako," said Thea quietly.

"I'm just saying what it is in a story." He held up one finger. "One. Better system. His light's smarter than ours. It talks to him. Tells him things. Ours don't." He looked at Hyvred. "You saw something curled up next to him on the bench. And Nan Gully said a light by his ear. And Leaka thinks it's called Alea. Maybe it's not a pet. Maybe it's a *guide*."

Chris wrote it down. *1. better system (talks?)*

"Two," said Kei, before Hako could. He'd caught it now. His voice had gone fast, the way it did when he got interested. "Somebody told him. Somebody who'd been here before." He looked round the table. "Nan Gully's song. *They came up the stair with their lights in their hands.* Hessa said there were knights who read, long ago. *Came up the stair with lights.* There were people here before us. Lots of them. Ages ago." He leaned forward. "What if one of them left something. Notes. A map. A guide. And he found it. On day one. In the white room, or the class hall, or—"

"A README," said Chris.

Everybody looked at him.

"A README," he said again. He felt his face go strange. "That's what that is. When somebody builds something and leaves a file for the next person. *Here's how it works. Here's where the bugs are. Don't touch floor 4.*" He looked down at his page. At *train longer on the third floor*. "It'd explain the note. It'd explain the chapel. It'd explain the storm, if they'd seen one. It'd explain all of it."

"It wouldn't explain floor 5," said Thea.

Chris looked at her.

"If he had a guide," said Thea, "why did floor 5 take him two weeks and two shields?"

"Maybe the guide was wrong about floor 5," said Kei. "Maybe things change. It's been a long time since whoever wrote it."

Thea didn't answer that. She looked at her book.

"What's three?" said Hyvred. "You said three things."

Hako opened his mouth.

He looked at Thea.

Thea was looking at him. Not hard. Not angrily. Just steadily, across the table, with her hand flat on the cover of her book. And Hako, who never in his life had stopped himself from saying a funny thing, closed his mouth.

"Three's dumb," he said. "Three's only in the really dumb ones. Forget three."

Nobody pushed. Chris didn't know why. He just knew that nobody at the table wanted to push, and that Thea had very slightly let out her breath.

He looked down at what he'd written. *1. better system. 2. README / earlier players.* And under it, *3.* and nothing.

He left it like that.

***

"So what are we?" said Hako, at the end, when it was late and the fish smell had got into everything. "If we're doing this. A secret society? Do we get a handshake?"

"We're not a secret anything," said Thea. "Drew's going to know. And Deston."

"No more pocket," said Chris.

"No more pocket. Anything that's a *fact*, it goes on the slate. Where everybody sees it." Thea put her hand on her book. "But this isn't facts. Not yet. It's lists. And a feeling. And guesses. You don't put guesses about a friend on a slate in front of thirty-five people." She looked round at them. "So we just read. Together. And when it's more than guesses, everybody hears."

"*Read*," said Hako. He looked round the table. Chris with his notebook. Thea with her book in her made-up alphabet. Hyvred, who read light. Kei and Hako, who'd read a thousand chapters of stories about towers. "Oh my *days*. We're a reading club."

Kei snorted. Hyvred laughed out loud, the first time all evening, bright and sudden.

"The reading club," said Hako, delighted. "Meets in the corner by the fish. Snacks provided by nobody. Membership: weird."

"Membership: five," said Chris.

"Six," said Thea. "Tomorrow."

Chris winced.

***

Winter found out on the hundred-and-fifteenth morning. Not tomorrow, because tomorrow had been the night of the storm. Ina had gone up the Kitecliff for his trial and Winter had sat at the bottom of the ladder all night in the sleet, and nobody had had the heart.

She came in from the bridges at dawn, soaked through, with ice in her silver hair and her face white with cold. Ina was behind her with his mask down. Chris was at the corner table with Thea and Hyvred and Kei and Hako and two notebooks. He hadn't slept either. None of them had. They'd sat up all night by the window watching a white light burn on top of the Kitecliff through the storm, talking in low voices, because nobody could sleep with that light up there.

Winter went straight past them to the kitchen to shout about eggs.

"Winter," called Chris. "When you've eaten. We need you."

"What for?" Her voice came back through the door, already chewing.

He looked at Thea. Thea nodded.

"Club meeting," said Chris.

There was a long pause from the kitchen.

Then Winter came back out. Slowly. With a plate in one hand and a fork in the other and her silver hair dripping on the floor. She looked at the corner table. At five people with two notebooks, and the look on their faces of people who'd been up all night doing something together.

"*Club*," she said.

"Winter—"

"How long?"

Chris looked at Thea.

"Four days," said Thea. "Since the night Niko came back from Gull Island." She held Winter's eye. "I'm sorry. We wanted to have something before we told you. And then the storm."

Winter stood there with her plate.

Chris knew that look. He'd known it since floor 1. *I hate it when people find things out last. It's the worst bug there is.* He'd seen it the night she made Hyvred go and get Deston's promise. He'd seen it a dozen times since. He had a horrible feeling he was about to see the worst of it.

Winter pulled out the sixth chair, sat down in it, put her plate on the table, and started eating.

"Go on, then," she said, through eggs. "From the start. All of it."

They told her. All of it. It took an hour again. She ate the whole time, steadily, and didn't interrupt once, which for Winter was so unusual that by the end Hako was visibly worried.

When they'd finished, she put her fork down.

"Better system," she said. "Or a README."

"Those are the guesses," said Chris.

"Those are *your* guesses." Winter turned and looked at Thea. Bluntly. Straight at her, the way she looked at everything, the way she'd looked at Ina on floor 2 and said *put it on more*. "What's yours?"

Thea didn't say anything.

"You've got one," said Winter. "You've got a word for it. I can see it on your face. You've had it for weeks. You're not saying it."

The table was very quiet.

"Not yet," said Thea.

"Why not?"

"Because it's a feeling," said Thea. "And if I say it out loud and it's wrong, I'll have put something on him that isn't true. In front of everybody." Her hand was flat on her book. "And if I say it and it's *right*—" She stopped. "Then it means something I'm not ready for it to mean. About what he's been doing. About what it's cost." She looked down. "About what it's been like for him."

Winter looked at her for a long time.

Then she picked her fork back up.

"Then you tell me the day it's a fact," she said. "The day. Not the day after. Not when you're ready. The *day*." She pointed the fork at Thea. "I'm not finding this out last. Not this one."

"The day," said Thea. "I promise."

"Good." Winter went back to her eggs. "Then I'm in. Six. Somebody tell me what the secret handshake is."

"There isn't one," said Chris.

"There *should* be one," said Hako.

***

They went to Drew that afternoon, all six, and told him. Not the lists. Not the guesses. Just that it existed. A reading club. Six people who were keeping notes on Voyager, and would bring anything that was a fact to the slate.

Drew looked at the six of them for a long time with his general's frown.

"Is this a pocket?" he said.

"No," said Thea. "It's a notebook. A pocket's when you hide something. We're telling you we've got a notebook."

Drew thought about that.

"Bring me facts," he said. "Not feelings. Not stories. The day you've got a fact, it goes on the slate, and everybody hears it the same day." He looked at Winter. "She'll make sure."

"I will," said Winter.

"Then fine." Drew turned back to the slate. Then he stopped, and turned round again, and looked at Thea. Only at Thea.

"Is it bad?" he said quietly.

Thea was quiet for a moment.

"I don't know yet," she said. "I think it's sad."

Drew looked at her a while longer. Then he nodded once and went back to the slate.

***

They went up on the hundred-and-twentieth day.

Everybody had done their trials by then. All thirty-five, through the padlock at twenty-five, on floor 5, one by one, easy or normal or hard. Ina's white light on the Kitecliff in the storm was the one people would talk about for years. Chris did his own on the hundred-and-seventeenth, the normal one, and came back with a migraine and the tier name *Sentinel* and nothing much else to say. They killed the Squall again in the morning, on the crown, among its three dead selves. Gabe stood in the middle of the crown leaning on the wind, with the finished shield on his back for the first time, unwrapped. Dark grey with a lamp in the middle and a rim the colour of old blood. He didn't use it. He used his own. The other one stayed on his back, the whole fight, like something he was carrying for somebody else.

Pell's people flew all afternoon. The boy did loops over Ina's head and wouldn't stop.

And then they went up the stair in the arch, in a long line. Calvin first, Rachael on his collar, Drew last.

Chris counted. Everybody did. Two hundred and eighty.

Somewhere around a hundred and fifty, Thea fell into step beside him on the dark stair, with the lantern. The amber light went up the steps in front of both of them.

"Chris," she said quietly.

"Yeah."

"Hyvred says his marks are on every step." She didn't look at him. "Still bright. Twenty-four days. Brighter than on four."

"Meaning?"

"Meaning he hasn't gone far," said Thea. "Meaning he's still up there. On six. He hasn't gone up." She was quiet for a few steps. "For the first time since the white room, he hasn't gone up before we came."

Chris thought about that. About the graph, and the line, and the shape of it. About floor 5, and two weeks, and two shields. *Something on floor 5 wasn't what he expected.*

"Something's stopped him," he said slowly.

"Yes."

"On six."

"Yes."

They climbed. *Two hundred and sixty. Two hundred and seventy.* Ahead of them, at the front of the line, Calvin's voice went up the stair, quieter than it used to be, steady. *Look at my back. Nobody runs.*

*Two hundred and eighty.*

The door opened. The air came down the stair to meet them. Hot and dry and enormously bright, and smelling of nothing at all.
