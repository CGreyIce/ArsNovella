Hako saw him first. He'd think about that for a long time afterwards, too. That out of ten people, it was him.

He was standing in the still place in the middle of the bowl. Three hundred metres off, maybe. Between them and the blue water. Just standing there on the glass in the gold morning light, with his weight on one hip, the way he always stood when he was waiting for somebody to stop being slow.

Black hair, messy, falling over his eyes. A streak of teal through it on one side. A white shirt with the sleeves rolled up and a black vest over it, the one with the teal tabs. Hako had seen that outfit a thousand times on a screen, years before the white room. It was the character Ice streamed as. The one on his emotes, the one in his profile picture. The one Hako had seen in the corner of a hundred calls while Ice taught him how to play the kind of shooter he'd always been terrible at. *No, Hako. Stop. You're peeking like a man walking into a supermarket. Again.*

And now he was standing in the middle of the bowl in the real sun, looking straight at them. And he grinned. That stupid grin. And put his arm up over his head and waved it.

"*HAKO*," he shouted.

It was his voice. It was exactly his voice. Not a voice like it. *His*. The voice from a thousand calls, a little hoarse, laughing at itself before it finished the sentence.

"*Took you lot long enough!*"

Hako's spear was in his hand. His shield was on his arm. He didn't remember deciding anything.

He ran.

***

He wasn't the only one. He saw that much, out of the corners of his eyes, in the first two strides.

Drew was running. Drew, who'd spent a hundred and twenty-three days being the calm one, the general, the one who said *nobody runs*. He was running across the glass with his mouth open and his knuckles still on, and his face had gone completely young. Hyvred was making a sound Hako had never heard her make, a sort of high, broken cry, and running. Kei was running, with Dose's head up and hissing something in his ear that he wasn't listening to. Higu, Higu who never hurried, was running with her bow still in her hand.

Chi had both hands over her mouth and was running.

Bee was running and saying something, over and over, very quietly. Hako heard it as she went past. "*The lamp's on. The lamp's on. I left the lamp on—*" He didn't understand it. He didn't try.

And Gigi.

Gigi was out in front of all of them. Somehow. Pale and sick and stubborn, the one who should have been lying down in a room in Deepwell. She was running faster than any of them, straight at the still place. And she wasn't saying *Ice*.

"*Thea*," Gigi was saying. "*Thea. Thea, wait—*"

Hako heard that, too. It went into his head and sat there and he didn't look at it. Because Ice was right there, two hundred metres away now, grinning, and his arms were open. Like he was going to hug somebody. Like he was finally going to stop running and let somebody catch him.

*Took you lot long enough.*

"*STOP!*"

The voice came from behind him. A woman's voice, cracked, as loud as it could go.

"*STOP! Don't—it's not him! It's NOT HIM! Look at the LIGHT!*"

Hako didn't stop.

He'd think about that too, later. On a lot of nights. That he heard her, and knew her voice, and kept running anyway. Because the voice in front of him was Ice's. And what was Thea's voice against that?

Then she went past him.

***

He'd never seen anybody run that fast. Not on any floor. Not Tiger. Not Calvin with Slipstream.

Thea came past him on his left side, close enough to touch, running flat out across the glass with her ponytail streaming out behind her. She was holding the little glass lantern up in front of her at arm's length, high, like a torch. The amber light swung with every step. And as she went past him, the light from the lantern swept across his face.

And then across the thing in front of them.

Hako saw it.

Just for a second, while the light was on it. Ice went *thin*. Not faded. Thin, like a picture of Ice painted on a pane of glass. Flat. He was still grinning, still had his arms open, but there was nothing behind the grin. Nothing in the eyes. It was a drawing of a man, very good, hanging in the air.

And behind it, through it, where the light fell, there was a *hole*.

He couldn't have described it better than that, afterwards. A hole in the morning. A place where the gold light and the hot moving air just stopped, the way a window stops, with an edge. A shape cut out of the day, about as tall as a man, standing upright on the glass. And its edges were sharp. He could see that. Sharp the way the edge of a broken bottle is sharp. Glittering. And they were moving. Slowly. Reaching.

Reaching for Gigi.

She was right there. Out in front of all of them, ten metres from it, with her arms out, saying *Thea, Thea*. Running toward something she was seeing that wasn't there. Into the edge of a hole in the air.

And Thea, the real one, with the lantern, was five metres behind her.

Then three.

Then she was there.

She didn't stop. She hit Gigi from the side, hard, both arms, the way you'd tackle somebody off a road. The lantern went out of her hand and spun away across the glass. Gigi went down sideways onto the dune. And the edge of the hole came down where Gigi had been.

Through Thea.

Hako saw it go into her. Across her left shoulder, and down her upper arm. A long, glittering edge, like a pane of glass falling. He heard the sound it made. Not a slash. A *tink*, very small, like a glass touching another glass. And then Thea was on the ground beside Gigi, on her side. Red was coming out of her shoulder onto the gold glass, fast. A lot of red.

She didn't scream. She made a small sound, through her teeth, and put her other hand over it.

And the lantern lay on the glass two metres away, on its side, still lit.

***

Hako was inside the light.

He hadn't meant to be. He'd been running, and the lantern had rolled, and the amber glow from it lay on the glass in a circle about five metres across. He'd run straight into it.

And inside it, there was no Ice.

There was the hole. Standing over Thea and Gigi on the ground, with its glittering edges. There was the blue water behind it, and the ring of black rocks. And there was nothing else. No grin. No waving arm. No voice. Just a gap in the day, shaped like a person, with edges like broken glass, standing over two women on the ground. And bending down.

Hako's body did the only thing it had ever really been trained to do.

He went in front.

He didn't think about it. He didn't think *Thea* or *Gigi* or anything. Something in his legs that had been learning it since floor 1 just took him there. He stepped over Thea and planted his feet on the glass between her and the hole, with his shield up and his spear out. Like a door. Like a wall. The way Gabe stood. The way everybody who'd ever tanked for the thirty-five stood, because they'd all learned it, one way or another, from the same person.

The hole came at him.

It didn't swing. It didn't strike. It just *came*, the whole of it at once, the way glass comes when a window falls out of a building. A dozen edges. He got the shield up. The edges hit it.

His shield was good. Steel, with an iron rim, the best Jared and Salty could make on floor 4. The hole went through the rim like it was paper. He felt it go. A long, high shriek of glass on iron, and then the top third of his shield just wasn't there. It was falling away in two pieces onto the dune. And an edge went past his face so close he felt the air of it on his cheek, and took a lock of his hair with it.

He drove his spear into the middle of it.

The spear went through. All the way. Through the hole and out of the far side into the air beyond. He felt nothing. No weight. No resistance. Like stabbing a doorway. He pulled it back and there was nothing on it.

"*It's not there*," he heard himself say. "*It's not there, there's nothing to hit—*"

The edges came again.

Something hit him in the chest and he was on his back on the glass, outside the light. Outside the circle of amber, in the plain gold morning.

And Ice was standing over him.

Grinning. Black hair, teal streak, white shirt, arms open.

"*Hako*," said Ice, warmly. "*Hey. Hey, it's fine. It's always fine, right? Come on. Get up. Took you long enough.*"

*It's always fine.* That was Hako's line. Hako said that. Hako had said it on floor 2 with frost-adder venom in his blood and his voice gone, lying in the snow, and Kei had saved him with a drop and a half of the same poison in honey. *See? Told you. Fine.*

Ice didn't say that. Ice had never once said that.

Hako lay on the glass and looked up at the face of his friend, grinning down at him, saying Hako's own words in Ice's voice. And understood, all at once, with a cold that went right through the heat, what it was.

It was made of him. Of what he wanted. And it had used the words it had to hand. *His* words.

"*Look*." A voice. Slow. Very slow. Very calm. Coming through everything, through the heat and the shouting and Ice's warm voice. "*At. The. Lantern.*"

Chi.

She was on her knees on the glass a little way off, with her hands down flat in front of her. She was saying it slowly, every word on its own. The way Hush worked: *say it slowly, they'll hear you through anything.*

"*Look. At. The. Lantern. Everyone. Now.*"

Hako turned his head. Away from Ice. Toward the amber glow, five metres off, lying on its side on the glass.

And in the edge of its light, he saw the hole again. Standing over Thea.

***

That was when the man came down the north slope.

Hako saw him because he was lying on his back with his head turned, and the north slope was right there in front of his eyes. A long gold slope of glass going up to the rim of the bowl. And coming down it, fast, a figure in white.

He didn't look like anything Hako had ever seen. That was the first thing. On every floor, they'd been the only players, and they all knew each other's shapes. This was a shape Hako didn't know. Tall-ish, slim, in a long cloak of grey-white that streamed out behind him as he ran. Light as paper, the cloak. It moved like snow. On his head, a helm. Closed. White. Smooth, almost silver in the sun, with no face at all. Just a narrow dark slit across the eyes, and a ridge up the middle like the keel of a boat. A sword in his right hand. And on his left arm, of all things, a shield that looked like the lid off a barrel. Round, black, tarred wood with iron bands. Ugly as anything.

He was running down a slope of loose glass in the heat of the day like it was flat ground.

And he wasn't looking up.

That was what Hako saw. That was what stuck. The man was running straight at the hole, straight at the middle of the bowl, toward ten people and a thing that was eating them. And his head was down. Not looking at it. Looking at the glass in front of his feet, the whole way. As if he knew what he'd see if he looked up. As if he'd tried looking before.

He came down onto the floor of the bowl and across it in a straight line. Not toward the hole. Toward *Thea*. As if there was a rope between them and he was following it.

He ran into the circle of amber light from the lantern.

And then, and only then, he looked up.

***

Hako would spend a long time, afterwards, trying to describe what happened next. To Kei, that night. To the club, later. To himself, lying awake. He was a tank. He'd been a tank for a hundred and twenty-three days, and before that he'd been the guy in calls who was always slightly behind, slightly late, slightly not getting it. Until Ice had sat with him for hours and said *again* and *no* and *again*, until he got it. He knew what fighting looked like. He knew what good looked like.

He'd never seen anything like it.

The man in white looked up at the hole in the lantern light. Hako saw him see it. Saw the helm go very still for half a second.

And then the hole went for Thea again. She was on the ground, holding her shoulder, still inside the circle. It bent down over her with its dozen glittering edges, the way it had bent over Gigi.

The man was already there.

Hako didn't see him move. He saw him *be* somewhere else. One moment he was at the edge of the light, three metres off. The next he was over Thea, in front of her, between her and it. Not planted. Not braced. Not standing like a door, the way Hako had stood, the way everybody stood. He was *moving*. All the time. Small and constant. His weight shifting, leaning, as if he were standing on the deck of a boat in a swell. His feet barely moved at all.

The edges came down. All of them, at once, the way they'd come at Hako.

The man didn't block them. He *leaned*. Hako saw it. He leaned into them, sideways, so that the barrel lid was angled to the glass, and the edges didn't hit it. They *slid* off it. Off the curve of the tarred wood, the way rain slides off a roof. A long, grating shriek. Chips of wood flew. The barrel lid split down the middle with a crack like a gunshot, and the man went down on one knee with it. And the edges went past him, both sides, and didn't touch Thea at all.

And Hako, lying on his back on the glass outside the light, heard him say something.

Low. Muffled, through the helm. Not shouted. Just said, quietly, to the thing in front of him. The way you'd say something you'd been waiting to say for a very long time.

"*Not this time.*"

Then he moved once.

Up off his knee, through the gap where the edges had gone past, into the hole. Not swinging. The sword went in point first, short, low, at a place in the middle of the hole that Hako hadn't seen until the sword was already in it. A tiny thing. A glint. A single point of something real in the middle of all that nothing, like a speck of glass in the eye. The only bit of the hole that wasn't a hole.

The sword found it.

The hole broke.

It broke the way a window breaks. All at once, with a sound so loud Hako felt it in his teeth. A great bright shattering. And then glass was falling. Real glass. Shards of it, everywhere, in a ring, raining down onto the gold glass of the bowl and bouncing and skittering. Little and big, pieces like teeth, pieces like knives. They fell round the man in white, and round Thea at his feet, and none of them touched either of them.

Then it was quiet.

Hako lay on his back on the glass and turned his head. Where Ice had been standing over him, there was nobody. Just the gold morning. The hot air, moving again everywhere, shimmering, the way it was supposed to.

From somewhere behind him, Hyvred started to cry.

***

The man in white was kneeling over Thea.

Hako sat up. He couldn't do anything else. Kei was beside him suddenly, from somewhere, with his hand on Hako's shoulder. Neither of them said anything. They sat on the glass outside the circle of amber light and watched.

The man had put the broken halves of the barrel lid down. He was on one knee beside Thea. She was on her back now, with her right hand clamped over her left shoulder and blood coming out between her fingers, a lot of it. Her face had gone white. Her eyes were open. She was looking up at him. At the slit of the helm.

He was looking down at her. Hako couldn't see his face. There was no face to see. Just the smooth white helm with its dark slit, bent toward her.

He put his hand out toward her shoulder. His left hand. Bare. Hako saw something on the palm of it as it came out of the shadow of the cloak. A line. A raised, pinkish line, right across the middle of it, from the thumb to the edge.

The hand stopped. A few centimetres from her shoulder. It hung there.

It was shaking.

And then Xola was there. Running in from the edge of the light, with water already curling round her fingers. She dropped to her knees on Thea's other side, and pushed Thea's hand off the wound and put her own over it. And the water went in. The man's hand drew back, slowly, and closed on nothing.

"*Babe*," Xola was saying. Very fast, very high. "*Babe, babe, I've got you, hold still, I've got it, I've got it—*"

Thea didn't look at Xola. She was still looking up at the slit of the helm.

And something moved on the man's shoulder.

Hako almost missed it. He was looking at Thea, at the blood, at Xola's hands. But it was right there in the edge of his vision, in the amber light. On the man's right shoulder, where nothing had been. Something small. A shape made of light. Not a ball. Not one of the soft white balls of light that sat by all thirty-five of them, the systems, the lights. This was a *shape*. Teal and white. With pointed ears. A long tail curled round. A narrow face. Two tiny white pinprick eyes.

A fox. A little fox made of light, sitting on his shoulder. It looked down at Thea.

Just for a second. One second. And Thea looked back at it, and her face did something Hako would never be able to describe.

Then the fox was gone. As if it had never been there.

Behind him, very quietly, Hako heard Bee's charcoal start to move.

***

The man stood up.

He picked up the broken halves of his barrel lid. He didn't look at any of them. Not Hako, not Kei, not Drew, who was standing frozen on the edge of the light with his hands open. Not Hyvred, crying. Not Gigi, sitting up on the dune with her face wet, staring at Thea. He looked once more down at Thea, with Xola's hands on her shoulder. For one long moment.

Then he turned round and ran.

North. Back the way he'd come. Up the long gold slope of the bowl, fast, with his head down and the white cloak streaming out behind him.

"*WAIT!*" Drew was shouting. "*Wait—Ice—ICE—*"

He didn't wait. He didn't look back. He went up the slope and over the north rim and was gone. There was the gold glass and the white sky, and nothing on the slope at all but a line of footprints going up it, filling slowly with sliding glass.

"It's him," said Hyvred.

She was standing in the middle of the bowl, where she'd stopped running, with tears running down her face. She was looking at the north slope.

"It's his light," she said. "The lamp. The same one. It's all over the slope, it's all over *here*." She pointed at the place where he'd knelt over Thea. "It's him, Drew. It's *him*. It's the light I've been following since floor 1. It went *enormous*. When he got here. When he saw her. It went up like a fire." Her voice broke. "And then it went so small. When he stood up. The smallest I've ever seen it. Smaller than the cistern."

Drew didn't say anything. He was standing very still on the edge of the light, looking at the north rim.

Gabe had stayed in Deepwell. So that he wouldn't run.

Hako thought about that and had to look away.

***

Afterwards, when Xola had stopped the bleeding, and Higu and Drew had made a shade out of cloaks and a spear and Hako's half-shield over Thea, and Chi had got Gigi to stop crying, and everybody was sitting in the gold bowl among the shards of broken glass with nothing to say, Hako found himself sitting next to Kei.

On the glass. A little way off from the others. With his ruined shield across his knees.

Kei had Dose round his neck. The little green snake had her head up and her tongue out, tasting the air where the hole had been. She hadn't said anything to Kei since it broke. Kei hadn't asked.

"Kei," said Hako.

"Yeah."

"The club. On five. In the corner by the fish." Hako was looking at his shield, the top third of it sheared clean off. "I said there were three things. In a story. And I didn't say three."

"I remember."

"Thea looked at me," said Hako, "and I didn't say it."

"I remember."

Hako was quiet for a while.

"He moved like Ice," he said. "The man. In the helm. He moved like Ice taught me to move. All of it. The way you check before you go in. The way you don't stand in one place. The way you wait and then go *once*." He swallowed. "Except he did it better than anybody I've ever seen. Better than Ice ever did it. Better than *anybody*."

Kei didn't say anything.

"Kei, I've done this a hundred and twenty-three days," said Hako. "Every day. Tanking. Since floor 1. I'm *good* now. I'm actually good." He looked at the place where the man had knelt. "And that's not a hundred and twenty-three days. You don't move like that in a hundred and twenty-three days. Not with a bonus. Not with a guide. Not with anything." He was very quiet. "That's ten thousand hours. Minimum. That's somebody who's done nothing else, every day, for years."

"Years," said Kei.

"Years."

They sat in the heat among the broken glass.

"*Not this time*," said Kei, very softly.

"You heard it."

"Everybody inside the light heard it." Kei looked across at Thea, under the shade of cloaks, with her eyes shut and Xola's hands still on her shoulder. "She heard it."

Hako looked at her too.

"*This* time," said Hako. "As in. There was another one."

Neither of them said anything for a long time.

Then Hako said it. Out loud. On the glass. The word he hadn't said in the club, because Thea had looked at him. The word every single one of those stories had in it. The one they'd both read a thousand chapters about, on their phones, on buses, in bed at two in the morning, in another world.

"Regressor," said Hako.

Kei closed his eyes.

"Yeah," he said.
