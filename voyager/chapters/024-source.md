Thea had been reading her window for five days, and on the sixth day it finally told her something.

Everyone else used their windows the way you'd use a phone: open it, check the thing, close it. Level, health, rankings, done. Thea had never in her life used anything that way. She was the kind of person who took things apart to find out where the voices were coming from, and read the code under a page to find out why it was pretty. And she'd spent her first morning in the Spire sitting in a corner of the Threshold, poking at her window one line at a time while everybody else panicked, because something about it had felt *wrong*.

Not broken. *Built*. Like a page whose source she couldn't see yet.

She'd been poking at it ever since. Every night, on the bed she shared with Xola in the inn, after the others had gone to sleep. Every morning, at the long table in the Copper Spoon, with Mother Crane muttering about Climbers who read at breakfast. She'd found the rankings tab before anyone, though she hadn't said so; Chris had got there in front of everybody and she'd let him have it. She'd found the chat tab, the settings nobody had noticed, the tiny arrow that folded the skill list away.

And on the sixth morning, sitting on a wall on the north fields with Higu and PenguChi resting beside her, she found the lock.

***

It was in the rankings. It was so small she'd almost missed it.

[ Rankings
1. Voyager · Lv 10 · Knight · Floor 2
]

She'd been looking at that line every morning, like everybody. Like Gabe, sitting at the long table with his porridge going cold. Like Hana, touching the iron rose in her crown. Like Yan, who'd gone up to floor 2 that morning with his whole party, and who'd stood at the bottom of the valley road looking up at it for a long time before he went.

*Lv 10*. It had said *Lv 10* yesterday evening too. It hadn't moved all night, and it hadn't moved all morning.

And next to the *10*, very small, so small it was almost part of the number, there was a mark. A tiny shape, like the head of a key. Like a padlock.

Thea leaned in.

She'd learned, over five days, that the windows did something if you looked at them hard enough. Not just looked: *read*, the way she read code. Line by line, asking each one what it was for. If you asked a window a question in that way, it sometimes answered. Lines opened up under lines. Small grey text she was fairly sure nobody else could see unfolded underneath the white, like the comments a programmer leaves in the margin for whoever comes next.

She looked at the tiny padlock and asked it, very clearly, in her head: *What are you?*

The line opened.

[ Voyager · Lv 10 · Knight · Floor 2
    — held: Tier trial required
]

Thea sat very still on the wall.

"Thea?" said PenguChi, beside her. Chi's voice was slow and calm and steady, the voice that had talked half her chat through bad nights. "You've got the face."

"What face?"

"The taking-it-apart face."

Thea had been told about that face before, by more than one person, on more than one late call. She was a little surprised Chi had noticed it already.

"Chi," she said. "Look at this. Look at the number next to his name."

Chi leaned over and looked at Thea's window, which she could see, because Thea had tipped it. People could only see your window if you let them.

"Ten," said Chi.

"It's not just ten. There's a lock on it. A tiny one, look, next to the zero." Thea opened her own window beside it and started pulling at her own level line, the way she'd just pulled at his. "He's not stuck. He's not hurt. He's *capped*. There's a—hang on—"

She asked her own level what it was.

[ Althea · Lv 6 · Tier 1: Neophyte · Mage
    — Tier 1: levels 1–10
    — at 10: Tier trial (three offered)
]

"Three offered," Thea said out loud. "Three what? Three *trials*?" She pulled at that line too, and it opened again, a smaller grey line under the grey line.

[ — easy · normal · hard · as the trial, so the reward ]

"Oh," said Thea. "Oh, that's *clever*."

"What's clever?" said Higu, from the other side. Higu didn't hurry. She'd been lying on the wall with her eyes half shut, the way she always looked, as if she were about to fall asleep or had just worked something out. Usually it was the second one.

"Levels have walls," said Thea. The words were coming out fast now, the way they always did when a thing clicked. "Every tier. Ten levels for tier one, and at the top of it, at ten, you stop. You can't go to eleven. You have to do a trial first. And they give you a choice, easy or normal or hard, and the harder it is, the more it gives you." She jabbed at the line under Voyager's name. "He's at ten. He's been at ten since last night. He's not *stuck*. He's at the wall. He's waiting to do his trial."

Higu opened her lilac-grey eyes all the way, which she very rarely did.

"So he's not hurt," she said slowly.

"He's not hurt. He's just—" Thea stopped.

She'd been going to say *waiting*. And it was true; the window said so. But something else had come up in her as she said it, under the words, the way the grey lines came up under the white. Something she couldn't defend.

*He's waiting for something on the next floor.*

***

She posted it in World. She didn't mean to make a thing of it. She just thought people should know, and particularly one person, who'd been sitting at the long table every morning with his porridge going cold.

[ World · Althea: hey. lv 10 is a wall. every tier caps at the top and you need a trial to go further. you get a choice of 3, easy normal hard. read the tiny lock next to his level. voyager isnt stuck or hurt. hes capped. hes waiting to do his trial. ]

World went quiet for a minute, the way it did when people were reading.

Then a line came. Just one.

[ World · Gabe: thank you ]

She looked at it for a long time.

She didn't know Gabe. She'd seen him at the long table every morning, a big man with tired eyes and a whisper window open, and she'd heard him shout *Declan* in the white room until his voice cracked. That was all. He was somebody else's friend, from somebody else's corner of Ice's world, the way almost everyone in the Spire was. She'd come into that world through Xola. And her bit of it had been very small, and very particular, and mostly at night.

*Thank you*, Gabe had said. Two words. To a stranger. Because she'd told him his friend wasn't hurt.

She shut the window and didn't say anything for a while. Chi, beside her, didn't say anything either. That was the thing about Chi. She knew when not to.

***

Thea had known Ice since she was eighteen. Four years.

Not the way Gabe had known him, or Yan, or Hana. Not from the start. Xola had brought him into a call one night, the way Xola brought people into everything, and he'd been funny. That was all, at first: funny, quick, a voice in a call. Then, somehow, it had become every night. Every single night for most of a year, the two of them on a call, after everyone else had gone to bed. He played games with her, or for her, when she was too tired to play and just wanted to watch. And she sang. She didn't know how that had started. One night she'd been humming something, and he'd said *louder*, and she'd said *no*, and he'd said *please*. And she'd sung it. After that it was every night: him playing, and her singing, and neither of them saying much in between.

She'd known he liked her. At one point. She wasn't stupid; she'd known. He'd never said anything, and she'd never said anything, and it had been sweet, and slightly awkward, and then, sometime in her second year of studying, it had passed. She'd felt it pass, the way you feel the weather change. He'd never made it weird. He'd just kept being there, every night, being funny, being kind. Until school got busy and the calls got fewer, and then fewer, and then they were the kind of friends who talked every few weeks and said *we should call more* and didn't.

She'd meant to call more.

She'd been meaning to call more for most of a year.

And then a white room, and a figure by the arch that three people had seen and she hadn't, because she'd been asleep. And a name at the top of a list that wasn't his name. And a note in Deston's pocket that she wasn't supposed to know about. She'd seen Deston touch the pocket, that was all; she didn't know what was in it. And an onion boy, and a camp on floor 2 with onion skins in the snow.

And now a padlock. *He's waiting.*

She opened the rankings again and looked at the name for a long time.

She was good at reading windows. She was good at reading code. She'd always thought she was good at reading *people*, too. That was the thing she'd been proudest of, before Gigi. Before she'd found out the hard way that she could be completely wrong about somebody she thought she knew.

She looked at the name, and at the little lock, and the line under it, and tried to read it the way she'd read anything.

He'd gone ahead on the first day, alone. He'd cleared the first floor alone, in the dark. He'd peeled onions in the snow on his own, under a pine. He'd gone up a mountain in a storm. He'd reached a wall at level ten and stopped there, not hurt, just waiting. Every single thing he'd done, he'd done alone, and fast. And he'd never once stopped to look round. Not once. Not even to answer Gabe.

It wasn't the shape of somebody running ahead.

She didn't know what it was the shape of. It was just a feeling. The kind of feeling she got sometimes, reading somebody else's code, when she knew something was wrong several lines before she could see what. She could never have defended it. If anyone had asked, she couldn't have explained it.

But it felt like somebody who'd already lost something here.

***

"You're doing it again," said Chi.

They were walking back to Hearthstead in the evening, the five of them, along the river path. Drew was in front with his buckler on his arm, and Xola was behind him chattering to Squiddles, who sat on her head and chattered back in a series of small round noises. Higu was half asleep on her feet. Waddles waddled at Chi's heels, occasionally stopping to stare at the river as if it owed him money.

"Doing what?"

"The taking-it-apart face. At the river." Chi's voice was very gentle. "You're thinking about him."

Thea didn't answer straight away. Ahead of them, the red roofs of Hearthstead were coming up out of the dusk, the white tower catching the last of the light on its rail.

"Chi," she said. "Do you think you can be sad about something that hasn't happened?"

Chi thought about it, properly, the way she thought about everything. She didn't hurry.

"I think so," she said finally. "If you're scared enough of it."

"What if you're not scared of it. What if it's just—" Thea struggled. "What if somebody acts like they're grieving, but nothing's happened yet. Nothing they could grieve."

Chi looked at her with her golden-amber eyes, the yellow heart-shaped tuft on top of her head bobbing as she walked.

"Then maybe something has," she said. "And you just don't know about it."

Thea walked in silence for a while.

At the edge of town they passed the bottom of Crooked Street. Down at the end of it, the window of the Copper Spoon was lit, and through it she could see the long table and the shapes of people round it. Big ones, small ones, horns and ears. She could see Gigi.

Gigi was sitting at the far end with her new party: Hyvred, who was laughing at something, loud and bright, with her horns catching the lamplight; and Bee; and Kei, with the little green snake on his wrist. Gigi was laughing too. Not the smug, tongue-out laugh from her streams. A real one. With her whole face.

Thea stopped walking.

She'd walked past Gigi in the white room. She'd stood in a column of gold light with four places to give, and she'd looked at Gigi, and she'd looked away, and she'd asked Drew instead. She'd told herself it was because she and Gigi would have fought, and a party that fought got people killed. That was true. It was also true that she'd been angry for two years, and hurt, and that she'd wanted, for one small, mean second in that white room, for Gigi to know what it felt like to be the one left standing.

And Hyvred had picked her up before the second was over. *Gigi! With me!* And now Gigi was laughing at the long table with her whole face.

"Thea," said Chi.

"I'm fine."

"I didn't ask."

"I know you didn't." Thea started walking again. "I'm fine. I'm glad. She looks happy." She meant it. That was the strange part. She was glad, and it still hurt. Both, at the same time, like two windows open on top of each other.

Chi walked beside her and didn't say anything else. At her heels, Waddles stopped, looked at the lit window of the Copper Spoon, and made a small, decided noise, as if he'd come to a conclusion about something and wasn't going to share it.

***

"You're staring at it like it's him," said Xola.

It was late. They were lying on the bed they shared at the inn, top to tail, because Xola said it was the only way she could get any sleep with Thea's elbows. Squiddles was on the pillow between them, a small round purple shape glowing very faintly in the dark. Thea's window was open in the air above her face.

"I'm not staring at anything."

"You *are*. You've been staring at that list since supper. You've got the face." Xola rolled over, her gills fanning out on the pillow in a soft purple spray. "Not the taking-it-apart face. The other one. The four-in-the-morning face."

Thea closed the window.

"I don't have a four-in-the-morning face."

"You absolutely do. You had it every night for a year. I used to come into the call at two to say goodnight, and you'd be on with him, and you'd have the face, and I'd leave again very quietly so as not to ruin it." Xola's voice had gone soft, which it almost never did. Xola was flowery and hyper and bratty, and she teased everybody about everything, but every once in a while, very late at night, something gentler came out from under it. "I brought him into that call, you know. The very first night. I just thought he was funny."

"He was funny."

"He was funny." Xola was quiet for a moment. "Best thing I ever did, bringing him in. Second best was you."

Thea didn't say anything for a while. Her throat was doing something.

"I didn't call him," she said finally. "For most of a year. I kept meaning to."

"I know."

"And now he's up there on his own, and I can't."

Xola reached across the bed in the dark and found Thea's ankle, which was the only bit of her within reach, and held on to it.

"You'll call him again," she said. "When we catch up. You'll sit him down and make him listen to you sing and he'll pretend he's not crying. I'll come in at two and leave very quietly." She squeezed the ankle. "Go to sleep, Thea."

She went quiet. After a minute, her breathing changed.

Thea lay in the dark, then opened her window one last time.

She didn't look at the rankings. She looked at her own window. At her own level, and her own tier, and her own small skill list, and the lines under the lines that only she seemed able to see. She asked it, very quietly, in her head, the way she'd asked the padlock:

*What are you? Who wrote you?*

The window didn't answer that. It never would, she suspected. Not yet.

But something else opened. A new line, at the bottom, gold at the edges.

[ Unique skill: Source
Every window has words under the words. You can read them.
]

Thea lay in the dark looking at it.

*Words under the words.* She'd been doing it for five days without knowing what it was called. The way she'd been reading code for years without knowing what she'd use it for, and singing for a boy at four in the morning for a year without knowing why.

She closed the window. Beside her, Xola mumbled something about jellyfish in her sleep and rolled over, and Squiddles, on the pillow, made a small round noise.

Somewhere above them, on floor 2, or by now somewhere past it, a name sat at level ten with a padlock beside it. It was waiting for something. She didn't know what. She was going to find out.

She'd been meaning to call more.
