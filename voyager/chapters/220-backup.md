For the first time on sixteen, Malcolm had three plans.

He wrote them on the slate. The same slate. Drew's, from floor 1, the one he'd borrowed on the first morning of Mother Crane's fever and never given back, because Drew had said *keep it, mate, you'll want it*, and he had. It still had *A is everybody* in the bottom corner, in his own hand, where he'd written it on the night the fever turned. He'd never rubbed it out. He wrote round it.

*A. John takes them away. We meet it at the terraces. We open it.*

*B. It comes to the town first. We meet it at the pool.*

*C. Whoever's holding the lantern, we stand next to them, and we don't move.*

He looked at C for a long time.

He'd had a C before. In Tell, before the Reckoning. *Whatever they read out, we stand next to him while they read it, and we don't move.* It had been the only one that always worked. He'd told Xav that, on a roof, and Xav had said *Malcolm has a plan B for his plan B*, and he'd said *plan C, keep up*.

He'd never told anybody the other thing. That C was always the one he believed in.

A and B were plans. You could get them wrong. A was John, and John's new skill, *Beckon*, and the hope that something as big as the dark would follow a light that wasn't anybody's down a slope to the old fields where thirty-six people were waiting for it. B was the pool, and two hundred people in the roots, and the street Corwen's mother's house had stood on, that wasn't there any more.

C wasn't a plan. C was just where you stood.

***

They had the council on the walkway outside the forge, on the first fall after John's light. All thirty-six and Corwen and Nesta and Isel and Tegan, who came with the baby, which nobody had expected, and sat at the back with her glim humming over its face like a bee over a flower.

Xav laid it out.

Malcolm watched him do it. He'd watched Xav lay things out since the first year of poly, in a canteen, with chopsticks. The way he'd wait until everybody had said everything, every bad idea and every good one, and then stand up, and say it once, in order, so that by the time he'd finished it sounded as if it had been obvious all along.

"It's coming for the lantern," said Xav. "Not the town. Not us. The lantern. It saw it open for a count of ten and it's been waiting at the bottom of the Hollow ever since for it to open again." He looked at Thea, who was sitting against the forge wall with the bundle in her lap. "Corwen says it came for the fire when the fire was out. For where the light had been. So it's not going to stop waiting."

Nobody said anything.

"So we choose where it opens," said Xav. "The terraces. The old fields. Half a fall down. No town. No roots. Walls to stand behind. Room." He looked at John. "John goes down first and sends lights. Beckon. Whatever comes up the Hollow ahead of it, the small ones, he takes them away. Down the slope. Off to one side." John nodded. He didn't say anything. He'd hardly said anything since the step. "And then the lantern opens. On the terraces. And it comes up to it." Xav looked round at all of them. "And we're there."

"And then?" said Corwen.

"Rui Xiang's seen it asleep. Its head. The place under the head where the body joins." Xav looked at Rui Xiang, who nodded once. "The same as the small ones. Just bigger." He was quiet. "And when it's open, the lights come out."

"You don't know that," said Corwen.

"No," said Xav. "We don't."

He sat down.

That was plan A. Malcolm had it on the slate. He looked at it and felt what he always felt about plan A, which was that it was a very good plan that was going to go wrong in some way nobody had thought of, because they always did, and that was what B was for.

"Who opens the lantern?" said Isel.

Everybody went quiet.

It was a small voice. Flat. She was sitting on the walkway with Edda's old lantern in her lap, and she said it the way she said the drip numbers, as if it were just the next thing.

Nobody answered. Nobody looked at Thea. Everybody felt themselves not looking at Thea, which was worse.

And Thea looked up from the bundle in her lap and said:

"Me."

***

Malcolm didn't look at Dec. He couldn't. He looked at the slate.

He'd known it was coming. Everybody had. You couldn't make a plan where the Moth came for the lantern and not have somebody hold the lantern. And there was only one person who'd ever held it. Since the Fen. Since floor 1. *Found the lantern. I'm carrying it.*

He heard Dec breathe in. Just once.

"It's mine," said Thea. Very steadily. "It saw me. It saw me open it. It's waiting for *me* to open it again." She put her hand flat on the bundle. "It leans toward him. It always has. But I'm the one who carries it."

Nobody said anything.

"I'm not going to step in front of anybody," said Thea. She wasn't looking at Dec either. Malcolm could feel her not looking. "I promised. On fourteen. On fifteen. I'm not stepping in front. I'm going to stand on the terraces where Xav says, and open it where Xav says, and stand there. And it'll come to me." She was quiet. "That's not in front. That's just where I am."

She stopped.

The walkway was very quiet. The drip hadn't come yet. Malcolm could hear the glims humming in their lanterns all over the roots. And Tegan's baby, at the back, making a small noise.

Dec didn't say anything for a long time.

Malcolm made himself look up.

Dec was sitting on the walkway across from Thea, with Tuli's pole across his knees and Gabe's terrible lantern on his belt. The light was still in it. Nobody had told him. He still hadn't looked. He was looking at Thea, and his face was doing something Malcolm had seen it do exactly once before: in a yard in Tell on thirteen, in from the Fell Gate with a fell-cat cut down his forearm, when he'd sat down on the trough and held out his arm and said *can you?*

It was the face of a man deciding to ask instead of do.

"Okay," said Dec.

Thea looked at him.

"Okay," said Dec again. Quietly. "Your lantern. Your light." He was quiet a moment. "Can I stand next to you?"

"Yes."

"Which side?"

Thea looked at him for a long moment. Then, very slightly, something happened at the corner of her mouth.

"My right," said Thea. "Where you always are."

"Then I'm on her left," said Gabe.

He said it from the forge door, where he was leaning with Hearthwall on his back. Everybody turned. Gabe didn't move. His face was doing nothing at all. But Malcolm knew. John had told him. *Cover the one beside you. When they open the light. Do not step in front.*

Thea looked at Gabe for a long time.

"Okay," said Thea.

Malcolm looked down at his slate.

He picked up the chalk and, under C, very small, wrote: *Thea. Dec on her right. Gabe on her left. Everybody else: next to them.*

Then he looked at it. And underlined *everybody*.

***

He hit seventy on the fourth fall, and he didn't even see what did it.

He was at the pool. At the edge, near the forge, healing Hako, who'd come back from the far walkways with a moth-cut across his scalp that wouldn't stop bleeding, because scalps didn't, and was sitting on the root saying *classic* every time Malcolm touched it. And something came over the water out of the dark, one moth, alone, low, the way they came now every few drips, and Corwen threw a net of woven wire on a pole over it, and it came down tangled on the walkway beside him, and Malcolm, without thinking, hit it with his mace.

Once. On the head. The way you'd swat a wasp.

It stopped moving.

He looked at the window. Then at the moth. Then at the mace in his hand, which he'd carried since floor 1 and used to hit perhaps forty things in two years, most of them by accident.

"Classic," said Hako.

[ Tier 5 trial · Cleric
Easy: Bless a hundred.
Normal: Bless one who will not know. ]

[ Hard: Second
Be there before you are needed.
Stay after they have gone.
Nobody will know it was you. ]

He read it twice.

He'd always thought he'd find his hard one hard. Everybody's had been. Dec's, every time. Rachael's, on the fifth wall, with her arms white to the shoulder. Azac's, in a line of strangers. Rui Xiang's, on the lip of the bowl, the hardest thing he'd ever done. John's, on a step, not looking.

He read his, and it didn't look hard at all.

It looked like a description.

*Be there before you are needed.* He'd been at the top of the Breath stair all night on ten, in case. He'd been at the bottom of the copy-room stairs in Tell, in case. He'd packed everything twice his whole life. He'd learned to heal so he could fix Dec's face when Gabe hit him, before anybody knew Gabe was going to.

*Stay after they have gone.* He always did. He was always the last one up from the fire. He was always the one still sitting on the step of the class hall at dawn with the slate.

*Nobody will know it was you.*

Well. Nobody ever did.

He sat on the root with the dead moth beside him and Hako's blood on his hands and the window open in the corner of his eye, and for a moment he was almost disappointed. Then he thought: *that's the trick, isn't it*. That was why it was the hard one. Because you couldn't do it on purpose. You couldn't decide to be there before you were needed. You had to already be the kind of person who was.

And then you had to do it on the night when it was all of it, all at once, and not be thanked.

"Hard," said Malcolm.

"Course," said Hako.

***

Xav's came on the fifth fall.

Malcolm was there. He was always there. He was sitting on the walkway outside the forge with the slate, going over A again, and Xav came back from the far side with Rui Xiang and John, from the terraces, from walking the ground for the third time, and sat down on the crate by the door, and Malcolm saw his eyes go to the corner.

Xav read it. His face did nothing at all.

Then he leaned over, without a word, and showed Malcolm.

He'd never done that. In twenty years. Xav didn't show people things. Xav waited until it didn't matter and then mentioned it.

[ Tier 5 trial · Warrior
Easy: Move once.
Normal: Move once, when it matters. ]

[ Hard: Once
Wait longer than you can.
Then move once.
It must be enough. ]

Malcolm read it twice. Then he looked at Xav.

"It's the same," said Malcolm. "That's your *skill*. That's just your skill again. With *longer than you can* on the front."

"Yeah," said Xav.

"*It must be enough.*"

"Yeah."

They sat there. Malcolm could hear the drip coming. You started to feel it, after a while on sixteen. A sort of tightening, in the air, before.

"When?" said Malcolm.

"When it comes," said Xav. "When I've waited longer than I can." He shut the window. "I'll know."

"What if you wait too long?"

Xav looked at him.

"Then you'll be there," said Xav. "Won't you."

*Plink.* Fourteen. Isel's voice, small, from the pool. *Fourteen.*

Malcolm looked down at his slate. At C. *Everybody else: next to them.*

"Yeah," said Malcolm. "I'll be there."

***

He went to see Dec on the sixth fall.

He didn't decide to. He was going past the root where Dec and Thea slept, with the slate under his arm, and Dec was sitting in the door of it on his own, which he never was. Thea was at the pool with the bundle and Xola and Higu. Dec was on his own, on the step, with his left sleeve rolled up and a long shallow cut down the forearm, bleeding slowly into his lap. Moth-dust in it. He'd been out hunting. He always went out hunting. Alone, at the edge of the dark, the way he levelled, with Xola watching from a root. Except Xola was at the pool.

He was looking at the cut. Not doing anything about it.

Malcolm stopped.

He'd stood at the top of a lot of stairs in two years. He knew the feeling of a thing about to happen that you were there for. He had it now.

Dec looked up.

"Mal," said Dec.

"You're bleeding."

"Yeah." Dec looked at the cut. "Sixty-four. I was going to—" He stopped. "I was going to do it myself. I was sitting here deciding." He looked up at Malcolm. "It's not sixty yet."

"It's sixty-four," said Malcolm. "And it's full of dust. And you're sitting on a step looking at it."

Dec looked at him for a long moment.

Then he held out his arm.

"Can you?" said Dec.

Malcolm sat down on the step beside him. He put the slate down. He put his hands on the cut, and found what it was trying to do, the way Rachael had taught him on four, and agreed with it. Twice. Once to close it, and once in case.

"You did two," said Dec.

"I always do two."

"I know. You said. In Tell." Dec looked at his arm. Pink now. A thin line. "I never used to stay still long enough to notice."

"You're still now."

"Yeah." Dec looked at the arm. "I'm practising."

Malcolm picked the slate up again. He didn't go. He sat on the step beside Dec, the way he'd sat on the step of the class hall on floor 1 with this slate, trying to write B, and Dec didn't tell him to go, which Dec would have done once.

Dec's belt was right there. Between them. On the step.

Gabe's terrible lantern. With the light in it.

It was sitting very still. Pale. Scattered through the bad knot to about forty points instead of a hundred, so you could nearly see the middle. Not humming. It hadn't hummed once, John said, since it went in. It just sat there. Waiting. Asking.

Dec hadn't looked at it.

Malcolm didn't either. Not straight on. He looked at the slate in his lap and saw the lantern out of the corner of his eye, on the step, a hand's width from his knee.

"Mal," said Dec.

"Yeah."

"On the terraces." Dec was looking out at the dark. "If it goes wrong. Plan A." He was quiet. "What's B?"

"B's the pool. If it comes here first."

"And C?"

Malcolm looked at him.

Dec didn't look back. He was looking at the dark. But he'd asked. He'd never once in twenty years asked Malcolm what plan C was. Nobody did. Plan C was Malcolm's. Nobody asked about it because nobody thought they'd need it.

Malcolm turned the slate round so Dec could see it.

*C. Whoever's holding the lantern, we stand next to them, and we don't move.*

*Thea. Dec on her right. Gabe on her left. Everybody else: next to them.*

*Everybody* underlined.

And in the corner, in his own hand, from the night on floor 1 when an old woman's fever turned: *A is everybody.*

Dec read it for a long time.

"That's the one that always works," said Malcolm. "C. I worked it out in Tell. A and B, you can get wrong. C you can't. Because it's not a plan. It's just where you stand."

Dec didn't say anything.

Then he reached over and, with his thumb, very carefully, not smudging the chalk, touched the word *everybody*. The line under it.

"Yeah," said Dec. Very quietly. "I know. You did it on fourteen. On the Grey. All of you." He took his thumb away. "I just didn't know it had a letter."

Malcolm laughed. He hadn't meant to. It came out of him like a cough.

Dec looked at him, surprised. Then he laughed too.

And on the step between them, in the terrible lantern on his belt, so faintly Malcolm wasn't sure he'd seen it, the light moved. Just a little. Toward the sound.

Dec didn't see.

Malcolm didn't say.

*Let him find it.*

***

The swarm came on the eighth fall.

Not the Moth. Its children. All of them.

Malcolm was awake. That was the thing he'd think about, later. He was awake, and he didn't know why. It was the sixth drip of the sleep, the dead middle of the night, and everybody in Lanthorn was asleep in the roots, and the thirty-six were asleep in their hollows, and Thea was asleep on the bundle with Dec's arm over her, and Malcolm was sitting on a root at the edge of the pool with the slate in his lap.

He hadn't been able to sleep. He'd lain in the hollow for two drips listening to Rui Xiang breathe and John not breathe, the way John didn't when he was dreaming through a mote, and Gabe snore, and Xav not move at all. And then he'd got up and come out to the pool with the slate. To go over A again. That was what he told himself.

He sat at the pool and didn't look at the slate.

He looked at Tegan's door.

It was across the water. A small root-hollow with a round window, near the forge. The door was shut. By the door, the lantern. Nesta's little one, very fine. Empty.

Because the baby's glim wasn't in it.

He knew that. Everybody did. A new glim didn't go into a lantern for a while. It stayed over the cradle, in the open, close, humming, the way a mother stays close. Isel had said. *For the first fall, or two, or ten. Till it knows the baby. Then it'll go in.* And baby Edda's was new. Three falls new. It was over the cradle, behind the round window, in the open. Humming.

The newest light in Lanthorn. The only one not in a lantern.

He sat at the pool and looked at the round window and couldn't stop looking.

He didn't know why. He'd think about that later too. Nothing was happening. The town was asleep. The bells were quiet. The dark past the roots was dark. There was no reason in the world to be sitting at the edge of a pool at the dead middle of the night staring at a baby's window.

He got up and walked round the pool to Tegan's door.

He didn't wake anybody. He didn't say anything. He just went and sat down on her step, the way John had, on the end, off to one side, with his back against the root and his mace across his knees and the slate in his lap. Under the empty lantern. Outside the round window with the light behind it.

He sat there. He didn't know what for.

*In case*, he thought.

It was what he always thought.

***

The bells went on the ninth drip.

*Tinktinktinktink*, all over the town, quick and quiet, and then not quick and quiet. Loud. All of them, every bell in Lanthorn, and voices, which Lanthorn never used at night, shouting. And the glims going into the lanterns, all at once, two hundred and thirty-five of them, a whole town going from lit to lace in a breath.

And out of the dark, past the roots, from every side at once: wings.

He'd never seen so many. Nobody had. They came up out of the Hollow like smoke out of a chimney, grey-brown, silent, hundreds of them, a whole sky of soft dusty wings with the rings on them like eyes, pouring up over the edge of the roots and through the walkways and over the pool. Not to the strays; the strays were in the empty lanterns now, scattered, the way John's light had brought them. Not to the thirty-six; their lights were in Jared's cages, scattered. Not to the glims.

To the points. To whatever was left in the open.

There was one thing left in the open.

He was on his feet before the first one reached the window.

It came over the pool, low, out of the swarm, straight at the round window of Tegan's hollow, wings open, tongue already uncoiling. And Malcolm was there. Already. Standing on the step, between it and the window, with his mace up.

He'd never in his life been fast. He'd told Xav that on a roof on ten. *I'm not fast. I'm never going to be fast.* He wasn't fast now. He was just there. He'd been there for three drips before it came, sitting on a step in the dark for no reason, and when it came he only had to stand up.

He hit it.

Not cleanly. He never hit anything cleanly. The mace caught it on the edge of the wing, and the wing crumpled, and it came down on the step half on top of him, a great soft dusty weight, tongue lashing, looking for the point, and he got his forearm under its head and pushed. It was like wrestling a wet mattress. He pushed it off the step. It went into the pool.

Another came. And another.

He was a cleric. He'd never once stood anywhere alone and held it. That was Gabe. That was Jared. That was Hako and Salty and Drew. He stood behind people. That was the whole of him. Second. *Backup*.

Behind him, through the round window, the baby started to cry.

He stood on Tegan's step with his mace and held it.

He blessed himself. He'd never done that either. You didn't. Blessings went on other people. He put one on his own arms, gold, and one behind it, in case, and hit the next moth with arms that weren't his own any more, they were stronger, and it went into the pool. He put one on the door behind him. He didn't know if you could bless a door. He did it anyway. It went gold for a moment, all round the frame.

And the moths came at the round window and the gold door, three, four at a time now, and he was there, and he hit them, and they came down on the step, on the walkway, in the pool. One got its tongue past him and onto the glass of the window, feeling, tapping, looking for the light inside, and he took it by the tongue, the way you'd take a snake, and pulled, and it came off the window and he put it into the pool.

He didn't know how long. There was no count. Isel wasn't counting; Isel was in a lantern-hung root somewhere with her gran's lantern held against her. He just stood on the step.

A tongue found his own light.

He felt it. On his collar. In Jared's little cage. A dry whisper, a tap, and then the tongue going round the iron, feeling, and finding the middle, because he was close and it was close and the knot only worked if they couldn't get near. And then the pull. Like somebody drinking something out of his chest through a straw. His light *screamed*, the way Hana's had, high and thin and alive, right by his ear.

He didn't move off the step.

He put his hand up and closed it round his own lantern, round the tongue, and put a blessing on his own hand, the third one, the last he had, and pulled. And the tongue came out of the cage like a thread out of a needle, and the moth reared up, and he hit it with the mace in his other hand, badly, on the side of the head, and it went sideways into the pool.

His light was still there. Smaller. Shaking. In its cage. Not screaming any more.

He put his hand over it. Not touching. Just over. The way Hana had.

*I'm here*, he thought at it. *I'm here. I'm always here. Sorry. I'm here.*

He didn't get off the step.

***

It ended the way it always ended. All at once. The swarm turning, every one, like iron filings, and going. Back over the roots and down into the dark, silent, the way it had come.

He was sitting on the step. He didn't remember sitting down. There were eleven dead moths on the walkway in front of Tegan's door and more in the pool, floating, grey, their wings spread out on the black water like dropped coats. His arms were shaking. His light was very small on his collar.

Behind him, through the round window, the baby was still crying. Furious. Alive. And over the cradle, the glim. Humming.

He sat there and breathed.

The door opened behind him. Tegan. White, with the baby in her arms and the glim over its face, and her big dark eyes going from him to the moths on the walkway to the pool and back to him.

"You were here," said Tegan.

"Yeah."

"Before." She was staring at him. "You were here before. I heard you sit down. On the sixth drip. I thought it was John." She looked at the moths. "How did you know?"

"I didn't," said Malcolm. Which was true. "I just— I couldn't sleep."

Tegan looked at him for a long, long time.

Then she bent down, with the baby in her arms, and put her forehead against the top of his head, the way people in Lanthorn did, very briefly, and went back inside, and shut the door.

He sat on the step.

People came. Later. In ones and twos, round the pool, along the walkways. Gabe first, with Hearthwall, grey with dust to the eyebrows, looking for him, *Mal, Mal, where's*— and stopping. Looking at the walkway. At the eleven moths. At the gold that was still very faintly round the frame of Tegan's door.

"Mate," said Gabe. "Were you *here*? The whole time?"

"Yeah."

"On your *own*?"

"There wasn't anybody else."

Gabe looked at him for a long time.

"We were all at the far side," said Gabe. "They came over the far roots first. We all went there. Everybody. Xav, Rui, Dec, all of— Nobody thought about—" He stopped. He looked at Tegan's door. "Nobody thought about here."

"I know."

"How did you—"

"I didn't," said Malcolm. "I was just here."

Gabe looked at him a moment longer. Then he sat down on the step beside him, heavily, with the shield, and didn't say anything else.

Other people came. Xav, who looked at the walkway and at Malcolm and said nothing at all, and sat down on his other side. John, off to one side. Rui Xiang, who looked at Malcolm's face for a long time and then away, the way he did when he'd read something he wasn't going to say. Corwen, with his net. Nesta, who looked at the eleven moths and said, flatly, *with a mace?*

Nobody said thank you. Nobody knew to. They didn't know what he'd done. They'd been on the far side. They thought he'd just been here when the swarm came round. They didn't know he'd been here for three drips before, on a step, in the dark, for no reason at all.

*Nobody will know it was you.*

He sat on the step and found that was fine. It was better than fine. It was the most comfortable he'd been since the arch.

***

They went back to sleep. Eventually. All of them.

It was the twelfth drip of the sleep. Ten more till the first of the fall. Everybody was so tired from the swarm that they went to sleep where they sat, nearly, in the hollows and on the walkways and against the roots. Gabe went to sleep on Tegan's step beside him, sitting up, with Hearthwall in his lap, and Malcolm let him. Xav went back to the hollow. John lay down on the walkway with his eyes half shut, sending nothing.

Malcolm didn't sleep.

*Stay after they have gone.*

He got up, carefully, so he didn't wake Gabe. He walked round the pool. Past Isel's ladder, where Edda's old lantern hung on its hook, with a stray in it now, scattered, asking. Past the forge. Past the root where Dec and Thea slept. He looked in, through the door. Thea on the bundle, asleep. Dec beside her, on his back, one arm over her and the bundle both, asleep. The terrible lantern on his belt, on the floor beside him.

The light in it was looking at him.

Malcolm didn't know how he knew. He just knew. The way you know somebody's awake in a dark room. It was sitting in Gabe's bad knot on the floor beside Dec's hip, very still, and it was turned toward Dec's face. The way the strays turned toward the goat. The way the lights under the Moth's wings had turned toward John's mote.

*Whose*, it was asking. Malcolm would have bet his slate on it. *Whose are you.*

He stood in the door for a while.

Then he went on. Out of the roots, to the edge of the town, to the far ladders, where John practised. Where the walkways ended and the dark began. He sat down on the last root, with the slate, and looked out at the Hollow going down into the dark.

He didn't know why. He'd think about that later, too. Everybody was asleep. The swarm was gone. Plan A was for the terraces, in two falls, when they'd walked the ground once more. There was nothing out there.

He sat on the root and looked at it anyway.

*In case.*

***

It was the eighteenth drip when the Hollow changed.

He felt it before he saw it. The air. A sort of pressure, coming up the slope out of the dark, slow, the way the air changes in a room when somebody opens a door a long way off. And the glims in their lanterns behind him, all over Lanthorn, all at once, went silent.

Then the strays.

Out in the dark past the roots, the few strays that were still out there, the ones that hadn't come up the path behind John's light. He saw them. Far down the slope, small and pale, drifting. And then all of them, at once, moving. Up. Toward the town. Fast. Faster than he'd ever seen a stray go. Not drifting. Fleeing.

And under them, a long way down, the dark began to rise.

He'd seen its eyes, at the pool, the night Thea opened the lantern for Edda. He'd heard Rui Xiang describe it. He'd thought he knew.

He hadn't.

The dark at the bottom of the Hollow was moving. All of it. Coming up the slope. Not like a thing. Like a tide. Like the whole dark had stood up. Grey-brown and soft and vast, filling the Hollow from side to side, wider than the slope, wider than the white trees, the edge of it lifting over the terraces and the dead villages as it came, the rings on it like great dark eyes the size of houses, one, ten, twenty. And through it, under it, faint, grey, in patches and spots, moving, the lights. Thousands of them. Under the wings. Coming up with it.

And above the near edge, high up, far off, two round dull red-black eyes. Open. All the way.

Looking up the Hollow.

At Lanthorn.

At a root by the pool where a girl was asleep on a bundle with an amber light inside it.

Not the terraces. Not plan A. It wasn't waiting for the lantern to open. It had waited nine falls and then twenty and the lantern hadn't opened, and something tonight had brought it, the swarm, or the baby's new glim behind its round window, or the light in Gabe's terrible lantern on a man's belt, looking at his face in the dark. Or nothing. Or just that it was ready.

It was coming here.

*B. It comes to the town first. We meet it at the pool.*

He was on his feet.

The bells. Nobody was ringing them. Everybody was asleep. He was the only one awake in the whole of Lanthorn. The only one who'd stayed after.

The nearest bell was on the post at the bottom of the far ladders. Small. Iron. On a hook. He took it off the hook.

He'd never rung one. Lanthorn rang them. Isel, Corwen, the people who knew how. Quick and quiet. *Tinktinktink*.

He didn't ring it quietly.

He rang it with both hands, as hard as he could, the way you'd ring a bell on a school field to bring everybody in, and he ran. Along the walkways, round the roots, toward the pool, ringing it, and shouting, which nobody ever did in Lanthorn at night, at the top of his voice:

"*IT'S COMING. IT'S COMING UP. PLAN B. PLAN B. EVERYBODY, PLAN B—*"

Lights came on behind him. Lanterns. Faces in doors. Voices. Gabe's voice, from Tegan's step, *Mal—*. Xav's, somewhere, sharp. Bells, other bells, starting, all over the town, quick and quiet and then not quiet.

He came round the last root to the pool, still ringing, and stopped.

Thea was standing at the edge of the water.

Awake. On her feet. With the bundle in her arms. Facing down the Hollow. Toward the dark coming up it. Her face doing nothing at all.

Dec beside her. On her right. Already there. With Tuli's pole in one hand and his sword in the other, and Gabe's terrible lantern on his belt.

The light in the lantern had gone bright.

Not humming. Not moving. Just bright. As bright as Malcolm had ever seen a stray. Shining out through Gabe's bad knot in forty points, so you could nearly see the middle. Turned toward Dec's face.

Dec still hadn't looked at it.

He was looking down the Hollow. At the eyes.

Gabe came past Malcolm at a run, with Hearthwall coming off his back onto his arm, and went round the pool, and stopped on Thea's left. And stood there. Not in front. Beside.

*When they open the light.*

Malcolm stood at the edge of the roots with the bell in his hand and the slate under his arm, and looked at the three of them at the edge of the black pool. Thea in the middle with the bundle. Dec on her right. Gabe on her left. And behind them, coming out of the roots, out of the hollows, down the ladders, half-asleep, grey with the swarm's dust, with their lights in their cages, thirty-three people. Coming to stand next to them.

And past them, at the far side of the pool, Lanthorn. Two hundred people in the door of every root. With their glims in their lanterns, silent.

He looked down at his slate. At C.

*Whoever's holding the lantern, we stand next to them, and we don't move.*

He put the bell down on the walkway.

He walked round the pool to stand next to them.
