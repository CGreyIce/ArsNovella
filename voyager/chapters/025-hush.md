PenguChi had never been the brave one, and she'd made her peace with that a long time ago.

She was the calm one. That was different. People mixed them up, because from the outside they looked the same: both of them stood still while things went wrong. But a brave person stood still because they weren't afraid. Chi stood still because she was, and had found that if she stood very still and spoke very slowly, other people stopped being afraid too. And then, quite often, so did she.

She'd done it on stream for years. When chat got ugly, or a game went badly, or somebody in a call was having a bad night, she slowed down. She lowered her voice. She said things like *it's okay* and *we'll figure it out* and *take a breath*, slow and even, like someone pouring water. People said her voice was soothing. She'd always thought of it as just *slow*.

She had no idea, on the morning of the eighth day, walking up the valley toward the Fold with a sword on her back and a penguin at her heels, that slow was about to save her party's lives.

***

"He wants to go back to her," said Chi.

Waddles had stopped in the middle of the path, again, and turned round, again, and was looking back down the valley toward Hearthstead with an expression of yearning. He was a small, round, yellow penguin, who had been a plush on her model for three years and had come through the white room alive. He'd spent the first ten minutes of his new life walking across the floor to stand on a stranger's foot.

"Who?" said Drew.

"Aleaka. The fox girl. In Hana's party." Chi bent and picked Waddles up and turned him round to face up the valley. He immediately turned back round. "He's been in love with her since the first morning. Every time we see her at the Spoon he goes and sits on her boot."

"Everyone's in love with her," said Xola. "She's got that thing. Animals follow her. Mother Crane's hens followed her down the lane. Jared said a goat's been following her for a week." She tossed her very long lavender hair. "Squiddles is the only one with any loyalty. Aren't you, Squiddles? *Aren't you?*"

Squiddles, on her head, made a small round noise that might have been agreement.

"Waddles is loyal," said Chi, with dignity. "He's just loyal to her as well."

"That's not how loyalty works," said Higu, from the back, without opening her eyes.

"It's how *his* works."

Waddles turned round again and looked longingly at Hearthstead. Chi sighed and picked him up and carried him the rest of the way under her arm.

***

Higu planned it.

Higu planned everything. She was the calmest person Chi had ever met, which was saying something, because Chi had met herself. Higu thought before she spoke, and then spoke, and then was right. On floor 1 she'd become the one who looked at a slope full of Woolbacks for a full minute without moving, and then pointed at a wall and said *there*. And she was always right about *there*.

They stopped at the last wall above the Fold, and Higu looked down at the Bellwether asleep in the middle of the cropped green hollow, and at the seven standing stones round it, one of them split from root to crown. She looked for a long time.

Then she said, "Thea."

"Yeah."

"Can you read it? The way you read the lock?"

Thea looked at her. Then she looked at the Bellwether, and Chi watched her face do the thing it did now. The taking-it-apart face, Xola called it, a kind of narrowing, like someone reading very small print.

"Hang on," said Thea.

]

She read it out loud. Everybody went quiet.

"Three point two seconds," said Drew slowly. "That's very exact."

"That's what it says."

"And the strap's got its own health."

"Forty. Deston's guide said a few stuns. That's—" Thea did the sum in her head. "If two of us hit it every stun, it's two stuns. Maybe three."

"What's *Afraid*?" said Xola.

Thea frowned at it. She pulled at the line the way Chi had seen her pull at lines all week, asking it what it was. It opened, a small grey line under the grey line.

[ — Afraid: cannot act until it passes ]

"Oh," said Thea. "That's the scream. When the bell comes off. Everyone's said it goes through you like cold water. It's not just a feeling. It's a—" She looked up. "It *stops* you. For a bit. You can't move."

"How long's a bit?"

"It doesn't say."

There was a short silence on the wall.

"And then it curves," said Drew, "and comes at whoever's standing there. While they can't move."

"Yeah," said Thea.

Higu looked at the Bellwether for another long minute. Then she opened her eyes all the way, which she hardly ever did.

"Then nobody's standing anywhere when the bell comes off," she said. "Drew holds it. Chi and Thea do the strap. Xola keeps Drew up. I'll call the stones." She looked at Drew. "And Drew, when the strap's at ten, you get out from in front of it and put a stone behind you first. So when it screams and you can't move, the stone's already there."

Drew looked at her for a moment. Then he smiled, slowly, the easy smile of a man who'd run tournaments for a living and knew good calls when he heard them.

"That's a good plan," he said.

"I know," said Higu, and closed her eyes again.

***

Drew woke it up.

He did it differently from anyone else; Chi had heard the stories of everyone else at the Spoon. He didn't bang a shield or throw a stone. He walked down into the Fold, quite calmly, and stood about eight metres in front of the sleeping ram with his little round buckler on his left arm and the bladed knuckles on his right fist, and cleared his throat.

"Morning," he said.

The Bellwether opened its eyes.

Chi had never seen anybody fight like Drew. Nobody in Ice's whole world fought like Drew, as far as she knew. He didn't dodge the ram the way the guide said, stepping aside so it went past into a stone. He *met* it. When it came at him the first time, head down, horns level, he stood his ground until the last possible half-second. Then he put the buckler against the side of its horn, not against the front, and turned his whole body like a door swinging on a hinge. The ram's own weight carried it past him, a little to the left, just enough. And then it hit the split stone it hadn't been aiming at.

Crack.

"*Three point two*," said Thea. "*Go!*"

Chi went. She'd been made a Knight at the class hall, a Warrior, with a plain sword. She'd never fought with a sword in her life, or with anything. But she'd been practising for eight days, and she was patient, and she'd found out that patience was most of it. She came across the cropped grass with Thea beside her, Thea's staff already crackling with the small sharp darts of light she'd learned to throw. And they both went for the strap, and hit it, and hit it, and Chi counted under her breath.

*One. Two. Three.*

"*Out!*" said Higu.

They got out. The Bellwether came off the stone, shaking its head, and the bell rang its deep cracked note, and the Woolbacks came over the north-east wall. Xola was there with a wash of something cool and blue that made the scrapes on Drew's arm close up while Chi watched. Squiddles was glowing on her head like a lamp.

[ bell strap: 22 / 40 ]

They did it again. Drew met the charge with his buckler and his hinge-turn, and the stone, and the crack. *Three point two.* Chi and Thea went in on the strap. Higu called the stones from the wall in her calm, unhurried voice: *left one. Now the far one. Now back*.

[ bell strap: 9 / 40 ]

"*Ten!*" called Higu. "*Drew! Get a stone behind you!*"

Drew got a stone behind him. He backed up until his shoulders were almost touching the split one, and put his feet down, and waited.

Thea and Chi went in for the last of the strap.

It parted.

The bell fell off.

And the Bellwether screamed.

***

It went through Chi like being dropped into a frozen lake.

She'd heard it described. *Like cold water*, everyone said. It wasn't cold water. It was the feeling she'd had, very occasionally, on the worst nights, alone in her room. The feeling of the floor going out from under her and her whole body locking up, too frightened to move, too frightened to breathe, waiting for something terrible that hadn't happened yet. Except it wasn't once in a while. It was everywhere at once, and it came out of the ram's open mouth and went into all of them.

[ Afraid ]

Everyone at the Spoon had said it lasted a heartbeat, the cold water, and then you shook it off. It didn't. It went on, and on, and she understood, very dimly, that it was going on because she was holding it. And so was Drew. And neither of them knew how to put it down.

She couldn't move. She was standing three metres from the Bellwether with her sword in her hand, and she couldn't lift it. She couldn't turn her head. She could only see what was in front of her, and what was in front of her was Drew.

Drew was frozen too.

He was standing with his back to the split stone, exactly where Higu had told him, buckler up, feet planted. But his eyes had gone wide and his whole body had gone rigid. Chi could see his hands shaking on the buckler strap. He couldn't move, any more than she could.

And the Bellwether had turned its head and was looking at him.

Not at Chi, who'd taken its bell. Not at Thea. At Drew, standing with his back to a stone, unable to move, the way you'd look at the easiest thing in a room.

It lowered its head.

Chi was afraid. She was more afraid than she'd ever been in her life. And somewhere under the fear, in the small calm place where she'd always gone when chat got ugly, a very slow, very quiet part of her said: *you've been here before*.

Not here. Not a ram. But here, this feeling: a room full of people too frightened to move, waiting for something terrible. She'd been in that room on stream a hundred times. And every time, she'd done the same thing.

She'd talked.

She couldn't move her arms. She couldn't move her feet. She wasn't sure she could move anything. But she opened her mouth, and found that she could do that, just. And she spoke the way she'd always spoken in that room: slowly, quietly, evenly, like somebody pouring water.

"*It's okay,*" said Chi. "*It's just loud. That's all it is. It's very loud. And it's going to come, and that's okay, because the stone's behind you. Higu put it there. Drew. Breathe in.*"

She saw his chest move.

"*Good. Now out. Slowly.*"

He breathed out.

"*The fear's not yours,*" said Chi, slowly, slowly. "*It put it in you. You can put it down. You don't have to hold it. Just put it down, like a bag. And on three, turn it into the stone. Like you've been doing all morning. You know how. One.*"

The Bellwether's hooves scraped the grass.

"*Two.*"

It came.

"*Three.*"

Drew moved.

***

She'd never forget it.

The ram was coming at him on its curve, head down, horns level, the whole thundering weight of it. And Drew, who had been frozen solid a breath ago, put his buckler against the side of its horn at the last half-second and *turned*. The hinge-swing, the door. The ram's own weight went past him, and its curve, the curve that followed you wherever you went, followed him round. Round and round, right into the split stone he'd put at his back.

The crack was enormous. The split in the stone went wider, and a slab came off the top of it and fell into the grass.

[ The Bellwether · Stunned ]

And Chi could move.

She didn't know when it had happened. Somewhere around *two*, maybe. The floor had come back under her feet, and her hands were her own again, and the fear was a bag she'd put down. Thea was moving too, and Xola, and Higu was on her feet on the wall. Squiddles was flashing on Xola's head like an alarm.

Chi went in under the ram's jaw, where Calvin had said there was no wool.

She'd never killed anything with a sword before the Spire. Eight days, and she'd done it maybe forty times, sheep and rats and once something like a badger. She did it now the same way she did everything: slowly, carefully, with all her weight behind it.

The Bellwether lay down on the grass like it was going to sleep.

Chi read the second window three times, sitting on the cropped grass with her sword across her knees, while Waddles, who'd hidden behind the wall for the entire fight, waddled out at last and climbed into her lap as if nothing had happened.

"*Hush*," said Thea, reading it over her shoulder. Thea could read anybody's window now, apparently, if they tipped it even a little. "Chi. That's your voice. That's your stream voice. The window turned your stream voice into a *skill*."

"It's just slow," said Chi.

"It's not just slow." Drew had sat down beside her. He was still very pale, and his hands were still shaking slightly, and he was looking at her in a way she wasn't used to being looked at. "I couldn't move, Chi. I couldn't move anything. I was standing there and that thing was coming, and I was going to stand there and let it hit me." He let out a breath. "And then there was your voice. Saying *breathe*. And I could."

Chi didn't know what to say. She looked down at Waddles.

"I just talked," she said. "I always talk. When everyone's scared."

"Yeah," said Drew. "I know. That's the skill."

***

They slept in the ascension room that night. It was safe and dry and round, with a fire-pit and old ashes in it from somebody before them. Xola said they deserved one night of not walking anywhere.

The second window came at supper, while they were sitting round the little fire Drew had made. *Floor 2 has been cleared.* Everyone looked at it. Nobody said much. Thea opened her rankings and looked at the name at the top for a long time, the little padlock still beside the ten, the floor now a 3. Then she closed it and said, very quietly, mostly to herself, "He's still waiting."

Chi watched her face while she said it.

She'd known Thea for a long time. Not as long as Xola had, but long enough. Long enough to know the difference between Thea worried and Thea sad. This wasn't worried. Thea had been looking at that name every night since the white room with an expression Chi had only seen on her once before, years ago, on a stream. Somebody in chat had asked her about a friend she didn't talk to any more, and Thea had laughed and changed the subject, and then, for one second, before she put her face back, she'd looked like this.

Chi had barely known Ice. She'd known him as a name in her chat when Thea brought him: the guy who was annoyingly good at the word game she played on stream, who always got the long word just before she did and then apologised for it in capitals. She'd liked him. She'd wished him well. She'd never thought much more about him than that.

She thought about him now, watching Thea's face in the firelight.

*Somebody who'd already lost something here*, Thea had said on the river path two nights ago. And then Chi had said, without quite knowing why: *maybe something has*.

She didn't know what she'd meant. She still didn't. But she was the calm one, and calm people noticed things. And she'd noticed that Thea, who could read anything, who could read the words under the words of every window in the Spire, had been trying for two days to read one name at the top of a list. And couldn't. And it was the only thing in the whole world she couldn't read that she wanted to.

***

In the morning they climbed the stair. A hundred and twelve steps; Xola counted, loudly, and complained about every one. At the top Drew opened the door, and the cold came through it like a slap, and beyond the door the whole world was white.

Waddles made a sound Chi had never heard him make.

It was a kind of shriek. A high, delighted, completely undignified penguin shriek. And before anyone could stop him, he'd gone out through the door, across the top of the slope, and down onto his round yellow belly in the snow. And he was *sliding*. Down the hill, faster and faster, flippers out, shrieking with joy, leaving a long smooth track behind him all the way to the trees.

Everyone stood in the doorway and watched him go.

"He's never seen snow," said Chi. Her voice had gone strange. "He's a penguin, and he's never once seen snow. He was a drawing. He was a *plush*."

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Then Thea started to laugh. Not the bright laugh from her streams, but a real one, the kind that comes out of somebody who's been sad for a week and hadn't known she was going to laugh until it happened. And then Xola was laughing, and Drew, and even Higu opened her eyes all the way and smiled. And down at the bottom of the hill, Waddles stood up in the snow, turned round, looked up at them all with his head on one side, and started waddling straight back up the slope so he could do it again.

Chi stood in the doorway in the cold and watched him come, and thought it was the most hopeful thing she'd seen since the white room.
