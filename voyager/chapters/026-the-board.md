Drew had run a tournament from his bedroom once, and he'd never quite stopped running it.

It had been years ago now. Dozens of teams, a long weekend, one spare room with a bad chair and a mug of tea that went cold every hour. He'd organised all of it himself: brackets, schedules, rules, the endless messages from people who'd missed their slot, or whose friend's internet had died. He'd sat in that chair all weekend and held the whole thing together with a spreadsheet and patience. At the end of it, two people he'd only half known had won. Yan, carrying. And Ice, cracking jokes the entire final while Yan won it for them.

And afterwards, when everyone had gone, one of them had stayed behind in the call.

*Hey*, Ice had said. *Drew. Thanks for running this. Seriously. Nobody ever thanks the guy running it.*

That was all. It had taken about ten seconds. Drew had sat in his bad chair at four in the morning with his cold tea and not known what to say, because nobody ever did thank the guy running it. Ice was right. And then Ice had said *anyway, goodnight, love you, except Newish, she's not here but it still counts* and left.

They'd been friends after that. Not close, not for long; maybe eight months. But Drew had liked him, in the specific, quiet way you like the one person who notices the work.

He thought about that a lot, the morning he walked into the trapper's hall in Larchwick and found it in chaos.

***

There were too many of them.

That was the problem. It was the twelfth day, and four whole parties were on floor 2 now: Yan's, Hana's, theirs, and Hyvred's, which had come up the stair that morning. That was twenty people in one small wooden town at the bottom of a valley, and every one of them wanted the same thing. Wolves. Experience. To go up.

And nobody was talking to anybody.

He stood in the doorway of the trapper's hall and watched it. It was like watching a tournament with no bracket. Yan's party was at the big table, arguing about the north slope, because Hana's party had gone up the north slope two days ago and pulled two packs at once and nearly died. Hana's party had only just come back down from the pass. They'd gone up the north slope because nobody had told them Yan's party had cleared the east slope and it was empty. Hyvred, who'd been on floor 2 for four hours, was standing in the middle of the room with her horns catching the firelight, asking everybody at once, loudly and cheerfully, where the wolves were, and getting four different answers.

And Thea was at a side table by herself, reading her window, very carefully not looking at the girl with silver-lilac hair who'd come in behind Hyvred.

Drew stood in the doorway and felt it, the old feeling, the bedroom feeling. *Nobody's running this.*

He went to find the trapper.

***

The trapper had a slate. Of course he did. Every trapper's hall in every cold place in every world had a slate, for chalking up what furs were wanted and what they'd pay. It was a big one, black, the size of a door, hanging on the wall by the fire. Drew asked if he could borrow it, and the trapper looked at him for a long moment and said *what for*. Drew told him, and the trapper looked at him a moment longer and then took it down off its hooks and handed it over, along with a stub of chalk. He seemed relieved that somebody was going to do something about the Climbers.

Drew leaned it against the wall by the big table and started writing.

He didn't say anything at first. He just wrote. He drew a rough map of the valley the way he'd heard it described, the river, the town, the slopes: north, east, south-west, the high woods, the pass. Then he wrote the parties down the side. *Suzuyun's. Hanarisu's. Althea's. Hyvred's.* Then he drew lines.

By the time he'd done the first line, half the room had stopped talking.

By the time he'd done the third, Yan had come over and was standing beside him, very quietly, looking at it.

"East slope's empty," said Yan, after a minute. "We cleared it yesterday."

Drew wrote *EAST: CLEARED (S) · day 11*.

"North's bad," said Hana, from the table. "Two packs. We pulled both. Don't go up there in fewer than five."

Drew wrote *NORTH: 2 PACKS. FULL PARTY ONLY.*

"The pass," said Newish. "There's an old lady at the top who'll shout at you."

Drew wrote *PASS: MOTHER TALLOW. BE NICE.*

"Don't be nice," said Newish. "She likes it when you're rude."

Drew rubbed out *BE NICE* and wrote *BE RUDE*.

People laughed. People came over. People started telling him things, all at once, and Drew did what he'd done all that weekend in his bedroom years ago. He listened to all of it and wrote it down, and asked short questions, and drew lines. Within an hour the slate was covered. Which party was on which slope tomorrow, so nobody pulled anybody else's wolves. Where the packs were. Which way they went round. Wendy had drawn a terrible picture of a wolf going round a stick in the corner, with an arrow and the word *CLOCKWISE*. The trapper had chalked prices for pelts down the side. Tiger had drawn a very small onion, for reasons he wouldn't explain.

And at the very bottom, in a different hand, small and neat, someone had written:

*VOYAGER — FL 3 — LV 10 — (locked, waiting for trial)*

Drew looked at it for a while. He knew whose hand it was. He'd watched her write it, when she thought nobody was looking.

He didn't rub it out.

***

Hyvred's party went up the north slope the next day, because it was on the board, and came back with seven pelts and nobody hurt. Hyvred stood in the doorway of the hall and announced that she was going to marry the board. Yan's party went to the high woods. Hana's went south-west. Theirs went east, where it was quiet, because Higu said they'd earned a quiet day, and Higu was always right.

And on the east slope, in the afternoon, Drew found out what the thing he'd done at the Fold was called.

It was a big wolf. An Alpha, with a torn ear and a scar down one side of its face, leading a pack of four. Higu saw it first, from a rock, and called it. Drew stepped out in front of the others with his buckler up, the way he'd done all morning, and waited for it.

It came at him. Not round. Straight. The big ones did that sometimes. They didn't need to circle. They just came.

And Drew did the thing.

He put the buckler against the side of its head, not the front, at the last half-second, and turned. Like a door on a hinge. All its weight, all its speed, went past him. And because it went past him, it went *into* the wolf that had been coming round his left side. The two of them hit each other at full speed in a white explosion of snow and teeth, and the pack came apart.

He stood in the snow looking at the window.

"*Hinge*," read Thea, beside him. She'd tipped her head to see it; she could read anybody's window now if they let her. "Drew. That's the door thing. That's what you did at the Fold."

"That's what I did at the Fold," Drew agreed.

"*Don't stop it. Turn it.*" She looked at him. "That's very you."

"Is it?"

"You do it with people," said Thea. "In the hall. With the board. Everybody was running into each other, and you didn't tell anybody to stop. You just—" She made a turning gesture with her hand. "You turned them. So they went past each other instead."

Drew thought about that.

"Huh," he said.

The wolf with the torn ear had got him on the way past, all the same: a long shallow rake down his left forearm, above the buckler strap. He didn't notice until Xola grabbed his wrist on the walk back and made a noise like a kettle.

"*Look* at you. Look at this. You've been bleeding on the snow for half a mile, Drew, there's a *trail*. The wolves are going to follow it home like breadcrumbs." She pushed his sleeve up without asking and put both hands over the scratches, and a cool blue wash came off her palms and sank in. Squiddles leaned down from the top of her head to watch, very interested. "Hold still. *Hold still.* I'm doing the thing."

The thing was Xola's. Nobody else's healing felt like it. It didn't close a wound all at once, the way Rachael's did in the stories from the Spoon. It made the wound *remember* how to close, and then it let it. The scratches went pink, then pale, then were gone. For half a minute afterwards the skin round them felt new, slightly tingling, as if it had been replaced.

"Axolotls regrow their legs," said Xola, with great satisfaction, letting go. "Did you know that? Their whole legs. I looked it up once for a stream. So a scratch is nothing. A scratch is an *insult*."

"Thanks, Xola."

"Don't thank me. Stop bleeding on things. You're the door guy now, the door guy doesn't bleed."

"I don't think that's how doors work."

"It's how *my* door works," said Xola, and went on ahead, chattering to Squiddles.

Higu fell into step beside him. She didn't say anything for a while; she rarely did. The light was going, the painted sky over the valley deepening from grey to dark blue. Up near the top of it, where on floor 1 there'd only been the switched-on stars, a small pale disc had come out. A moon. Painted, like everything, but a moon all the same. Higu had been looking up at it every night since they'd come up the stair.

"She's going to work it out," said Higu.

Drew looked at her.

"Thea." Higu's lilac-grey eyes were half-lidded, calm, fixed on the pale painted moon. "Whatever she's trying to read about him. She'll work it out. She always does. She takes things apart until they tell her." She was quiet for a step or two. "Don't push her. Let her do it at her own speed. It's faster that way."

"I wasn't going to push her."

"I know," said Higu. "I'm just telling you. In case you were going to want to."

She was right. He had wanted to. He walked the rest of the way back to Larchwick beside her in silence, under the painted moon, thinking about how Higu always seemed to know what you were going to do just before you did.

***

He found Gigi at the edge of the stockade that evening, on her own, looking at the light on the mountain.

It was the first time he'd been alone with her since the white room. He'd known her for years, the way he knew most people in Ice's world, through Maigo, through calls. She was blunt and bratty, with a smug, tongue-out laugh, and caring underneath in a way she'd never admit. He'd always liked her. He'd watched Thea walk past her in the white room, and he'd watched Hyvred catch her. And he'd thought, at the time: *that's going to cost somebody something*.

She looked round when she heard him on the snow, and her face went guarded for a second, then eased.

"Oh," she said. "It's you."

"It's me."

"Come to tell me to make up with her?"

"No."

"Good." She looked back at the mountain. The beacon on the pass was a gold spark against the dark, steady, unmoving. "Because I'm not going to."

"Okay."

They stood there. Drew didn't say anything. That was the trick, mostly. Most people, if you stood next to them and didn't say anything, ended up saying the thing they'd come out into the cold to not say.

"Is she sleeping?" said Gigi, finally.

Drew looked at her.

"She looks like she's not sleeping," said Gigi. She didn't look at him. "She's got the thing under her eyes. She used to get it before exams. She'd stay up all night and then pretend she hadn't." A pause. "I'm not asking because I care. I'm just asking."

"She's not sleeping much," said Drew.

"Is it him?"

"It's him."

Gigi nodded slowly. She didn't say anything for a long time. The wind came off the mountain and moved her silver hair, and the forget-me-nots pinned in it.

"She used to stay up with him," she said. "Every night. For a whole year. She'd sing and he'd play something, and she'd turn up the next day looking like a ghost and smiling like an idiot." She pulled her fur tighter. "I used to be so annoyed about it. Not because of him. Because she'd stopped staying up with *me*."

Drew didn't say anything.

"That's stupid," said Gigi. "I know that's stupid."

"It's not stupid."

"It's a bit stupid." She was quiet. "Tell her—"

She stopped.

"Don't tell her anything," she said. "Never mind." And she turned round and went back into the stockade, quickly, before he could say anything, her boots crunching in the snow.

Drew stood at the edge of the town on his own for a while, looking at the light on the mountain.

***

He found Thea at the board.

It was late. The trapper's hall had emptied out, and the fire was down to red, and she was standing in front of the slate with a stub of chalk in her hand. She was looking at the line at the very bottom, the one in her own small neat hand. *VOYAGER — FL 3 — LV 10 — (locked, waiting for trial).*

She didn't jump when he came up. She'd heard him.

"She asked if you were sleeping," said Drew.

Thea went very still.

"Gigi," said Drew. "Out by the stockade. She asked if you were sleeping. She said you've got the thing under your eyes, from exams." He paused. "She said she wasn't asking because she cared."

Thea didn't say anything for a long time. Then she put the chalk down on the ledge under the slate, very carefully, as if it might break.

"She always noticed that," she said. Her voice wasn't quite steady. "The exam thing. Nobody else ever did."

"I know."

"Did she say anything else?"

Drew thought about *tell her*, and *never mind*, and Gigi going back into the stockade too fast.

"No," he said. "But she wanted to."

Thea nodded slowly. She didn't cry. Drew had the feeling she'd decided some time ago not to, about Gigi, and was sticking to it. But something in her shoulders let go, very slightly, like a fist opening.

They stood in front of the board together.

"Can I tell you something?" said Thea. "Something stupid?"

"Sure."

"I've got this feeling. About him." She nodded at the line at the bottom of the slate. "I can't explain it. I've tried. I keep trying to read it, the way I read the windows, and it doesn't open. It's just a feeling." She swallowed. "He's not running ahead of us, Drew. I don't think he's running at all. It's like he's going somewhere he's already been. Somewhere it went wrong. And he's going back to fix it." She laughed a little, not very well. "That doesn't make any sense. I know it doesn't make any sense."

Drew looked at the line on the slate. *Locked, waiting for trial.*

He thought about a man staying behind in a call at four in the morning, to thank the guy running it. He thought about Mother Tallow's door, and the claw marks Newish said were all round it. He thought about a name at the top of a list that never once looked back.

"Keep it," he said.

Thea looked at him.

"The feeling," said Drew. "Keep it. Don't explain it yet." He picked up the chalk from the ledge and turned it over in his fingers. "When I was running that tournament, I used to get feelings like that. About a team, or a match, or a bracket. Something's going to go wrong here. And I'd try to explain it, to the others, the admins, and I never could. And because I couldn't explain it, they'd talk me out of it." He shrugged. "And then it'd go wrong. Exactly where I felt it."

"So you're saying I'm right?"

"I'm saying keep it," said Drew. "Feelings get worse when you explain them too early. People start arguing with the explanation and forget the feeling was there first." He held out the chalk. "Write it down somewhere if you have to. Somewhere only you can read."

Thea took the chalk. She looked at it for a moment.

Then she reached up to the bottom corner of the slate, under the line about Voyager, and wrote something very small. She wrote it in the tiny, careful hand she used for things she didn't want anyone to see, and it went on the slate in a language that wasn't English. Something she'd made up, maybe. Some private code of her own. Drew couldn't read a word of it.

"There," she said. "Kept."

"Kept," said Drew.

She went to bed. Drew stayed by the board for a while, looking at the whole of it. The map, the slopes, the lines. Wendy's terrible wolf going round its stick, and Tiger's onion, and the prices, and the parties. Twenty people, four parties, one valley, all of it on one slate, all of it talking to each other for the first time since the white room.

*Nobody ever thanks the guy running it.*

He thought there'd be a lot more slates, before this was over. Bigger ones, with more names on them. Eventually, somewhere up there on floor 3, all thirty-five names on the same one, in a room. And somebody would have to stand in front of it with the chalk.

He thought, a bit ruefully, touching the *88EXP* nameplate on its chain at his throat, that it would probably be him.

He went to bed.
