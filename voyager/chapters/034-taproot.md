Maigo had been quiet for sixteen days, and he'd only just worked out why.

Everybody thought it was the white room. That he'd woken up in a body the size of a ten-year-old, made of bark, with branches coming out of his head and white light where his eyes should be, and had gone quiet with the shock of it. People had been very kind about it. Chris checked on him. Yan watched him from across rooms with that calm half-lidded look, the one that meant he was worried and didn't want you to know. Even Winter, who didn't check on anybody, had once silently handed him the last bread roll.

It wasn't the body. He liked the body. He'd drawn it, or had it drawn, years ago, a cocky little sapling in an orange hoodie and pixel sunglasses. He'd been delighted to wake up in it. He was still delighted. He was a *tree*. How many people got to be a tree?

It was the winter.

He worked it out on the first afternoon on floor 2. He came out of the door at the top of the stair into the cold and felt himself slow down. Not his legs. Something under his legs. Something in him that ran up from the bare root-foot he'd always had, the one without a shoe, and through the whole of him like sap. It went thick and slow in the cold. It wanted to *sleep*.

Trees went quiet in winter. Of course they did. He'd been walking around in a tree's body for two weeks with a tree's sense of the seasons. Floor 1 had been an endless gentle spring, and now here was snow, and his whole body was saying *shh. Not now. Wait for the thaw.*

He thought that was probably funny. He'd tell somebody, eventually. When he felt like talking.

***

Yan was in the trapper's hall when they came in.

Maigo saw him straight away. Long silver-white hair in a topknot, black-and-crimson robes under a heavy fur, Suzu round his neck like a white scarf. He was sitting at the big table by the fire with Salty and Wendy and Tiger and Cursiffe. He looked up when the door opened and the cold came in, and his eyes found Maigo before anybody else's did.

Maigo stood in the doorway in his orange hoodie with his hood up and his sunglasses on, a small brown shape at about everybody's waist height, and looked back.

Sixteen days. Yan had given him away in the white room, to Chris, because the maths only went one way. He'd crouched down so they were the same height and asked, and Maigo had said *you'd come back for me*. And Yan had said *I'd come back for you*.

Yan got up from the table. He walked across the trapper's hall, through everyone, round the board, past the fire. He crouched down in front of Maigo in the doorway, slowly, the way he did everything.

"Hi," said Yan.

"Hi," said Maigo.

"You came up."

"I came up." Maigo thought about it. "You didn't come back for me. I came up to you. That's cheating."

Yan's mouth went up at the corner.

"I'll do better next time," he said.

And then Salty was there. Salty didn't say anything at all; he just picked Maigo up. Under the arms, like a child, which was humiliating. Maigo let him, because it was Salty, and Salty only did things like that when he'd been worried and didn't have words for it. Wendy was behind him, smiling the small knowing smile from her model. Tiger was behind her, grinning like an idiot, because Maigo had brought Tiger into all of this, years ago, into calls and games and Ice's whole world. Tiger never forgot it.

"Put me down," said Maigo.

"No," said Salty.

"I'm a tree. You're holding a tree."

"I know what I'm holding," said Salty, and didn't put him down for quite a long time.

When he finally did, Maigo found Chris standing in front of the slate on the wall by the fire with his mouth open.

It was a big black slate, the size of a door, covered from edge to edge in chalk. A map of the valley. Slopes and arrows and lines. Which party was where, which packs went which way round, what the trapper paid for pelts. A very bad drawing of a wolf going round a stick. A tiny onion in one corner. And at the bottom, small and neat: *VOYAGER — FL 3 — LV 10 — (locked, waiting for trial)*. Somebody had crossed out the *10* and written *16* above it, and crossed out *locked*.

Chris was looking at it the way some people look at paintings in museums.

"Who did this?" he said.

"Drew," said Tiger. "First day all four parties were up here. Took it off the trapper and did it in an afternoon."

Chris didn't say anything for a moment. Then he took out his little notebook, opened it to a fresh page, and started copying the board into it. Every line. Every arrow. The terrible wolf. The tiny onion.

Drew came in from the cold about ten minutes later. He found a short man in his thirties standing in front of his slate, filling a notebook with it, with an expression of pure, uncomplicated joy.

"Hi," said Drew.

"You made a *board*," said Chris.

Drew looked at the notebook in Chris's hands. At the little graph on the back page, which Chris had left open: days along the bottom, levels up the side, a flat line of dots and then one going straight up.

"You made a *graph*," said Drew.

They looked at each other for a long moment. Then they both started talking at once. Maigo, who'd been leaning against the wall by the fire with his hood back up, watched them stand in front of the slate together for the next hour, pointing at things, interrupting each other, writing things down. He thought it was one of the nicest things he'd seen since the white room.

Two people who liked putting things in order, finding each other. In a wooden town, at the bottom of a valley, in the snow. Ice would have made a joke about it.

Maigo didn't. He just watched, and felt the warmth from the fire on his bark, and let himself be quiet.

***

He went to the pine with Yan that afternoon.

Everybody knew about the pine. Tiger had put it in World on the sixth day: *found a camp on floor 2. east of the stair, under a big pine. somebody peeled onions here.* Since then, half the people on the floor had walked out along the treeline to look at it, the way you'd go and look at a place in a story. Gabe had gone. Gabe had sat there, people said, by the cold fire, until Xav made him get up.

Maigo hadn't gone to look at the fire. He'd gone to look at the tree.

It was enormous. He felt it before he saw it. He'd been feeling trees since floor 1, through the bare root-foot, faintly, the way you feel music through a floor. The pines on floor 2 were all slow and cold and half asleep, like him. But this one was something else. It was *old*. It came up through his foot from a long way off, a deep, slow, enormous presence under the snow, like standing on the roof of a cathedral.

He stood under its branches and put his root-foot flat on the frozen ground, between two of its great roots, and closed his white eyes behind the sunglasses.

The tree didn't talk. Trees didn't talk. But they *remembered*, in a slow, vague, wordless way. Weight, mostly. Warmth. The places where something had pressed against them for a long time.

It remembered someone.

Not long ago. A couple of weeks, which was nothing, to a tree. Someone had sat with their back against its trunk, right *there*, all night, while snow fell outside its branches. Someone warm. Warmer than people usually were. Warm in a way that had a colour to it, if warmth could have a colour. And something smaller beside him, curled up, that was warm too, but differently.

The tree remembered that they'd been very still. The way things are still when they're holding on.

Maigo stood there with his foot on the root for a long time.

"Maigo?" said Yan quietly, behind him.

"He sat here," said Maigo. "Against the tree. All night. The tree remembers."

Yan didn't say anything. Suzu, round his neck, lifted her head.

"He was warm," said Maigo. "Really warm. Warmer than people are. Like he had a fire in him." He didn't open his eyes. "And he had something with him. Something little. That slept next to him."

"Hyvred said the same thing," said Yan. "About the ascension room. A little light, curled up next to his."

"Then it's true."

"It's true," said Yan.

Maigo took his foot off the root and opened his eyes, and looked at the ring of cold stones, the old ashes, and the onion skins. There were only a couple left. Everybody who came had taken one.

He'd known Ice for two years. Two years of calls and games and constant, endless, stupid banter, the two of them trading insults across Yan's careful rotations like kids passing notes in class. Ice was the only person who'd ever called him *Maigotron*. Nobody else did. He'd never asked anyone else to. It was a stupid name. It sounded like a robot. It was the best name anybody had ever given him.

He hadn't heard it in sixteen days. He'd been in a white room and a tower and two worlds, and nobody had called him that, because the one person who did was a floor ahead, alone, with something small asleep beside him.

"Maigotron," he said, very quietly, to the cold fire.

Yan heard him. Of course he did. Yan heard everything. He didn't say anything. He just crouched down beside Maigo by the ring of stones, close, the way you'd sit next to somebody at a funeral who didn't want to be talked to.

***

Yan's hands were white.

Maigo saw it while they were crouching there. Yan had them tucked into the sleeves of his fur, but when he crouched they came out, and they were white. Not cold-pale, *white*, the colour of paper, from the fingertips to past the wrists. He'd been out on the slopes that morning with his party, and something had gone wrong, and he'd done the thing with his hands that he did. Shelter, Salty called it, darkly. The thing where Yan cupped his hands round someone's light and the cold went into him instead.

"Your hands," said Maigo.

"It's fine. It wears off."

"How long?"

Yan didn't answer straight away. Which meant it was longer than he wanted to say.

"Since this morning," he said. "It's taking longer up here. The cold, I think. They don't warm up."

Salty had made him a hand-warmer, everybody knew. A little iron box with a coal in it. Yan had given it to Cursiffe that morning, to keep his ink from freezing, because Yan always gave things away.

Maigo looked at Yan's white hands for a long moment.

Then he looked down at his own root-foot, bare on the frozen ground under the great pine, between its two enormous roots. And he thought: *the tree is warm*.

Not on top. On top it was snow and cold and half asleep, like everything on floor 2. But underneath, a long way down, where the great roots went into the ground further than anything else on the mountain, it was warm. It had to be. It was alive. It had been alive for a very long time, through every winter. Down there, deep, under everything, it kept something warm going, all the way through, and waited for spring.

He'd been healing by growing since floor 1. Little green shoots out of the ground, winding up people's legs, closing their hurts. It was slow, and it cost him, and it was always *his*. It came out of him, out of his own small sap.

He'd never thought to ask the ground.

He put his root-foot down between the great pine's roots. And this time he didn't just feel. He *reached*. Down, the way roots reach. Down through the frozen top of the ground, through the cold, down past where the snow could get to, down and down along the pine's great roots to the place where it was warm.

He drank.

It came up into him like sun coming up through a floor. Slow, deep, enormous. Warm. It filled the cold thick sap in him and made it move again, made it *run*, and for the first time since he'd come out of the stair door onto floor 2, he didn't feel like sleeping.

He took Yan's white hands in his own small brown ones.

"Hold still," he said.

He gave it away.

The warmth went out of him and into Yan's hands, steady, deep, the warmth of something very old that had kept itself alive through every winter on the mountain. Maigo watched the white go out of them. From the wrists down, slowly, like snow melting off a branch in the sun. Pale, then pink, then warm and living and Yan's own.

Yan looked down at his hands.

He turned them over. He flexed them. He looked at Maigo, small and brown and crouching in the snow in front of him with his root-foot down and his sunglasses crooked. Yan's pale cyan eyes were very bright.

"Maigo," he said.

"Don't," said Maigo.

"I wasn't going to say anything."

"You were going to say something nice." Maigo let go of his hands. "Don't. Just don't do the hand thing so much."

"I'll do it as much as I have to," said Yan.

"I know." Maigo pulled his root-foot up out of the ground and stood. He was warm, all the way through, for the first time in two days. "That's why I'll be standing next to you."

***

The window came that evening, in the trapper's hall, while they were all at the big table. Yan's party and Chris's party, together, for the first time since the white room. Salty had his arm round Wendy and Tiger was telling a very long story, badly, about a wolf.

[ Floor 3 has been cleared. ]

Everyone stopped.

Maigo looked at it for a long time, sitting at the table with his hood down and his little branch antlers brushing the edge of Salty's elbow. *Floor 3.* On his own. On the sixteenth day. While the rest of them sat at a table on floor 2 eating barley.

He looked across the table at Yan. Yan was looking at the window too, with his newly warm hands wrapped round a bowl, and Suzu's head resting on his collarbone. His face was very still.

"He's going to go up," said Maigo.

Everybody looked at him. It was the first thing he'd said at the table all night.

"To four," said Maigo. "He's cleared three. He'll go up. Tonight or tomorrow." He didn't know how he knew. He just did, the way the tree had known about the warmth. "He doesn't wait. He never waits. In every game, he'd clear something and be in the next room before the loot had finished dropping."

"He waited a week at level ten," said Chris quietly, from the end of the table. He had his notebook out. "On floor 2."

"He wasn't waiting for himself," said Maigo. "He was waiting for something to be ready." He looked down at his own small brown hands on the table. "He's going up to four. On his own. And none of us are going to be there."

Nobody said anything.

***

Niko got a nosebleed the next morning, trying to put a spell through his sword, and Maigo was the one who fixed it.

Niko had been reading spellbooks since floor 1. Old ones, borrowed from the little library at the back of Quill's class hall. Mage books, for Mages, not for Knights with swords. He'd been reading them every evening, by candlelight, in his calm, put-together way, as if it were the most normal thing in the world. Nobody had asked him why. Niko was the kind of person you didn't ask why. He'd tell you, eventually, when he was ready, and he'd make it sound obvious.

That morning, outside the stockade, he stood in the snow with his plain Knight's sword in one hand and a spellbook open in the other. He read a line under his breath, and *pushed*.

The sword flickered. Just for a second. A thin line of orange light ran up the blade from the hilt like a struck match. Then it went out, and Niko staggered, and blood came out of his nose and onto the snow.

He sat down quite suddenly.

Maigo was beside him before anyone else had moved. He put his root-foot down in the snow, reached, drank, and gave it away. A little green warmth went into Niko's face, and the bleeding stopped.

Niko sat in the snow with blood on his lip and the spellbook in his lap, looking at his sword.

"It lit," he said. He sounded mildly interested. "For a second. Did you see it light?"

"I saw it light," said Maigo. "Then I saw you bleed."

"Yes." Niko wiped his lip on his sleeve and considered the blood. "That's not right yet." He closed the spellbook. "Not yet. I don't know what I'm doing. I'll know later."

He said it the way he said everything, chill and calm and sure. As if *later* were a place he'd already been and come back from.

Maigo looked at him for a long time. Then he sat down in the snow beside him, both of them looking at the sword.

"He'd have called you something," said Maigo. "If he saw that. He'd have made a joke."

Niko was quiet for a moment.

"He would," he agreed. "He always had a name for me nobody else used." He didn't say what it was. He didn't need to. Maigo knew. Everyone in Ice's old squad knew. It was the same as *Maigotron*. Two people, two stupid names, and only one person who'd ever used them.

They sat in the snow together, the tree and the man with the sword, outside the stockade at the bottom of the white valley. Neither of them said the names out loud.
