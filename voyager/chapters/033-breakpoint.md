*Day 1*, said the first line in Chris's notebook. *Woke up in a white room. Font on the windows: excellent. Everything else: concerning.*

He'd written it on the first afternoon, sitting on the steps of the white tower in Hearthstead with a stub of pencil and a little notebook he'd talked Quill into giving him. The rest of his party had been standing around him in the sun, looking at the town. He'd needed somewhere to put things. He'd always needed somewhere to put things. On Earth it had been a text file on his desktop called *notes_FINAL_v3_actualfinal.txt*, nine years long. Everything had gone in it. Bugs, ideas, recipes, a list of every time his left knee had made a noise.

Here he had a notebook. So everything went in the notebook.

By the fourteenth day it was nearly full.

***

*Day 2. The rankings say VOYAGER. Was blank last night. Name assigned overnight? Name item? Possibly a reward for the floor 1 clear. Note: find out what drops from the ram.*

*Day 2. Calvin: stab sheep under the jaw. Tested: true. Calvin is a hero.*

*Day 3. My sword arm is slower on the backswing than the foreswing. Probably the shoulder. Investigate.*

*Day 4. It's the shoulder. Left foot is also wrong. I think my left foot has a memory leak. Winter says that's not a real sentence. It's a real sentence.*

*Day 5. Voyager: 9 → 10 at 21:40. Floor 2.*

*Day 6. Voyager: 10. Not moving. All day.*

*Day 6. THEA FOUND A PADLOCK. Lv 10 is a wall. Tier trial. 3 options. Read her post three times. She's brilliant. Nobody else had noticed. I didn't notice. I was looking straight at it. Embarrassing.*

*Day 7. Voyager: 10. Padlock.*

*Day 8. Voyager: 10. Padlock. Floor 3 (!!). Floor 2 cleared 19:12.*

*Day 9. Voyager: 10. Padlock.*

*Day 10. Voyager: 10.*

*Day 11. 10.*

*Day 12. 10.*

He'd started drawing it, around day 9. A little graph on the back pages, the way he'd have drawn a graph of anything: days along the bottom, levels up the side, a dot for every morning. On Earth he'd drawn graphs of build times and memory use and, once, during a very long weekend, of how many times Newish said *rude* per hour on a call. It was how he thought. You couldn't see what something was doing until you could see its shape.

Voyager's shape was strange.

Six levels on the first day. Four more by the fifth. Then a flat line. Seven days of flat line, all the way along the bottom of the back page. A dot at ten, a dot at ten, a dot at ten, like a heartbeat monitor that had stopped.

Except it hadn't stopped. That was the thing. It said *held*. Thea's padlock. Something was still going on under the line. He just couldn't see what.

***

They were the last party on floor 1.

Chris didn't mind, exactly. Somebody had to be last, and he'd been last at a lot of things in his life and it had usually worked out. But he knew why. He'd written it down, on day 10, because writing it down was how he stopped it bothering him.

*We are slow because:*
*1. I redo everything until it's right.*
*2. Niko spends every evening reading spellbooks he's not supposed to be able to use.*
*3. Winter won't fight next to anyone she doesn't trust yet, and she doesn't trust anyone yet.*
*4. Ina won't wear the mask, and without the mask his fire is half as hot.*
*5. Maigo doesn't talk. (Not a reason. Just noticing.)*

None of them were problems, exactly. They were just *things*. Things you had to work round. Chris had spent nine years working round things in code that didn't do what it was supposed to, and he'd found that most of the time, if you were patient and wrote everything down, the code turned out to be doing something very sensible. Just not what you'd expected.

People were the same. He'd been fairly sure of that since he was about twelve.

So he'd been patient. And they'd got slowly, steadily, unglamorously good. And now it was the fourteenth day, and everybody else was on floor 2, and the only other people still on floor 1 were Deston's party.

Deston's party had come back down.

They'd gone up on the fifth day, the first party to clear the ram. Then they'd come straight back down to tell everyone what floor 2 was like. And then they'd just... stayed. Deston said they were training. Deston said they wanted to be sure. But Chris had noticed that Deston's party only ever seemed to be training in whatever field Chris's party was in. They always ate at the long table at the Spoon at the same time. And Deston always, at the end of the day, asked how they were getting on with the Fold.

Chris had written that down too.

*Day 11. I think Deston is babysitting us.*

*Day 12. Deston is definitely babysitting us.*

*Day 13. Asked him. He said "no, we're training." Then he asked how we were getting on with the Fold. He's babysitting us. I find it very touching.*

***

On the fourteenth morning, Chris opened the rankings at breakfast the way he did every morning. He looked at the top line. Then he put his spoon down so suddenly it rang against the bowl, and the whole long table at the Copper Spoon turned to look at him.

[ Rankings
1. Voyager · Lv 16 · Knight · Floor 3
]

"Sixteen," said Chris.

"What?" said Ina, from across the table. He was eating porridge with great elegance, which Chris hadn't thought was possible.

"*Sixteen*. He's sixteen. He was ten last night. I wrote it down. I've got it here." He was already flipping the notebook open, finding the back page, the little graph with its flat line of dots. "Ten, ten, ten, ten, ten, for a week, and this morning he's *sixteen*."

He drew the dot. It went straight up off the flat line, six squares in one day, like a pin pulled out of a grenade.

The table had gone quiet.

"So he did the trial," said Niko. Niko said everything as if it were slightly interesting and not at all alarming. He was thin and short and very calm, with a spellbook open beside his porridge that he wasn't supposed to be able to use. "The tier thing. Thea's padlock. He did it."

"He did it, and then—" Chris was working it out on the page as he talked. "Thea said *held*. Experience held. That means he's been earning it all along. Every day at ten, every fight, the experience was going *somewhere*. Into a pot. And the trial opened the pot." He looked up. "Six levels. In one night. That's a week of experience he couldn't use, all at once."

"Which means," said Winter slowly. She was sitting very straight at the end of the bench, with her silver hair pulled back and her pale blue eye and her pink eye both on the notebook. "He kept fighting. At the wall. For a week. Knowing it was going into a pot."

Chris looked at her.

"Yeah," he said.

"Thea only found the padlock on the sixth day," said Winter. "He'd been at the wall since the fifth. And he kept killing things the whole time anyway." She was quiet for a moment. "You don't do that unless you know it's being saved. You'd stop. You'd think you were stuck."

Nobody said anything.

Chris looked down at his little graph. At the flat line. At the dot going straight up off the end of it.

*He knew*, he thought. *Whatever the padlock was, whatever "held" meant, he knew before Thea did. He knew on day five.*

He wrote it down. He didn't know what else to do with it.

*Day 14. Voyager: 10 → 16 overnight. Did his trial. Held XP released all at once. Note (Winter): he kept fighting at the cap for a week. Knew it was being saved. Before anyone found the padlock.*

*How?*

***

He posted it in World, because it was useful and people should know, and because he couldn't not.

[ World · Chris: VOYAGER 10 -> 16 OVERNIGHT. he did his tier trial. all the "held" xp from the last week came out at once. so: if you're at 10 (everyone soon) KEEP KILLING THINGS. it's not wasted. it's banking. ]

***

They did the Fold the next day. With Deston's whole party sitting on the last wall above it, watching.

Chris pretended not to know why they were there. Deston pretended he'd just happened to be walking up the valley that morning with all four of his friends, and had stopped for a rest on the wall that overlooked the boss. Chris let him have it. It seemed important to Deston, and Chris had always believed in letting people have the things that were important to them.

They had the guide. Everybody had the guide by now. It was a long one, a whole history of the ram in World, written by six different parties:

- Deston's four lines.
- Calvin's jaw.
- Thea's strap health and the stun timing, *three point two seconds*.
- Chi's *talk slowly when it screams*.
- Hyvred's *aim for the thin bit under its left ear*.
- Xav's party's note, which just said *wait for it to commit, then move once*. Chris was fairly sure that one was Xav.

Chris had copied all of it into the notebook in order and drawn diagrams.

It went well. Better than well, for a while. Maigo stood in the grass behind them with his eyes glowing and his little branch antlers trembling. Every time someone got hit, small green shoots came up out of the ground round their feet and wound up their legs, and the hurt closed up. Winter was on a rock with her bow, and her arrows came off the string with a faint frost on them and hit exactly where she meant them to. Ina had his fire going, but not the mask; never the mask. And Niko was in the middle of it with his plain Knight's sword, quiet and calm, doing exactly what he was supposed to.

Chris held the front. He'd taken the shield at the class hall without thinking. He wanted to be the one who saw everything coming, and you saw everything from the front.

They cut the strap in two stuns.

The bell fell.

The Bellwether screamed.

***

Everyone had described it. Cold water. A floor going out from under you. *Afraid*, Thea's notes said. *You can't move until it passes, and it lasts as long as you hold on to it.*

For Chris it wasn't like that at all.

For Chris, when the scream hit, everything *stopped*.

Not him. Everything else. The ram, mid-turn, head coming round. Niko, mid-step, one foot off the grass. Winter's arrow, half a metre off the string, hanging in the air with frost on its tip. Ina's fire, a ball of orange standing perfectly still above his palm. A blade of grass, bent over by somebody's boot, not springing back. All of it held. Frozen. Like a frame from a film, or a program paused halfway through a line.

He knew this. He'd lived half his life in this. Halfway through a program that wasn't doing what you wanted, you put in a breakpoint. The program stopped on that exact line, with everything held where it was. You could look at every single variable, every value, every state, slowly, as long as you liked, before you let it go on.

He couldn't move. He could *look*.

So he looked.

He looked at the ram. Its head was coming round to the left, toward Niko, who'd been nearest when the strap went. Its weight was on its right foreleg. Its left foreleg was coming down. When it came down, that was the push, and the curve would bend round toward wherever Niko went.

He looked at Niko. Niko was standing exactly in the line of it. In about a second and a half he'd still be frozen, and he'd still be standing there.

He looked at the split stone. It was four metres behind Niko and to the right.

He looked at Maigo's shoots in the grass, and at Winter's arrow, hanging. At the angle of it. It was going to hit the ram in the left shoulder, which was useless. If it had been ten centimetres higher, it would have gone in under the ear, where Hyvred said, and the ram would have flinched to the *right*.

Chris looked at all of it. Slowly. As long as he liked.

Then he let it go.

***

"*NIKO, RIGHT, TWO STEPS, NOW*—"

He didn't know how he'd got the words out. The scream was still going. Everybody was still locked up. But the words came out of him, very fast and very clear, the way they came out when he was reading a stack trace aloud to somebody at three in the morning. And Niko, who'd been frozen, *moved*. Not because he'd stopped being afraid. Because Chris had told him exactly where to go, and Niko had always been very good at being told where to go.

Two steps right. The ram's left foreleg came down and it pushed, and it curved round after Niko, toward the place where Niko had been. And Niko wasn't there, and the ram's curve went round, and round, and into the split stone four metres behind him.

Crack.

"*WINTER, HIGHER, UNDER THE EAR*—"

The next arrow came off the string ten centimetres higher, frost on its tip. It went in under the ear, and the ram flinched right, into the stone again.

"*Everyone, now, it's stuck, three seconds*—"

It was enough.

***

[ Floor Guardian defeated · Chris's party ]

[ Unique skill: Breakpoint
Everything stops. Look at all of it. Then let it go on.
]

Chris sat on the grass with his notebook open on his knee and read it, and laughed, and couldn't stop.

"*Breakpoint*," said Niko, reading over his shoulder, with an expression of very slight amusement. "That's a coding thing, isn't it."

"That's a coding thing. That's the *best* coding thing. That's the thing you do when you can't work out why it's broken. You stop it, and you look at everything, and you find the one value that's wrong." Chris looked up at Niko, grinning. "Everything stopped, Niko. When it screamed. Everything just *stopped*, and I could look at it. I could see where you'd be. I could see where Winter's arrow would land." He laughed again. "I've been doing it for nine years in a chair. I never thought it would work on a *sheep*."

Winter came down off her rock and stood in front of him, with her bow in her hand.

"Higher," she said. "Under the ear."

"Yeah."

"You saw where my arrow was going before it got there."

"Yeah. Sorry. I didn't mean to—"

"No," said Winter. She looked at him for a long moment with her mismatched eyes. Pale blue, pink, both very steady. Then she said, very bluntly, the way she said everything: "That was good. You were right." And she turned round and went to pick up her arrows.

Chris looked after her. In the time he'd known her, which was six hundred games at least, he couldn't remember Winter ever saying *you were right* to anybody. Particularly not to a man.

He wrote it down.

***

Deston came down off the wall when it was over, with his whole party behind him. He walked across the cropped grass of the Fold to where Chris was sitting with his notebook, and looked down at him. Chris couldn't read his face at all. It was the face of a man who'd been holding something heavy for two weeks and had just been allowed to put it down.

"That's everyone," said Deston.

Chris looked up.

"All seven parties," said Deston. "Off floor 1. That's everyone." He let out a long breath. "Everyone's up."

"You were babysitting us," said Chris.

"I was training."

"You were babysitting us. I wrote it down. Day eleven." Chris held up the notebook. "I find it very touching."

Deston looked at him for a second. Then he laughed, short and tired and real, and sat down on the grass beside him. He didn't say anything for a while. He put his hand flat on his shirt pocket, the way Chris had seen him do a hundred times, and left it there.

"Floor 3," said Deston finally. "When everybody's ready. All of us. Together." He looked up the valley at the door in the hill. "Not one party at a time. All of us."

"Why?"

Deston didn't answer straight away.

"Because somebody told me to be careful," he said. "And I want to be careful all at once. With everyone in the same room."

Chris looked at Deston's hand on his pocket.

He didn't ask. He didn't need to. He'd been logging Deston's pocket for fourteen days, the same way he'd been logging Voyager. He knew there was something in it, and he knew it mattered, and he knew Deston wasn't ready to say what.

He opened the notebook to a fresh page. He wrote the date. Then, under it:

*Day 15. Floor 1: all parties clear. Everyone's up.*

*Deston: "Floor 3. All of us. Together."*

*Something in his pocket. Not asking yet.*
