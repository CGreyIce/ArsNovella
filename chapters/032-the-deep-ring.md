The seal was a door, in the end. A real one: oak and iron, nine feet tall, set into the stone at the bottom of the first ring of the Hollow, where the old stair went on down into the dark. The guild had built it in a week, after the third party ran into something that shouldn't have been that shallow and only two of them came back. It had a bar across it as thick as John's thigh. It had two guards on it, day and night, in shifts.

And since the start of the winter, it had John.

He'd volunteered. Nobody had asked him to. He'd just walked down to the seal one night in the second week after Azac got up, and sat on the cold step beside the guards, and looked at the door. The guild master had come down the next morning to ask what the summoned boy was doing sitting on his stairs, and John had said, "Watching the light," and the guild master had looked at him as if he were mad. Then Garrick had explained what John could do, and the guild master had looked at him differently.

So now John watched the light.

***

That was his path. He'd known since the first month, in the Hollow, when he'd started seeing it before anyone else: the faint change in the dark half a second before something came round a corner, like a sound cue in a game, except it wasn't a sound. Light had a *shape*, down here. It moved. It pooled in the low places and thinned in the high ones. It leaked round corners. And when something alive moved through it, it bent, just slightly, the way water bends round a stone.

He could see the bending. That was the whole trick. He couldn't make light; he was still an Initiate, still sensing, still reading. But he could read it better than anyone in Duskhollow, and the guild master, after a week, had stopped calling him the summoned boy and started calling him *the lamp*.

The door had a gap at the bottom. A finger's width, between the oak and the stone. And through that gap, every night, John watched the light from below.

There shouldn't have been any.

That was the thing he'd noticed first, and the thing that kept him coming back. Below the seal, the old stair went down into the second ring, and the third, and the deep places where nobody had been in a year. There were no lamps down there. No torches. No windows to the sky. It should have been dark. Perfectly, absolutely dark.

It wasn't.

***

"You're doing it again," said Azac.

He'd come down the stair with two mugs of something hot, the way he'd started doing most nights. He was walking properly again now, without the stick. There was a long pale scar down his side that he showed to anyone who asked and several people who didn't. He sat down on the step beside John and handed him a mug.

"Doing what?"

"Staring at a door."

"I'm staring at the gap *under* the door." John took the mug. "There's light down there. I keep telling you."

"You keep telling all of us." Azac sipped his drink. "Mal's written a whole page about it. *IF John's light thing turns out to be a monster.* It's got sub-headings."

"It probably is a monster," said John. "That's sort of the point."

They sat in silence for a while, watching the gap. The guards at the door had stopped paying attention to them weeks ago.

"What's it look like tonight?" said Azac.

John let his eyes go soft. It was easier now than it had been. He'd been doing it every night for two months.

The gap under the door was a thin line of black. And in the black, if you knew how to look, there was a glow. So faint it was barely there. Pale, cold, colourless, like the light off snow under a cloudy moon. It came up from below, from far down the stair. And it moved.

Not like torchlight. Torchlight flickered. This didn't flicker. It *pulsed*. Slowly. Regularly. Brighter, then dimmer, then brighter again, like breathing.

"Same as always," said John. "Pale. Breathing." He frowned. "Brighter than last week, though. I think."

Azac didn't say anything for a moment.

"Declan would have opened it," he said eventually.

John laughed before he could stop himself. "He would *not*."

"He would. You know he would. He'd have said *I'm just going to take a look, I'll be two seconds, trust*, and then he'd have opened it, and we'd have spent the rest of the night pulling him back out by his ankles."

"Yeah," said John, after a moment. "Yeah. He would've."

They drank their drinks, and watched the gap, and didn't open the door.

***

Thea's letter had come at the beginning of the month.

It had come to every group; John knew that, because it said so at the top. *To everyone. I'm sorry this is long.* And it was long: four pages, closely written, in Thea's careful hand. But John had read every word of it twice by the light of the seal-guards' brazier, sitting on the cold step, and then a third time in his room, and then he'd gone to find Xav and made him read it too.

*You can't get over the edge by reading. You've all been reading. Sensing. Feeling. That's half of it. The other half is writing. You have to put something back into the world. One small line. In its language, not yours.*

*For me it was light, because I was looking at a fire. I don't know what it'll be for you. Look at whatever you look at most. Find the gap where a line should be. And write it.*

John looked at light most. He'd known that the moment he read it.

So he'd tried.

Every night, at the seal, after Azac went back up with the empty mugs, he'd sat alone on the step and looked at the light. Not the strange pale breathing light under the door. The ordinary light, from the brazier: warm and orange and flickering, throwing the guards' shadows huge against the wall. He'd looked at it the way he looked at everything, reading the shape of it, the way it moved and pooled and bent.

And he'd looked for the gap.

It had taken him nine nights to find one. A place, just above his own open palm, where the brazier-light thinned out into nothing. A tiny dark pocket in the air, no bigger than a coin, that the light flowed round but never into. As if it were waiting.

On the tenth night, he'd tried to write into it.

He didn't know how. Thea's letter didn't say how; it couldn't, because it was different for everyone. He just sat there with his hand open and stared at the little dark pocket above it and thought, as hard and as clearly as he'd ever thought anything:

*Here. Look.*

And for one heartbeat there was a mote of light in his palm.

Tiny. A speck. Whiter than the brazier, cleaner, like a single star. It sat there above his skin, and turned once, and was gone.

He'd sat on the step afterwards for an hour, shaking.

He hadn't been able to do it again since. Not once, in twenty nights of trying. But he'd done it once. He'd *written* something. And his window had jumped four percent overnight, and that had never happened before, not even after the Hollow.

He hadn't told anyone but Xav. He wasn't sure why. Maybe because he wasn't sure it had happened. Maybe because if he said it out loud and couldn't do it again, it would mean it hadn't.

***

The window numbers that week, in the tavern, were the best they'd ever been.

"Twenty-two," said Xav. "Petra says I stood still for forty minutes yesterday without moving. She says that's nearly halfway to good."

"Twenty-one," said RX, and grinned. "And the *Unseen* slot's gone from grey to... sort of silver. Like it's waking up." He opened his window and looked at it, as he did about fifty times a day. "Still locked. But it's *closer*."

"Twenty," said Azac. "I'm catching up. You lot had a five-week head start while I was in bed."

"Nineteen," said Malcolm. He looked at his own number with deep suspicion. "It's gone up every week since Thea's letter. I don't trust it."

"You don't trust anything," said RX.

"That's why I'm still alive."

"Twenty-four," said John.

They all looked at him.

"That's the highest," said Malcolm slowly.

"I know."

"You were at twenty last week. How'd you jump four?"

John hesitated. Then he looked at Xav, and Xav gave him a tiny nod, and John told them. The brazier. The dark pocket above his palm. *Here. Look.* The single mote of light, like a star, for one heartbeat.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"You wrote it," said RX. "Like Thea said. You actually wrote something."

"Once," said John. "Only once. I haven't been able to do it since."

"Once is the hardest one," said Xav quietly. "That's what Drew wrote to Tiger. *Once is the hardest one.*"

Malcolm was already writing in his notebook. John leaned over to see. *IF John's light works → John scouts deep ring before anyone goes down. NOT before Tier 1. NOT before Garrick says. NOT ALONE.*

"I wasn't going to go alone," said John.

"I know," said Malcolm. "I'm writing it down so you remember that you weren't."

***

He went back down to the seal that night, and it was brighter.

He saw it from the top of the stair, before he'd even reached the door: the gap at the bottom, glowing. Not faint anymore. Not barely there. A thin pale line along the bottom of the oak, cold and colourless, pulsing slowly, so bright that the two guards on the door had both stepped back from it and were standing with their halberds levelled, looking at each other.

"It started an hour ago," said one of them, a grizzled old guildsman called Fenn. His voice was very quiet. "Just... came up. Getting brighter. I sent a runner for Garrick."

John sat down on the step. He didn't decide to. His legs just folded.

And he looked.

He let his eyes go soft, the way he'd done every night for two months, and he looked at the light coming through the gap under the door, and he read it.

It wasn't just light. That was the first thing. It had *shape*. All light had a shape, if you knew how to read it, but this had more than that. It had *pattern*. It was coming up the stair from somewhere very far down, and it wasn't spreading out the way light spreads. It was moving in lines. Curves. Rings inside rings, and spokes running across them, and the whole thing turning, very slowly, like something being drawn.

He'd seen that shape before.

Not with his own eyes. In a letter. Kei's letter, from Thornwall, in the autumn, which Group 5 had sent round to every group: a hut on a hill above an empty village, with shoes lined up against the wall and a circle burned into the floor. *A ring as wide as I am tall, with lines running across it in a pattern that made my eyes hurt. Something that seemed to go* down, *the longer you looked at it.*

John sat on the cold step with the pale light pulsing through the gap under the door, and understood that somewhere far below him, in the deep rings of the Hollow, something was drawing that circle.

And it was getting brighter every night.

***

Garrick came down the stair at a run, with his staff in his hand and his hat gone, and Xav behind him, and Petra behind Xav with her sword already drawn.

They stopped at the top of the last flight and looked at the door. At the line of pale light along the bottom of it. At John, sitting on the step in front of it, staring.

"Lamp," said Garrick quietly. "What do you see?"

John told him.

The rings. The spokes. The slow turning, like something being drawn. Kei's letter. The hut above Coldwater with the shoes against the wall.

When he'd finished, nobody said anything. The light under the door pulsed. Brighter. Dimmer. Brighter.

Then, very slowly, it faded. Dimmer and dimmer, over a long minute, until the gap at the bottom of the door was only a thin black line again. The guards lowered their halberds. Fenn let out a long breath that shook.

"It's gone," he said.

"It's not gone," said John. He couldn't take his eyes off the gap. "It's resting. It does that. Every night, it comes up, and then it goes down. But every night it comes up a bit further." He swallowed. "Two months ago it was so faint I could barely see it. Tonight it was bright enough for *you* to see."

Petra came down the last steps, past Garrick, past Xav, and stood in front of the door with her sword in her hand, very still. She looked at the gap for a long time.

"Three sides," she said finally, very low. "That's what the pessimist keeps writing. East, west, underneath." She turned her head and looked at Garrick. "He's right."

***

They wrote to everyone that night. Malcolm wrote it, because his handwriting was clearest, and because, he said, somebody had to make sure it was depressing enough.

*Duskhollow. The deep rings. Something under the Hollow is drawing the same circle Group 5 found burned into the floor of the hut above Coldwater. John can see it in the light coming up under the seal. It gets brighter every night.*

*East, west, underneath. Three sides.*

*We don't know what it means. We're writing it down so you know too.*

Xav added a line at the bottom, in his own hand, before they sealed it.

*Thea: John wrote something. Once. Light, in his palm, like a star. Your letter did that. Thank you.*

John watched him write it, from the end of the table, and felt his face go hot.

Afterwards, when the others had gone up, he stayed in the empty tavern for a while with the last of the fire. He held out his hand over the embers, palm up, and looked at the warm orange light flowing over it, and looked for the gap.

It was there. A tiny dark pocket, no bigger than a coin, just above his skin.

*Here,* he thought. *Look.*

Nothing.

He tried again. And again. And on the ninth try, just before he gave up and went to bed, the speck of white light came back. Just for a heartbeat. Turned once in his palm, clean and bright as a star, and was gone.

Twice, now.

He sat there in the dark tavern with his hand still open, and thought about the pale light far below, drawing its circle in the deep. And about a friend who'd have opened the door, and said *trust*, and been dragged back out by his ankles.

And he thought: *whatever's down there is learning to write too.*

He didn't sleep much that night.
