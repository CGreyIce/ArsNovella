Deston was weak on his own.

He'd known it since the first week. Emrys had looked at his window on the river road and said *bond magic*, and then, carefully, the way Emrys said things he thought might hurt: *you'll be strong with people. On your own, not much.* And Deston had nodded, and not minded, because it had always been true. On Earth too. In the choir, at thirteen, he'd had a perfectly ordinary voice. On its own, nobody would have looked twice. But put it beside Gabe's, and Declan's, and something happened. Three ordinary voices, and a chord. You could hear it in the back row of a concert hall.

He'd spent a year and a half seeing threads. Grey for strangers. Gold for love. Thick or thin, taut or slack, running from every person to every person they knew, all over Kingsferry, like a loom. He'd felt them tug. He'd followed one north every night for a year, in the dark, from a roof.

He'd never once been able to *do* anything with them.

Emrys said he would. *Link their mana. Let them hear each other. Let them cast together, like voices.* At the first tier, Emrys said. When it came.

He'd been at ninety-nine percent for a month.

***

The cliffs came up out of the morning like a door closing.

Malcolm had read them the plan at dawn, all eleven pages, sitting on his coil of rope with the notebook on his knees and his small tilted voice going steadily through every IF and every THEN while the gorge got closer. It was a good plan. Deston had listened to all of it. It had positions for everyone. It had backups for the backups. At the bottom of every page it said *nobody goes anywhere alone*.

He'd known, listening to it, that none of it would survive the first minute. Malcolm knew it too. It was why he'd written eleven pages.

The Narrows at Gallow Rock. Haskett had described it, and Haskett had been right, and it was worse. The river went into the hills between two walls of grey rock, wet and black and going straight up, sixty feet, then a hundred, so high that the sky above was a ragged strip of white. The water ran fast and dark and loud between them, half the width of the river below, piling up white against the rocks at the edges. And the sound of it filled the gorge like the inside of a drum.

No wind. Calvin had felt it go the moment the bow went in. Deston saw his face on the crosstree, white. The sail hung slack. The tailwind that had carried them north for eleven days just stopped, at the mouth of the gorge, as if it had hit a wall.

"Ropes," roared Haskett. "*Ropes, boys!*"

***

The towpath was a ledge.

Cut into the west cliff, a man's height above the water, a long time ago, by people who'd had horses to pull barges and the patience to cut a road into rock. It was wet. It was narrow; two men could walk it side by side if neither of them was Gabe. It ran away ahead along the cliff into the dark of the gorge, round a bend, out of sight.

They put the ropes on it. Haskett's boys first, and then Gabe, because Gabe was the strongest, and Azac, because Azac was the next strongest, and Sabine, because Malcolm's plan said a guard on the towpath, and Sabine read the plan and said *fine* and went. They leaned into the ropes on the wet ledge, four of them and the two boys, and the *Patient Widow* moved. A foot. Another. Into the gorge. Against the current. With Haskett at the tiller and Dennis and Calvin on the poles and everyone else on the deck.

Rachael in the middle. Between Xav and Petra, now, with Gabe and Azac on the ropes. Maud's needle case in her coat. Gabe's knife at her belt.

Deston at the bow.

He was looking up.

***

He'd been looking up since the gorge began. At the cliff tops. At the ragged strip of sky between them, a hundred feet up. At the edges, where the rock met the trees that grew right to the lip on both sides.

He couldn't see much. It was dark down here. The sky was bright up there, and the cliff tops were black against it.

But he could see threads.

That was the thing about threads. You didn't need light. They were just *there*, the way a sound is there in the dark. Every person, every thread. Grey ones for strangers, gold ones for people you loved. He'd learned to see them on faces in crowds, on the quays of Kingsferry, at the night market. He'd learned to see the people who had *none*.

There were people on the cliff tops.

He could feel them. Not see them. Up there, in the trees at the lip, on both sides. A lot of them. Standing. Waiting. And none of them, not one, had a single thread.

"John," said Deston. Very quietly.

John, at the stern, didn't look round. He was already looking up too.

"I know," said John. His voice was flat. "Their light's wrong. All of them. Both sides." He swallowed. "There's more than the village. A lot more."

***

They came at the bend.

Deston would remember it afterwards in pieces, the way you remember a dream. The barge coming round the bend in the gorge, a foot at a time, on the ropes, with the river roaring and the walls going up on both sides. And the towpath ahead, on the west cliff, running on into the dark.

And people on it.

Standing on the ledge. Twenty paces ahead of Gabe and the boys and the ropes. In a row, on the wet rock, between the cliff and the drop to the river. Farm clothes. A woman with a basket on her hip. A boy with a cap pulled down over his ears.

The boy smiled.

And then the cliff tops moved.

***

They came down the cliffs.

Not climbing. *Coming down*. Both cliffs at once, from the trees at the top, a hundred feet of wet black rock, and they came down it head first. The way a spider comes down a wall. Their hands and feet finding holds that weren't there, their bodies bending in ways bodies didn't bend, quick and quiet and wrong, dozens of them, out of the strip of sky and down into the dark of the gorge, toward the river, toward the barge.

Toward the middle of the deck.

Somebody screamed. One of Haskett's boys, on the ledge. The rope went slack in his hands.

And on the cliff top on the east side, above all of it, standing at the very lip in the light, a man in a good coat with his hat in his hands, turning it by the brim.

Deston knew him. He'd seen him on the customs-house steps in Kingsferry, at the night market, turning his hat. On the far quay, the night the hunter came, walking into the river. Smiling at Dennis.

He had no threads at all.

He was looking at Rachael.

***

Everything happened at once. It always did.

Sabine on the ledge, drawing, her Reaching edge going a hand's width past the steel, and the first of the cliff-crawlers coming down the rock beside her and her blade going through it, and it didn't bleed. It just fell, into the river, without a sound.

Gabe on the ledge, not drawing, holding the rope, because if he let go of the rope the current would take the barge and smash it on the rocks, and he knew it, and he stood there on the wet ledge with the rope in his hands and his feet planted and something coming down the cliff toward him head first, and didn't let go.

Azac letting go of the rope, because Malcolm's plan said *IF they come, THEN Azac to Rachael*, and jumping. Off the ledge. Onto the deck of the barge, six feet, with his shield, landing badly, rolling, coming up in front of Rachael with the shield up.

Petra at the bow, drawing. Xav beside Rachael, not drawing. Waiting. The way he always waited.

RX gone.

Dennis on the pole, white-faced, looking up at the man with the hat.

Calvin on the crosstree, with no wind, screaming at the air.

And the barge, with half its tow rope gone slack, swinging. Slowly. Out from the cliff, into the current, toward the rocks on the east side, where the water piled up white.

***

Deston couldn't do anything.

He stood at the bow with his hands empty and the threads all round him, all over the deck, all over the gorge. Gold ones. More gold ones than he'd ever seen in one place. Group 4 and Group 6 and Emrys and Sabine and Petra, on one deck, on one ledge, every one of them tied to every other one with lines of gold, thick as rope, the way you'd tie a boat to a jetty in a storm.

He could see all of them. He could see Xav's to Gabe, and John's to Malcolm, and Rachael's to all of them, and Calvin's on the mast to Deston himself. He could see Gabe's to him, the thickest he'd ever had, since he was fifteen, gold and taut and singing with strain as Gabe held the rope on the ledge.

He could see them all, and he couldn't do anything with any of them.

He was weak on his own. He'd always been weak on his own.

And something came over the rail at the bow, head first, out of the dark, and reached for him with hands that were mostly fingers, and Petra cut it in half without looking, and it fell back into the river, and Deston stood there and did nothing.

***

He thought about the choir.

He didn't decide to. It just came up, the way things came up when the world was coming apart. A rehearsal room in Singapore, at fifteen, with bad carpet and a piano out of tune. Three boys in the back row. Gabe on the low part, Deston in the middle, Declan on top, too loud, always too loud, getting told off. *Seasons of Love.* The bit where the three parts came in on top of each other, one at a time, and for a moment, just a moment, three ordinary voices made something none of them could make on their own.

He'd heard it every night since, on a roof, with a gap where Declan's part should be.

He thought: *it isn't the voices.*

He'd always thought it was. Three voices, three threads, and he was the weak one in the middle. But it wasn't the voices. It had never been the voices. It was the *listening*. Each of them hearing the other two. Gabe hearing where Declan was going and coming in under him. Declan hearing Gabe and going higher. Deston in the middle, hearing both, holding it together. Three people listening so hard to each other that they stopped being three.

He'd never been weak on his own because he'd never been on his own. He'd been the one in the middle. The one who listened.

He looked at the gold threads all over the deck, all over the ledge, all over the gorge. Twelve people. Thirteen. Every one of them fighting. Every one of them on their own, in the dark, in pieces, not hearing each other at all.

He found the edge. It was right there, in the middle of all the threads, where it had been all along.

He didn't write *link*. He didn't write *together*. He wrote the thing Emrys had said to them, on the river road, in the first week, teaching chants as singing. The thing their choir master had said, since they were thirteen, every single rehearsal, until they were sick of it.

*Listen.*

***

The threads lit.

All of them. At once. Every gold thread on the deck and the ledge and the gorge, from every person to every person, went bright, the way a wire goes bright when the current goes into it. And Deston felt them go. All of them. Through him. Like a chord.

And then everybody could hear each other.

***

He didn't know how else to say it, afterwards. He tried, in the channel, that night, and couldn't. Emrys tried, later, for him, and got closer. *Like a choir*, Emrys said. *When the choir's good. When every voice can hear every other voice, and nobody has to look at the conductor.*

Xav could hear John.

Not his voice. His *sight*. John at the stern, who saw light change before things came round corners, and Xav beside Rachael felt it come down the thread between them like water down a pipe: *left, high, the east cliff, three of them, now*. And Xav, who'd been waiting, didn't wait anymore. He moved. Once. And the first of the three came off the rail in two pieces.

RX could hear John too. Wherever RX was, which was nowhere, which was unseen, which was a gap in the dark on the towpath ledge, John's light came down the thread to him, *there, the boy with the cap, behind the woman*, and RX was there before the boy knew there was anything to be behind.

Malcolm could hear Azac. Malcolm, on the deck, with his hand out, and the backup he'd been holding all day in case the first one failed, *Hold*, the line he'd written on Azac's shield in the Hollow, and he felt Azac's shield through the thread, felt exactly where it was and how hard it was being hit, and *Hold* went down the thread into it, and the shield took a blow from something that came off the cliff like a falling sack of stones, and didn't break.

Dennis could hear the hat man.

Not hear. *Feel*. The man on the cliff top, with no threads, didn't have a thread to feel. But everybody else on the deck had a thread to Rachael, gold and thick, and the hat man's eyes were on Rachael, and Dennis felt *that*: where the danger was looking. And he did what he'd done on the night of the hunter. He moved her. Not forty paces, this time. A hand's width, a foot, a pace to the left, a pace to the right, over and over, so fast that every one of the turned coming down the cliffs for the middle of the deck reached for a woman who wasn't quite there.

The hat man on the cliff top stopped turning his hat.

***

And Calvin could hear the wind.

Not the wind in the gorge. There wasn't any. The wind *above* it. Going over the top of the cliffs, a hundred feet up, the way Haskett said, never coming down. Calvin on the crosstree couldn't feel it. He was too far down.

But Petra could.

Petra, at the bow, Reaching, who'd spent eight years since a ditch on the Redmarch learning to feel everything round her, the way the Black Blade had. She could feel the air moving at the top of the cliffs. And it came down the thread between her and Calvin, faint, a thread so new it was barely gold. And Calvin, on the crosstree, felt it.

"*CARRY US*," screamed Calvin. At the sky.

And the wind came down.

Down off the top of the cliffs, into the gorge, the whole strip of sky emptying into it, a gust, a *fall* of air, straight down the gorge from the north, the way it had never come down in all the years since people cut a road into that rock. It hit the sail and filled it and the *Patient Widow* stopped swinging toward the rocks and *leaned* and went north again, against the current, against everything, with her tow rope slack and nobody pulling it.

***

Gabe, on the ledge, felt the rope go slack.

Deston felt him feel it. Through the thread. Through the thickest thread he had, gold and singing. He felt Gabe let go of the rope at last, because he didn't need to hold it anymore, and turn round on the wet ledge to face the thing coming down the cliff at him head first. And draw his blade.

And Deston felt everybody else feel it too.

That was the thing. That was the thing he'd remember for the rest of his life. Not just him. *Everybody*. Every person on that deck and that ledge, through every gold thread, at the same moment, felt Gabe let go of the rope and turn round and draw, and felt what it was like to be him. Steady. Planted. Not frightened. A wall going up.

And everybody, at the same moment, stood a little straighter.

***

The hat man went for Rachael himself.

Deston felt it coming. Down every thread at once, from everyone, because everyone had a thread to her and everyone felt the danger look at her. The man in the good coat stepped off the cliff top on the east side. Stepped off. Into a hundred feet of air. And came down it the way the others had, head first, but faster, much faster, a dark shape against the black rock, straight down toward the deck of the barge and the woman in the middle of it.

Dennis moved her. A pace left.

The hat man came down a pace left.

He'd learned. Dennis had said so, in the autumn, standing at the hole in the infirmary wall, watching him walk into the river. *It learned something.* It had learned where she really was.

He hit the deck between Azac's shield and Xav's blade, in the space Dennis had moved her into, and reached for her with hands that were too long, and Rachael—

Rachael didn't wait.

Deston felt it. Through her thread. Not fear. Something else. Something that had been building in her for a year and a half, since a stranger on a waterfront offered her a place *in my master's household in the hills, west*. She had Gabe's knife in her hand. She'd had it out since the cliffs moved. And she didn't step back. She stepped *in*.

The knife went into the hat man's hand. The same hand. The same place as the night market, where she'd cut him the first time and the black stuff had come out and the skin had moved like water.

It moved like water now. And then it stopped moving. And went grey.

The hat man looked down at his hand.

Then at Rachael.

He didn't smile.

***

Then Xav moved. Once. And Petra, from the bow, Reaching, a hand's width past the steel. And Azac's shield came round, with Malcolm's *Hold* in it, and hit the hat man in the chest like a door slamming.

He went over the rail.

Into the river. Into the white water piling up against the rocks. Without a splash. The way they always went.

The others went with him. All of them, on both cliffs, at once, the way a flock turns. Up the rock, head first, the way they'd come down. Back into the trees at the lip. Gone. The boy with the cap last of all, stopping at the top of the east cliff, against the strip of sky, to look down at the barge.

At Rachael.

Then at Deston.

Then he was gone too.

***

The threads were still lit.

Deston was on his knees at the bow. He didn't remember kneeling. The chord was still going through him, every gold thread on the barge and the ledge, twelve people listening to each other, so loud he couldn't hear the river. He didn't know how to stop it. He didn't want to.

And through it, under it, the way a low note runs under a chord, he felt the other one.

The faded thread. From his own chest. North. Out of the gorge, up the river, past the hills. Not faded anymore. Not grey. *Warm*. Warmer than it had ever been. Gold, almost. Taut. Singing.

And he felt everybody else feel it.

All of them. Through every lit thread on the deck. At once. Gabe on the ledge with his blade dripping. Xav beside Rachael. John at the stern. Malcolm with his hand still out. Azac. RX, wherever he was. Dennis. Calvin on the crosstree, with the wind still pouring down into the gorge round him. Rachael. Emrys. Sabine. Petra.

Every one of them, for one long heartbeat, felt what Deston had been feeling every night for a year, on a roof, with his hand out over a parapet.

A thread. Running north. To somebody they all knew.

Warm.

***

The chord let go.

Gently. The way a note fades at the end of a song, when the choir's good and nobody has to look at the conductor. The threads dimmed. The gold went back to gold. The river came back, loud, roaring between the walls of the gorge. The wind kept pouring down off the cliff tops into the sail. The *Patient Widow* kept going north.

Deston knelt at the bow and opened his window, because his hands were doing it before he told them to.

[ Deston · Tier 1: Spark · Skills: Chorus ]

The nineteenth.

He looked at it for a long time.

*Chorus.* Not *bond*. Not *thread*. *Chorus*. Lots of voices. Listening.

He started laughing. It came out wet.

***

Nobody said anything for a long time.

They came out of the top of the gorge an hour later, into grey daylight, with the cliffs dropping away behind them and the river widening again between low hills. Gabe and Sabine and the boys came down off the towpath ledge where it ended at a little stone landing, and Haskett brought the barge in, and they climbed aboard. Nobody had died. Nobody had been taken. Azac's shield had a dent in it the size of a man's head. One of Haskett's boys had a broken wrist. Sabine had a cut on her cheek that she wouldn't let anyone look at.

Gabe came down the deck to the bow, wet to the knees, with his blade still in his hand, and stopped in front of Deston.

He didn't say anything. He just looked at him.

"You felt it," said Deston.

"I felt it," said Gabe.

"All of you."

"All of us." Gabe looked back down the deck. At the others. At Xav, who was standing very still beside Rachael with his blade lowered, looking north. At John and Malcolm, side by side at the rail, not talking. At Calvin, sliding down the mast. At Petra and Sabine at the stern, looking at each other.

At Rachael, sitting on the deck with Gabe's knife in her hand and grey on the blade, looking at nothing.

"Everybody felt it," said Gabe. "The thread. North." His voice went. "Deston. It's *warm*."

"I know," said Deston. "I've been telling you for four months."

"I know," said Gabe. "I just didn't know it was *that* warm."

He sat down on the deck beside Deston, very heavily, the way he'd sat down on the roof above the chandler's the night Deston finally told him, and put his blade across his knees, and his head in his hands.

Deston put his hand on his back.

And at the stern, very quietly, so quietly that it might have been the wind, somebody started to hum. Emrys, with his cracked voice. A song that didn't exist in this world. That he'd heard once, a year and a half ago, on a roof, with a gap in it.

He left the gap.

Nobody filled it. Not yet.

But every one of them, Deston thought, looking down the deck at their faces, knew exactly whose it was.
