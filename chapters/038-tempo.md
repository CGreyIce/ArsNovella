Everybody had a beat.

Newish had noticed it in the autumn, without knowing what it was. In the practice yard behind the Lists, sparring with wooden swords, she'd started to feel a kind of rhythm under people's movements. Not their footwork; that was something you could see. Something underneath it. A pulse. A tick. The moment, just before someone moved, when everything in them gathered up and *leaned*.

By the winter she could hear it every time. By the spring she could hear it in the street.

The baker's boy, running deliveries, had a quick light skipping beat, like a sparrow. The old armourer on the corner had a slow, heavy one that only came twice a minute, and that was why he never dropped anything. Lucan's beat was enormous, a big swinging rhythm like a dance tune, which explained absolutely everything about Lucan. Ysolde's was so steady and so quiet that Newish had to stand right next to her and hold her breath to hear it at all.

Hako's was cheerful and a little off-time, like someone humming while they walked. Chris's was fast and uneven and kept changing, like a song being written while you listened to it. Ina's was a deep, slow thrum, warm, like a cello. Winter's was very, very quiet. Most days you could barely hear it. But every so often, in the yard at dawn with Ysolde on the fence, Newish would catch it: cold and clear and steady, like water dripping in a cave.

"Tempo," Lucan had told her, back in the winter, peering at her over his ridiculous cloak. "That's what you've got. Not speed. *Timing.* You don't make yourself faster. You make everyone else's moment arrive exactly when you want it to." He'd beamed at her. "It's a support path. A very rare one. You'll be the most annoying person on any battlefield you ever stand on."

"I already am," Newish had said.

"Yes," Lucan had agreed happily. "That's how I knew."

***

The Lists reopened in the spring with a parade.

Valcrest did nothing by halves, and the first bout of the season was apparently a holiday. Every shop in the Street of Armourers was shut, and every balcony on the square was hung with banners, and the children in their paper horns had multiplied until there seemed to be more of them than adults. The paper horns had a new feature this spring: little white masks stitched to the front, in honour of Ina.

"I've become merchandise," said Ina, staring at a stall selling them six for a copper.

"You've become a *brand*," said Newish. "That's different. That's better."

"Is it?"

"It is if you're getting a cut." She raised her voice at the stallholder. "Is he getting a cut?"

The stallholder looked at Ina, and at Newish, and at Ysolde standing behind them with her arms folded, and very quickly offered Ina a cut.

The tournament of the summoned was still postponed. The decree was still nailed to the gate of the Lists, faded now, with somebody's paper horns pinned to it like a protest. But the ordinary bouts went on, the way they always had, every week through spring and summer. Lucan had been given permission by the Crown to enter the five of them in the junior lists as "exhibitors," which as far as Newish could tell meant that they fought for free and Lucan kept the ticket money.

"It's for the *kingdom,*" Lucan said, when Chris pointed this out.

"The kingdom's getting a cut?" said Newish.

Lucan pretended not to hear.

***

They'd fought six bouts since the thaw, and won five. The one they'd lost had been against a team of veteran caravan guards Ysolde apparently knew personally, and who had been, Newish admitted afterwards, very good, and also very annoying, which she respected.

They still couldn't use magic. None of them could. But they were *close*, closer than they'd ever been, and since Thea's letter in the winter everything had started to move faster. Winter's frost came most mornings now, on purpose, in the yard. Ina's heat could stay lit under the mask for whole minutes. Hako could hold a stance with his spear for an hour without his arms shaking. Chris's logs had become a thing of genuine terror, three notebooks full, cross-referenced, with diagrams.

He kept the numbers too, every Sunday night, in a column at the back of the first notebook. Ina 29. Winter 28. Hako 26. Chris 25. And at the bottom, in Chris's neat square hand:

[ Newish · Tier 0: Initiate · Progress to Spark: 27% ]

Twenty-seven percent, after nine months. Lucan said that was very fast. Newish privately thought it was the slowest thing that had ever happened to her.

And Newish could hear everyone's beat.

In the Lists, it was like cheating. She'd stand at the edge of a scramble with her practice sword and *listen*, and she'd hear the moment each of the other team was about to commit: a big swing, a lunge, a turn. And she'd call it. "*Now,*" or "*left*," or "*Hako, he's going*". And her team would already be moving, because they'd learned to trust her the way they'd learned to trust each other in the old days on Apex, in a dozen different trios, at three in the morning.

"It's not cheating," Chris had said, when she'd worried about it out loud. "It's comms. You're just very, very good at comms."

***

The trouble came on the first day of the spring season, after the parade, in the third bout.

They'd been scheduled to fight a team from the eastern garrison: five young soldiers who'd come to Valcrest on leave. Newish had watched them practise. They were all right. Nothing special.

And then, an hour before the bout, Lucan came into the waiting room under the stands with a face like thunder.

"Change of opponent," he said.

"Who?" said Chris.

Lucan didn't answer. He didn't need to. Through the archway, on the far side of the sand, five young men were walking out into the sunlight in beautiful matching armour, fair-haired, laughing, with a little crowd of friends cheering them from the noble boxes. The one in front was tall and handsome and very familiar.

"Aldric Vane," said Ina quietly.

"He paid the eastern garrison boys to withdraw," said Lucan. "Paid them *very* well. And then he put his own team in their slot. Friends of his. Sons of the Council." His mouth twisted. "It's within the rules. Just barely. I checked twice."

"He's come for a rematch," said Hako.

"He's come," said Ysolde, from the doorway, in her flattest voice, "for something."

***

It was supposed to be wooden swords. It was always wooden swords, in the junior lists. That was the whole point of the junior lists.

Aldric's team carried wooden swords onto the sand. Newish watched them do it. Beautiful polished practice blades, oak, with leather grips. Exactly regulation.

And then the flag went up, and the bout began, and Newish heard it.

Not the beat. Something under the beat. A *wrongness*. Aldric's team moved forward in a line, and four of them had ordinary beats, fast and eager, a bit sloppy, young men who'd been told this would be easy. But the fifth, the big one on the left, beside Aldric, had a beat she'd never heard before. Slow and heavy and very careful. The beat of someone holding something they weren't supposed to have.

She looked at his sword.

It was wood. Oak. Polished. Regulation.

Except that it caught the light wrong, along the edge, like there was something under the varnish.

"*Chris,*" she said, very low. "Left man. His sword's not right."

Chris didn't ask how she knew. He never did. He just said, very quietly, to everyone: "Watch the left one. Don't let him close."

They didn't let him close. For a while.

The bout went the way their bouts always went now: split, let them commit, punish the commit. Aldric's friends were brave and badly trained and had never fought as a team in their lives, and Hako's spear and Winter's quick precise sword took two of them out of the bout inside a minute. The crowd roared. The noble boxes went quiet.

And then the big one on the left went for Ina.

Newish heard it before he moved. That slow, careful, heavy beat suddenly *tightening*, gathering, everything in him leaning toward Ina's back as Ina turned to cover Winter. And under the beat, under the gathering, something she'd never heard in a bout before. Not *I'm going to hit him.*

*I'm going to hurt him.*

"*INA, DOWN!*"

She'd never shouted so loud in her life. Ina dropped. He didn't think, he didn't look, he just dropped flat on the sand the way you drop when someone you trust screams at you. The big man's sword went through the place where his neck had been.

And it *rang*.

Wood doesn't ring. The whole stand heard it: a clean high metallic note, like a bell. The sword had hit the edge of Hako's spear shaft on the follow-through, and the polished oak had split along the edge, and under it, gleaming in the sun, there was steel.

A steel blade, cased in a thin skin of varnished wood.

For one long second the Lists went completely, absolutely silent.

***

Then a lot of things happened at once.

Ysolde came over the barrier. Newish had never seen her move that fast. She didn't draw her sword. She didn't need to. She simply walked across the sand to the big man with the cased blade, took his wrist in one hand, and *turned* it, and he dropped the sword and went to his knees with a sound like a kicked dog.

Lucan was on the master's platform, shouting. Newish couldn't hear what. Something about *disqualified* and *the Crown* and *every one of your fathers*. The crowd had found its voice again, and it wasn't cheering. It was a low, rising, angry roar, the sound of eight thousand people who'd come to see a game and had just seen someone try to cut a man's throat in the middle of it.

And Ina was getting up off the sand.

Slowly. Carefully. He stood up and brushed himself off and looked at the split sword lying in the sand, at the steel under the wood. Then he looked across at Aldric Vane, standing frozen in the middle of his remaining friends, white to the lips.

Ina didn't say anything. He didn't need to.

He just reached up and took off his mask. And held it in his hand. And looked at Aldric with his own face: his ordinary, slightly too-pale, very human face, with a single purple horn and a cut on his cheek from the sand.

Then he put the mask back on, and turned his back on Aldric, and walked back to his team.

The crowd went *insane*.

***

They won the bout. Of course they won. Aldric's remaining friends had no fight left in them; one of them actually dropped his sword and walked off the sand before the flag came down. Hako took the banner. The noble boxes emptied very fast.

Aldric was the last of his team to leave. He walked off the sand alone, through the archway on the far side, with eight thousand people booing him, and at the archway he stopped and looked back.

Not at Ina. At Newish.

She felt it like cold water. He knew. Somehow, he knew she'd called it. That she'd heard what his friend was about to do before he did it.

She looked back at him. And then, because she was Newish, and because she was furious, and because her hands were shaking so hard she had to do *something* with them, she raised one hand above her head, in front of the whole of the Lists, and waved at him.

Cheerfully. Like an old friend.

Aldric's face went from white to red. He turned and went through the archway and was gone.

"That," said Chris, faintly, beside her, "was the most dangerous thing I've ever seen you do."

"He tried to cut Ina's throat."

"I know."

"In a *junior bout*."

"I know."

"With a sword *in a sword*." Newish's voice cracked. "Who *does* that? Who even *thinks* of that?"

Chris didn't answer. He just put his arm round her shoulders, very gently, and held on, while the crowd roared and roared.

***

The Crown's investigator came that evening: a tired grey woman from the magistrate's office who took statements from everyone, and examined the cased sword, and wrote everything down in a very small, very neat hand.

"The man who carried it is a household guard of the Vane estate," she said, closing her book. "He says he did it on his own initiative. He says he'd heard the summoned were demons and wanted to prove it."

"And Aldric?" said Lucan.

"Says he knew nothing. Says he's horrified." The investigator's face didn't move. "His father has already written to the magistrate expressing the same."

"So nothing happens," said Winter flatly. It was the first thing she'd said since the bout.

The investigator looked at her for a long moment.

"The guard will hang," she said. "Lord Vane's son will be very publicly embarrassed. And everyone in this city who matters now knows exactly what the Vane family is willing to do." She stood up. "It's not nothing. It's just not enough."

She left. Nobody said anything for a while.

***

That night, at the table in the Broken Lance, Newish couldn't stop shaking.

She didn't understand it. She'd been fine in the bout. She'd been fine afterwards, waving at Aldric. She'd been fine all through the investigator. And then they'd sat down to supper and the barmaid had dropped a tray of cups behind the bar, and the crash had gone through Newish like a blade, and suddenly she was shaking so hard she couldn't hold her spoon.

Ina was sitting next to her. He didn't say anything. He just put his hand flat on the table near hers, not touching, in case she wanted it.

She wanted it. She took it.

"You called it," he said quietly. "Before he moved. Before *anyone* could have seen."

"I heard it."

"I know. I'm alive because you heard it." He squeezed her hand. "Thank you."

Newish looked down at the table. At their two hands. She thought about the beat she'd heard under the big man's beat. *I'm going to hurt him.* She thought about how long it had taken her to understand what she was hearing. A second, maybe. Less. If it had taken her one heartbeat longer—

"I was in the call," she said suddenly.

Ina looked at her.

"With Ice. The last night. Before he died." She didn't know why she was saying it. It just came out. "Me and Yan and Niko and Gabe. We were playing CS. He missed a shot and blamed the server. And then he said he was going to bed, and I told him he was a coward, and he said *love you guys, except Newish.*" She laughed, shakily. "And I said *rude*, and he said *joking, mostly, trust*, and then he left the call. That was the last thing he ever said to me." She was crying now. She didn't try to stop. "Three years, and I still think about it. That I said *rude*. That that was the last thing I ever said to him."

Nobody at the table said anything. Chris had stopped writing. Hako had put down his spoon. Winter was looking at Newish with her strange two-coloured eyes, very steady.

"And today," said Newish, "I heard someone about to die, and I said *down*, and they didn't." She wiped her face. "I don't know. I don't know why that matters. It just does."

"It matters," said Winter quietly. "It matters a lot."

***

Later, when the others had gone up, Chris stayed at the table with his notebooks. Newish stayed with him, because she couldn't sleep, and because it was easier not to be alone.

"There's something else," said Chris, after a while. He had the *ALL GROUPS* notebook open, the one with thirty-five columns of numbers. "I didn't want to say it in front of everyone. Not tonight."

"What?"

He turned the notebook round. The columns for Group 5 had stopped. Not the numbers; the *dates*. The last entry in every one of Group 5's columns was from five weeks ago.

"Tiger promised to write," said Chris. "Two letters ago. He said he had something to tell us, about a soldier he'd been racing. He said he'd write as soon as he knew more." He tapped the empty space under the last date. "Nothing since. Not from Tiger, not from Hyvred, not from Kei, not from Drew. Five weeks."

Newish looked at the empty columns.

"Maybe they're busy," she said. "Maybe something's happened at Thornwall."

"Maybe," said Chris. "But Group 2's have stopped too. And Group 3's. Yan writes every fortnight, regular as a clock, and the last one I got from him was six weeks ago." He closed the notebook. "Either everyone's stopped writing at once. Or something's stopping the letters."

They sat there for a long time in the empty common room, with the fire burning down.

And Newish thought about Aldric Vane at the archway, looking back at her. *He knew.* He'd known exactly where to look. Which of them to watch. Which of them to send a cased sword at.

He'd known them very well, for a man who'd only met them twice.

"Chris," she said slowly. "How did he know which one was Ina's weak side?"

Chris looked up.

"He's been watching us," said Newish. "Reading about us. Somebody's been telling him things." Her voice was very quiet. "Somebody's been reading our letters."

The fire popped. Neither of them said anything else. But neither of them went to bed for a long time.
