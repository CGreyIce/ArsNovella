At 1:47 in the morning, in his room in Malacca, Ice missed the easiest shot of his life.

The enemy had walked straight into his crosshair. He'd been holding that angle for forty seconds, the AWP steady, the ceiling fan clicking on every third turn, and when the man finally stepped out, Ice fired a clean, confident, beautiful shot into the wall beside his head.

The call went silent for exactly one second.

Then Newish started laughing so hard her microphone peaked.

"No," she wheezed. "No, no, no. Say something. Explain yourself."

"The server," Ice said gravely, "is in a different country from me."

"So are we!"

"That's why I don't trust any of you."

Gabe made the noise he always made, a short breath out through his nose that meant he'd known Ice for eleven years and was tired of him in the specific, permanent way only family is allowed to be.

"Bro," Gabe said. "You had him."

"I let him live. It's called mercy. Look it up."

"You can't let people live in Counter-Strike," Newish said. "That's not a thing. I've played this game for, like, three days, and even I know that's not a thing."

"Two days," said Yan.

Yan's voice always arrived a beat after everyone else's, deep and unhurried, as if he'd already thought through the whole round and found it a little funny. It was the voice that called their Apex rotations, the voice that had dragged Ice through the final circle of a tournament they had no business winning. In Apex, Yan carried. In CS, it was supposed to be the other way round.

Tonight it wasn't going either way.

"Mashi," Ice said. "Back me up."

"No," said Mashi, pleasantly.

Ice was the only one who called him that. Everyone else said Niko.

"Wow. Betrayal. In my own home."

"You're not in your home, you're in Malacca," Gabe said.

"Malacca *is* my home now. I have a fan. I have a kettle. I have a view of a wall." He leaned back, and the chair creaked as if it had opinions. "I'm thriving."

They lost the round. Then the match, thirteen to nine, which Newish took personally on everyone's behalf. She decided the loss belonged to Ice, and specifically to his AWP shot, which would be "brought up at his wedding." Ice said that was fine, because he'd be bringing up her first-ever grenade, the one that had bounced off a doorframe and killed Gabe.

"That was a team-building exercise," Newish said.

"I'm still recovering," said Gabe.

By the time the scoreboard faded it was past two. Ice's eyes had the sandy, overstretched feeling they always got when he'd been staring at a screen since dinner. He stretched until his shoulder popped.

"Okay," he said. "I'm going to sleep."

The reaction was immediate and unanimous.

"Coward."

"One more."

"You said that an hour ago."

"I'll be on early tomorrow," Ice said. "First thing. Before any of you. I'll have warmed up, I'll have eaten breakfast, I'll have the server picked out. Trust."

There was a pause while all four of them weighed the complete historical record of Ice saying *trust.*

"You have never," Yan said slowly, "been early in your life."

"And tomorrow I make history."

Newish was laughing again. Mashi said, "Goodnight, Ice," in the calm, put-together way he said everything. Gabe said, "Sleep, Declan. Seriously." He was the only one in the call who ever used Ice's real name, and he meant it the way he'd meant it since they were kids in the back row of a school choir, trying not to laugh during the quiet part of a song.

"Goodnight," Ice said. "Love you guys. Except Newish."

"*Rude.*"

"Joking. Mostly. Trust."

He left the call before she could answer, because the last word was the best part of any joke, and he liked to keep it.

The room was very quiet without them. The fan turned. Down the street a motorbike went past and faded. Ice plugged his phone in, set no alarm, because he was absolutely not going to be on early tomorrow, and lay down with one arm under the pillow the way he always did.

He thought, vaguely, that he should text Gabe about the weekend. He thought the AWP shot had genuinely been the server. He thought that if Yan ever stopped saying *two days* in that voice, the world would feel a little less right.

He was asleep before the fan came back around.

He did not wake up in Malacca.
