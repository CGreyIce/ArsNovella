Malcolm's notebook had a page at the back that he'd never written on.

He'd been keeping the book since the month Azac nearly died: a cheap thing from the stationer's on the market square, bound in grey cloth, its pages filling up with his small square handwriting, all in English, all in the same shape. *IF this, THEN that.* IF Azac steps in front when he shouldn't. IF John's light goes out underground. IF Xav and Garrick disagree in the middle of a fight. IF the guild closes the Hollow for good. IF the money runs out. IF one of us gets sick, IF one of us gets hurt, IF one of us doesn't come back. Dozens of them. Hundreds, by the spring. Every bad thing he could think of, and under each one, what they'd do.

The others teased him about it. Gently, mostly. Azac called it *the book of doom*. RX had once flicked through it and said, "Bro, you've planned for us getting eaten by a horse," and Malcolm had said, "It was a very specific horse," and RX had laughed so hard he'd had to sit down.

But the last page he'd left blank. He'd written a title at the top in the first week, and then never anything under it, all autumn and winter and spring. Every so often he'd turn to it, and look at the title, and turn back.

*WORST CASE.*

He didn't know what went there yet. He only knew that when he did know, it would be something he'd been building toward the whole time, one IF at a time, without wanting to look straight at it.

***

The guild announced the expedition on a wet morning in the last month of spring, and Group 6 went to the guildhall to ask to go, and were told no.

They'd known they would be. Xav had said so on the walk over. But they asked anyway, the five of them standing in a row in front of the guild master's desk with Garrick behind them and Petra by the door, and the guild master, a heavy grey woman called Harrow who'd run the Duskhollow guild for twenty years, listened to Xav make the case very calmly and very well, and said no.

"You're Initiates," she said. "All five of you. I know you're quick. I know Garrick thinks you're the best party he's ever trained. I know what the boy can do with light." She nodded at John. "And I know what's coming up that stair. Don't you dare make me write to the Advisor that I sent five of his summoned down the deep stair before they could cast a spark."

"We've been watching it every night for three months," said Xav. "John knows the light better than anyone in this town."

"I know," said Harrow. "That's why he'll be at the door. Watching." She looked at John. "If anything comes up that stair that shouldn't, I want the first person to see it to be the one who can tell what it is."

Xav didn't argue. That was the thing about Xav: he knew when a fight was lost, and he never wasted a move on it. He just nodded, very slightly, and said "Understood," in the same calm voice, and they left.

Malcolm walked out of the guildhall into the rain behind the others, and felt something he didn't like.

Relief.

He was relieved. Nobody was going down that stair. Not John, who'd have been first, because John's light was the only thing that could see in the deep. Not Azac, who'd have been in front, because Azac was always in front. Not Xav, who'd have gone because the others did. Not RX, not Malcolm himself. None of them. They'd be at the top, at the door, safe.

He was so relieved his knees felt weak. And he hated it, because the relief meant he'd been sure, somewhere underneath, that if they went down they wouldn't all come back up.

He got out the notebook that night, and wrote under a new IF, in very small letters: *IF we'd been allowed to go.* And then sat for a long time looking at the space under it, and didn't write a THEN.

***

The expedition was twelve.

Malcolm watched them gather in the square by the Hollow's arch at dawn, three days later, in the grey light, with half the town come out to see them off. He'd made himself come. He didn't know why. He stood at the edge of the crowd with the others and looked at each of them in turn, and wrote them down in his head, the way he wrote everything.

The leader was a woman called Orla Fane. Reaching-tier, like Petra, which meant there were perhaps a dozen swordsmen in the kingdom who could stand against her. Tall and lean and dark, with a long scar from her ear to her collarbone and a quiet that reminded Malcolm of Xav. Petra knew her. They'd nodded to each other across the square, once, the way soldiers do.

Behind her, the best the guild had. Three Gleaming swordsmen, all veterans. Four Honed, younger, eager, checking their straps too often. A Bonfire mage, old and bald and cheerful, called Tobiah, who'd spent forty years in the Hollow and who stood in the square telling jokes to the crowd as if this were a picnic. Two Torch-tier mages, a married pair, with matching grey cloaks. And a Lantern-tier scout, a slight girl not much older than twenty, who carried a lamp on a pole and kept glancing at the arch.

Twelve. Three to five tiers above anything in Group 6. More strength in one square than Malcolm had seen in one place since the summoning hall.

"They'll be fine," said Azac, beside him. He said it the way he narrated fights: confidently, a beat before it happened, so that it would. "That's a raid party. That's a proper raid party. Look at the Bonfire guy. He's *bored*."

"He's not bored," said RX quietly. "He's scared. He's just better at hiding it than the Honed lot."

Azac looked at him. RX shrugged. He saw things like that now. Since the winter, since the silver had started creeping into the locked slot on his skill page, RX had got quieter and sharper, as if part of him had already started stepping out of sight.

Orla Fane raised her hand. The crowd went quiet. She didn't make a speech. She just looked round at her eleven, nodded once, and walked through the arch into the Hollow. They followed her in a long line, one after another, and the lamp on the scout's pole went bobbing away into the dark, smaller and smaller, until it was gone.

Malcolm stood in the square for a long time after.

*IF they don't come back*, he thought. And then, because he couldn't stop himself: *THEN what?*

***

They waited at the seal.

All five of them, and Garrick and Petra, and Fenn and the two other old guildsmen who'd guarded that door every night since the winter. Down the long stair of the first ring, in the cold, by the light of a single brazier, with the great oak-and-iron door standing open for the first time since it had been built.

Beyond it, the old stair went on down into the dark. Malcolm could see perhaps twenty steps by the brazier's light. After that, nothing.

John sat on the step, the way he always did. His eyes were half closed. He wasn't looking at the stair. He was looking at the light, the way he'd learned to, all through the winter, night after night on this cold step: not with his eyes, exactly, but with the part of him that knew what light was doing even when it was too faint to see.

"They're going down," he said, every so often, very quietly. "The lamp. I can still feel it. Second ring." A long pause. "Third."

The hours went by.

Malcolm didn't sit. He couldn't. He stood by the wall with his notebook in his hands, not writing in it, and watched the others. Azac with his shield, the big round guild shield he'd carried since he got out of bed, sitting with it across his knees. RX against the wall, so still and so quiet that Malcolm kept losing track of where he was. Xav standing very straight at the top of the stair, beside Petra, with his hands loose at his sides. Not moving. Not fidgeting. Just waiting, the way Petra had taught him, the way he'd waited for twenty minutes in the yard in the winter until she said *better*.

He looked, Malcolm thought, exactly like Declan used to look in the last round of a close match. Holding an angle. Not moving. Waiting for someone to step out.

Malcolm had to look away.

***

Around midnight, he did something he hadn't planned.

He went and knelt beside Azac, and put his hand on the shield across Azac's knees.

"What are you doing?" said Azac.

"I don't know yet." Malcolm didn't look up. He was looking at the shield: the scarred painted wood, the iron rim, the dent where the stone-coloured thing had split the last one in the autumn. "Just in case."

He'd been trying for weeks. Since Thea's letter. *One small line.* He'd felt for the gap every day, in Xav's practice sword, in John's lamp, in the boots by the door, and found nothing, because he was trying too hard, because he couldn't stop thinking of all the ways it might go wrong. *IF it works and then fails at the worst moment. IF it works on the wrong thing. IF I'm not good enough.* The IFs stood in front of the gap like a crowd in a doorway.

Tonight, kneeling on the cold step with his hand on the shield, he wasn't trying. He was just afraid. Afraid for Azac, who'd always be in front. Afraid for all of them. And for once, the fear didn't come out as an IF.

It came out as one word.

*Hold.*

He felt the gap open under his hand, and the word go into it. And the shield, for one heartbeat, two, three, *shone*: a thin pale line of light running all the way round the iron rim, like frost on a window, like the edge of Petra's sword when she put her aura into it.

Then it faded.

Azac was staring at him.

"Mal."

"I know."

"*Mal.*"

"I know." Malcolm sat back on his heels. His hands were shaking. And then, because he was who he was, and couldn't help it, he did it again. Laid his hand on the shield, a little lower down, beside where the first one had been, and wrote it again, smaller.

*Hold.*

It shone. Fainter. Two heartbeats.

"Why'd you do it twice?" said Azac.

"Backup," said Malcolm. "In case the first one fails."

Azac looked at him for a long moment. Then he started to laugh, very quietly, so as not to break the silence on the stair, shaking with it, with the shield across his knees.

"Of course," he whispered. "Of *course* you did."

***

The light came up an hour after that.

John felt it first. He sat up on the step very suddenly, with his eyes wide open, and said, "Something's happening," in a voice Malcolm had never heard him use. "Down there. Deep. Deeper than the lamp. Something's—"

Then they could all see it.

It came up the stair like water rising in a well. Pale, colourless, breathing. The same light John had been watching under the door all winter. But it had never been like this. Not this bright. Not this *close*. It filled the stair from wall to wall and came up it, step by step, slowly, until it was lapping at the edge of the door and spilling across the floor of the landing round their feet. Malcolm could see his own hands in it. He could see the shape in it.

Rings. Rings inside rings. Spokes. Turning, slowly, the way John had described. The way Kei had described, in the hut above Coldwater. A pattern of light laid over the stone at their feet, as if the whole landing were the bottom of a pool and something enormous were being drawn in the deep below, and its reflection was coming up through the water.

"Back," said Petra. "Away from the door. *Now.*"

They went back. All of them, up the last flight of the stair, as the light came up over the threshold. Azac in front, with the shield. Xav at his shoulder. Malcolm behind them with John, holding on to John's sleeve because John looked like he might walk *into* it.

"It's not the lamp," John was saying. "Their lamp's gone. I can't feel it anymore. I can only feel *this*." He was shaking. "It's huge. It's so much bigger than I thought. It's all the way down, Mal, the whole of the deep ring, it's—"

And then it flared.

***

Malcolm would try, afterwards, many times, to write down what it was like, and never manage.

It was white. That was the closest he could get. Not bright, exactly. *White*. As if everything in the world had lost its colour at once, and there was only shape left. The stair. The door. The brazier. Azac's shield in front of him with the thin pale lines Malcolm had written on it blazing round the rim like wire. Xav's back. His own hands. All of them standing in the white, in the shape of the circle, in the middle of something being drawn.

He felt it. That was the worst part. Not with his skin. Somewhere deeper. Somewhere he'd felt only once before in his life, in a stone hall under a painted ceiling, ten months ago, when the floor had opened under thirty-five strangers and pulled them through.

*Pulled.*

He felt himself pulled. Down. Toward the deep. Toward the middle of the rings. Gently, the way a current pulls, the way you feel the tide go out round your ankles. And he heard himself say "*no*" out loud, and grabbed John with both hands, and felt Azac in front of him plant his feet and *lean*, and the shield in Azac's hands hummed like a struck bell—

And it stopped.

The white went out like a candle.

They were standing on the stair in the dark. The brazier had gone out. Fenn was swearing, very quietly and steadily, somewhere below them. Somebody was breathing in short hard gasps that might have been Malcolm. The landing at the bottom was black. The door was black. The stair beyond it was black.

No light came up it at all.

***

It was nearly dawn before anyone came up the stair.

They'd relit the brazier. They'd closed the door, all but a hand's width. Petra stood at the gap with her sword drawn and her aura running a hand's width past the edge of it, the way it had the day she'd killed the stone thing in the autumn, and nobody told her to sit down.

John heard him first. Not the light; there wasn't any. Footsteps. Slow, uneven, dragging, coming up the stair from a long way down.

"One," said John. "Just one."

Malcolm's heart was going so hard he could feel it in his teeth.

*Ossie*, he thought. He'd read Tiger's letter three days ago, the copy in Jared's hand, folded inside Jared's own. They'd all read it. The gate at dusk, the dead boy's face, the way it hadn't breathed unless it remembered to. *Someone wearing someone.*

*IF one comes back*, he thought. He'd written that one. Weeks ago. Long before Tiger's letter. *IF one comes back and the rest don't—*

*THEN don't open the door.*

The footsteps came closer. A shape in the dark beyond the gap. A man, clinging to the wall, one arm hanging. He reached the landing and saw the brazier light through the gap in the door, and made a sound, a terrible sound, like a child.

"*Please*," he said. "*Please. Please.*"

Petra didn't move. Nobody moved.

"John," said Xav, very quietly.

John was already looking. He'd got up off the step and gone as close to the gap as Petra would let him, and was staring at the man on the landing with his eyes narrowed, the way he looked at light.

"His light's his own," John said at last. His voice cracked. "It's... it's *his*. It's not like Tiger said. There's no hand inside it. It's just a person's light. It's all over the place, it's shaking, it's—" He swallowed. "He's himself. I'm sure. He's just really, really scared."

Petra looked at John for one long second.

Then she opened the door.

***

His name was Pryce. He was one of the four Honed swordsmen, the youngest, twenty-four. Malcolm had watched him check his straps three times in the square that morning. His left arm was broken in two places and he'd lost his sword and one boot and most of the skin off both knees, and he'd climbed six hundred steps in the dark with nothing to see by, because the light had gone and the scout's lamp had gone with it.

They got him onto the landing and wrapped him in Garrick's coat and Azac gave him water, and Fenn ran for the guild master, and for a long time Pryce couldn't say anything that made sense. He just shook, and drank, and said *please*, and *the light*, and *Orla*, over and over.

Then, slowly, it came out.

They'd gone down through the second ring and the third. Things in the dark there, but nothing that wanted to fight. Things that *moved aside*. Into the deep ring, where nobody had been in a year. And at the bottom of the deep ring, there was a cave. A huge one. Bigger than the guildhall. Bigger than the square.

And in the floor of the cave there was a circle.

"Burned in," Pryce whispered. "Into the rock. Black. Like glass. Rings and lines. So big you couldn't see across it. And it was... it was *lit*. All the lines. Glowing, under the rock, the way coals glow." He was staring at nothing. "And there were people standing in it."

The landing went very quiet.

"People," said Garrick.

"Standing on the lines. In rings. All facing in. Dozens of them." Pryce's teeth were chattering. "Just standing. Not moving. Not talking. Like they were *waiting*." He swallowed. "There were miners. In miners' clothes. And farmers. And some of them had *packs*, guild packs, like ours, and—"

He stopped. His face crumpled.

"Corrigan's party," said Garrick, very quietly. "The one that didn't come back. Last spring."

Pryce nodded, crying.

"Orla knew one of them," he whispered. "Her brother. He was in Corrigan's party. She saw him standing there, on the lines, in the middle, and she said his name, and he *turned his head*." Pryce shuddered. "He turned his head and looked at her and smiled. And she walked toward him. She just walked. Into the circle. And Tobiah shouted, and grabbed for her, and then everything went—"

"White," said Malcolm.

Pryce looked at him.

"Yes," he said. "White."

"And then?"

"I don't know." Pryce was shaking his head, over and over. "I was at the back. By the entrance. I was the last one in. I felt it *pull*, like a river, and I grabbed the rock, and I held on, and when it stopped, the lamp was gone and it was dark and I couldn't hear anyone. Not Orla. Not Tobiah. Not anyone." He looked round at them, wild-eyed. "I called and called. Nobody answered. So I ran. I ran up in the dark, I don't know how long, I fell, I broke my arm, I kept going." He was crying properly now. "I left them. I left them all down there. I *ran*."

Nobody said anything. Then Azac, very gently, put his big hand on the young man's good shoulder and kept it there.

"You came back," said Azac. "That's not running. Someone had to come back and tell us. That was you."

***

Harrow came, with half the guild behind her, and listened to it all, and went grey.

By noon the door was shut. By dusk it was bricked up behind, a wall of new stone three feet thick, with Fenn laying the last course himself and swearing at every brick. A rider went to the capital with a letter for the Advisor. Another went to Kingsferry and Wrenhaven with orders that nobody, nobody, was to go below the first ring of the Hollow again until the Crown said otherwise.

Eleven of the best the Duskhollow guild had. Gone in one breath of white light, in a cave at the bottom of the world, walking toward people who'd been missing for a year.

Group 6 walked home through the town at dusk without saying anything. The lamps were being lit along the street. People stood in doorways, watching them pass. Somewhere a woman was crying, behind a shutter; Malcolm thought it might be the wife of one of the Honed swordsmen, and he walked faster, and was ashamed of walking faster.

At Mother Annick's, Xav went straight up to their room, and when Malcolm followed him up, he found him sitting on the bed with a letter in his lap. Jared's letter, from the capital, the one with Tiger's folded inside. He wasn't reading it. He was looking at a drawing at the bottom of it: a badly-drawn rectangle with a scalloped edge, and inside it a stick figure eating something.

*GREETINGS FROM THE CAPITAL*, it said, across the top. *He still doesn't miss you.*

"I don't know what this means," said Xav, quietly, without looking up. "I've been looking at it for three days. I think it's a joke. I think it's someone else's joke, that I'm not in." He touched the edge of the drawing with one finger. "It's the nicest thing anyone's sent me since we got here."

Malcolm sat down on the other bed. He didn't know what to say. So he didn't say anything, and after a while Xav folded the letter up, very carefully, with the drawing on the outside, and lay down with it on his chest.

***

He wrote the page that night.

He sat at the table by the window in the room at Mother Annick's, with the others asleep or pretending to be, and the lamp turned low, and the notebook open at the back. At the blank page. *WORST CASE.*

And he found, at last, that he knew what went there. He'd known for a while. He'd been building to it one IF at a time all year, the way you build a wall one brick at a time without looking at how high it's getting. The hut above Coldwater, with the shoes lined up against the wall. The circle burned into the floor. The tracks going north and never coming back. The thing with Ossie's face. The cave at the bottom of the Hollow, and people standing on the lines, facing in, waiting.

*Pulled.* He'd felt it pull. The same way the floor had pulled, ten months ago, in the summoning hall.

He wrote it down. Slowly. In small square letters.

*IF the circle under the Hollow is the same kind of circle as the one that brought us here,*

*THEN somebody is summoning.*

*And they didn't ask either.*

He looked at it for a long time. Then he kept going, underneath. Everything. Every IF he'd written all year that pointed this way, all in one place, in order, until the page was full and he had to turn over. *IF they're the same people. IF the people who walk in come out wearing someone. IF they want healers. IF they want the one who looks. IF they're on both sides of the kingdom and underneath it too.* It took a long time. When he finished, his hand ached, and the lamp had burned down, and the sky outside the window was starting to go grey.

He read it all through once. It was the most frightening thing he'd ever written.

And then he turned the page, and found that he was already writing the next one.

*THEN:*

*Tell everyone. Every group. Tonight.*

*John learns to see the circle from further away.*

*Azac's shield holds. (Two lines. Backup.)*

*RX learns to not be there.*

*Xav learns to wait longer than they can.*

*I learn to make things hold.*

*Nobody goes anywhere alone.*

He stopped. He looked at the two pages side by side in the grey light. The worst case, on the left. And on the right, under it, what they'd do.

And he understood something he'd never let himself understand, in ten months of being teased about the book of doom.

He'd started it the month Azac nearly died. But that wasn't really when it started. It had started three years ago, on an ordinary morning, with a message in the class group chat that nobody had seen coming. Declan, in his sleep, in Malacca. No warning. No IF. Nothing anyone could have planned for. Just *next year*, and *next year*, and then there wasn't one.

He'd never been ready for that. He'd sworn, without ever saying it, that he'd never be unready like that again.

Every IF in the book was a way of saying *I love you, and I'm not going to be caught out*. Every THEN was a promise. The pessimism wasn't despair. It never had been. It was getting ready. It was the only way he knew to hold on to people: to think of every way he could lose them, and stand in front of each one.

Malcolm sat at the window with the book open in front of him, and the sun coming up over Duskhollow, and for the first time in three years, he cried for Declan properly. Quietly, so as not to wake anyone. For a long time.

When he'd finished, he opened his window.

[ Malcolm · Tier 0: Initiate · Progress to Spark: 33% ]

It had been twenty-six the day before.

***

Azac found him there an hour later, still at the table, and read the two pages over his shoulder, slowly, the way Azac read everything, moving his lips.

"Mal," he said at last.

"I know."

"This is the most depressing thing I've ever read in my life."

"I know."

Azac was quiet for a moment.

"Can I have a copy?" he said.

Malcolm looked up at him. Azac's face was perfectly serious.

"Yeah," said Malcolm. His voice came out rough. "Yeah. I'll make five."

He made seven, in the end. Five for them, one for Garrick, and one, folded small and sealed with a blue strip and numbered at the top, to go downriver to Kingsferry, to Deston and Gabe, who'd known Declan even longer than Group 6 had, and who'd understand what it meant that Malcolm had finally written the last page.

*No. 1*, he wrote at the top. And under it, because he couldn't help himself: *Backup copy.*

***

Two days later a fast barge came upriver from Kingsferry, and on it a letter from Deston, sealed with a blue strip.

Xav read it at the table. Then he sat very still for a while. Then he held it out to Malcolm without a word, with his thumb under one line.

*I felt you last night. On the thread. You were standing very still, like you were waiting for something. I don't know if you felt me. I squeezed.*

Malcolm looked at the date at the top. Then at Xav.

"The stair," he said.

"The stair," said Xav. His face was very calm, the way it always was. His eyes weren't. "I didn't feel it, Mal. I didn't feel anything. I was just standing there, waiting for something to come up." He took the letter back and folded it, and put it inside his shirt, next to Jared's. "But he did."

Malcolm opened the notebook to a fresh page. He wrote at the top: *IF Deston can feel us from Kingsferry*, and stopped, with the pen over the paper.

Then, for the first time all year, he wrote a THEN that wasn't a plan for something going wrong.

*THEN none of us is ever really on our own.*
