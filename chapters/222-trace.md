*Session 640*, Chris wrote, on the roof, at four in the morning on midsummer day. *Swiss, round 5. Deciders. 2–2 vs 2–2. Winner through, loser out. Me vs Niko.*

He looked at it.

*Known issues*, he wrote, under it. And then sat with the pencil over the page for a long time, in the grey, with his back against the chimney stack and the whole of Valcrest going pale below him, and couldn't think of a single one.

That was the issue.

***

He'd drawn the Swiss in the winter. He'd drawn it for this. Not for him and Niko; you couldn't draw a Swiss for any one bout, it found you on its own, by the sums, the way water finds the bottom of a hill. But for this kind of morning. The fifth round. The deciders. In a Major, on Earth, at two in the morning on a stream with four hundred thousand people watching, the deciders were the best thing in the whole tournament, and everybody knew it. Two teams who'd won two and lost two, on the same day, in the same building, with nothing left. Win and you went through to the stage. Lose and you went home. No second chances. No lower bracket. One map.

He'd watched a hundred of them. With Ice, on a call, at two in the morning, both of them with the stream open and the sound off and the voice chat on, saying nothing for whole rounds and then both saying *oh* at the same time.

He'd never thought he'd be in one.

Two–two. He'd beaten Bee on the first morning, carefully, because a drawing that moved was a thing you had to wait for, and he'd logged her charcoal dogs for two years in the channel and knew they ran left. He'd lost to Gabe on the second, because there was nothing in two years of logs about what to do when a man the size of a door planted a blade in the sand and leaned on it and *Wall* made the whole of him as immovable as a gate. He'd beaten Azac on the third, through the side of the shield, where Malcolm's *Hold* didn't go round. He'd lost to Jared on the fourth, because Jared's feet didn't move and Chris's *Release* was a thing for changing your mind halfway, and you can't change your mind halfway into a man who isn't going to move whatever you do.

Two–two.

And Niko had won one and lost one and won one and lost one, the same four days the rest of the Lists had found out what a Tier 1 spellblade looked like on the sand. Which was: a thin quiet man with his hands in his pockets until *begin*, and a blunted short sword, and something that happened in the middle of a bout that made the marshals look at each other.

The wall said so. In gold, and lampblack. Two boxes in the fifth column, at the bottom, side by side, with a line between them that Chris had ruled himself at midnight with his hand not quite steady.

CHRIS. NIKORASU.

*0–0 like everyone*, he'd written, in the winter, on Niko's line in the notebook, when Niko had asked in the channel. It was the only line in the notebook that had ever had *like everyone* on it.

***

He'd known Niko through Yan. Everybody had known everybody through Yan, a little. Yan had been the middle of a wheel on Earth the way Hana was the middle of one here, except Yan never noticed. Chris had met Niko in a voice call in Yan's server, three or four years before any of this, and thought, the way everybody thought, *quiet guy*. Chill. Hands in his pockets, if a voice could have hands in its pockets. Played every class in every game and never picked one.

And then, in the second autumn, a line in the channel that had stopped Chris dead on a roof in Valcrest with his notebook on his knees.

*two heartbeats again. no faint.*

He'd logged it. He'd logged every one after. Niko's heartbeats. Every night, last thing, for two years and a half, the way he'd logged everyone's count; except Niko's count wasn't a percentage, it was a number of heartbeats, how long he could hold two things at once before one of them went out. Two. Three. Five. Eleven, in the Lists last summer, at dawn, on the empty sand, with a man pouring Dominion aura over the whole bowl like a tide. Nineteen, at Cinderhold, tossing it like a wok. Twenty-nine. And then, on the last morning of the old moon, on a knoll, with blood on his nose and a hunter coming over him: *struck. spellblade*. And the bell.

Chris had every one of them. In order. On a page of their own at the back of the notebook, with a date beside each one. *Niko: heartbeats*.

He'd never told Niko. It wasn't the kind of thing you told people. It was just the kind of thing Chris did.

He'd logged Niko's last four bouts too, from the rail. He'd logged everybody's. That was why he'd drawn the wall: so he could watch all of them. He'd watched Niko beat Calvin on the first morning by standing so still inside Calvin's wind that the wind went round him. He'd watched him lose to Milli on the second, at full *Pounce*, because Milli was very fast and Niko's thing took a breath to happen, and Milli hadn't given him a breath. Beat Dennis on the third by hitting the real one of three without looking at any of them. And lose to Hako on the fourth, because Hako put the whole of himself into one strike, *Commit*, nothing held back, and there was nothing for Niko's bell to ring against but a man already past him.

He had four bouts of logs. Two years and a half of heartbeats. He knew Niko's tells. He knew Niko's breath.

And he still couldn't think of a single known issue.

***

"You're thinking with your breath," said Winter.

She'd come up the hatch. He hadn't heard her; he never did. She sat down beside him on the leaded roof with her back to the warm chimney bricks and her silver-white hair loose and her knees up, on his left, where she'd sat for a year now, since the fourth day of the Lists last summer when he'd stopped holding the space open for somebody who was sitting in the stands.

"That's Tavrin's," said Chris. "Niko said it. In the channel. The old elf said it to him in the winter."

"I know," said Winter. "I read it. I'm saying it to you." She looked at the notebook on his knee, at *Known issues* and the blank under it. "You've been sitting up here for an hour with your pencil over a page not writing anything. You're doing what he does. You're holding your breath until you know what to do."

"I don't know what to do."

"No," said Winter. She was blunt; she'd been blunt in the first year to the point of cruelty, and she'd got kinder, and she was still blunt. "Because there isn't anything to do. You've got two years of logs on him and none of them help, because he's never once in two years done the same thing twice. You've logged him trying. You've never logged him *finished*." She was quiet. "Nobody has. He's only been finished for a season."

Chris looked at her.

"You're going to lose," said Winter.

"I know."

"Good," said Winter. "Then you can stop holding your breath."

Newish said it at breakfast, too, in a different way.

"His beat's changed," said Newish. Out of nowhere, with her spoon in the air, at the long table, the way she said things. "Niko's. Since the bell. It used to be two things. Like a drum and a cymbal, not quite together. You could hear them catch on each other." She put her spoon down. "Now it's one thing. And it's on the off-beat. Where nobody's listening."

"What does that mean?" said Chris.

"I don't know," said Newish. "I'm just telling you what I hear. Don't ask me what it *means*, I'm a support main." She picked her spoon back up. "Mostly it means don't be where you think the beat is."

Hako, at the end of the table, with his spear across his knees, oiling the shaft for the third time that morning, said, "It'll be fine."

"You always say that."

"I've always been right," said Hako. "Eventually."

***

The main sand at noon on midsummer day was the fullest it had been all week.

Every deciders' bout was on the main sand; Chris had drawn it that way. One after another, the whole afternoon. Two–twos. Win and go to Drew's bracket at the right-hand end of the wall, eight boxes, single elimination, the last three days. Lose and go home. The city had worked out the sums on the first day and been arguing about them in the streets ever since, and every one of forty thousand seats had somebody in it and somebody else standing behind them, and the noise came down off the stands onto the sand like weather.

He went out of the south tunnel. Niko came out of the north.

The chalk ring. Eight paces. The marshal, the big grey one who'd said *it's a finger off* yesterday in a voice nobody had ever heard him use. Chris with his longsword, the plain one, Ysolde's, the one she'd put in his hand on the first morning in Valcrest and said *feel for it*. Niko with a blunted short sword from the Lists' armoury, held loose, low, and his other hand in his pocket.

He stood in the ring and looked at Chris.

Thin. Quiet. Chill. Hands in his pockets, until *begin*. The slowest of the thirty-five to rank up, the one everybody had kept underestimating, the one who'd been told *pick one* in every party he'd ever been in. The last of them. Thirty-five of thirty-five. *First in two worlds*, Aldous had said, on the knoll, writing it on the endpaper of the book of the dead.

"Hi," said Niko.

"Hi."

"You've got a page on me," said Niko. "Haven't you. At the back. My heartbeats."

Chris's face went hot.

"Yan told me," said Niko. "He saw it on the roof last summer. Over your shoulder." He didn't smile. Niko didn't smile, much. But his face did the thing at the edges that was Niko's version. "It's okay. I'd have done the same. I've got a page on you."

"You've got a page on me?"

"In my head," said Niko. "Every time you hit *Release*. Every time you drop a move halfway and start another one." He took his hand out of his pocket. "There's a gap. When you drop it. It's tiny. You can't see it. But there's a moment when you've stopped one thing and you haven't started the next, and you're nobody." He looked at Chris. "I'm going to hit you there."

"Why are you telling me?"

"Because it won't help," said Niko. Simply. Not unkindly. The way you'd tell somebody the weather. "You can't not have a gap. It's what *Release* is. You'd have to stop being you." He settled his feet. "And because you'd have done the same. Told me. You're honest. It's in your logs."

The marshal said *begin*.

***

He'd log it afterwards. Every second of it. He'd sit on the roof that night with the notebook on his knee and write it down in pseudo-code, line after line, and it would take him three pages, and he'd read it back and know that none of it said what had happened.

What had happened was this.

Niko didn't move.

Not the way Xav didn't move, waiting; not the way Rui Xiang wasn't there. Niko stood in the chalk ring with the short sword low and his hand out of his pocket and his eyes on Chris, and Chris felt the two things in him. He'd never felt them before; he'd only ever read them, in the channel, in a number at the end of a night. The aura, going out, low, slow, the way it went out of every aura user, the way it went out of Chris. And something else. Something cold and bright, coming *in*, like breath. Mana. The mage's thing. Coming in from outside him the way it came into Thea and Ina and Yan.

Both. At once. In one body. Not catching on each other any more. Going round.

He'd known. He'd read it a hundred times. He hadn't *known*.

He went first. He had to. He couldn't wait for Niko; nobody could wait for Niko, there was nothing to wait for, Niko's thing just went round and round like a wheel and never gathered. So Chris went. A cut, high, from the right, the way Ysolde had taught him on the first morning, blade up, on the line.

Niko's eyes didn't follow it.

They followed *him*. Chris saw them do it. Not the blade. His shoulders. His hands. The way you'd read a man, Ice used to say, who you couldn't read the face of: the shoulders, the hands, the way he held still. And Chris felt, halfway through the cut, that it was wrong. That Niko had already seen where it was going and wasn't where it was going to be.

So he dropped it.

*Release*. He'd had it since the fourth day of the Lists last summer. It was the thing he'd found when he found the leak: you could drop a move halfway, clean, like cancelling an animation, and start another one with no hitch, no lag, nothing for anyone to read. He'd won the Trios with it. He'd beaten Azac with it three days ago, cutting high and dropping it and coming in low under the shield. He dropped the high cut, clean, halfway, and started the low one.

And there was a gap.

He felt it. For the first time in a year he *felt* it, because Niko had told him it was there, and now he knew where to look. Between the one and the other. A moment so small there wasn't a word for it, where he'd stopped and hadn't started, where his aura had let go of one shape and not yet taken the next. Where he was nobody.

Niko was in it.

He didn't see Niko move. Nobody did, John would tell him that night, playing it back in the yard in his little light a dozen times: you could slow it down to nothing and you still didn't see him move. One moment Niko was a pace and a half away with the short sword low. The next he was inside the gap, inside the place where Chris was nobody, with the short sword coming up, and both the things in him, the aura going out and the mana coming in, going round once, twice, and *out through his hand into the steel*.

The blade touched Chris's longsword.

Not hard. Not even fast. The way you'd touch a bell with your knuckle to hear it.

It rang.

***

He felt it ring.

That was the thing he could never get into the logs. He felt it ring *in* him. Not the sound; the sound went out across the bowl, high and clear and long, like a bell struck in a valley, so that forty thousand people went quiet in the middle of a breath and somewhere on the fourth tier of the east stand Maigo said *that's the note* very loudly into the silence. But under the sound, through the blunted steel, through Ysolde's longsword and the grip of it and his hands and his wrists and his arms, the *ring* went into him. The way it had gone into a black stone on a knoll and come back up through the green.

It went down into his aura. Into all of it. All the way to the bottom, to the place where *Release* lived, where his aura had been holding its shapes and dropping them and taking new ones for a year like a man juggling. And it rang there too.

And every shape his aura had ever held came up.

All at once. Every one. Every cut he'd ever made, every parry, every dropped move and every new one, two years and a half of them, six hundred and forty sessions, every line in every page of the notebook he'd kept on the roof in English pseudo-code because he couldn't write the kingdom's script: they came up out of the bottom of him in the ring of the bell like silt coming up off a riverbed when you strike the water. *IF grip == correct AND stance == correct*. *aura NOT flowing. investigate*. *Release: drop at 0.5, restart at 0.5, no hitch*. Two years. All of it. Every blow he'd ever thrown and every blow he'd ever watched, Ysolde's, Hako's, Winter's frost on the sand, Newish's slow, Ina's fire, Gabe's wall, Jared's feet, Rui Xiang coming round the left. Every one. Logged.

And Niko's.

The page at the back. *Niko: heartbeats*. Two years and a half of a number at the end of a night, and four bouts from the rail this week, and one just now, and the gap, and the bell.

It all came up, in the ring, at once, and it *lined up*.

He saw it. In the air. Not with his eyes; with whatever it was in him that had been logging for two years and a half. A line. Thin and faint and silver, going out from the tip of Niko's short sword, still touching his longsword, still ringing: going round, down, under, and up, in a curve, to the place under his left arm where his ribs were.

The next blow. The one Niko hadn't thrown yet. Traced in the air in front of him, faint as a pencil line under ink, where it was going to go.

He saw it. He knew exactly where it was going to land.

He couldn't move.

He was still in the gap. Still nobody. The ring still going through him, holding him the way a struck bell holds the air. He saw the line of the next blow, traced in silver, clear as anything he'd ever written in the notebook; and he stood inside the gap with his longsword ringing and his aura full of every shape he'd ever held and watched Niko's short sword follow the line exactly, round, down, under, and up, and touch him, very lightly, under the left arm, on the ribs.

"*Touch*," said the marshal.

***

He sat down on the sand.

He didn't decide to. His legs did it. The ring was still going in him, fading, slowly, the way a bell fades, and he sat down in the middle of the chalk ring with Ysolde's longsword across his knees and his window open in front of his face.

He hadn't opened it.

And under it, grey, the way Thea's *Handshake* had been grey since the autumn:

*Trace · locked · requires Tier 2*

He looked at it.

Ninety-nine point nine. Of course it was. It was the most Chris number there had ever been.

*Trace*.

He'd done tracing on Earth. Every programmer had. You ran the thing, and you logged every step it took, every call and every return, so that when it went wrong you could go back through the log and *see* it, the whole path, laid out, line by line, where it had gone and where it was going to go. A stack trace. The line of everything that had happened, so you could see what happened next.

Two years and a half of logs. And a bell.

He'd seen it. The line of the next blow. Before it came. Not because he'd guessed; because he'd watched every blow he'd ever seen and written it down, and the bell had rung it all up out of the bottom of him at once and laid it end to end, and at the end of the line, where it went next, there had been a curve going under his left arm.

He'd seen it. Too late. He'd been in the gap.

That was the point one, he understood, sitting in the sand. The skill wasn't seeing it. Any fool with a notebook could see where a thing had been going, afterwards. The skill was seeing it *in time*.

He'd lost.

He started to laugh.

***

"*Through*," said the marshal, over him. To the stands. In his big grey voice, which wasn't quite steady. "*Nikorasu*. Three–two. *Through to the bracket*."

The bowl stood up.

Not all at once. In a wave, the way the bowl had stood up for Rachael on the first day, section by section, round the whole of the Lists. They'd heard the bell. They'd heard it on the first day too, faintly, when Niko beat Calvin, and on the second, and they'd heard it in the ballad, which had got to Valcrest a month before the Lists did: *and the boy with two hands struck the stone like a bell*. They knew what they'd heard. A Tier 1. A Tier 1 who'd put a Drawn of two years and a half on the sand with a touch like a knuckle on a bell.

Niko was standing over him.

He'd put the short sword down. On the sand, at his feet. His hands were back in his pockets. His face was doing nothing at all, which was what Niko's face did when it was doing everything.

"You saw it," said Niko.

"I saw it."

"The next one. You saw where it was going." Niko looked at him. "I felt you see it. Right at the end. Like somebody reading over my shoulder."

"Too late," said Chris.

"This time," said Niko.

He held out his hand. Out of his pocket. To Chris, on the sand.

Chris took it. He let Niko pull him up. He stood in the chalk ring with Ysolde's longsword in his other hand and the ring finally gone out of him and his window still faintly at the edge of his eye and looked at the thin quiet man in front of him who'd been told to pick one in every party he'd ever been in.

"Thirty-five of thirty-five," said Chris. "I logged it. On the knoll. With blood on your nose."

"I know."

"I've logged everyone," said Chris. "Two years and a half. Everyone's count. Every heartbeat. Every percentage." He looked at the wall. You could see it from the main sand, the whole forty feet of it, across the bowl, under the west stand, with its forty lamps and its thirty-six boxes. "I've ranked everyone. I said the count wasn't a ranking. It was. Of course it was. I'm me." He looked back at Niko. "I don't know where to put you."

Niko didn't say anything for a moment.

Then he did the thing at the edges of his face. A little more than usual.

"0–0," said Niko. "Like everyone."

He wrote it on the wall himself.

He went straight there from the sand, through the south tunnel and round under the west stand, with the longsword still in his hand and Winter on his left and the whole of Group 7 behind him, and Lucan's painters on their ladders at the right-hand end with their pots of gold and their pots of black, waiting to move the names.

He took the brush off the head painter. The man let him. Lucan, at the bottom of the ladder, in his good coat, with his eyes already filling, started to say something about *the painters, Chris, it's the painters' job* and then didn't, and put his hand over his mouth.

He went up the ladder.

He drew the line himself. From the fifth column, from the bottom box, NIKORASU, in gold, across to the right-hand end, to the eight boxes of Drew's bracket, where the first two names were already lettered, XAVIER and GABE, from the third day, and below them WENDY and MILLI and JARED and HAKO from the fourth. He drew the line across to the seventh of the eight. Clean. Black. Straight. The way he'd drawn every line on the wall in the winter with Ysolde holding the other end of the chalk.

He didn't draw a line from his own box. His own box stayed where it was, at the bottom of the fifth column, with *2–3* under it in his own small square hand, and nothing going out of it.

He looked at the seventh box of Drew's bracket, empty, with his line going into it.

Then he dipped the brush in the gold, and lettered it himself. Not as neatly as Lucan's painters. A programmer's hand. Small and square and careful.

NIKORASU.

And under it, in the black, smaller, the way he wrote at the bottom of a page in the notebook, so small you'd only see it if you stood right under the box and looked up:

*Tier 1. Can't rank him.*

He came down the ladder.

Lucan was crying, obviously. Ysolde, beside him, wasn't. Ysolde looked up at the box for a long moment with her ram's horns catching the lamplight from the forty lamps that Ina had lit and her arms folded.

"Good," said Ysolde.

He sat on the roof that night with the notebook on his knee and wrote it all down. Three pages of it, in pseudo-code, line by line, everything that had happened in the chalk ring, from *begin* to the bell. He read it back. None of it said what had happened. He'd known it wouldn't.

At the bottom of the third page he wrote the count, the way he always did.

*Tier 2 or above: 8 of 35. Tier 3 or above: 0 of 35, Thea close. All 35 present. 33 in Valcrest. 2 on a tower.*

He sat with the pencil over the next line for a long time.

He'd written it every night since the Lists last summer. In the notebook, and in the channel. *Line 36: still blank. still there*. Last night, for the first time, he'd written a different one, on the back page, small, where nobody would look. *Line 36: here*.

Tonight he looked across the backs of the houses toward the Street of Coopers, which you couldn't see from the roof of the Lance, because the roofs were in the way.

He thought about the north stand. High up, at the back, under the overhang, where the seats were cheapest. Where Deston had kept his head turned the other way all week, very carefully, smiling.

He hadn't looked. Not once, all day. Not from the sand. Not even when the bell rang and forty thousand people went quiet and he'd sat down in the middle of the chalk ring with his window open.

He hadn't needed to. Because when the bell had rung through him, and every shape his aura had ever held had come up out of the bottom of him at once and lined up into a trace, there'd been one more line in it. Faint. Silver. Not from Niko's sword. From much further off, high up, at the back of the north stand.

A line of something going *out*. Not toward him. Toward the whole bowl, all of it, for a heartbeat, the way a tide goes out over a beach. Dominion aura. Held in all week so tightly that Deston could only hear its thread, and let go, for one heartbeat, when the bell rang, the way a man's breath goes out of him when he sees something he's waited a very long time to see.

Then pulled back in. At once. Before anybody else could feel it.

Chris had felt it. In the trace. One line, out of the north stand, going out and coming back, like the line on a graph when something spikes.

He'd know that line anywhere. He'd watched it pour over the whole bowl of the Lists at dawn last summer, with a thin quiet man in the middle of the sand going past five heartbeats for the first time in his life.

He wrote it down.

*Line 36: here. Saw it.*

Then he closed the notebook, and put the pencil behind his ear, and sat on the roof with his back against the warm chimney bricks and Winter asleep against his left shoulder, where she'd been for a year, and looked at the dark where the north stand was, and didn't log anything else at all.
