Gigichi had a message she'd never answered.

It wasn't a long one. Two words and a question mark, in a little grey box in a chat window, sent at some ugly hour of the morning during the worst month of the worst year she'd had. She'd stopped streaming by then. Not on purpose. She'd just started skipping days, and then weeks, and then she'd looked up one morning and realised she hadn't gone live in a month, and nobody had said anything.

Nobody except him.

*you okay?*

That was all. No emoji. No joke. He always made a joke; that was the thing about Ice, he couldn't help it. But not that time. Just two words, from a man she didn't talk to that much anymore, who for some reason had noticed she was gone when nobody else had.

She'd read it. She'd sat on her bed with her phone in her hand and read it maybe twenty times. She'd started typing an answer a dozen times. *yeah fine* and *lol yeah* and once, at four in the morning, something much longer and much truer that she'd deleted one word at a time until the box was empty again.

She never sent anything. And then one morning three months later Xola had called her crying, and there wasn't anyone left to send it to.

She'd told the others about it once, by the fire, in the winter. *I should have answered.* That was all she'd said. She hadn't told them the rest: that she still had the screenshot, on a phone that was in another world now, and that some nights before she fell asleep she still wrote him answers in her head.

*Yeah. No. Not really. Thanks for asking.*

***

Higu told them at breakfast.

She did it the way Higu did everything: calmly, between one mouthful of porridge and the next, as if she were mentioning the weather. "I reached Spark last night," she said. "On the roof. My skill's called Moth."

Xola dropped her spoon.

Then everything happened at once. Xola shrieking, and Squiddles on her head going a startled shade of pink and waving all his tiny arms; PenguChi standing up and coming round the table and hugging Higu very slowly and very hard; Thea grabbing Higu's wrist, demanding to see the window, *show me show me show me*. Even Bram, at the end of the table, put down his tea and said "Well, I'll be," and Maren, coming in from the yard with her book under her arm, stopped in the doorway, and looked at Higu for a long moment, and said, "Congratulations," in a voice that, for Maren, was practically a song.

Gigichi didn't shriek. She wasn't a shrieker. She sat where she was and watched Higu sit very still in the middle of all of it, being hugged and shaken and congratulated, with her calm face and her half-lidded eyes. And she saw, under the calm, something that wasn't calm at all. Something very tired and very far away, as if Higu had seen a great deal more last night than a moth.

*Huh,* thought Gigichi.

She didn't say anything. She just reached across the table and squeezed Higu's hand once, briefly, the way you'd tap someone's shoulder in a game to say *I see you*. And Higu looked at her, and something in her face eased, very slightly.

Six years, they'd been friends, she and Higu. Longer than Gigichi had been friends with almost anyone. You learned somebody's face in six years, even through a screen. You learned what the calm was covering.

"Rule three," said Higu. "Good news loudly." And she opened her window and wrote it in the channel, and three seconds later Maigo, on the other side of the kingdom, had replied in capitals so large Gigichi could practically hear them:

[ Maigo: THIRD
Maigo: THIRD IS JUST FIRST BUT LATER BUT LATER
Maigo: CONGRATULATIONS HIGU ]

***

Alea came at the usual time, with four fish in the basket and her ears up and her tail swinging, and they told her about the channel.

They hadn't discussed whether to. It just came out of all of them at once, because it was the biggest thing that had happened since Thea's Spark and Alea was their friend and Alea had been there for everything. Thea built a chat. In our windows. All thirty-five of us. We can talk to Thornwall and Valcrest and the capital, right now, this second; it's instant, nobody can steal it, the courier can't lose it—

Gigichi was watching Alea's face. She didn't know why. Maybe because she'd been watching Higu's face all morning and was in the habit of it. Maybe because she'd learned, in a bad year, that people's faces were where the truth went when their mouths were busy.

So she saw it.

Alea went pale.

Not much. Not for long. A fox girl with a quick bright face, standing in the yard of the militia hall with the basket on her arm and the sun in her ears, and for one heartbeat all the colour went out of her. Like someone who'd just heard a door open in a house she'd thought was empty.

Then it was gone, and she was smiling. "That's amazing," she said. "Thea, that's *amazing*. All of you? At once?"

"All of us," said Thea, glowing.

"Can I see it?"

Thea's face fell a little. "No. Sorry. It's only windows from the circle. Yours wouldn't..." She stopped, and caught herself, because Alea always said she didn't have a window, and they all always pretended to believe her. "It's only us," she finished.

"Oh," said Alea. "Of course." She put the basket down on the bench by the door, very carefully, the way she did everything with the basket. Then she straightened up, and looked at them, all five of them, one after another.

"Can I ask you something?" she said. "A favour."

"Anything," said PenguChi at once.

Alea took a breath.

"Don't put my name in it," she said. "Please. In the chat. Don't write my name. Don't write about me." She was looking at the flagstones. "Not my name. Not my dad. Not the fish. Not anything."

There was a short silence in the yard.

"Why?" said Gigichi.

She hadn't meant to. It came out flat and blunt, the way most things came out of her. Xola shot her a look. Gigichi ignored it. She was watching Alea, and Alea's face was doing that thing again: going still, carefully, the way a pond goes still when something big moves underneath it.

"Because," said Alea slowly, "I'd like to be the one who tells people about me. When I'm ready." She lifted her chin. "Not someone in a chat. Not a line in a window, in Valcrest, that I'll never see. Me."

"That's not an answer," said Gigichi.

"I know." Alea met her eyes. There was something in them Gigichi recognised: something tired and stubborn and a little sad. "I know it's not. It's all I've got. I'm sorry."

Gigichi looked at her for a long moment.

She thought about the first week. The road to the market, and the fight with Thea in front of half of Wrenhaven, and the weeks afterwards when she couldn't look at Thea across the yard and couldn't look at anyone else either. And Alea, who'd been there four days, coming and sitting down beside her on the wall at the end of practice, not saying anything for a long time. And then, finally, out of nowhere: *My dad says a road's only blocked if you stop on it.* And getting up, and going home.

Gigichi had thought about that sentence for a month. It had been the first thing anyone said to her in this world that wasn't about the fight.

"Fine," said Gigichi.

Everyone looked at her.

"Fine," she said again. "We won't. Nobody writes your name." She glared round at the others, as if any of them had been about to argue. "Right?"

"Right," said PenguChi.

"Of course," said Xola.

"Right," said Thea, a little slowly, looking at Alea with an expression Gigichi couldn't read.

Higu hadn't said anything. She was looking at Alea, too. Then she nodded, once.

"Rule five," she said quietly.

"There are only four rules," said Xola.

"There are five now," said Higu. "We just don't write that one down."

Alea let out a breath Gigichi hadn't realised she was holding. She smiled. Not the quick bright smile. A small one, crooked, real.

"Thank you," she said. And then, because she was Alea, she picked up the basket again and held it out. "Trout," she said. "Four. They were very cooperative this morning."

***

The channel was chaos, the first week. Gigichi had expected that. What she hadn't expected was how much she'd like it.

It was like the old days. Not her own chat; she'd never had a big chat, even at her best, a few dozen people who mostly showed up to argue with her about plants. It was like the group chats. The ones she'd been in back home with Xola and Thea and Higu and the others, before everything drifted: the ones that went off at three in the morning because somebody had found a cursed picture of a cat, the ones where forty messages piled up while you were in the shower and you came back and had to scroll for five minutes to find out why everyone was suddenly arguing about soup.

Except this one only kept forty lines.

That was the first thing everyone learned. You could look away for a minute, and when you looked back the whole conversation had scrolled off the top and was gone forever, and there was nothing left but the tail end of Newish and Maigo arguing about something, with no way of finding out what.

[ Newish: no because that's exactly what i said
Maigo: IT IS NOT
Newish: scroll up
Maigo: I CAN'T SCROLL UP. NOBODY CAN SCROLL UP. THAT'S THE WHOLE PROBLEM
Althea: I'm working on it.
Althea: I'm not working on it. I can't. Sorry. Forty lines is forty lines.
Nikorasu: what were you arguing about
Newish: honestly i don't remember anymore ]

People wrote at all hours. That was the second thing. Thornwall talked at dawn, before patrol. Valcrest talked late, after the Lists closed, loud and giddy. Group 6 talked in the middle of the night, because apparently nobody in Group 6 had slept properly in a year. Cinderhold talked in short bursts, between drills. The capital talked in long careful paragraphs that took up six lines each and annoyed everyone.

It was a lot. Gigichi found herself opening her window twenty times a day, just to see. Just to see who was there.

And then, on the third day, the arguments started.

***

It wasn't anyone's fault. It was just thirty-five frightened people, all reading the same bad news at the same moment, with nowhere to put it.

Group 6's cave was still fresh. Everyone had questions. Most of them didn't have answers. And somewhere in the middle of the third night, between Duskhollow and Cinderhold, two people who'd never met in their lives started going at each other: Wendy, saying that if there were circles in the west and circles under the Hollow then the Advisor had to have known, and was lying to them; and someone from Group 6, RX, saying that was paranoid and that the Advisor was the only reason any of them were alive. And then Milli joining in, from the capital, cold and clipped, on Wendy's side. And Rachael joining in, hot and fast, on the other.

It went on for twenty lines. Then thirty. The forty-line window filled up with it, top to bottom, all anger, nothing else, so that anyone who opened their window in that hour saw nothing but people they loved being horrible to each other.

Gigichi read it in bed, by the light of the window, with her jaw getting tighter and tighter.

Then she got up, and went down the corridor in her nightshirt, and knocked on Thea's door.

Thea opened it at once. She was awake too. She had her window open. She looked sick.

"You seeing this?" said Gigichi.

"Yeah."

"Can you shut it up?"

"No." Thea rubbed her face. "I can't delete anything. I can't mute anyone. It's *rudimentary*, Chi 1. It's a pipe. Things go in one end and come out the other."

Gigichi looked at the window over Thea's shoulder. Another line had come in. Rachael, furious. Then Wendy, colder.

"Okay," said Gigichi. "You can't shut it up. Fine. You do the code." She shouldered past Thea into the room and sat down on the end of the bed and opened her own window. "I'll do the yelling."

And she wrote, for the first time since the channel opened:

[ Gigichi: Hi. Rule one.
Gigichi: Everyone here is scared. Including you two. Including me.
Gigichi: Wendy, if the Advisor lied, being mean to RX won't make him tell the truth.
Gigichi: RX, if he didn't, being mean to Wendy won't prove it.
Gigichi: Rachael and Milli, I can see you. Stop it.
Gigichi: It's two in the morning. Everyone go to sleep. We'll still be scared tomorrow. We can be scared in daylight. ]

There was a long pause.

Then:

[ Wendy: sorry
Wendy: rx, sorry. i'm just scared
RX: same. sorry wendy
Rachael: Sorry. Two in the morning is a bad time for me.
Milli: Fair.
Higu: Thank you, Chi 1. ]

Gigichi closed her window. Her hands were shaking a little. She hadn't yelled at anyone like that in years. She'd forgotten how.

When she looked up, Thea was sitting on the floor with her back against the door, looking at her.

"What?" said Gigichi.

"Nothing." Thea's mouth twitched. "*We can be scared in daylight.*"

"Shut up."

"No, it was good." Thea pulled her knees up and wrapped her arms round them. "It was really good. You used to do that. In the old group chat. When Xola and I got into it about something stupid. You'd just come in and say *stop it* and everyone would stop it."

Gigichi looked at her hands.

"I used to do a lot of things," she said.

It was very quiet in the little room. Outside the window, the town was dark. Somewhere down the hill a dog barked once and stopped.

"I wasn't a good friend," said Gigichi. "Back then. Before. You were right, on the road. You were doing everything. I let you."

Thea didn't say anything for a while.

"I know," she said at last. "I'm still a bit angry about it."

"I know."

"A bit." Thea leaned her head back against the door. "Less than I was. A lot less, actually." She was quiet again. Then: "You did the yelling. I did the code. That's what we used to be like. Before it got bad."

"Yeah."

"I missed it."

Gigichi didn't answer that. She couldn't. She just sat there on the end of the bed, with her silver hair coming out of its braid and the forget-me-nots she always wore crushed flat on one side from her pillow, and looked at the floor, and nodded.

***

Higu named them the next morning. Not in the channel; at breakfast, out loud, over the porridge.

"Mods," she said, pointing her spoon at Thea and then at Gigichi. "You two. You're the mods."

"There aren't any mods," said Thea. "It's a pipe."

"Then you're the mods of the pipe," said Higu. "Thea fixes it when it breaks. Chi 1 tells people to go to sleep." She looked at Gigichi with her calm half-lidded eyes. "Someone has to. I can't do it every time."

"Why not?"

"Because I'm the strategist," said Higu. "And you're better at yelling."

Xola laughed so hard Squiddles fell off her head.

He did it the way Chris did everything, in long careful bursts, a line at a time, all through the evening, while the rest of the channel sat back and watched it come in:

[ Chris: ok. compiling. if you've crossed or nearly crossed, what was your line? the thing you wrote. one word or a few.
Althea: Not a word. The world's own language. I copied a line out of the fire.
Tiger: GO

Malcolm: Hold. Then again, lower. Backup.

Rachael: close

Bee: Tell him.

Calvin: Turn as well, for the wind. Huh.

Gigichi read the list three times.

*They're all asking something to do what it already wants to.*

She thought about it all evening. She thought about it at supper, and in bed, and the next morning in the yard, standing in front of the hedge.

***

The hedge ran round the back of the militia hall yard, along the wall, between the yard and the lane behind. Hawthorn, old and tangled, half dead in places, with gaps you could get a goat through. Gigichi had been practising on it since the snow melted. Her path was nature. Thorns and roots and barriers: things that grew between you and what you were afraid of. Maren had been very clear about that, in the autumn, reading her window. *A defender's path. A wall that grows.*

She'd tried everything. Pushing. Pulling. Asking nicely. Standing in front of it for an hour with her hands out, feeling for the place under the bark where the hedge was alive, and finding it, and getting nothing back. It was like talking to someone who was asleep.

*They're all asking something to do what it already wants to.*

What did a hedge want?

She stood there in the cold morning in her cardigan, with her hands in her sleeves, looking at it. It was a hawthorn hedge. It was old. It had been planted, a long time ago, by somebody who'd wanted to keep something out. Goats, probably. Foxes. Thieves. It had spent fifty years standing between the yard and the lane, getting tangled, getting thick, getting gaps knocked in it by carts and children, and growing back.

A hedge wanted to be a hedge. That was all. It wanted to stand between.

Gigichi put out her hand and laid it on the nearest branch, carefully, between the thorns. She felt for it: the slow, sleepy, green warmth under the bark. And under that, very small, the gap. The place Thea had written about.

She didn't have a word. She'd thought she'd need one. But standing there with her hand on the hedge, she found the only thing she had was the thing she'd always had. The thing she'd said to Thea on the road to the market, in the worst way, at the worst moment, in front of everyone. *Then walk around.* The thing she'd said to herself every day of the bad year.

*Not here.*

She wrote it into the gap.

The hedge moved.

Not much. Not for long. But all along the wall, for perhaps ten paces in each direction, every branch of the old hawthorn shivered at once, and *drew together*. The gaps closed. The thin places thickened. Thorns that had been pointing every which way turned, all of them, outward, toward the lane. For three heartbeats the whole back of the yard was a wall: dense, green, bristling, with not a chink in it a mouse could have got through.

Then it relaxed. Slowly. Like a breath let out. The gaps came back. The thorns drooped.

But not all the way. Not quite. Two of the gaps were smaller than they'd been. Gigichi could see it.

"Huh," she said out loud.

Behind her, somebody clapped. Slowly. She turned round. Bram was sitting on his bucket by the door, the way he always was, with his mug of tea, watching her.

"Not bad," he said. "For a Tuesday."

Gigichi opened her window. Thirty-four. It had been twenty-nine last night.

She didn't post it in the channel. She didn't know why. She just stood there by the hedge for a while, with her hand still tingling, and looked at the two gaps that were smaller than they'd been.

***

Tiger's line came in that night. Not the list. Something else.

Group 5 had been quiet since Ossie. Tiger, especially. Tiger, who'd been all capitals on the first night, *THEA YOU ABSOLUTE LEGEND*, had hardly written since. A line here and there. *morning.* *night.* Once, *the passes are still snowed in, fenwick says another month.*

Then, late, when the channel was quiet, he wrote:

[ Tiger: ossie's dad is coming
Tiger: the watch wrote to him. missing. he wrote back. he's walking here. from his village. he gets here in about a week
Tiger: i don't know what to say to him ]

Nobody answered. Not straight away. Gigichi watched the three lines sit there in the window, alone, at the bottom of the channel, with nothing under them.

She knew why nobody was answering. It was the same reason nobody had said anything to her, in the bad year. Not because they didn't care. Because they didn't know what to say, and they were afraid of saying the wrong thing, and so they waited for someone else to say the right thing first. And nobody did. And the silence got longer, and longer, until it was too long to break.

She had her hand up before she'd thought about it.

She knew Tiger. Not well. Back home he'd been one of those people who was in all the same places she was: the same raids, the same collab streams, the same Discord servers at three in the morning. Here, she knew him from the letters. She had Drew's letters and Hyvred's letters, and Tiger had written to her twice, cheerful, untidy, mostly about his sheds.

Two words. That was all he'd written to her. That was all it took.

[ Gigichi: Tiger.
Gigichi: you okay? ]

She looked at it, sitting there in the channel. Two words and a question mark. Gigichi, who never sent anything.

The answer came after a long time.

[ Tiger: no
Tiger: not really
Tiger: but thanks for asking chi 1
Tiger: genuinely. nobody asked ]

Gigichi read it. Then she read it again. Then she put her hand over her mouth and sat on the end of her bed in the dark for a long time.

*Yeah. No. Not really. Thanks for asking.*

She'd been writing that answer in her head for three years. And here it was. Not from her. *To* her. From a boy with tiger ears in a fort on the other side of the kingdom, who'd built three walls of a shed for a friend who wasn't coming back.

She understood, all at once, what it had been like on the other end. What Ice had felt, sending those two words into a silence. Not knowing if anyone was there. Not knowing if it would help. Sending it anyway.

She wrote back.

[ Gigichi: you don't have to know what to say to him.
Gigichi: just be there. that's most of it.
Gigichi: somebody told me that once. ]

Nobody had. Not in words. But somebody had sent two words into a silence once, and she'd never answered, and it had taken her three years and another world to understand what they meant.

Underneath her line, after a moment, Drew wrote: *I'll be there with him.* And then Hyvred, and Kei, and Bee, and then people from everywhere, one after another, filling the window, until Tiger's three lonely lines had scrolled off the top and were gone.

***

Yan's message came at the end of the week.

It was late. The channel had been quiet for an hour. Gigichi was in bed, not asleep, with her window open on the ceiling because she'd got into the habit, in the bad year, of falling asleep looking at a screen. The last few lines were Newish, saying goodnight to everyone individually, which took a while.

Then, from Cinderhold:

[ Suzuyun: Something crossed the Redmarch tonight. South of the fort.
Suzuyun: Not a scout. Bigger. Cold. Colder than anything I've felt out here.
Suzuyun: It didn't stop to count us. It went straight past. Fast. Heading north-west.
Suzuyun: I don't know where it's going.
Suzuyun: Nobody be alone at night. Please. Anyone. ]

The channel was silent for a long time after that.

Gigichi lay in bed and read it four times. North-west. From the Redmarch. She tried to picture Oswin's map, the one Aleaka had described in a letter, with all the postings on it. Cinderhold in the east. Thornwall in the west. The capital in the middle. And up in the north, past the capital, the river and the lake country.

North-west of the Redmarch was a lot of places.

She got up. She didn't know why. She went to the window and looked out, past the dark roofs of Wrenhaven, at the slope going down to the lake. The moon was up, thin and late. The lake was a pale smudge. There was one lamp lit, very small, very far away, down by the water. A cabin.

She looked at it for a while.

Then she went back down the stairs in her nightshirt, and out into the yard, barefoot on the cold flagstones, to the back wall. She laid her hand on the hawthorn hedge, between the thorns. And she stood there in the dark, and felt for the gap, and wrote into it, as hard as she could.

*Not here.*

The hedge drew together under her hand, all along the wall, and held. Five heartbeats, this time.

When she went back up to bed, the gaps were smaller still.
