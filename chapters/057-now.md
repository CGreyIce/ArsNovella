The best thing Declan ever did in a game, Xav thought, was nothing.

Everyone remembered the flashy things. The AWP flicks. The four-kill rounds. The time he'd jumped off a roof in Apex and landed on two people at once and shouted *TRUST THE PROCESS* into the mic so loud that John had dropped his drink. People told those stories. People told them in the channel, the night Hako asked.

But the thing Xav remembered, the thing he'd watched Declan do a thousand times across a thousand rounds, from the seat beside him in the computer lab at poly and then from the other end of a call for years after that, was the waiting.

Last round. One against three. Everyone else dead and spectating, everyone in the call shouting advice. And Declan, holding an angle. Not moving. Not peeking. Not trying anything clever. Just sitting there, with his crosshair on a doorway, while the clock ran down and the whole call went mad around him. Ten seconds. Twenty. Forty.

And then, right before someone stepped out, always right before, he'd say something under his breath. In Latin, usually. Some phrase he'd picked up somewhere, that he never translated, no matter how much they begged.

And then he'd move once. And win.

*How do you know?* Xav had asked him once, at two in the morning, after a round like that. *When to go. How do you know?*

*I don't*, Declan had said. *I just wait until I do.*

***

Duskhollow had been quiet since the stair.

The guild had bricked up the seal three feet thick, with Fenn laying the last course himself. Nobody went below the first ring. The light that had come up under the door every night all winter didn't come anymore; or if it did, it came up against three feet of new stone and stopped there. John still went down every night and sat on the step in front of the bricks, the way he'd sat in front of the door. He said he could feel it, sometimes, faintly, through the wall. Breathing.

Pryce was out of bed, with his arm in a sling. He'd taken to sitting in the common room at Mother Annick's in the evenings, because he couldn't stand to be in the guild hall, with the empty pegs where eleven cloaks used to hang. Group 6 let him. Azac talked to him for hours, about nothing. Malcolm had given him a copy of his worst-case pages, which Pryce had read, and then asked very politely for a copy of the *other* pages, the THEN ones, which Malcolm had given him without a word.

And Xav had read the books.

All of them. Every word. Cursiffe had copied the first one into the channel for him, forty lines at a time, over nine nights, and Xav had copied them out again into a notebook of his own, by candlelight, at the table in the common room, while the channel scrolled on without him. It had taken nine nights. He'd done it the way he did everything: slowly, carefully, all the way through, without skipping.

He'd read the bait. *Trust.* He'd read the nets. *Yes, ma'am.* He'd read the fox and the hens, the spiteful pumpkins, *On Waiting*.

*Most people miss the first tug, because they are too busy pulling.*

He'd read that line and put the pen down and sat at the table for a long time.

*I just wait until I do.*

***

He asked Petra in the yard, the morning after he finished.

They trained at dawn, the two of them, the way they had since the winter: in the walled yard behind the guild hall, with the dew still on the cobbles and the town not yet awake. Petra had taught him to stand still. That was what most of their training was. Not forms, not drills, not sparring. Standing. Facing each other, a few paces apart, with practice swords, not moving, for as long as it took one of them to decide.

The first time, he'd lasted four minutes. Now he could last an hour. Petra could last longer. He'd never once seen her move first.

She was waiting for him when he came out, the way she always was, in her plain grey coat, with her sword already in her hand and the long pale scar along her jaw catching the early light. She didn't say good morning. She never did.

"I want to ask you something," said Xav. "Before we start."

Petra looked at him. She didn't say *what*. She just waited.

"The Redmarch," said Xav. "The ditch. The forty of you, and the man who came."

Something went across her face. Quick, there and gone. He'd only ever seen it once before, in the autumn, the first time she'd told the story.

"You said he only ever moved once," said Xav. "Every time. You said that was what you remembered. That he'd stand there, in the middle of all of it, not moving, and then move once, and it was over."

"Yes."

"Did he say anything?"

Petra didn't answer for a long time. The yard was very quiet. A blackbird was singing somewhere on the guild hall roof.

"Why?" she said.

"Because of the sword." Xav kept his voice level. Calm. The way he talked when he was laying out a plan, the way that made people trust him; he'd been told that all his life, and he'd learned to use it. "The carving on it. The scholar in the capital read it. It's in a language from our world. Old. Dead. Latin." He paused. "And the man I sat next to for nine years, at school and after, used to say things in Latin before he moved. Under his breath. Every time. And never tell anyone what they meant."

Petra stood very still.

"So I'm asking," said Xav. "Did he say anything?"

***

She told him.

Slowly. Not looking at him. Looking at the cobbles, at the dew on them, as if she could see something there that he couldn't.

"Yes," she said. "Every time."

Xav didn't breathe.

"I was in the ditch," said Petra. "The whole time. I couldn't get out. Nobody could. So I watched him. That's all I could do. I watched him fight four of them. Five. More. Over and over. All night." Her voice was very flat. "And every time, before he moved, he said something. Under his breath. Not loud. I only heard it because I was close. Because the last one was close. Right above the ditch."

"What did it sound like?"

Petra shook her head.

"I don't know," she said. "I've tried. For seven years. I've tried to remember it. To say it." She lifted one hand and touched her own mouth, very lightly, as if it belonged to someone else. "My mouth doesn't know the shapes. They weren't our sounds. They weren't any sounds I'd ever heard. A few words. Three, four. The same every time. Like a man saying a thing he'd said a thousand times before." She paused. "I thought it was a prayer. For a long time. To a god nobody here knew."

"And now?"

Petra looked up at him.

"Now," she said, "I think it might have been a joke."

***

Xav stood in the yard and felt the ground go out from under him.

Not much. Not so that anyone would have seen. He'd learned, years ago, to keep his face still when the round went wrong. But inside, everything tilted, the way the floor of the summoning hall had tilted under him a year ago, the moment before it went soft.

*A joke. A thing he'd said a thousand times before.*

He could see it. He could see it as clearly as if he were there. A man standing on the red earth in the middle of a battle, with the ditch at his feet and the dead all round him and a demon coming, and under his breath, before he moved, the way you'd say it before the last round of a match you were about to win: some old Latin phrase, half joke, half prayer, that nobody here could ever understand.

The way Declan had said them. Every time. For nine years.

*It doesn't fit*, said the part of him that laid out plans. *It can't fit. The Hero's been the Hero for twenty years. Declan died in Malacca. You were at the funeral. You saw his father's face.*

*It doesn't matter if it fits*, said the other part of him. *You know how he moved.*

He stood there between the two of them for a long time. Petra didn't say anything. She just watched him, with her sword in her hand.

"Thank you," said Xav at last. His voice sounded normal. He was proud of that. "For telling me."

Petra nodded, once.

"Now stand," she said.

***

They stood.

The way they always did. A few paces apart, facing each other, practice swords held low, on the wet cobbles in the dawn. Petra perfectly still. Xav perfectly still. The blackbird still singing on the roof.

He'd done this a hundred times. He knew the shape of it. The first few minutes, when your body wanted to move and you had to tell it no. The middle, when your mind wanted to move instead, and went everywhere: what you'd have for breakfast, what Malcolm was writing in his book, what the light was doing under the bricks. And then, if you lasted that long, the long flat quiet at the end, where there was nothing but the other person, and the space between you, and waiting.

He went through all of it. He didn't fight it. He let it come and go, the way Petra had taught him in the winter. *Twenty minutes. Still. Better.*

But this morning, something was different.

He found that he wasn't thinking about breakfast. Or Malcolm, or the bricks. He was thinking about a computer lab at poly, at two in the morning, the fans humming, and the boy in the next seat with his crosshair on a doorway, not moving, while everyone in the call went mad around him. Ten seconds. Twenty. Forty.

*How do you know?*

*I don't. I just wait until I do.*

Xav breathed out. All the way out, until his chest was empty and still. And let the yard go quiet around him.

***

Somewhere along the way, the others came out.

He didn't see them. He didn't look. But he knew they were there, the way you know someone's come into a room behind you. Garrick first, with his mug of tea, on the step of the guild hall, the way he did most mornings. Then Azac, big and quiet, leaning in the gateway. Then Malcolm, with his book, not writing in it. Then John. Then RX, somewhere, nowhere, the way RX was always somewhere and nowhere now.

And Pryce, with his arm in a sling, standing at the back.

Nobody said anything. They just watched.

Half an hour. Xav didn't move. Neither did Petra.

An hour. The sun came up over the wall of the yard and lay across the cobbles, gold, and the dew started to steam. Xav didn't move. His legs ached. He let them ache. Petra didn't move either. He couldn't see her breathing.

An hour and a half.

*You're holding an angle*, he thought. *That's all this is. You've done it ten thousand times. You're holding an angle, and she's behind the door, and one of you is going to step out.*

*Don't step out first.*

*Don't step out at all.*

*Wait until you know.*

***

Two hours.

The yard had gone completely silent. The blackbird had stopped. Garrick had put his tea down on the step and was standing very still. Azac had come in from the gateway without noticing he'd done it.

And Xav found, standing there in the sun on the steaming cobbles with his practice sword low and his legs gone past aching into something else, that he could feel her.

Not see. Feel. The way Deston felt threads, maybe, or Hyvred saw light. A sort of pressure, coming off Petra, in the space between them. Her aura. Banked low, the way she always kept it, a deep steady heat like the coals at the bottom of a forge, waiting. He'd never felt it before. Not like this. Not from the outside.

And under it, very faint, something moving. A tightening. A gathering. The way a cat gathers before it jumps.

*She's going to move*, he thought.

He'd never once, in eight months, seen her move first.

He didn't think. He didn't decide. He felt the gap open, somewhere in the middle of him, the place Thea had written about in the winter, *one small line, in its language*. He'd been feeling for it for months. He'd never known what to put into it.

He knew now. It was the only word he'd ever really needed. The word he'd been waiting for since he sat down in that computer lab at seventeen. The word Declan never said out loud, because he never needed to. He just did it.

*Now.*

***

He moved once.

He felt it go out of him as he moved: the aura, all of it, up from the bottom of him and down his arm and into the practice sword in a single rush, like a held breath let go. He didn't push it. He didn't chase it. It just came, all at once, at exactly the right moment, the way a fish takes the hook when you've stopped pulling.

One step. One stroke. Low and from the side, under her guard, the way he'd have pre-aimed a doorway.

Petra's sword was moving too. She'd started a heartbeat after him. Half a heartbeat. He felt it come round, fast, so fast, faster than anyone he'd ever fought.

His sword touched her coat, at the ribs, before hers touched him.

Just touched. A tap. Wood on wool.

And then they were both standing still again, in the middle of the yard, with the sun on them. Close, now. Closer than they'd ever been, at the end of a bout. His sword at her ribs. Hers stopped a hand's width from his neck.

Nobody in the yard made a sound.

***

Petra looked down at the practice sword against her ribs.

Then she looked up at him. For a long time. With her quiet intense face that never gave anything away, and the long pale scar along her jaw, and something in her eyes that he'd never seen there before.

"Better," she said.

Xav let out a breath he felt like he'd been holding for two hours. Behind him, somebody, Azac probably, made a sound like a laugh trying to get out of a closed door.

But Petra wasn't finished. She was still looking at him. And she said, very quietly, so quietly that only he could hear:

"That's how he moved."

Xav stood very still.

"Once," said Petra. "Right before the other one could. As if he'd heard it coming." She lowered her sword. "I've been trying to do that for seven years." She looked at him a moment longer. Then something in her face did a thing he'd never seen it do. It *softened*. Just a little. Just at the corners. "You did it in eight months."

"I had a good teacher."

"You had two," said Petra.

She turned away, and walked back across the yard to the wall, and picked up her coat from where she'd hung it on a nail, and put it on. And didn't look back.

***

He opened his window in the yard, with the others crowding round him, Azac's big hand on his shoulder, John grinning, Malcolm already writing something in his book.

[ Xavier · Tier 1: Drawn ]

Eighth. After Thea and Maigo and Higu and Salty and Rachael and Hyvred and Ina.

"*Drawn*," said Azac, shaking him. "*Drawn!* Xav! You're the first of us! You're the first of *us*!"

"I know," said Xav.

"Say it louder! Rule three! Good news loudly!"

"I know," said Xav, a bit louder. And then, because Azac wouldn't stop shaking him, he started to laugh. He couldn't help it. He stood in the yard in the sun with his legs shaking and his practice sword in his hand and his friends round him, and laughed until his eyes ran.

Malcolm, at the edge of the group, closed his book.

"What did you write?" said Xav, when he could.

Malcolm looked at him over the top of it. "*IF Xav waits long enough,*" he said, "*THEN he wins.*" He tucked the book under his arm. "I wrote that in the winter. I've been waiting to tick it off."

***

He put it in the channel that afternoon. Just one line.

[ Xavier: Drawn. Eighth. I waited. ]

The channel went the way it always went now. Capitals from Maigo. *EIGHTH*. Tiger, *XAV!!!* Hyvred, *SCOPE SALUTES YOU*. Gabe, from Kingsferry, short: *about time bro*. And then Deston, under it: *He said "about time" but he's been grinning at the window for five minutes.*

Xav read all of it, sitting at the table in the common room at Mother Annick's, with his copied book in front of him and the sun going down outside.

He didn't write anything else.

He thought about it. He sat there with the channel open and his hand over it and thought about writing it. *Petra says the Black Blade talked to himself before he moved. In a language nobody here knew. Three or four words. The same every time. Like a joke.* Everyone would read it. Everyone would know what it meant. Gabe would. Deston would. Aleaka, Jared, Yan, all of them. It would go through the channel like a fire through dry grass.

And then everyone would have to stop and look at the thing nobody could make fit. *Twenty years. Malacca. The funeral.* And they'd argue, and some of them would believe, and some of them wouldn't, and some of them would get hurt, and it would all happen in forty lines at two in the morning, in a chat window, the way the worst things always did.

*I just wait until I do.*

He didn't know. Not yet. Not all the way. He knew how a man had moved, on the Redmarch, seven years ago. He knew a phrase on a sword. He knew a voice in a book. He didn't know how any of it could be true.

So he waited.

***

He wrote one letter that night. Not to the channel. To Kingsferry, sealed with a blue strip, to the two people in either world who'd known Declan longest.

*Deston. Gabe. Number seven.*

*Petra says the Black Blade talked to himself before he moved. Every time. Under his breath. Three or four words, in a language nobody here had ever heard. The same words, every time, like a man saying something he'd said a thousand times before.*

*She thought it was a prayer. Now she thinks it was a joke.*

*I'm not saying anything. I don't know anything. I'm just telling you.*

*You know who used to do that.*

He sealed it. Then he sat for a long time with the candle burning down, and the copied book open in front of him at *On Waiting*.

*Most people miss the first tug, because they are too busy pulling.*

*I have never once, in all my life, caught a fish by shouting at the water.*

He closed the book, and blew out the candle, and went to bed. And lay in the dark for a long time, thinking about a computer lab, and a doorway, and a boy in the next seat who never moved until he knew.
