The grey man didn't raise his voice. Hanarisu noticed that first. A hall full of frightened strangers, and he spoke to them the way you'd speak to someone at a bedside: quietly, so that they had to go still to hear him.

It worked. It was working on her, and she didn't like it.

She knew perhaps half the faces in the hall. By the end of the hour she would know every name, because she made a point of it.

"This is the kingdom of Aurelan," he said. "You are in Solmere, its capital, in the castle of King Severin. I advise the king. Most people here call me the Advisor, and you may do the same."

No name, then. She filed that away.

"Seven years ago, the demon kingdom of Kharvos crossed our eastern border with an army larger than any we had ever faced. It was broken at a place we now call the Redmarch." He paused. "It was broken by one man. But the demon king did not die. He is preparing again, and we have reason to believe that this time he will not come alone."

"Alone meaning what?" said Xav, from the far side of the hall.

"Meaning that there may be something else out there. Something that wants the same thing he does. If they come from two sides, we cannot hold both." The Advisor folded his hands back into his sleeves. "So we did something that has never been done. We asked for help from outside this world. We asked for *you*."

Nobody spoke. Somewhere to Hanarisu's left, Tiger whispered "*Hype*" with total sincerity, and somebody shushed him.

"You will have noticed your status," the Advisor went on.

He said it oddly. Every other word he'd spoken had come to her as clean, ordinary English, but that one arrived differently: carefully, with an accent, as if it were a borrowed word he'd learned to say. *Stay-tus.* Beside her, Cursiffe went very still. Then, without a sound, he unstoppered the little vial of ink on the chain around his neck, wet a fingertip, and wrote on the back of his own hand.

STATUS.

"It shows you who you are, what you can do, and how far you have come," the Advisor said. "Every one of you begins at the bottom, at Initiate. Above that there are ten tiers. You will choose a path. A mage climbs by how brightly they burn: Spark, then Candle, then Lantern, and on upward. A swordsman climbs by what their aura can do: Drawn, Tempered, Honed. Magic is easier to learn. Aura is harder, and it is worth it."

"Why not both?" said a quiet voice. Niko.

The Advisor looked at him for a moment. "Because no one has ever managed both."

Niko nodded slowly, as if that were an interesting answer rather than a final one.

"You can see your progress," the Advisor said. "We cannot. My people train for years without knowing how close they are to the next tier. You will know. That is why you will grow faster than any of us, and that is why we called you."

"And when we're done?" said Milli. Hanarisu felt him lean forward beside her, impatient, the way he got when someone was taking too long to reach the point. "You said we'd hear how to go home. So what's the catch?"

"There is one way that we know of," said the Advisor. "A mage of the tenth tier, Sun, could open the way back. In theory."

"In theory," said John. He had his arms folded and his chin up, the look of a man who'd spent his whole life winning arguments by asking the next question. "Okay. Has anyone ever reached Sun tier?"

The Advisor didn't look away from him. Hanarisu gave him that, at least.

"No one we can confirm."

The hall went silent.

It was a different silence from before. Before, it had been the silence of people listening. This was the silence of thirty-five people doing the same arithmetic at once, and getting the same answer. Over by the pillars, Malcolm hadn't moved at all. He was staring at the floor with the face of a man who'd finished the sum a minute ago.

Drew broke it, gently. "Okay," he said, to the room rather than the Advisor. "Okay. Breathe. That's not a no."

"It's not a yes either," said Gigichi.

"How long?"

The voice was deep and slow, and Hanarisu knew it: Yan, with a white snake around his throat, standing very straight in front of his friends. "How long would it take," he said, "to reach Sun?"

The Advisor opened his mouth. Then he closed it again.

Yan nodded once, as if he'd been answered. Hanarisu watched him turn back to his group and say something light, and watched his friends laugh, and watched none of them see what she saw cross his face before he turned. She didn't know what he'd left behind on Earth. She knew it was heavy.

"So we're soldiers," said Salty bluntly. Next to him, Wendy said, "If you go, I go. Doesn't mean I believe him," just loud enough to be heard.

"You took us," said Bee. She had her arms crossed and her wings pressed flat, and she said it the way she said everything, straight out. "That's the word for it. You kidnapped us."

"Yes," said the Advisor.

That was somehow worse than any excuse would have been.

"You didn't ask," said RX. He was fast and sharp and, Hanarisu would learn later, almost unreasonably fair. "We had lives. You didn't ask a single one of us."

"No. There was no way to ask." The Advisor's voice didn't change. "I won't pretend that makes it right. I decided that the lives of everyone in this kingdom were worth more than your consent. I may have been wrong. I will carry it either way."

"You had a hero last time, though," said a cheerful, puzzled voice: Calvin, asking it the way he asked everything, as if it had simply not occurred to anyone else. "The one-man-army guy. Why not ask him?"

For the first time, the Advisor hesitated.

"He has given enough," he said.

Hanarisu turned that over carefully. Not *he is dead*. Not *he is gone*. *He has given enough.* Somewhere out there, then, there was a man who had broken an army by himself and been allowed to stop. She wondered what kind of man that was. She wondered what kind of man the Advisor would protect by summoning thirty-five strangers instead.

"What are we being paid?" said Dennis.

There was a startled ripple of laughter, and Dennis looked around at all of them with genuine offence. "What? Somebody had to ask. If I'm going to be a soldier, I want to know my rate."

To Hanarisu's surprise, the Advisor answered him seriously. Lodging, food, equipment, and a stipend from the royal treasury. Dennis asked how much. The Advisor told him. Dennis asked whether that was per month or per year, and whether it rose with tier, and the Advisor, after a pause, said that it could. By the end of the exchange Dennis had negotiated a raise for all thirty-five of them, and the hall had stopped feeling quite so much like a funeral.

"Who else can see a status window?" Deston asked, when it had quietened. He was the tallest of them, courteous and interested, the kind of person who asked questions to understand things rather than to win them.

"Very few," said the Advisor. "Fewer than a hundred in all of Aurelan. And every one of them was taught by someone who was taught." He glanced, briefly, at nothing. "It started with a single man."

"Then why do you call it *status?*" said Xav. "That's not your word, is it?"

"It's an old word. The man who saw his window first gave it that name, and we have used his words ever since." The Advisor inclined his head. "Status. Inventory. Skills. We call them the Hero's words."

Hanarisu felt Cursiffe's eyes on her. She didn't look back. She didn't need to.

From the edge of the hall, Ina spoke for the first time, calm and clear, his mask hanging at his shoulder. "You took us without asking, and your soldiers have their hands on their swords. I'll hear you out." He let the pause sit. "From here."

"That's fair," said the Advisor.

Then Rachael, from Deston's group, said, "He fixed me."

Heads turned. Rachael flushed and lifted her chin anyway.

"I've been sick my whole life. My immune system's been broken since I was a kid. And he walked over and touched my wrist, and it's—" She pressed her hand flat to her chest. "It's gone. I can feel it's gone. So I don't know about the rest of you, but I'm going to hear him out."

Aleaka leaned close to Hanarisu's ear. "He means it," she breathed. "Hana, look at him. People who are lying don't look that *sorry.*"

Hanarisu looked at him. Aleaka was right. That was the most dangerous thing about him.

Around her, the hall was making up its mind. Higu told her friends, calm and certain, "We work with them, for now." Jared pushed off his pillar and said, "Alright. What's step one?" The big one in the far group, Azac, rolled his shoulders and said, "Fine. Let's go," and Hako, from the group with the horned girl, said with total confidence, "It'll work out," and somehow people seemed to believe him.

She kept her face pleasant, and said nothing.

***

Hyvred wasn't really listening anymore.

She was listening a little: enough to know about the demon king and the tiers and the way home nobody had ever opened. But mostly she was looking, because she couldn't stop. At the ceiling. At the soldiers, every one of them, all at once. At the Advisor.

Because there was something wrong with him.

She didn't know how she knew. She just *saw* it. Around everyone in the hall there was a kind of faint shimmer, a light just under the skin, so dim she'd thought at first it was her new eyes playing tricks. Some of the soldiers had more of it. The thirty-five of them had almost none.

The Advisor's had nearly gone out.

What was left of it guttered around him like the last of a candle in a draught, thin and grey and shaking, and when she looked at the floor beneath his feet she could see where it had gone: down into the carved lines, out along the spokes of the circle, all of it poured into the stone to bring them here.

"I cannot make you fight," the Advisor was saying. "I can only ask. If you are willing to—"

"Yes," said Hyvred.

Bee's head snapped round. "*Hyv.*"

"Sorry. Sorry, I didn't mean to interrupt." She hadn't meant to. It had just come out, the way things did. She could feel her face going hot, but she couldn't stop now. "It's just. You're almost out. The light in you, it's almost gone. You put it all into the floor, didn't you? To bring us here."

The Advisor turned and looked at her properly for the first time.

It was not the look he had given anyone else. It was the look of a man hearing something that should not be possible.

"What," he said quietly, "did you just say?"

***

The doors at the end of the hall opened, and the air got heavier.

Chris felt it before he saw anything: a pressure on his chest, on his shoulders, on the backs of his eyes, as if the whole room had sunk a few metres underwater. Next to him Hako swayed. Winter's hand closed hard on Chris's sleeve.

A man walked in.

He was tall and broad, grey at the temples, somewhere past sixty and moving like he'd never been told. He wore no crown, only a plain dark coat and a sword at his hip, and the soldiers along the walls went down on one knee as he passed, all of them, without a word. The weight in the air came with him. It came *from* him.

"That's the king," said Hako, in a strangled whisper.

"Love that for him," whispered Newish. "Watch this."

"Newish, *no*—"

She was already moving. Three steps, chin up, a grin on her face that Chris had seen on a hundred late-night Apex calls, usually just before she said something that started a fight. "Hi, your majesty! Big fan of the whole—"

A woman in the king's colours stepped into her path so fast Chris didn't see her move.

She didn't draw her sword. She didn't need to. She simply stood there, close enough that Newish had to stop or walk into her, one hand resting on the hilt, her expression one of profound and practiced boredom.

"Only the strong," the guard said, "may speak to the king."

Newish looked her up and down. "I'm very strong. Emotionally."

"Back up."

Newish backed up.

The king hadn't even glanced at her. He'd stopped at the foot of the dais and was looking at the thirty-five of them, one by one, the same way the Advisor had: as if he were weighing them. When his eyes reached Chris, Chris felt it like a hand on the top of his head.

"Thirty-five," the king said. His voice was rough and low and carried to every corner of the hall. "Good."

Then, to all of them: "Grow strong. Then we will speak."

He turned and left the way he'd come, and the weight went with him, and thirty-five people remembered how to breathe.

"Okay," said Chris, after a moment. "Okay. So that's the final boss of HR."

Winter let go of his sleeve. She was almost smiling.

***

Afterward, when the Advisor had told them that each group would be given a guide and a guard and sent somewhere different in the kingdom, and that they would leave in the morning, the hall began to empty in small, quiet clusters.

Thea stayed sitting on the floor, her status window open in front of her.

[ Althea · Tier 0: Initiate · Progress to Spark: 48% ]

Forty-eight percent. Everyone else she'd heard was at zero, or one or two. She didn't know what that meant yet. She didn't know what most of it meant.

But she knew how to read. And under the neat little panel, deep in the lattice of light, she'd found the line again: the one that didn't belong to her. She followed it carefully this time, thread by thread, the way she'd trace a bug through someone else's code, and at the bottom of it she found something like a label.

*Medium: one blade.*

Beside it was a second value, and that one she couldn't read at all. It wasn't written in the window's language, or in the lattice's. It was simply sealed, like a door with no handle.

Thea looked up, across the emptying hall, at the black sword still standing in the stone. Nobody had moved it. She had a feeling nobody here could.

"Whose are you?" she said under her breath.

The sword, being a sword, didn't answer.

***

Across the hall, Group 6 hadn't moved either.

"He said *status,*" said John. "In English. Not translated. You heard it."

"Inventory too," said RX. "And skills."

"The Hero's words," said Azac.

Malcolm didn't look up from the floor. "Somebody taught a medieval kingdom MMO vocabulary," he said. "Somebody who'd played a lot of games."

Xav was quiet for a long moment. Then he said, "Okay. That's the first thing we find out."

***

Yan walked out with his friends around him, and Maigo talking, and Salty arguing with Wendy about whether a greatsword would be too heavy, and Niko already asking a passing soldier what a Spark felt like. He laughed when they laughed. He answered when they asked him things.

He didn't tell any of them that the Advisor hadn't answered his question. They'd all heard it. He didn't need to.

He just carried it, quietly, the way he carried everything.

***

Hanarisu was one of the last to leave.

At the door she glanced back once, at the grey man standing alone on the dais with what was left of his light. He was looking at the golden-horned girl as she went out with her friends, and his face was very thoughtful.

"Honey?" said Milli. "You coming?"

"Yes," she said, and smiled at him, and took his hand.

Nobody in the hall had any idea what she thought. That was exactly how she wanted it.
